In Texas, your **Option Period** is often just **5 to 10 days long**. During that tiny window, you have to schedule a general home inspection, evaluate foundation and roof specialists if needed, read the seller's disclosures, and negotiate repair amendments before your unrestricted right to terminate expires.

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, most residential sellers must deliver either the **TREC OP-H Seller's Disclosure Notice** or the **Texas Realtors (TXR-1406)** disclosure form. While TXR-1406 includes extra questions (such as five-year insurance claim history and remediation details), both forms leave critical blind spots if you don't know how to read between the lines.

---

## 1. Expansive Clay Soil & "Prior Foundation Repair With Transferable Warranty"
Across Dallas-Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, and Houston, expansive blackland clay swells during heavy rain and shrinks during summer droughts, cracking post-tension and slab-on-grade foundations.
- **What sellers write:** *"Foundation leveled in 2019—lifetime transferable warranty included."*
- **Why buyers get burned:** Many "lifetime" pier warranties exclude plumbing leaks beneath the slab, charge $500–$1,500 transfer/re-adjustment fees, cover only the exact perimeter piers installed in 2019 (not interior piers), or were issued by companies that have since gone out of business. Always check the pier map, transfer deadline (often 30 days after closing!), and perform a **hydrostatic / sewer camera test** after any foundation work.

## 2. Hail Damage & Unclosed Roof Insurance Claims (CLUE History)
Texas leads the nation in severe hail claims. On the TREC OP-H form, sellers must disclose known roof defects, **but TREC OP-H asks far fewer questions about past insurance claims than TXR-1406**.
- **The hidden trap:** A seller collects a $16,000 ACV insurance payout after a hailstorm, replaces two broken vent boots for $400, and lists the home without replacing the bruised shingles. When your insurer inspects the roof 30 days after closing, they issue a **Notice of Cancellation** unless you replace the roof out of pocket ($14,000–$28,000).

## 3. Cast-Iron Drain Pipes Under Pre-1980 Slabs
If you are buying a 1950s–1979 ranch home in Texas, the underground drain lines beneath the concrete slab are almost certainly **cast iron**. After 50+ years, cast iron corrodes along the bottom ("channeling"), allowing tree roots and soil to back up sewage—and leaking water directly swells the clay under the slab.
- Tunneling under a Texas slab to replace deteriorated cast-iron plumbing routinely costs **$12,000 to $30,000+**, and standard home inspections do **not** include a sewer scope.

## 4. Municipal Utility District (MUD) & PID Notices
Texas has no state income tax, which means property taxes carry the load—and **MUD (Municipal Utility District)** or **PID (Public Improvement District)** assessments in newer suburban developments can add **0.50% to 1.40%+** on top of county and school district tax rates.
- Under **Texas Water Code § 49.452**, failing to deliver the statutory MUD Notice prior to closing gives buyers powerful termination remedies, yet buyers routinely sign the MUD addendum electronically without calculating the **$300 to $700/month** payment impact.

## 5. French Drains, Surface Grading & Floodplain Updates
Look closely at **Section 4 / Flooding & Drainage** on the Texas disclosure notice. Texas law expanded flood disclosure requirements (TREC Flood Hazard Information) after Hurricane Harvey, requiring sellers to disclose:
- Present flood insurance coverage
- Any prior Reservoir/Floodwater/Surface runoff water penetration into any structure
- Past FEMA or flood-damage insurance claims
Even if a home sits in **FEMA Zone X**, localized street or lot drainage ("improperly graded swales") can flood a slab home during a 4-inch Texas downpour.

## 6. Wood-Destroying Insects (Subterranean & Formosan Termites)
From Gulf Coast Formosan colonies in Houston and Corpus Christi to subterranean colonies across North and Central Texas, check both "Active Infestation" and "Previous Treatment" checkboxes—and cross-reference with a licensed **Texas TDA WDI Report**. See our [5 Seller Disclosure Red Flags Guide](/blog/5-disclosure-red-flags/) for common euphemisms sellers use to disguise untreated wood rot.

## 7. Unpermitted Patio Enclosures, Garage Conversions & Attic HVAC Work
Many Texas suburbs and municipalities strictly enforce impervious-cover limits, HOA architectural approvals, and mechanical permits for water heaters and attic furnace replacements. Improvised attic water-heater pans without proper exterior drain lines are ticking ceiling-collapse hazards.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Texas Seller Disclosures

### What if the seller hasn't delivered the Seller's Disclosure Notice yet?
Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008(f)**, if the contract is signed before the seller delivers the required Seller's Disclosure Notice, the buyer may terminate the contract **for any reason within 7 days after receiving the notice** (or before closing, whichever is earlier) and recover their earnest money—even after the Option Period expires!

### What is the difference between TREC OP-H and TXR-1406?
**TREC OP-H** tracks the minimum statutory questions required by the Texas Property Code and can be used by any seller (including FSBOs). **TXR-1406** (copyrighted by Texas Realtors for member agents) includes all statutory items *plus* deeper buyer-protective questions covering past insurance claims, remediation contractors, and environmental conditions.
