## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Syracuse and Central New York

Syracuse’s heavy winter snowpack, spring meltwater saturation, and century-old timber framing create fertile ground for subterranean wood-destroying organisms.

### Why Syracuse Housing Stock and Lake-Effect Hydrology Intensify Pest Hazards

Several distinct environmental and structural dynamics elevate termite vulnerability across Syracuse:

- **Lake-Effect Snowmelt and Alluvial Saturation:** Syracuse consistently ranks among the snowiest metropolitan areas in the United States. When heavy lake-effect snow melts rapidly in March and April, the high clay content in Onondaga County soils prevents rapid drainage. The water table rises, soaking subgrade foundation walls and driving subterranean colonies upward toward dry structural framing.
- **Porous Stone and Unreinforced Masonry Foundations:** In historic neighborhoods like Strathmore, Sedgwick, and Westcott, homes built between 1890 and 1935 rest on rough fieldstone, native Onondaga limestone, or multi-wythe brick foundations. Decades of freeze-thaw cycles cause lime mortar to disintegrate, creating unsealed internal channels where termites travel directly from wet soil into structural sill plates.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Syracuse Neighborhoods:**
  - *Sedgwick & Strathmore:* Expansive historic homes with intricate exterior timber half-timbering, massive covered front verandas, and full basements where wood wainscoting and finished plaster walls conceal foundation mudsills.
  - *Westcott & University Hill:* Early 20th-century wood-frame colonials and Queen Anne multi-family student rentals. Heavy wear, neglected downspouts, and wood siding extending below grade level invite subterranean entry.
  - *Eastwood & Tipperary Hill:* Post-WWI bungalows and Cape Cods with low foundation clearance, enclosed front sunrooms, and direct earth-to-wood contact at basement exterior bulkheads.

### Biology of the Eastern Subterranean Termite in Upstate New York

Homebuyers must understand the behavior and destructive capabilities of **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)**:

- **Subterranean Soil Nesting & Mud Shelter Tubes:** Subterranean termites require continuous soil moisture to survive. To cross exposed foundation stone, concrete blocks, or metal chimney bases, they build earthen mud shelter tubes composed of soil particles, chewed cellulose, and saliva.
- **Cryptic Interior Timber Feeding:** Termites consume the soft springwood fibers inside structural framing along the grain, leaving the outer painted surface completely intact. A structural sill plate or primary floor joist can be completely hollowed out while appearing solid during a cursory visual walkthrough.
- **Spring Swarming Events:** Between April and June, warm sunny days following heavy spring rains trigger nuptial swarming. Winged reproductive termites (alates) emerge in swarms to establish new colonies. Discovering piles of shed wings on basement window sills or near the central boiler is a critical indicator of an active structural infestation.

## New York Real Estate Disclosure Laws: RPL § 462 and the Mandatory PCDS

New York real estate disclosure law recently underwent the most significant statutory transformation in decades.

### The Historic Overhaul of the Property Condition Disclosure Act (PCDA)

Under **New York Real Property Law (RPL) § 462**, sellers of residential real property must complete and deliver the statutory **Property Condition Disclosure Statement (PCDS)**. Historically, New York sellers could pay a nominal $500 credit at closing to avoid completing the form. However, under landmark statutory amendments:

- **Elimination of the $500 Credit Loophole:** Sellers can no longer opt out. Delivering the completed 48-question PCDS is now mandatory across all non-exempt residential sales in New York State.
- **Wood-Destroying Insect Mandates (Questions 21, 22, and 23):** Specifically requires sellers to disclose:
  - *"Are there any past or present infestations of termites, wood-destroying insects, or dry rot?"*
  - *"Are there any past or present structural repairs made to address insect or pest damage?"*
  - *"Is the property currently under an active pest control warranty or treatment contract?"*
- **Actual Knowledge and Fraud:** While the seller’s duty is based on actual knowledge, New York law establishes that a seller who intentionally conceals known active infestations, paints over mud tubes, or makes fraudulent misrepresentations on the PCDS is liable for actual damages, repair costs, and intentional fraud.

### NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Reports in New York

Standard New York home inspections conducted under New York Department of State (DOS) licensing regulations specifically disclaim wood-destroying insect identification. Buyers must retain an independent, certified pest technician licensed by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (NYSDEC) who issues an **NPMA-33** report:

- **Section II Findings:** Discloses active termite presence, inactive damage, or evidence of previous subterranean chemical drilling in foundation walls.
- **Section III Obstructions:** Documents inaccessible areas—including finished basement walls, wood paneling, drop ceilings, insulation batts along rim joists, and low-clearance crawlspaces.
- **Inspection Contingency Windows:** Under standard Central New York (GSAR) purchase contracts, buyers generally have 5 to 7 business days to complete structural inspections and issue formal repair demands.

### What Syracuse Sellers Strategically Reveal vs. Conceal

In Upstate New York transactions, sellers frequently employ subtle phrasing:

- *"Perimeter barrier treated in 2021 as standard preventive maintenance."* (Masks the fact that termites were swarming out of the basement coal bin).
- *"Basement floor framing reinforced during laundry renovation."* (Conceals that structural floor joists were hollowed out by termites and sistered with undersized 2x4s).
- *"Property sold strictly As-Is."* (Under New York law, an 'as-is' clause does not protect a seller who actively conceals known latent structural defects or commits fraud on the mandatory PCDS).

### Fragmented Red Flags Across Disparate Documents

Critical termite warnings are rarely centralized. Instead, vital clues are fragmented across separate documents:

- The General Home Inspector writes on page 39: *"High moisture levels and mineral efflorescence noted along west fieldstone foundation wall near exterior driveway."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatment on Question 21 of the PCDS, adding: *"Routine perimeter spray performed by previous owner over 8 years ago; no current issues."*
- The NPMA-33 Pest Report notes under Section III: *"West foundation wall completely covered by wooden workbench and pegboard storage; visual inspection obstructed."*

Independently, none of these notes seem alarming. But synthesized together, they tell a dangerous story: driveway runoff soaked the west fieldstone wall, dissolving mortar and allowing subterranean termites to hollow out the concealed structural sill plate behind the workbench.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Syracuse

### 1. Are termites common in cold Syracuse, New York?
Yes. Despite severe lake-effect snow and freezing winter temperatures, Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*) are widespread across Onondaga County. They survive winter by tunneling below the 48-inch frost line, re-emerging during spring snowmelts to attack wet timber foundations.

### 2. Can a Syracuse seller still pay a $500 credit to avoid disclosing termite damage?
No. Effective March 2024, New York State law eliminated the $500 opt-out credit under RPL § 462. All non-exempt residential sellers must now complete and deliver the statutory 48-question Property Condition Disclosure Statement (PCDS).

### 3. What legal liability does a New York seller have for concealing termite damage?
Under New York Real Property Law § 462 and New York common law fraud, if a buyer proves that a seller had actual knowledge of an active infestation or structural termite defect and knowingly failed to disclose it on the mandatory PCDS form, the seller is liable for actual repair damages, court costs, and potential fraud penalties.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance pay for termite structural repairs in Central New York?
No. Homeowners insurance policies across New York strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, insects, or vermin. Insurers classify insect infestations as preventable homeowner maintenance, leaving all reconstruction expenses entirely to the property owner.
