### How Metro Detroit Freeze-Thaw Cycles and Split-Level Basements Fuel Infestations

Several localized environmental and architectural characteristics make Sterling Heights homes uniquely vulnerable to subterranean termite invasions:

- **Severe Great Lakes Freeze-Thaw Cycles and Frost Heave:** Sterling Heights experiences cold winters with frost lines penetrating up to 42 inches into the soil. As high-moisture glacial clay soils freeze and thaw, they exert intense lateral hydrostatic pressure against basement foundation walls. Over decades, this cyclical movement creates horizontal mortar line cracks in cinder block foundations and vertical shear cracks in poured concrete walls, establishing direct subterranean highways for foraging termites.
- **1960s and 1970s Split-Level and Tri-Level Architecture:** Thousands of Sterling Heights residences constructed between 1965 and 1980 feature split-level or tri-level designs. These architectural layouts feature multiple foundation elevations—combining full basements, partial crawlspaces, and concrete slab-on-grade family rooms. The seams where these varying foundation levels connect create multiple cold joints and expansion cracks. Subterranean termites easily penetrate these concealed foundation seams.
- **Finished Basements Concealing Perimeter Foundation Walls:** In Sterling Heights, finished lower levels are standard across suburban homes, functioning as home theaters, children’s playrooms, and home offices. Homeowners routinely install framed stud walls, fiberglass insulation, and drywall directly against concrete foundation walls. Subterranean termites construct mud tubes through hairline foundation settlement cracks, breaching bottom wood plates and traveling upward into floor joists completely out of sight.
- **High Seasonal Groundwater and Clinton River Drainage:** Central Macomb County features heavy glacial clay soils with poor natural drainage. During wet Michigan springs, snowmelt and heavy rains saturate the soil around foundation perimeters. Subterranean termites require continuous moisture to prevent dehydration; the damp soils surrounding Sterling Heights basements provide an ideal habitat year-round.

### Pest Biology: Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)

Homebuyers in Macomb County contend primarily with the **Eastern Subterranean Termite**:

- **Subterranean Survival Strategies:** Subterranean termites do not freeze to death during harsh Michigan winters. When surface soils freeze, colonies descend deep beneath the frost line. Even more frequently, they migrate toward heated basement foundation walls, warm sewer lines, and sub-slab perimeter gravel beds, remaining active all winter inside heated crawlspaces and basements.
- **Shelter Mud Tubes:** To protect their soft bodies from the dry, ambient indoor air of heated homes, worker termites construct protective mud tubes composed of soil, saliva, and fecal matter. They build these tubes across interior basement block walls, chimney bases, and plumbing pipes to reach wood floor joists.
- **Destructive Hidden Feasting:** Termites consume the cellulose within wood framing from the inside out, following the softer spring growth rings. Because they leave the exterior wood surface intact, load-bearing floor joists, band boards, and wall studs often look completely sound until a probe penetrates hollow wood or a floor begins to bounce and sag.

## Michigan Real Estate Disclosure Laws: MCL 565.951 and the Seller's Disclosure Act

Michigan provides statutory consumer protections for residential real estate purchasers through the **Michigan Seller Disclosure Act (Public Act 92 of 1993, MCL 565.951 et seq.)**.

### The Statutory Framework: The Michigan Seller's Disclosure Statement (SDS)

Under MCL 565.957, sellers of residential 1-to-4 unit properties must complete and deliver the standardized Michigan **Seller's Disclosure Statement (SDS)** to prospective buyers before an offer is submitted:

- **Section: Structural Conditions and Basement Water:** Sellers must explicitly disclose known foundation cracks, basement settlement, or water seepage history. Because termites in Sterling Heights heavily exploit moisture and basement cracks, these answers must be closely cross-referenced against pest findings.
- **Section: Environmental and Pest Conditions:** The SDS mandates that sellers disclose:
  - Any history of termites, carpenter ants, or wood-destroying insect infestations.
  - Prior pest treatments, chemical barriers, or structural repairs conducted to address insect damage.
  - Whether there are active pest control service contracts or warranties in effect.
- **Good Faith Standard under MCL 565.960:** The Seller Disclosure Act requires sellers to complete the disclosure in "good faith," defined as honesty in fact. A seller cannot deliberately conceal known defects by claiming lack of knowledge or failing to disclose prior treatments that occurred during their ownership.
- **The Doctrine of Silent Fraud: *M&D, Inc. v. McConkey* (231 Mich. App. 22, 585 N.W.2d 33):** Under landmark Michigan case law, when a seller makes a partial or misleading disclosure that gives a false impression, or fails to disclose a known latent defect when asked a direct question, the seller is liable for "silent fraud" (fraudulent concealment). An "as-is" clause in a Michigan real estate contract will not insulate a seller from claims of intentional misrepresentation or silent fraud.

### The Critical Limitation of General Home Inspection Reports

General home inspectors in Michigan are not licensed structural pest control operators. Standard Michigan home inspection agreements contain strict liability disclaimers:
- *"This inspection is visual only and does NOT cover wood-destroying organisms, termites, carpenter ants, or hidden structural defects behind finished basement walls, drop ceilings, or subflooring."*

When buyers skim past these disclaimers late at night, they assume a clean general inspection report guarantees a sound home. In Sterling Heights’ split-level homes with finished basements, subterranean termites frequently devour rim joists and subfloors completely undetected behind drywall and ceiling tiles.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Sterling Heights

### 1. Does Michigan law require a termite inspection when buying a home in Sterling Heights?
Michigan state law does not legally mandate a termite inspection for cash real estate sales. However, major mortgage lenders (particularly for VA and FHA loans) routinely require an official Wood-Destroying Insect inspection report (form NPMA-33) performed by a certified Michigan pesticide applicator before approving loan proceeds. Even for conventional purchases, ordering an independent WDI inspection on a split-level or tri-level home in Macomb County is an essential protection.

### 2. What must sellers disclose about wood-destroying insects under the Michigan Seller Disclosure Act?
Under the Michigan Seller Disclosure Act (MCL 565.951 et seq.), sellers must explicitly disclose any known active infestations of wood-destroying insects (including termites and carpenter ants), any history of past infestations, any structural damage caused by wood-destroying organisms, and any previous chemical treatments or ongoing pest warranties. Failure to disclose known pest history constitutes silent fraud under Michigan law.

### 3. Why are split-level and bi-level homes in Macomb County particularly susceptible to termites?
Split-level and bi-level homes feature multi-tiered foundation levels combining full basements, partial crawlspaces, and concrete slabs. The seams where these different foundation levels connect create multiple cold joints and expansion cracks. Subterranean termites exploit these foundation seams to enter the home undetected behind finished drywall and paneling.

### 4. How can Sterling Heights buyers protect themselves from hidden termite damage during escrow?
Buyers should:
- Order a separate, dedicated Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) inspection by a licensed Michigan pesticide applicator rather than relying solely on a general home inspector.
- Scrutinize the Michigan SDS for any mentions of "prior pest treatments," "settlement cracks," or "basement seepage repairs," and demand copies of all historical repair receipts and warranties.
- Request permission to inspect behind accessible basement ceiling tiles and utility closets adjacent to perimeter foundation walls.
