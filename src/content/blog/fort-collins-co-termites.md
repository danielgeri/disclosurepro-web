## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Fort Collins and Larimer County

Fort Collins's geographic location at the base of the Rocky Mountain foothills features unique soil geology, cold winters, hot dry summers, and extensive residential landscape irrigation. These factors combine to create localized micro-habitats where subterranean termites thrive.

### Why Fort Collins Housing Stock and Front Range Geology Elevate Pest Risk

A combination of local building practices, expansive soil mechanics, and urban microclimates increases termite risks in Fort Collins homes:

- **Bentonite Expansive Soils and Foundation Fractures:** Larimer County is known for expansive bentonite clay soils that swell when saturated and shrink during dry periods. This soil movement exerts lateral pressure against basement foundation walls, generating micro-fractures in concrete slabs and stem walls. Subterranean termites require an opening of only 1/32 of an inch to enter finished basement framing.
- **Heated Basements and Year-Round Nesting:** Unlike surface pests that freeze during Colorado winters, Western Subterranean Termites thrive beneath homes with heated basements and finished lower levels. Heated concrete slabs keep the underlying soil warm year-round, allowing termite colonies to feed on structural mudsills, floor joists, and stud framing uninterrupted throughout the winter.
- **Finished Basements Obscuring Structural Timber:** The vast majority of single-family homes across Fossil Creek, Rigden Farm, and Bucking Horse feature finished basements with drywall, baseboards, and carpet. Structural mudsills, rim joists, and concrete foundation walls are completely covered, allowing subterranean termite mud tubes to remain hidden behind finished walls for years.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Fort Collins Neighborhoods:**
  - *Old Town & Avery Park:* 1890s–1940s craftsman bungalows and historic brick homes with unsealed crawl spaces, direct wood-to-earth contact at porch stairs, and aging post-and-beam floor systems.
  - *Warren Shores & Clarendon Hills:* 1970s–1990s homes near urban lakes and irrigation canals where high groundwater tables create localized soil moisture pockets that attract subterranean colonies.
  - *Fossil Creek & Bucking Horse:* 2000s–2020s two-story homes with walk-out basements where exterior landscaping mulch and flower beds are placed directly against exterior stucco or stone siding.

### Pest Biology: Western Subterranean Termites in Colorado

Fort Collins homebuyers face threats primarily from one destructive wood-destroying insect species:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Social insects that nest in underground colonies deep in the soil to avoid extreme surface temperatures. They construct earthen mud shelter tubes over concrete foundation stems and basement walls to reach structural wood. Subterranean termites consume the soft springwood along the timber grain, hollowing out structural framing while leaving an intact outer veneer of wood or paint that masks extensive internal devastation.

## Colorado Real Estate Disclosure Laws: CREC Form SPD19 and Common Law Duties

Colorado real estate transactions operate under statutory disclosure requirements established by the Colorado Real Estate Commission (CREC) and robust judicial precedent governing latent property defects.

### The Colorado Seller's Property Disclosure (Form SPD19)

Under Colorado Real Estate Commission rules, sellers of residential real estate are expected to complete the standard **Seller’s Property Disclosure (Residential) (Form SPD19)**:

- **Section E (Environmental Conditions & Pests):** Specifically requires the seller to disclose:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present infestation or damage caused by termites, carpenter ants, or other wood-destroying pests?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any pest treatments or chemical barrier applications conducted on the property?"*
  - *"Is there an active transferable pest warranty or service contract?"*
- **Section C (Structural & Foundation Conditions):** Inquires into known foundation movement, basement wall cracks, floor heaving, and moisture intrusion—conditions closely tied to termite access and subterranean nesting.

### The Judicial Duty to Disclose Latent Defects (*Cohen v. Vivian*)

Under Colorado common law established by the landmark Supreme Court decision ***Cohen v. Vivian*, 141 Colo. 443 (1960)**:
- Sellers have an affirmative legal duty to disclose latent, known material defects affecting property value that are not readily observable to a prospective purchaser.
- An "AS IS" clause in a standard Colorado Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate does not protect a seller from liability for fraudulent concealment or failure to disclose known structural pest infestations or concealed timber damage.

### Professional Pest Inspections in Colorado

While standard home inspectors evaluate general systems, a specialized **Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Pest Inspection** is performed by a licensed pesticide applicator certified under the **Colorado Department of Agriculture (CDA)**. The inspection report details:
- **Active Infestation:** Visible presence of live Western Subterranean termites or active mud tubes along basement stems.
- **Prior Damage:** Evidence of past structural wood damage, hollowed framing members, or chemical drill patterns in concrete slabs.
- **Conducive Conditions:** High soil-to-wood contact, mulch piled against siding, basement moisture seepage, and unvented crawl spaces.

### What Fort Collins Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers in Larimer County often use ambiguous phrasing on disclosure forms:
- *"Preventative perimeter pest treatment applied in 2022."* (Conceals that chemical perimeter trenching was applied after termites breached basement framing).
- *"Basement drywall patched after minor settling crack."* (Downplays the structural sistering of termite-hollowed studs behind the drywall).
- *"Property monitored under annual pest contract."* (Fails to disclose that bait stations were installed in response to an active termite swarm in the basement).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Crucial Clues in 200-Page Closing Packets

During Colorado's standard inspection objection period under the CREC Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate, buyers must review an extensive volume of documentation:
- CREC Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate and Inspection Objection/Resolution Addenda
- Colorado Seller's Property Disclosure (SPD19)
- 40-to-70-page Comprehensive General Home Inspection Report
- Colorado Licensed WDO Pest Inspection Report
- Larimer County Property Tax Records, HOA Disclosures, and Title Commitments

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Material structural hazards rarely appear as an explicit, single warning. Instead, critical indicators are scattered across separate disclosures:
- The Home Inspector writes on page 39: *"Minor hairline settlement crack and elevated moisture detected in northwest basement concrete slab."*
- The WDO Pest Inspector notes under Inaccessible Zones: *"Finished drywall and carpet in basement prevented visual inspection of foundation sill plates."*
- The Seller checks "No" to active infestations on the SPD19 disclosure, but writes under maintenance: *"Repaired basement sump pump and applied perimeter insect barrier in 2023."*

When read separately, each detail seems routine. But when cross-referenced, the full picture emerges: high groundwater activated subterranean termites, which entered through the basement slab crack and hollowed out the structural mudsill behind the finished drywall.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Fort Collins

### 1. Are termites a real problem in Fort Collins, Colorado?
Yes. Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) are active across Fort Collins, Larimer County, and the Front Range. While Colorado’s surface winters are cold, subterranean termites nest deep below the frost line and exploit heated basement slabs, crawl spaces, and foundation cracks to feed on structural timber year-round.

### 2. What does Colorado law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under the Colorado Real Estate Commission (CREC) Seller's Property Disclosure (SPD19) and common law fraud precedents (*Cohen v. Vivian*), sellers must disclose all known past or present termite infestations, structural wood damage, previous treatments, and active pest control contracts.

### 3. Why are termites so difficult to detect in Fort Collins homes?
The majority of homes in Fort Collins feature finished basements where framing mudsills, rim joists, and concrete walls are concealed behind drywall, insulation, and carpet. Subterranean termites enter through sub-slab cracks and feed inside structural timber without creating visible external signs until severe damage has occurred.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs in Colorado?
No. Standard Colorado homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites and other wood-destroying insects. Insurance carriers treat insect damage as a preventable maintenance issue, leaving property owners responsible for all structural repair and restoration costs.
