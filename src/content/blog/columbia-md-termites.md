## Maryland Disclosure Laws: Real Property § 10-702 & MDA WDI Inspections

Maryland enforces a distinctive dual-track disclosure statute that catches out-of-state buyers off guard if they do not read the seller's election carefully before ratifying the contract.

### 1. Maryland Property Disclosure vs. Disclaimer Statement (§ 10-702)

Under **Maryland Code, Real Property § 10-702**, sellers of single-family residential properties must deliver the statutory **Maryland Residential Property Disclosure and Disclaimer Statement** on or before entering into a contract of sale. Sellers choose **one** of two options:

1. **Property Condition Disclosure Statement:** The seller answers a detailed checklist based on actual knowledge, explicitly addressing past or present wood-destroying insects (termites, carpenter ants, powderpost beetles), prior pest treatments, and structural framing damage.
2. **Property Condition Disclaimer Statement ("AS IS"):** The seller makes **no representations or warranties** about property condition—**EXCEPT for known latent defects**.

**The Critical Latent Defect Rule:** Even when a Columbia seller chooses the Disclaimer ("AS IS") path, Maryland law strictly requires disclosing any known **latent defect**—defined as a material defect that a purchaser would not reasonably be expected to discover through a careful visual inspection and that poses a direct threat to the health or safety of the purchaser or occupants. Structurally compromised load-bearing sill plates, hollowed floor joists, or concealed active infestations that a seller patched over violate this statutory duty.

**5-Day Statutory Right of Rescission (§ 10-702(g)):** If the seller fails to deliver the Disclosure or Disclaimer Statement on or before contract ratification, the buyer holds a statutory right to rescind the contract in writing within **5 days** after receiving the statement (prior to loan application or closing) and receive a full refund of earnest money.

### 2. Why You Must Add the Maryland Realtors WDI Addendum & Hire an MDA-Licensed Inspector

Standard Maryland home inspectors licensed through the **Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC)** visually inspect accessible components, but standard home inspection contracts explicitly disclaim wood-destroying insect certification.

Always attach the **Maryland Realtors Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) Inspection Addendum** to your offer and hire an independent pest operator licensed by the **Maryland Department of Agriculture (MDA)** to issue an official **NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect Inspection Report**:
- **Section II & III:** Records live insects, dead mud tubes, frass, damaged structural wood, and signs of previous chemical trenching or bait systems.
- **Section IV (Obstructed & Inaccessible Areas):** Lists every concealed zone the technician could not inspect—including finished walkout basement walls, insulated rim joists, and enclosed under-deck crawlspaces.
- **Lender Conditions:** VA loans (and FHA/Conventional loans when an appraiser flags wood decay) require a clean NPMA-33 report and active infestation clearance prior to underwriting approval. ## Frequently Asked Questions About Termites in Columbia, MD

### Are subterranean termites really a major risk in Columbia and Howard County?
Yes. Columbia's preserved hardwood forests, thick leaf litter, damp micaceous Piedmont soils, and 1960s–1980s timber and walkout-basement construction create heavy year-round pressure from **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)**. Colonies forage continuously through soil and decaying tree roots and can hollow out load-bearing sill plates in under a year without surface signs.

### What is the difference between a Maryland Disclosure and a Disclaimer Statement?
Under **Maryland Real Property Code § 10-702**, sellers can complete either a **Disclosure Statement** (answering specific defect questions based on actual knowledge) or a **Disclaimer Statement** (selling "AS IS" without condition warranties). Crucially, even under a Disclaimer, Maryland law still requires sellers to disclose known **latent defects** that are not discoverable through a careful visual inspection and threaten occupant health or safety.

### Does a standard Maryland home inspection cover termites?
No. Home inspectors licensed by the **Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC)** visually inspect accessible components, and their agreements explicitly exclude wood-destroying insect certification. Always hire a pest technician licensed by the **Maryland Department of Agriculture (MDA)** to issue an official **NPMA-33 WDI Report**.

### Why are contemporary homes in Wilde Lake and Harper's Choice especially vulnerable to termite damage?
Late-1960s and 1970s contemporary homes in Wilde Lake and Harper's Choice frequently combine vertical cedar siding installed close to soil grade, wooded shaded lots, walkout lower levels built into hillsides, and cantilevered wooden balconies. These details create hidden wood-to-earth contact and moisture traps behind deck ledgers where subterranean termites enter undetected.
