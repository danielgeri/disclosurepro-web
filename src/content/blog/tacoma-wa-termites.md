## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Dampwood Termites in Tacoma

Many Pacific Northwest buyers assume termites are strictly a warm-climate issue, associating local wood damage solely with carpenter ants or moisture rot. In reality, Western subterranean termites and Pacific dampwood termites cause millions of dollars in structural damage across Pierce County every year.

### Why Tacoma's Maritime Climate and Historic Housing Stock Accelerate Wood Decay

Several regional, climatic, and structural dynamics make residential real estate across Tacoma particularly prone to wood-destroying insect activity:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The primary subterranean species in Pierce County. Living in expansive underground colonies, worker termites tunnel through Tacoma's damp glacial till soils, entering residential structures through hairline cracks in concrete foundation stem walls, unsealed mudsills, and plumbing penetrations.
- **Pacific Dampwood Termites (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*):** As the largest termite species in North America, dampwood termites thrive in the cool, humid coastal conditions of Puget Sound. They do not require soil contact, establishing colonies directly in moisture-compromised structural wood such as roof eaves, crawlspace sills, and deck ledger boards.
- **Historic Post-and-Beam Crawlspace Foundations:** A significant portion of Tacoma's housing stock in neighborhoods like the North End and Lincoln District features raised post-and-beam crawlspaces built between 1890 and 1930. Inadequate cross-ventilation, combined with missing ground vapor barriers, keeps crawlspace wood moisture levels well above 20%, creating an ideal habitat for subterranean colonies.
- **Old-Growth Douglas Fir and Cedar Framing:** While early-20th-century Northwest timber framing is exceptionally dense, a century of persistent roof runoff, foundation settling, and coastal fog softens outer timber layers, allowing termites to hollow out load-bearing members.
- **Earth-to-Wood Contact on Decks and Porches:** Historic Tacoma homes frequently feature covered front porches and wooden staircases with structural support posts set directly into soil or concrete footings without moisture standoffs. Subterranean termites use these wood posts as direct, concealed bridges into main structural floor framing.

### Biology and Mechanics: How Termites Compromise Tacoma Homes

Western subterranean termites cannot survive exposure to open air. To cross exposed concrete foundation stem walls, stone footings, or chimney masonry, worker termites build earthen shelter tubes (mud tubes) made of soil, saliva, and digested cellulose. Once inside, they feed along the softer springwood growth rings, hollowing out structural sills and joists from within.

Pacific dampwood termites, on the other hand, enter structural wood directly where rainwater intrusion or plumbing leaks maintain high wood moisture. They bore large, irregular galleries across the wood grain, often plugging external entry points with distinctive oval fecal pellets. Because both species consume wood internally, structural framing can lose 80% of its load-bearing capacity while looking perfectly sound on the surface.

## Washington Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Tacoma Buyers Must Know

Washington State provides strong statutory consumer protections for residential real estate purchasers through mandatory seller disclosure questionnaires.

### Washington RCW 64.06 and the NWMLS Form 17

Under **Revised Code of Washington (RCW) § 64.06.020**, sellers of residential real property must deliver a completed **Seller Disclosure Statement (NWMLS Form 17)** to the buyer within five business days of mutual contract acceptance.

On Form 17, Section 4 (Structural) establishes strict disclosure mandates:
- **Question 4.C asks:** *"Are there any pest or insect infestations, or wood-destroying organisms on the property?"*
- **Question 4.D asks:** *"Has the property been treated for pests, insects, or wood-destroying organisms? If yes, explain."*
- **Question 4.E asks:** *"Are there any structural defects with the foundation, roof, floors, or load-bearing walls?"*

Under Washington law, sellers must complete Form 17 based on their actual knowledge. If a seller fails to provide Form 17, the buyer has the statutory right to cancel the purchase contract up until closing. Furthermore, under Washington case law (*Alejandre v. Bull* and *Stieneke v. Russi*), sellers who make fraudulent statements or actively conceal known structural defects can be held liable for damages in state court.

### Washington State Licensed Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) Reports

In Washington, pest inspections must be performed by a **Structural Pest Inspector (SPI)** licensed by the Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA) under RCW 15.58.

The WSDA SPI report specifically documents:
- **Active Wood Destroying Organisms:** Visual evidence of active subterranean termites, dampwood termites, carpenter ants, or wood-boring beetles.
- **Structural Wood Decay:** Damage to mudsills, floor joists, carrier beams, or subflooring caused by past insect infestations or fungal rot.
- **Conducive Conditions:** High soil grade, missing crawlspace vapor barriers, standing water, or poor foundation ventilation that increases susceptibility to future infestations.

Mortgage lenders (including conventional conforming loans, FHA, and VA) routinely require complete chemical treatment and structural repairs of any active wood-destroying organisms prior to loan funding.

### What Tacoma Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Even with Form 17 mandates, Tacoma sellers frequently provide vague or minimizing statements on their disclosure forms:
- *"Crawlspace perimeter treated preventatively in 2021."* (Concealing that an exterminator treated active subterranean mud tubes on the south foundation wall).
- *"Previous minor moisture damage repaired under primary bathroom."* (Failing to disclose that dampwood termites had colonized the main carrier beam).
- *"Seller has never occupied property (estate sale)."* (Using non-occupancy to disclaim knowledge of known past pest issues).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Red Flags in Massive Due Diligence Packets

In standard Northwest Multiple Listing Service (NWMLS) purchase agreements, buyers typically negotiate a **10-to-14-day Inspection Contingency Period**.

During this brief window, a Tacoma buyer must analyze a large stack of due diligence documents:
- The completed Washington NWMLS Form 17 Seller Disclosure Statement
- A 60-to-90-page General Home Inspection Report with crawlspace moisture readings
- The WSDA Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) Report
- Pierce County property tax records, building permits, and recorded easements
- Preliminary Title insurance commitments

### Technical Jargon That Conceals Catastrophic Risk

Inspectors often use cautious technical language that downplays serious structural risks:
- An inspector might write: *"Wood moisture equivalent (WME) readings in crawlspace exceed 20%; localized probed softening observed at west mudsill."* A buyer might assume this is cosmetic moisture, unaware that "probed softening" indicates hollowed structural wood caused by subterranean termites.
- An SPI report disclaimer might state: *"Crawlspace floor joists 80% obscured by suspended fiberglass batt insulation; inspection limited to visual line-of-sight."* This disclaimer shields the inspector while leaving the buyer fully responsible for hidden framing damage.

Manually cross-referencing hundreds of pages under strict contractual deadlines creates significant financial vulnerabilities.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Tacoma

**1. Are termites common in Tacoma and Western Washington?**
Yes. Both Western subterranean termites and Pacific dampwood termites are prevalent throughout Pierce County. Subterranean termites tunnel through damp glacial till soils into foundations, while dampwood termites attack high-moisture structural lumber and crawlspace framing.

**2. Is a seller in Tacoma legally required to disclose past termite treatments?**
Yes. Under Washington RCW 64.06 (Form 17), sellers must explicitly disclose whether the property has ever been treated for pests, insects, or wood-destroying organisms. Providing false statements or concealing past treatments can expose the seller to legal liability.

**3. What is a WSDA Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) report?**
In Washington State, pest inspections must be conducted by a Structural Pest Inspector licensed by the Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA). The SPI report identifies active wood-destroying organisms, structural wood decay, and conditions conducive to future infestations.

**4. Why are historic post-and-beam crawlspaces in Tacoma particularly vulnerable?**
Historic homes in Tacoma built between 1890 and 1930 often feature unsealed crawlspaces with inadequate cross-ventilation. Persistent regional rainfall and ground dampness keep subfloor wood moisture levels high, creating ideal conditions for both fungal decay and termite infestations.
