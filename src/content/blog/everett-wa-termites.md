## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Dampwood Termites in Everett, WA

Everett’s coastal Puget Sound geography, persistent maritime precipitation, and historic timber construction create unique structural hazards for homebuyers.

### How Puget Sound Rain, Saturated Glacial Till, and Crawlspaces Drive Infestations

Several environmental and architectural factors make Everett properties prime targets for timber pests:

- **Pacific Northwest Maritime Climate:** Everett experiences overcast, humid conditions for much of the year, with continuous precipitation between October and May. Saturated ground conditions and low solar evaporation keep sub-grade soils damp year-round. Subterranean termites require continuous ambient moisture to survive, and Everett’s moisture-laden soils provide an inexhaustible supply.
- **Damp Crawlspaces and Saturated Rim Joists:** Many of Everett’s older homes in Bayside, Northwest Everett, and Port Gardner are built over uninsulated dirt crawlspaces with perimeter concrete stem walls or unreinforced masonry. Inadequate foundation ventilation, broken perimeter footing drains, and roof runoff against foundations frequently drive crawlspace wood moisture levels above 25 percent—creating prime conditions for Pacific dampwood termites.
- **Vulnerabilities Across Everett Neighborhoods:**
  - *Bayside & Northwest Everett:* Historic Craftsman, Queen Anne, and colonial homes built between 1900 and 1930. Many feature old-growth Douglas fir post-and-beam crawlspaces where historic wood posts directly contact dirt floors, providing an unobstructed highway for subterranean termites.
  - *Port Gardner:* Early 20th-century timber-mill worker cottages. Porch framing, exterior stair stringers, and wood-framed sunrooms often sit in direct contact with damp garden soil, inviting dual infestations of dampwood and subterranean termites into mudsills.
  - *Silver Lake & Glacier View:* Mid-century split-levels and 1980s homes featuring daylight basements or slab-on-grade additions. Shifting glacial till soils produce foundation settling cracks, allowing subterranean termites direct access to lower wall plates and finished basement framing.

### Pest Biology: Western Subterranean vs. Pacific Dampwood Termites

Understanding the biological profiles of local wood-destroying insects is essential for evaluating property risks in Snohomish County:

- **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Lives in underground colonies nesting deep in the soil to escape surface cold. They construct durable earthen mud tubes over concrete stem walls, foundation piers, and plumbing lines to reach structural wood sills. They feed along the grain of structural softwoods, leaving the outer wood shell deceptively intact.
- **Pacific Dampwood Termite (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*):** The largest termite species in North America, with winged swarmers reaching an inch in length. Unlike subterranean species, dampwood termites do not require soil contact; they colonize damp, decaying wood directly. If an unaddressed roof, plumbing, or crawlspace moisture issue raises wood moisture above 20 percent, dampwood termites will hollow out major carrying beams, floor joists, and rim joists.
- **Swarming Timing in Western Washington:** Western subterranean termites swarm on warm, sunny afternoons following spring rains (April through June). Pacific dampwood termites swarm on warm, humid late-summer and early-fall evenings (August through October). Finding discarded large, translucent wings along crawlspace vents or basement window wells indicates an established infestation.

## Washington Real Estate Disclosure Laws: RCW Chapter 64.06 and Form 17

Navigating a real estate transaction in Everett requires a clear understanding of statutory disclosure requirements in Washington State.

### The Seller Disclosure Statement (NWMLS Form 17)

Under the **Revised Code of Washington (RCW) § 64.06.020**, a seller of residential real property must deliver to the buyer a completed Seller Disclosure Statement (standardized as NWMLS Form 17) within five business days of mutual contract acceptance:

- **Mandatory Pest and Wood-Destroying Organism Questions:** Form 17 contains specific inquiries regarding structural integrity and biological organisms:
  - *Section 5.C (Systems & Fixtures):* Inquiries regarding drainage and sump pumps.
  - *Section 7.C (Environmental):* "Are there any wood-destroying organisms or pest infestations on the property?"
  - *Section 7.D (Environmental):* "Has the property been treated for wood-destroying organisms or pests?"
  - *Section 1.D (Structural):* "Has there been any settling, slippage, or sliding of the property or its foundations?"
  - *Section 1.E (Structural):* "Are there any foundation repairs, structural additions, or modifications?"
- **The Buyer’s Statutory Right of Rescission (RCW § 64.06.030):** Upon receiving Form 17, the buyer has three business days to examine the disclosure statement. If the buyer is dissatisfied with any disclosed condition, they may rescind the purchase agreement and receive a full refund of their earnest money deposit without forfeiture.
- **Seller Liability for Intentional Concealment:** While Form 17 is technically not a warranty by the seller, Washington common law (*Alejandre v. Bull*) establishes that a seller who knowingly conceals latent defects or commits fraudulent misrepresentation remains liable for damages, rescission, and legal fees.

### Deceptive Language in Everett Disclosures

Sellers often draft minimal responses on Form 17 designed to satisfy disclosure rules without alarming buyers:

- *"Crawlspace treated for pests in 2021 by local pest contractor; routine service."* (Downplays the fact that dampwood termites had eaten into the main carrying girder).
- *"Vapor barrier updated and minor wood rot replaced along south foundation wall."* (Conceals that subterranean termites had tunneled into the structural sill plate).
- *"Normal seasonal crawlspace moisture managed with foundation vents."* (Obscures chronic 28% wood moisture that created ideal conditions for wood-decay fungi and termites).

## The Manual Review Problem: Concealed Red Flags in Puget Sound Closings

An Everett home purchase generates an enormous volume of technical documentation: the NWMLS purchase agreement, title commitments, sewer scope videos, geological slope hazard evaluations, and 60-page general inspection reports.

Under Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA) rules, a standard general home inspection does not qualify as a complete Wood Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection unless the inspector holds a dedicated Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) license. Standard general inspection reports frequently include disclaimers such as: "Crawlspace crawl clearance limited to 18 inches; northwest rim joists obscured by fiberglass insulation batts; unable to verify presence of wood-destroying organisms."

When tired buyers manually review these lengthy documents, critical cross-references slip by unnoticed. An inspector’s passing note citing "fiberglass insulation fallen in crawlspace due to moisture" often correlates directly with a cryptic note in Form 17 mentioning "crawlspace spot treatment in 2022." Without AI-assisted document synthesis, buyers fail to recognize the active subterranean termite corridor until floor joists sag.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Everett, WA

### 1. Are termites common in Everett and Snohomish County?
Yes. While many residents believe termites only live in warm southern climates, Western subterranean termites and Pacific dampwood termites are native and widespread throughout the Puget Sound region. High rainfall, damp soil, and historic wood framing make Everett homes highly susceptible.

### 2. What is the difference between a general home inspector and a Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) in Washington?
In Washington, general home inspectors are not authorized to issue official Wood Destroying Organism (WDO) reports unless they hold an active Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) license issued by the Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA). An SPI undergoes specialized training to detect termites, carpenter ants, and wood-destroying fungi.

### 3. What is the difference between subterranean termites and dampwood termites?
Subterranean termites nest in the soil and build mud tubes to travel upward into dry or damp structural wood. Dampwood termites do not need contact with the soil; they infest wood with high moisture content (typically over 20%) caused by roof leaks, plumbing drips, or unvented crawlspaces.

### 4. What are the key warning signs of termites in an Everett home?
Look for earthen mud tubes climbing foundation concrete stem walls, soft or spongy wood along crawlspace sill plates, piles of wood-colored fecal pellets near damp timbers, springy or sloping hardwood floors, and large winged swarmers around crawlspace vents in late summer.
