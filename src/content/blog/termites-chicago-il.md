When purchasing a property in Chicago, buyers are handed the Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Report alongside a stack of inspection reports. Hidden within those dense pages of legal jargon and handwritten notes can be one of the most destructive and expensive issues an Illinois homeowner can face: **subterranean termites**.

## The Chicago Termite Problem

Chicago's harsh winters might seem inhospitable to pests, but subterranean termites thrive beneath the frost line and actively feed on the wooden structures of historic greystones and modern bungalows alike. Because these pests destroy wood from the inside out, the damage is rarely visible during a standard walkthrough.

The warning signs are usually buried in the paperwork. Sellers are legally required to disclose known material defects, but the language used is often vague or deliberately minimized:
- *"Evidence of past pest activity in the basement joists, treated by prior owner."*
- *"Minor wood rot near foundation sill plate."*

## Why Manual Review Fails

Homebuyers and even seasoned real estate agents typically spend just 15 to 20 minutes skimming these massive disclosure packets. It is incredibly difficult and risky to manually hunt for subtle mentions of termite damage across pest control invoices, general inspection addendums, and seller questionnaires. 

Missing a brief note about "frass" or "mud tubes" can lead to structural failures requiring tens of thousands of dollars in emergency repairs immediately after you receive the keys.

## The Cost of Overlooking the Signs

Termite damage goes beyond just replacing a few boards. It compromises the structural integrity of load-bearing walls and floors. If a seller's disclosure mentions past treatments but fails to provide a structural engineer's clearance, you could be buying a severely compromised home.
