## The Hidden Threat: Termite Vulnerabilities Across Ann Arbor and Washtenaw County

Ann Arbor’s river valley geography, dense urban tree canopy, and historic residential construction combine to create acute vulnerabilities to subterranean termite infestations.

### Why "Tree Town" Root Networks and Glacial Soils Foster Infestations

Several localized environmental and structural factors heighten termite susceptibility across Ann Arbor properties:

- **The Urban Forest and Subterranean Root Networks:** Ann Arbor’s famous tree canopy features mature native white oaks, sugar maples, and black walnuts lining historic residential parcels. As large shade trees age or are removed, their subterranean root systems slowly decompose beneath lawns and foundations. Subterranean termites feed on these underground cellulose networks, following decaying root paths directly up to and beneath residential foundation footings.
- **The Huron River Basin and Glacial Soil Moisture:** The Huron River meanders directly through Ann Arbor, creating a valley floor characterized by glacial moraines, sandy gravel, and heavy clay loams with elevated moisture tables. Subterranean termites require constant humidity to survive; damp subsoils beneath residential foundations provide a permanent subterranean sanctuary that sustains super-colonies year-round.
- **Heated Basements as Cold-Climate Refuges:** While outdoor winter temperatures in Ann Arbor regularly plunge below freezing, modern heated basements maintain soil temperatures between 55°F and 68°F directly adjacent to foundation walls. Termites remain active year-round by foraging within the warm perimeter soils and concrete blocks surrounding heated basements.
- **Neighborhood Architectural Vulnerabilities in Ann Arbor:**
  - *Old West Side & Kerrytown:* 1870s–1910s Queen Anne, Italianate, and worker cottages with fieldstone or brick cellar foundations where deteriorating lime mortar allows termites to enter structural wood framing without visible exterior mud tubes.
  - *Burns Park & Eberwhite:* 1910s–1930s Craftsman homes with extensive wood porch framing built over unexcavated soil and unventilated crawlspaces beneath converted sunrooms.
  - *Water Hill & Ann Arbor Hills:* 1950s–1960s ranches and mid-century split-levels where concrete block foundation cores and slab plumbing penetrations provide direct vertical avenues to wood sills.

### Pest Biology: The Eastern Subterranean Termite

Homeowners in Washtenaw County contend primarily with one pervasive structural pest:

- **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** These social insects nest deep in the subsoil beneath the frost line. Colony populations frequently exceed several hundred thousand foraging workers. Because their soft, pale bodies lose moisture rapidly upon exposure to open air, they construct protective mud tubes made of soil, saliva, and partially digested wood fibers over foundation surfaces. They feed along the soft springwood growth rings of structural timbers, hollowing out joists and mudsills while leaving an intact outer surface veneer of painted wood that conceals structural failure until framing collapses under load.
- **Swarming Patterns in Southeast Michigan:** Subterranean termites in Ann Arbor typically swarm between April and June on warm, sunny mornings immediately following a spring rain shower. Finding discarded insect wings accumulated on basement windowsills, near cellar bulkheads, or along floor registers indicates a mature subterranean colony nesting directly beneath the foundation.

## Michigan Real Estate Disclosure Laws: MCL 565.951 & Seller Disclosure Statement

Purchasing residential real estate in Ann Arbor requires understanding the statutory disclosure obligations established by the Michigan Seller Disclosure Act (Public Act 92 of 1993, MCL 565.951 et seq.).

### The Statutory Framework: The Michigan Seller's Disclosure Statement

Under Michigan law, a seller of residential property of 1 to 4 units must deliver a written Seller's Disclosure Statement to the buyer before executing a binding purchase agreement:

- **The Statutory Disclosure Form:** Mandated under MCL 565.957, the standard disclosure document includes specific sections addressing structural and pest conditions:
  - *Structural Conditions Section:* Inquires into foundation settling, basement leaks, water seepage, and foundation wall cracking.
  - *Other Items Section:* Explicitly asks: *"History of infestation, if any: termites, carpenter ants, pests, etc.?"* and *"Structural modifications, alterations, or repairs made without necessary permits or not in compliance with building codes?"*
- **The Seller's Standard of Good Faith and Actual Knowledge:** Under MCL 565.955, the seller is required to make disclosures in good faith based on information known to the seller. While a seller is not liable for defects they did not know about, a seller who intentionally conceals past infestations, paints over active mud tubes, or hides prior pest invoices faces legal exposure for fraudulent misrepresentation.
- **The NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report:** In Michigan, official pest inspections are documented on Form NPMA-33 by a structural pest applicator certified through the Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development (MDARD). The report records:
  - Active insect infestations observed.
  - Previous infestation evidence (inactive mud tubes, hollowed framing).
  - Previous chemical treatment markings (drilled holes in basement slabs or exterior perimeter trenching).
  - Inaccessible areas of the home (e.g., behind finished basement paneling, low-clearance crawlspaces, or enclosed ceiling cavities).

### Common Disclosure Evasions in Ann Arbor Real Estate

Sellers and listing agents in Washtenaw County often employ subtle phrasing to satisfy disclosure obligations while obscuring severe underlying structural damage:

- *"Preventative perimeter soil treatment applied in 2021 by local pest control company."* (Fails to disclose that treatment was commissioned after active mud tubes were found in the basement rim joists).
- *"Hairline seasonal settling cracks in basement concrete floor; typical for older Ann Arbor homes."* (Obscures the fact that subterranean termites entered through the concrete expansion joint crack and consumed the wall baseplates).
- *"Minor wood rot repaired on exterior porch framing prior to listing."* (Conceals that subterranean termites tunneled from the front porch dirt foundation into the home's primary timber carry beam).

- Washtenaw County property tax records, title commitments, and historic district guidelines.

### The Limitation of Standard Home Inspections

Prospective buyers often believe that hiring a licensed home inspector provides complete protection against wood-destroying organisms. However, Michigan home inspectors adhere to national standards of practice (such as ASHI or InterNACHI) that explicitly exclude pest detection:

> *"The inspection is an objective visual examination of the physical structure and systems of a home. The inspector is not licensed to inspect for wood-destroying organisms, termites, dry rot, or pests, and is not required to move insulation, disassemble finished basement walls, or probe concealed framing."*

In historic Ann Arbor homes with finished basements, drywall paneling, or low-clearance crawlspaces beneath rear additions, a general home inspector will not dismantle finishes. If a buyer does not cross-reference vague statements on the Seller's Disclosure Statement against the inaccessible area disclaimers in the NPMA-33 report, severe structural devastation remains completely undetected until floors begin to bounce.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Ann Arbor, MI

### 1. Are termite inspections mandatory when buying a home in Ann Arbor, Michigan?
Michigan state law does not legally mandate a pest inspection for cash transactions. However, mortgage lenders—particularly those funding FHA, VA, and conventional loans—almost universally require a clean NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect report prior to loan closing. In Washtenaw County, where subterranean termite pressure is fueled by decaying urban tree roots, an independent inspection by an MDARD-certified structural pest applicator is an essential safeguard.

### 2. Can subterranean termites survive an Ann Arbor winter?
Subterranean termites survive Michigan winters by burrowing below the frost line into deeper, warmer subsoils. In urban areas like Ann Arbor, termites thrive primarily by nesting directly beneath the heated sub-slab soils of residential basements, near water heaters, and along underground plumbing lines, where temperatures remain above 50°F throughout the winter.

### 3. What legal remedies exist if an Ann Arbor seller concealed termite damage?
Under the Michigan Seller Disclosure Act (MCL 565.951) and Michigan common law fraud doctrines, a seller who knowingly conceals active termite damage, prior infestations, or treatment history on the disclosure statement can be sued for fraudulent misrepresentation. To succeed in Washtenaw County Circuit Court, the buyer must prove that the seller had actual knowledge of the defect at the time of disclosure and intentionally misled the purchaser.

### 4. What visible warning signs of termite activity should Ann Arbor buyers watch for?
Key warning signs include:
- Earthen mud shelter tubes crawling up basement stone walls, concrete blocks, or plumbing pipes.
- Discarded insect wings accumulating along basement windowsills or cellar bulkheads in early spring.
- Hollow-sounding baseboards, door moldings, or window jambs when lightly tapped with a screwdriver handle.
- Patched round drill holes spaced 12 inches apart along basement concrete slabs or exterior brick porches, which reveal past chemical injection treatments.
