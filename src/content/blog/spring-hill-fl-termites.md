## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean, Formosan & Drywood Termites in Spring Hill, FL

Spring Hill’s subtropical climate, porous karst geology, and residential building methods create sustained pressure from three distinct wood-destroying insect species.

### How Nature Coast Karst Sands, High Humidity, and Concrete Slabs Drive Infestations

Several localized environmental and architectural factors make Spring Hill homes prime targets for wood-destroying organisms:

- **Karst Geology and Deep Sandy Soils:** Spring Hill sits on the limestone karst terrain of Hernando County. Porous surface sands allow rainfall to penetrate rapidly, but high underground water tables around the Weeki Wachee spring network keep subterranean soils persistently humid. Because subterranean termites require high relative humidity (near 100%) within their underground colonies to prevent fatal desiccation, the subterranean sands of Spring Hill provide an ideal subterranean paradise.
- **The Concrete Block (CBS) Misconception:** While exterior perimeter walls are formed of concrete masonry units (CMU), the interior living space relies heavily on wood framing. Wood 1x2 furring strips are glued or nailed directly to interior block walls to support drywall, and all interior partition walls, closets, and door frames are wood-framed. Subterranean termites enter through slab plumbing penetrations (bathtubs, showers, and kitchen waste lines) or crawl between the slab and exterior block, tunneling straight into interior drywall studs without ever damaging exterior masonry.
- **Architectural Stock and Construction Vulnerabilities:**
  - *Spring Hill Drive & Mariner Blvd Corridors:* 1970s and 1980s CBS ranches. Exterior stucco applied below soil grade or covered by decorative mulch beds creates hidden subterranean bridges, allowing termites to tunnel directly behind exterior stucco and into wood wall furring.
  - *Screened Pool Lanais & Patios:* Screened pool enclosures anchored to exterior walls and concrete slabs create moisture collection traps. The connection ledger where the lanai meets the fascia or exterior wall is a primary entry portal for both subterranean and drywood termites.
  - *Timber Pines & Forest Oaks:* Custom homes with complex roof valleys and vaulted ceilings. Subtropical downpours cause minor roof flashing leaks that moisten attic wood trusses, creating ideal moisture conditions for Formosan aerial colonies.

### Pest Biology: Formosan, Subterranean, and Drywood Termites

Homeowners in Spring Hill face threats from three distinct termite species:

- **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** The most aggressive termite species in North America. Formosan colonies are enormous, frequently containing three to ten million individuals. Unlike native termites, Formosans can construct secondary aerial nests ("carton" nests) within wall cavities or attics using chewed wood, soil, and saliva. If they find an interior moisture source (such as AC condensation or roof leaks), they can survive without returning to the soil, devouring wood framing at terrifying speeds.
- **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** The native subterranean species in Central Florida. They live in underground soil colonies and construct earthen mud shelter tubes to cross foundation concrete, feeding along the softer springwood grain.
- **West Indian Drywood Termite (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Unlike subterranean termites, drywood termites live entirely inside dry structural wood and require no contact with soil or external moisture. They infest attic rafters, door casings, and wooden cabinets, expelling tiny sand-like fecal pellets (frass) through pinhole exit holes.
- **Swarming Timelines in Hernando County:** Subterranean termites swarm on warm spring mornings from February through April following rain. Formosan termites swarm aggressively on warm, humid evenings in May and June at dusk, drawn by the thousands to outdoor lights and illuminated pool enclosures. Drywood termites swarm on warm summer evenings from May through August.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis & The FR/BAR Contract

Florida real estate disclosure jurisprudence is among the most unique and strictly enforced in the United States, governed by a foundational Supreme Court precedent.

### The Landmark Johnson v. Davis Doctrine (480 So. 2d 625)

Under the seminal Florida Supreme Court ruling in ***Johnson v. Davis***, Florida fundamentally departed from traditional *caveat emptor*:
> *"Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under a duty to disclose them to the buyer."*

- **Applicability to "AS IS" Contracts:** A critical legal principle that every Florida buyer must understand is that the *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure duty applies equally to standard contracts and Florida Realtors / Florida Bar (FR/BAR) "AS IS" Residential Contracts. Selling a home "AS IS" does NOT excuse a Florida seller from disclosing known hidden termite infestations or structural framing damage.
- **Florida Seller's Property Disclosure Form:** Sellers typically complete the Florida Realtors SPDC form, which explicitly queries:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present wood-destroying organisms (termites, powder post beetles, wood-decaying fungus)?"*
  - *"Has the property been inspected or treated for wood-destroying organisms? If yes, provide dates, treating companies, and documentation."*
  - *"Are you aware of any structural damage caused by wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Is there a current termite bond or warranty in place? If yes, is it transferrable?"*

### Seller Knowledge vs. Fraudulent Concealment under Florida Law

While Florida sellers are only obligated to disclose facts within their *actual knowledge*, Florida courts enforce strict liability for fraudulent concealment:
- **Fraudulent Concealment:** If a seller actively conceals termite damage (such as applying fresh drywall mud, repainting baseboards over active tunneling, or removing frass pellets before open houses), the buyer has grounds for contract rescission, compensatory damages, and punitive damages.
- **Deceptive Minimizing Language in Nature Coast Disclosures:** Watch for minimizing statements on Florida seller forms:
  - *"Routine preventative perimeter soil treatment completed in 2021; bond expired."* (Frequently masks active Formosan termite tunneling discovered in bathroom walls).
  - *"Repaired cosmetic baseboard in master suite."* (Often conceals structural wood furring strips destroyed behind drywall).
  - *"Stucco crack sealed around lanai enclosure."* (Can indicate an attempt to conceal active subterranean termite mud tubes).

### The WDO / FDACS-13645 Inspection in Florida

In Florida, standard home inspections explicitly disclaim wood-destroying organism certifications.

Buyers in Spring Hill should always include a dedicated Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection contingency:
- Inspections must be performed by a certified pest control operator licensed under Florida Statutes Chapter 482 by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)**.
- Findings are documented on the official **FDACS Form 13645 Wood-Destroying Organism Inspection Report**, which evaluates visible evidence of active or previous infestations of subterranean termites, Formosan termites, drywood termites, and wood-rotting fungus, along with conducive conditions.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Spring Hill, FL

### 1. Can termites damage concrete block (CBS) homes in Spring Hill?
Yes. While termites do not eat concrete block, virtually all CBS homes in Spring Hill contain interior wood framing, including 1x2 wood furring strips on exterior walls, wood-framed interior partition walls, door frames, and wooden roof trusses. Subterranean termites enter through slab expansion joints and plumbing blockouts, devouring interior wood framing while exterior masonry remains untouched.

### 2. What is Florida's Johnson v. Davis rule, and does it apply to "AS IS" contracts?
Under the Florida Supreme Court decision in *Johnson v. Davis*, a seller has a legal duty to disclose any known latent defect that materially affects the value of the property and is not readily observable by the buyer. Crucially, this legal obligation applies to ALL residential transactions, including properties sold under standard FR/BAR "AS IS" contracts.

### 3. What is the difference between subterranean termites and drywood termites in Florida?
Subterranean termites (including native species and Formosan termites) nest in the soil and require high moisture, constructing earthen mud tubes to reach wood. Drywood termites live entirely inside dry structural wood without soil contact, infesting attic rafters and door frames and expelling distinct hexagonal sand-like fecal pellets (frass).

### 4. Does a standard Florida home inspection include a certified termite report?
No. Florida licensed home inspectors perform general visual inspections, and standard home inspection contracts specifically disclaim wood-destroying organism certifications. Buyers should always hire a pest control operator certified by FDACS under Chapter 482 to execute an official FDACS Form 13645 WDO inspection.
