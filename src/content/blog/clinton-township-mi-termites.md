## The Hidden Threat: Eastern Subterranean Termites in Clinton Township, MI

Clinton Township’s river watershed geography, clay-heavy subsoils, and prevalent mid-century building stock create significant vulnerabilities to wood-destroying organisms.

### How Clinton River Hydrology, Glacial Lake Clay, and Crawlspaces Drive Infestations

Several localized environmental and construction factors heighten termite risks across Clinton Township properties:

- **Clinton River Basin Hydrology & Perched Water Tables:** The presence of the Clinton River and its tributary drainage ditches sustains elevated soil moisture levels throughout the township. During spring thaws and heavy Great Lakes rainstorms, the surrounding flat glacial lake plain soils become saturated. Subterranean termites require continuous ambient humidity near 100% to survive; the trapped moisture in Clinton Township's subsoil creates an ideal subterranean habitat.
- **Glacial Clay Lake Plain Soils:** Macomb County subsoils consist primarily of dense silty clay and clay loam. These tight soils restrict vertical percolation, causing water to pool around foundation perimeters. When exterior footing drains clog or downspouts discharge near foundation walls, the resulting hydrostatic pressure forces moisture through concrete block mortar joints, beckoning subterranean termites.
- **Architectural Stock and Construction Vulnerabilities:**
  - *Moravian Drive & Clinton River Corridor:* 1950s through 1970s custom ranches and split-levels built near river flood zones. Crawlspaces without adequate 6-mil polyethylene vapor barriers create humid subterranean microclimates that foster wood-decay fungus and subterranean termites simultaneously.
  - *Clinton Valley & Prince Drew:* 1960s brick ranches featuring hollow-core concrete block basement walls. Termites enter microscopic fissures in mortar joints below grade, traveling upward through hollow block cores directly into floor joist pockets without ever exposing themselves to exterior view.
  - *Canal Road & Romeo Plank Corridors:* 1980s and 1990s multi-level colonials and contemporary homes with attached ground-level wood decks, landscape timbers, and mulched flowerbeds piled against exterior brick veneer weepholes, creating direct cellulose bridges over foundation walls.

### Pest Biology: The Eastern Subterranean Termite

The Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*) accounts for the vast majority of structural insect damage in Southeast Michigan:

- **Colony Mechanics and Underground Foraging:** Mature colonies contain between 100,000 and two million sterile workers living in deep underground networks. Foraging workers construct airtight earthen mud shelter tubes composed of moist soil, masticated wood, and saliva to bridge exposed masonry foundations and reach structural lumber.
- **Structural Concealment:** Subterranean termites consume wood along the softer springwood grain, leaving the harder summerwood rings and a paper-thin exterior shell intact. A structural carrying beam or rim joist can appear entirely solid upon casual visual inspection while the interior load-bearing timber has been reduced to fragile, soil-filled honeycomb.
- **Spring Swarming in Macomb County:** Nuptial swarming in Southeast Michigan occurs primarily on warm, sunny spring mornings from April through early June, particularly after rain when temperatures surpass 70°F. Discovering hundreds of shed, iridescent wings on basement window sills, laundry sinks, or garage floors is definitive proof of an active, reproductive colony within or directly beneath the home.

## Michigan Real Estate Disclosure Laws: MCL § 565.951 & The Seller Disclosure Act

In Michigan, property condition disclosures are governed by comprehensive statutory mandates established to protect purchasers against latent defects and structural deception.

### The Michigan Seller Disclosure Statement (SDA)

Under the **Michigan Seller Disclosure Act (Public Act 92 of 1993, MCL § 565.951 et seq.)**, sellers of residential property consisting of one to four units must deliver a completed statutory Seller's Disclosure Statement to prospective buyers before signing a binding purchase agreement:

- **Mandatory Pest Disclosures:** The statutory form specifically queries sellers regarding:
  - *"Infestation: Is there any history of infestation by termites, carpenter ants, or other wood-destroying insects? If yes, explain."*
  - *"Has the property ever been treated for wood-destroying insects? If yes, provide details."*
- **Structural & Moisture Disclosures:** The disclosure also requires sellers to declare:
  - Basement water seepage, standing water, sump pump failures, or foundation wall cracks.
  - Structural foundation defects, settlement, or repairs to bearing beams, posts, and joists.
- **Buyer's Statutory Right of Termination:** Under MCL § 565.954, if the seller delivers the disclosure statement after the purchase agreement has been executed, the buyer retains the unilateral statutory right to terminate the contract within 72 hours of personal delivery (or 120 hours if delivered by registered mail) and obtain an immediate, full refund of earnest money.

### Seller Knowledge, Silent Fraud, and Innocent Misrepresentation

Under Michigan law, the Seller Disclosure Statement is based on the seller's *actual knowledge* at the time of completion. However, Michigan courts recognize potent legal remedies for aggrieved buyers:

- **Silent Fraud (Fraudulent Concealment):** Under Michigan jurisprudence (e.g., *M&D, Inc. v. McConkey*), if a seller responds to a specific disclosure inquiry with partial truths designed to mislead the buyer—such as mentioning a "preventative treatment" while concealing that termites destroyed structural wood—the seller is liable for silent fraud.
- **Innocent Misrepresentation:** Michigan is one of the few jurisdictions recognizing the doctrine of innocent misrepresentation, allowing a buyer to rescind a contract or recover damages if the seller makes an untrue factual representation upon which the buyer relies to their financial injury, even if the seller lacked fraudulent intent.
- **Deceptive Minimizing Phrases in Macomb County Disclosures:**
  - *"Preventative perimeter bait stations installed in 2021."* (Frequently conceals active termite activity discovered during remodeling).
  - *"Repointed block foundation mortar joint."* (Often masks masonry disintegration caused by subterranean tunneling).
  - *"Cosmetic baseboard replaced in basement family room."* (Can hide structural sill plates eaten away behind wood paneling).

### The WDI / NPMA-33 Pest Inspection in Michigan

While Michigan licensed home inspectors examine general property conditions, standard home inspection contracts specifically disclaim wood-destroying insect certification.

Homebuyers in Clinton Township should always insist on an independent Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) inspection contingency:
- Inspections must be performed by a commercial pesticide applicator certified in Category 7A (General Pest Management and Wood Destroying Pests) by the **Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development (MDARD)**.
- The inspector issues the official **NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect Inspection Report**, documenting visible evidence of active or prior infestations, past chemical treatments, and conducive conditions like earth-to-wood contact and high crawlspace moisture.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Clinton Township, MI

### 1. Are subterranean termites active in Clinton Township despite Michigan winters?
Yes. Subterranean termites are active throughout Southeast Michigan, particularly in Macomb, Oakland, and Wayne counties. They survive sub-zero winters by retreating below the frost line into damp soils along the Clinton River basin or feeding year-round within the heated perimeters of residential basements and crawlspaces.

### 2. What red flags should buyers look for on the Michigan Seller Disclosure Statement?
Look closely for minimizing terminology such as "preventative pest treatment," "minor floor settlement," "crawlspace vapor barrier upgrade," or "block wall repointing." Whenever a seller mentions past pest management, insist on seeing the original pest control contract, target organism documentation, and any accompanying repair receipts.

### 3. Does a standard Michigan home inspection include a certified termite inspection?
No. General home inspectors perform non-invasive visual examinations and standard Michigan home inspection contracts explicitly disclaim pest warranties. Buyers should always hire a pest professional certified in Category 7A by MDARD to complete an official NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect report.

### 4. What legal remedies are available if a seller concealed termite damage in Clinton Township?
Under Michigan law, a buyer who discovers undisclosed termite damage can pursue claims for silent fraud, fraudulent misrepresentation, or innocent misrepresentation. If the seller actively concealed evidence of an infestation (such as installing new paneling or drywall over rotted framing immediately prior to listing), the buyer may seek compensatory damages or contract rescission.
