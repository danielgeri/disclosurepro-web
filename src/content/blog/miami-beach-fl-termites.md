## Why Miami Beach Homes Face Severe Barrier-Island Termite Pressure

Miami Beach properties—ranging from 1920s–1940s Mediterranean Revival villas in **Flamingo Park** and Art Deco residences in **South Beach** to Mid-Century Modern (MiMo) homes in **North Beach** and waterfront estates along **North Bay Road** and the **Venetian Islands**—combine coastal humidity with concealed timber cavities that defeat surface-only walkthroughs.

### Three Aggressive Termite Species Threatening Miami Beach

1. **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** An invasive tropical species entrenched across South Florida barrier islands. Colonies grow to several million workers and consume structural lumber at multiples of native species' speed, swarming heavily on warm, humid evenings between **February and April**.
2. **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Capable of building moisture-retaining **aerial carton nests** inside CBS wall voids, parapet cavities, and flat-roof assemblies—meaning an active infestation can survive above ground without continuous soil contact if a roof or plumbing leak supplies moisture. Major nocturnal swarms peak between **May and July**.
3. **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Require zero soil contact. Winged swarmers enter directly through attic soffits, barrel-tile eaves, decorative vigas, and window casings between **April and July**, hollowing structural rafters and kicking out piles of uniform, six-sided sand-like fecal pellets (**frass**).

## Florida Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis, SPDR & FDACS-13645 WDO Rules

Unlike *caveat emptor* ("buyer beware") states, Florida residential sales are governed by the Florida Supreme Court's landmark ruling in **_Johnson v. Davis_, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**, plus strict pest inspection regulations under **Chapter 482, Florida Statutes**. For a full breakdown of statewide seller duties—including 4-Point insurance roofs, Flood Disclosure § 689.302, and condo SIRS reserves—read our [Florida Seller Property Disclosure & Johnson v. Davis guide](/blog/florida-johnson-v-davis-sellers-disclosure-guide/).

### 1. Seller Disclosure Duties Under Johnson v. Davis & FAR/BAR "AS IS"

- **Affirmative Duty to Disclose Latent Defects:** When a Miami Beach seller knows of facts materially affecting property value that are not readily observable and not known to the buyer—including prior structural termite damage, recurring swarms, or failed spot treatments—the seller has a non-waivable legal duty to disclose them.
- **"AS IS" Contracts Do Not Excuse Concealment:** Most Miami Beach deals use the **Florida Realtors/Florida Bar "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase (FAR/BAR AS IS)**. Checking "AS IS" limits the seller's obligation to perform repairs, **not** their legal duty under *Johnson v. Davis*. Painting over mud tubes, patching frass pinholes, or withholding prior WDO reports exposes sellers to rescission and fraud liability.
- **Auditing the Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR):** Check the Wood-Destroying Organisms / Pests section carefully for two separate questions: (1) past or present **treatment** and (2) past or present **structural damage**. Sellers routinely disclose a "2021 preventative booster" while checking "No" to damage—even when no contractor ever opened concealed framing.

### 2. Decoding the Official Florida FDACS-13645 WDO Report

Under **Chapter 482, Florida Statutes**, only a pest operator licensed by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)** can issue an official **FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organisms Inspection Report**. Unlike standard WDI forms in northern states, Florida's WDO report covers **both wood-destroying insects AND wood-decaying fungi (rot)**, and requires posting a permanent **Notice of Inspection sticker** (typically inside the electrical panel or under the kitchen sink).

Scrutinize three zones of every Miami Beach FDACS-13645 PDF:
- **Section 2 (Inspection Findings):** Separates live WDOs, dead evidence (mud tubes, frass, damaged wood), and visible evidence of previous treatment (slab drill plugs, bait stations).
- **Section 3 (Obstructions & Inaccessible Areas):** Lists every area the inspector could not probe—frequently including blown-in attic insulation, low-clearance barrel-tile eaves, furred-out CBS walls, and historic plaster ceilings.
- **Notice of Treatment Stickers & Termite Bond Contracts:** Verify whether the seller's "transferable termite bond" is a **retreatment-only warranty** (which pays **$0** toward chewed rafters or drywall restoration) versus a true **retreatment-and-repair bond** (which often excludes pre-existing damage and fungal rot). Review every contract trap in our [buying a house with a history of termites disclosure guide](/blog/buying-house-with-termite-history-disclosure-guide/).

1. **Demand Full Historical Pest Files on Day 1:** Request all prior FDACS-13645 reports, treatment diagrams, chemical invoices, and the complete multi-page termite bond contract—not just a one-page renewal receipt.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termites in Miami Beach, FL

### Can termites really destroy historic Dade County Pine in Miami Beach?
Yes. While historic **Dade County Pine** (*Pinus elliottii var. densa*) contains dense, resinous heartwood that resists native pests, invasive **Asian subterranean termites** (*Coptotermes gestroi*) and **Formosan subterranean termites** (*Coptotermes formosanus*) readily consume mature Dade County pine—especially when decades of coastal humidity or minor barrel-tile leaks soften the timber.

### Does a Florida "AS IS" contract release a Miami Beach seller from disclosing termites?
No. Under the Florida Supreme Court's ruling in **_Johnson v. Davis_**, residential sellers have a non-waivable affirmative duty to disclose known latent defects that materially affect property value and are not readily observable. Using a FAR/BAR "AS IS" contract never protects a seller who conceals active termites, hides prior WDO reports, or paints over structural wood damage.

### What is the difference between a treatment-only termite bond and a repair bond in Florida?
A **treatment-only (retreatment) bond** guarantees the pest company will return to re-apply chemicals or bait if termites reappear, but pays **$0** toward carpentry or framing repairs. A **retreatment-and-repair bond** includes structural repair coverage up to a stated dollar limit, though most contracts still exclude pre-existing damage, fungal wood rot, and homes with uncorrected moisture conditions.

### Who pays for the WDO inspection and termite treatment when buying a home in Miami Beach?
Under standard FAR/BAR contracts, the **buyer** pays the $100–$250 FDACS-13645 WDO inspection fee during the inspection period. Under a **FAR/BAR Standard Contract**, sellers have negotiated repair limits for WDO treatment and damage; under a **FAR/BAR "AS IS" Contract**, all treatment and structural repair credits are negotiated directly with the seller during the inspection period before the buyer elects to proceed or cancel.
