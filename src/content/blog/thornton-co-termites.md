## The Hidden Threat: Termites Across Thornton and Adams County

Thornton sits at an elevation exceeding 5,300 feet on the high plains of the Front Range, bounded by Westminster to the west and Northglenn to the south. ### How Front Range Expansive Soils and Floating Basements Exacerbate Termite Infiltration

Several unique geological and architectural factors make Thornton properties susceptible to subterranean termite activity:

- **Expansive Bentonite Clay Soils:** Adams County and the northern Denver metro area are famous for expansive clay-bearing bedrock (such as the Denver and Dawson formations). When wet, bentonite clays expand with immense force; when dry, they shrink and desiccate. This continuous soil movement causes significant foundation shifting, cracking poured concrete stem walls and separating the perimeter cold joint where basement floor slabs meet foundation footings. Termites use these deep soil fissures and foundation cracks as direct subterranean expressways.
- **Floating Basement Slab Construction:** Because Front Range expansive soils heave unpredictably, Colorado building codes traditionally mandate "floating" basement foundation systems—where basement floor slabs are structurally isolated from perimeter load-bearing foundation walls by expansion joints, and interior partition walls are suspended with floating baseplates. Subterranean termites enter through these continuous floor-to-wall isolation joints, climbing unseen behind finished basement drywall.
- **Suburban Lawn Irrigation against Foundations:** In an arid climate, homeowners heavily irrigate turf grass with automated sprinkler systems. Sprinklers spraying against foundation walls, coupled with dense decorative mulch beds, create continuous pockets of moist, warm soil. Subterranean termites require constant moisture to survive; over-irrigated foundation perimeters provide an ideal microhabitat throughout the hot summer months.
- **Finished Basements Concealing Structural Infiltration:** The vast majority of homes in Thornton feature finished basements with recreation rooms, bedrooms, and bathrooms. Framing lumber, drywall, and insulation are installed directly against concrete foundation walls, completely concealing termite mud tubes and hollowed wood framing from visual home inspections.

### Pest Biology: Arid-Land and Western Subterranean Termites

Thornton homebuyers contend with two primary subterranean wood-destroying pest species:

1. **Arid-Land Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes tibialis*):** The most widespread subterranean termite in Colorado. Highly adapted to high-altitude, semi-arid environments, these insects construct underground colonies several feet deep in the soil to escape freezing surface winter temperatures. In late spring, warming temperatures and sudden rainstorms stimulate daytime nuptial swarms of dark-winged reproductives (alates).
2. **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Frequently encountered in established suburban neighborhoods with mature landscaping and heavy irrigation. They build protective earthen mud shelter tubes over concrete foundation walls and plumbing pipes to shelter their soft bodies from the dry Colorado air while foraging for cellulose in sill plates and subflooring.

## Colorado Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Form SPD19 and Seller Disclosure Duties

Colorado real estate transactions operate under standardized disclosure forms established by the **Colorado Real Estate Commission (CREC)**, governed by state statutes and judicial case law enforcing seller honesty.

### The Statutory Framework: Colorado Seller's Property Disclosure (SPD19)

Under standard Colorado Real Estate Commission-approved residential contracts, sellers complete the comprehensive **Seller's Property Disclosure (Residential) (Form SPD19)**:

- **Section D: Structural and Foundation Conditions:** Sellers must disclose known foundation settlement, heaving, foundation wall cracks, or past structural stabilization (such as foundation helical piers or carbon fiber straps). Because termite entry in Thornton is directly tied to foundation movement, these disclosures must be closely scrutinized.
- **Section I: Environmental and Pest Conditions:** Form SPD19 explicitly requires sellers to disclose:
  - Any known past or current infestations of termites, carpenter ants, or other wood-destroying organisms.
  - Prior pest treatments, chemical soil applications, or ongoing pest control service contracts.
  - Any known structural damage resulting from wood-destroying pests.
- **The Duty to Disclose Latent Defects: *In re Estate of Gattis* (2013 COA 145):** Under Colorado appellate precedent, a residential seller owes an independent legal duty of disclosure to the buyer. Sellers are legally obligated to disclose known latent defects that materially affect the property’s value or habitability that would not be readily discoverable by a buyer’s reasonable inspection.
- **Limitations of "As-Is" Contract Clauses:** In Colorado, executing an "As-Is" contract addendum only protects a seller from claims regarding *unknown* property flaws. An "as-is" clause offers zero legal protection if a seller intentionally conceals known active termite infestations or misrepresents prior damage on the SPD19 disclosure form.

### Deceptive Seller Wording to Watch for in Thornton Transactions

Sellers in Adams County frequently minimize termite history using ambiguous language:
- *"Minor cosmetic settling cracks in basement slab, typical for Colorado expansive soils."* (Obscures the fact that subterranean termites were found entering through those exact slab cracks).
- *"Routine exterior barrier spraying completed annually for spiders and perimeter insects."* (Conceals that the "routine spraying" was an emergency liquid termiticide treatment following swarm activity).
- *"Lower-level baseboards replaced during recent cosmetic upgrade."* (Masks the removal of termite-chewed trim and drywall patching over mud tubes).

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Thornton

### 1. Are termite inspections standard in Thornton and the Denver metro area?
While Colorado state law does not mandate a termite inspection for cash transactions, most federally insured mortgage programs (including VA and FHA loans) require a dedicated Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) inspection if the appraiser notes potential pest damage or moisture conditions. Even for conventional mortgage purchases, ordering an independent WDI inspection by a licensed Colorado structural pest applicator is an essential protection for homes with finished basements.

### 2. How do Colorado's expansive soils contribute to termite infestations?
Front Range expansive bentonite clay soils expand when wet and contract when dry. This continuous movement causes concrete foundation walls to crack and separates the cold joint between basement floor slabs and perimeter foundation walls. Subterranean termites exploit these foundation fissures and expansion joints as direct underground pathways to reach structural wood framing.

### 3. What disclosure protections does the Colorado SPD19 form offer homebuyers?
Section I of the Colorado Real Estate Commission's Form SPD19 requires sellers to disclose any known past or active infestations of termites, carpenter ants, or wood-destroying insects, any history of pest treatments, and any known structural damage. Sellers who knowingly conceal past infestations or treatments face civil liability for fraudulent misrepresentation and breach of statutory disclosure obligations.

### 4. What are the signs of Arid-land subterranean termites in a Front Range home?
Key warning signs include:
- Earthen mud shelter tubes running across concrete foundation walls, crawlspaces, or plumbing pipes.
- Springtime nuptial swarms of dark-bodied, winged insects emerging near windows or light fixtures.
- Wavy drywall, bubbling paint, or hollow-sounding baseboards along exterior foundation walls.
- Discarded insect wings accumulating along interior basement windowsills.
