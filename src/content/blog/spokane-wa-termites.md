## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Spokane and the Inland Northwest

While Western Washington battles dampwood termites and moisture-loving wood decay, Spokane's semi-arid continental climate—characterized by freezing snowy winters, rapid spring thawing, and hot, dry summers—creates a unique microclimate where Western subterranean termites flourish.

### Why Spokane's Glacial Geography and Historic Architecture Accelerate Infestations

Several geographic, climatic, and structural factors make residential properties across Spokane particularly vulnerable to subterranean termite attacks:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The predominant wood-destroying insect in Spokane is the Western Subterranean Termite. Worker termites maintain vast underground colonies that survive freezing winter temperatures by retreating 3 to 5 feet below ground, below Spokane's seasonal frost line.
- **Spring Snowmelt and Basalt Foundation Saturation:** Spokane winters routinely bring substantial snowfall. When winter snowpacks melt rapidly in March and April, soil surrounding residential foundations becomes saturated. This deluge pushes subsoil moisture directly against basement walls, triggering aggressive subterranean termite foraging toward warm foundation perimeters.
- **Historic Basalt Stone and Rubble Foundations in South Hill and Brown's Addition:** Many homes built between 1890 and 1930 feature unreinforced basalt stone, river rock, or brick foundations held together by vintage lime mortar. Over a century of freeze-thaw cycles, mortar deteriorates, creating subterranean cavities that termites use as protected entry tunnels directly into wooden mudsills.
- **Ground-Level Wood Framing and Grade Contact:** In historic neighborhoods like West Central and the Garland District, vintage porches, deck ledger boards, and basement egress window frames frequently sit in direct contact with garden topsoil or mulch beds, offering termites direct access without requiring visible mud tubes.
- **Finished Basements Masking Uninsulated Rim Joists:** In many renovated Spokane properties, basement rim joists and mudsills are enclosed behind fiberglass insulation and sheetrock. Moisture condensation along cold concrete stem walls creates a concealed, high-humidity feeding zone for subterranean colonies.

### Biology and Mechanics: How Subterranean Termites Compromise Spokane Homes

Because subterranean termites lack a hardened exoskeleton, they must remain in humid environments to avoid desiccation. To travel across exposed basement foundation stone, concrete footings, or heating ducts, worker termites build earthen shelter tubes (mud tubes) composed of soil, saliva, and digested cellulose.

Once they breach the sill plate, worker termites feed parallel to the soft grain of structural fir and pine framing. They hollow out floor joists, main structural beams, and wall studs while leaving a paper-thin exterior veneer of wood and paint. Because the damage is entirely internal, homeowners often discover the problem only when heavy winter snow loads or newly installed granite countertops cause noticeable floor dips and wall cracking.

## Washington Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Spokane Buyers Must Know

Washington State law provides clear disclosure obligations for residential real estate sellers through statutory questionnaires.

### Washington RCW 64.06 and the NWMLS Form 17

Under **Revised Code of Washington (RCW) § 64.06.020**, sellers of residential real property must complete and deliver the statutory **Seller Disclosure Statement (commonly executed as NWMLS Form 17)** within five business days of mutual contract acceptance.

On Form 17, Section 4 (Structural) and Section 5 (Systems and Fixtures) impose strict disclosure mandates:
- **Question 4.C asks:** *"Are there any pest or insect infestations, or wood-destroying organisms on the property?"*
- **Question 4.D asks:** *"Has the property been treated for pests, insects, or wood-destroying organisms? If yes, explain."*
- **Question 4.E asks:** *"Are there any structural defects with the foundation, roof, floors, or load-bearing walls?"*

Under Washington law, sellers must answer based on their "actual knowledge." If a seller fails to provide Form 17, the buyer has the statutory right to rescind the purchase agreement up until the date of closing. Furthermore, under Washington case law (*Alejandre v. Bull* and *Stieneke v. Russi*), sellers who make fraudulent misrepresentations or actively conceal known structural defects can be held liable for damages.

### Washington State Licensed Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) Reports

In Washington, pest inspections must be performed by a **Structural Pest Inspector (SPI)** licensed by the Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA) under RCW 15.58.

A WSDA-compliant SPI inspection report specifically categorizes:
- **Active Wood Destroying Organisms:** Live infestations of subterranean termites, dampwood termites, wood-boring beetles, or carpenter ants.
- **Structural Pest Damage:** Any structural weakening of sills, posts, joists, or subflooring caused by past insect activity.
- **Conducive Conditions:** High soil grade, poor crawlspace vapor barriers, standing subfloor water, or earth-to-wood contact that increases vulnerability to future infestations.

Mortgage lenders (including conventional conforming loans, FHA, and VA) typically require complete remediation of active infestations and repair of compromised structural elements before issuing final loan approval.

### What Spokane Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Even with Form 17 obligations, Spokane sellers frequently use vague disclosures to avoid scaring away buyers:
- *"Basement perimeter treated preventatively 4 years ago."* (Concealing that an exterminator treated live termite mud tubes discovered along the north sill plate).
- *"Previous minor wood rot repaired near side entry door."* (Omitting that termites had tunneled from the exterior threshold into the subfloor framing).
- *"Seller has occupied home for 2 years and has no knowledge of past insect damage."* (Relying on short occupancy history to avoid investigating known past reports).

During this intense review window, a Spokane buyer must analyze:

### Technical Jargon That Conceals Catastrophic Risk

Inspectors often couch serious pest findings in restrained technical phrases:
- An inspector might write: *"Cellulose debris and efflorescence observed in crawlspace; some probed softening at rim joist near western sill."* A buyer might assume this is cosmetic aging, unaware that "probed softening" indicates hollowed structural wood caused by subterranean termites.
- An SPI report disclaimer might state: *"Basement rim joists 90% concealed by spray-foam insulation; inspection limited to visual line-of-sight."* This disclaimer shields the inspector while leaving the buyer fully exposed to hidden framing damage.

Manually cross-referencing seller answers, general inspection notes, and pest inspection disclaimers under tight contractual deadlines creates serious financial vulnerabilities.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Spokane

**1. Do termites really survive Spokane's freezing winters?**
Yes. Western subterranean termites survive freezing Eastern Washington winters by retreating deep into the soil beneath the regional frost line (3 to 5 feet deep). When soil temperatures rise and spring snowmelt saturates foundation walls, termites travel upward into residential framing.

**2. Is a seller required to disclose past termite treatments in Spokane?**
Yes. Under Washington RCW 64.06 (Form 17), sellers must explicitly disclose whether the property has ever been treated for pests, insects, or wood-destroying organisms. Answering falsely or concealing past treatments can expose the seller to legal liability.

**3. What is a WSDA Structural Pest Inspector (SPI) report?**
In Washington State, pest inspections must be performed by a Structural Pest Inspector licensed by the Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA). The SPI report identifies active wood-destroying insects, structural wood decay, and conditions conducive to future infestations.

**4. Why are historic homes on Spokane's South Hill particularly vulnerable?**
Many historic homes on South Hill feature basalt stone, river rock, or brick foundations with vintage lime-based mortar. Over decades of freeze-thaw cycles, mortar voids develop, allowing subterranean termites to bypass exterior barriers and access wooden mudsills and floor joists unseen.
