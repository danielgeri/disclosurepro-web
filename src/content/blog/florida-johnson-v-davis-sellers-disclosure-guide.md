Missing a concealed defect inside a Florida real estate transaction—such as an uninsurable 16-year-old roof, corroded cast-iron slab drains, prior flood claims, or an unfunded **Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)**—routinely saddles homebuyers with **$12,000 to $85,000+ in post-closing expenses and special assessments**. Unlike states with a single state-authored disclosure checklist, Florida disclosure law is anchored by the landmark Florida Supreme Court decision **_Johnson v. Davis_**, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985), paired with strict statutory mandates under **Fla. Stat. § 689.302** (Flood Disclosures), **Fla. Stat. § 718.503** (Condominium Governance & SIRS), and **Fla. Stat. § 627.7011** (Roof Underwriting Rules).

Whether you are purchasing a single-family home under the widely used **Florida Realtors/Florida Bar "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase** or reviewing a high-rise **Florida Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure – Condominium** packet, understanding what sellers are legally required to reveal—and what standard disclosures leave completely uncovered—is essential before your inspection period closes.

## *Johnson v. Davis*: Florida's Mandatory Latent Defect Rule (Even "As-Is")

In 1985, the Florida Supreme Court abolished traditional *caveat emptor* ("buyer beware") for residential real estate in **_Johnson v. Davis_**. The Court established an affirmative legal duty:

> Where the seller of a home knows of facts **materially affecting the value of the property** which are **not readily observable** and are **not known to the buyer**, the seller is under a duty to disclose them to the buyer.

### What *Johnson v. Davis* Covers—and Where It Stops
Three legal boundaries govern how *Johnson v. Davis* operates during your due diligence:

1. **"AS IS" Contracts Do NOT Waive *Johnson v. Davis*:** Many buyers mistakenly assume that signing a Florida "AS IS" Residential Contract relieves the seller of disclosure obligations. It does not. An "AS IS" clause means the seller is not obligated to make repairs after your inspection—it **never permits a seller to conceal or fail to disclose known latent material defects**.
2. **Written Form vs. Legal Duty:** Florida statutes do not mandate a single universal general-condition questionnaire across all sales. Instead, most brokerages use the **Florida Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure – Residential (`SPDR`)** or **Condominium** form to document *Johnson v. Davis* compliance. Even if a seller refuses to fill out the `SPDR` form (common in probate, investor flips, or bank-owned sales), the underlying *Johnson v. Davis* legal duty still applies if the seller has actual knowledge of hidden defects.
3. **"Readily Observable" Exclusions:** A seller is generally not liable under *Johnson v. Davis* for conditions a buyer or home inspector could easily see—such as a visibly stained ceiling, cracked driveway, or missing shingle tabs—or defects the seller genuinely did not know about.

## Decoding the Florida Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure & Statutory Forms

When reviewing a Florida residential disclosure packet, look beyond the basic `Yes / No / Don't Know` checkboxes on the **Florida Realtors SPDR**. Watch closely for these statutory addenda and high-risk disclosure traps:

### 1. Mandatory Florida Flood Disclosure (Fla. Stat. § 689.302)
Enacted under HB 1049 and expanded under SB 948, **Fla. Stat. § 689.302** requires residential sellers (and condominium developers) to deliver a standalone written **Flood Disclosure** at or before contract execution. The seller must affirmatively state whether:
- The property has suffered **flood damage** during the seller's ownership.
- The seller has ever **filed an insurance claim** for flood damage (including National Flood Insurance Program claims).
- The seller has ever **received federal assistance** (such as FEMA grants or SBA disaster loans) for flood damage.

**Buyer Warning:** A home in **FEMA Zone X** can still suffer catastrophic street or pluvial stormwater intrusion during tropical downpours. Always verify the § 689.302 flood form against municipal elevation certificates and prior insurance loss-history reports.

### 2. Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) & Termite Bonds
Florida sits inside the highest subterranean, Formosan, and West Indian drywood termite zone in the country. As we break down in our [Gainesville termite disclosure guide](/blog/gainesville-fl-termites/), sellers routinely mark "No" to active termites while listing a "transferable pest bond"—which may be a **retreat-only** contract that pays **$0 toward structural framing replacement**. Always order a dedicated **FDACS Form 13645 WDO Inspection**.

### 3. Mandatory Radon, Property Tax & HOA Summaries
Florida contracts also require statutory notices for **Radon Gas (Fla. Stat. § 404.056(5))**, **Property Tax Disclosure Summary (Fla. Stat. § 689.261)**—warning buyers that Florida's *Save Our Homes* homestead cap resets upon sale, often doubling or tripling annual property taxes—and **HOA Disclosure Summaries (Fla. Stat. § 720.401)** for deed-restricted communities.

## Why Florida Insurers Reject Homes: 4-Point, Wind Mitigation & Citizens Roof Rules

In Florida, passing a general home inspection is only half the battle. If the property cannot pass insurance underwriting, your lender will refuse to fund the mortgage—or force-place coverage costing thousands per year.

### The 4-Point Inspection Trap
Florida property insurers routinely demand a **4-Point Inspection** on homes 15 to 30+ years old, evaluating four specific underwriting triggers:
1. **Roof:** Shingle/tile condition, remaining useful life, and active leaks.
2. **Electrical:** Insurers automatically blacklist **Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok**, **Zinsco**, **Sylvania**, knob-and-tube, and unmitigated single-strand aluminum branch wiring.
3. **Plumbing:** Grey **polybutylene (PB2110)** supply pipes (common in 1978–1995 Florida subdivisions), galvanized steel, and pre-1975 **cast-iron drain lines** under concrete slabs are frequent instant-denial triggers.
4. **HVAC:** Operational heat and cooling systems free of visible rust or condensation damage.

### Florida Statute § 627.7011 & Citizens Roof Underwriting
Under **Fla. Stat. § 627.7011(5)**, Florida insurers cannot refuse to issue or renew a homeowner's policy *solely* based on roof age if the roof is **under 15 years old**. Once a roof reaches **15 years of age or older**, the homeowner must be allowed to submit a licensed roof inspection certifying at least **5 years of remaining useful life** before an insurer can non-renew solely due to age.

However, **Citizens Property Insurance** and private Florida carriers still reject older roofs showing curling tabs, granule loss, or prior patching—and many policies shift roofs over 10–15 years old to **Actual Cash Value (ACV)** loss settlement rather than Replacement Cost Value. A seller who marks *"No known roof leaks"* on a 19-year-old shingle roof is telling the literal truth while leaving you with a **$24,000 roof replacement mandate within 30 days of closing**.

### Wind Mitigation Inspection (Form OIR-B1-1802)
Always order a **Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection (OIR-B1-1802)** during your inspection window. Documenting **hurricane clips or double wraps** (roof-to-wall straps), **Secondary Water Resistance (SWR)** peel-and-stick underlayment, and **impact-rated windows/shutters** can slash your annual windstorm premium by **$1,500 to $4,500+ per year**.

## Post-Surfside Condo Laws: Milestone Inspections, SIRS & the 7-Day Review Window

Following the Champlain Towers South collapse in Surfside, Florida enacted transformative condominium legislation (**SB 4-D, SB 154, and subsequent 2024–2025 statutory updates**) that radically changed how buyers evaluate condo disclosures:

### 1. Structural Integrity Reserve Studies (SIRS)
Under **Fla. Stat. § 718.112**, condominium associations with buildings **3 habitable stories or higher** were mandated to complete an initial **Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)** by the extended statutory deadline of **December 31, 2025**. Crucially:
- **Non-Waivable Reserves:** Associations can no longer vote to waive or underfund reserves for 10 critical structural/safety components—including roofs, load-bearing walls/structural systems, fireproofing, plumbing, electrical, waterproofing/exterior painting, and exterior windows/doors.
- **Assessment Shock:** Buildings that spent decades suppressing monthly HOA dues by waiving reserves now face massive fee hikes and **$15,000 to $100,000+ per-unit special assessments** to fund SIRS baselines.

### 2. Milestone Structural Inspections (Fla. Stat. § 553.899)
Buildings 3+ stories high must undergo a **Phase 1 Milestone Inspection** at 30 years of age (or 25 years in certain coastal jurisdictions/local rules). If Phase 1 uncovers substantial structural deterioration (such as concrete rebar spalling or saltwater corrosion), a **Phase 2 invasive engineering inspection** is triggered immediately.

### 3. Expanded 7-Business-Day Buyer Rescission Window (Fla. Stat. § 718.503)
Effective **July 1, 2025**, Florida amended **Fla. Stat. § 718.503** to expand a resale condominium buyer's statutory right to review association documents—including the **Milestone Inspection Summary** and **SIRS Report**—from 3 days to **7 days (excluding weekends and legal holidays)**. If the seller fails to deliver these required engineering and financial disclosures, or if the numbers reveal severe reserve shortfalls, you retain a statutory right to void the contract before the 7-business-day clock expires.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Florida Seller Property Disclosures

### Does a Florida "AS IS" contract mean the seller does not have to disclose defects?
No. Under the Florida Supreme Court ruling in *Johnson v. Davis*, even when using the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar "AS IS" Residential Contract, a seller is legally obligated to disclose all known facts materially affecting the property's value that are not readily observable to the buyer. "As-Is" only removes the seller's obligation to perform repairs—not their duty of honesty.

### Is a written Seller's Property Disclosure form legally mandatory in Florida?
While Florida law imposes the *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure duty on every residential seller, Florida does not have a single state-mandated general property questionnaire—the **Florida Realtors SPDR** is technically voluntary, though standard in most transactions. However, specific standalone disclosures **are** strictly mandatory by statute, including the **Fla. Stat. § 689.302 Flood Disclosure**, radon notice, property tax summary, and condo/HOA governance disclosures.

### What happens if a Florida condo seller hasn't provided the SIRS or Milestone Inspection report?
Under **Fla. Stat. § 718.503** (as amended effective July 1, 2025), sellers of units in applicable buildings must provide the association's Milestone Inspection summary and Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) along with core condo financial documents. Buyers receive a **7-day review period (excluding weekends and holidays)** from receipt of the complete document package to cancel the contract and recover their earnest money deposit.

### Can Citizens Property Insurance deny my mortgage closing because of an old roof?
Yes. Even though **Fla. Stat. § 627.7011(5)** prevents carriers from rejecting a roof under 15 years old solely by age—and gives owners of 15+ year roofs the right to prove 5+ years of remaining useful life—insurers can still decline coverage based on physical wear, lifted tabs, prior patches, or failed 4-Point inspection criteria. Without bindable property insurance, your mortgage lender will not fund closing.
