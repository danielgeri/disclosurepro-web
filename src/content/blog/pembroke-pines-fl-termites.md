## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Pembroke Pines and Broward County

Pembroke Pines is situated in southwestern Broward County directly adjacent to the Florida Everglades. Our local environment features a tropical climate with high humidity, year-round warm temperatures, a shallow water table, and severe tropical storm activity—conditions that support hyper-aggressive termite colonies.

### Why Pembroke Pines Housing Stock and Tropical Climate Elevate Pest Risk

Several architectural, environmental, and regional factors make residential properties across Pembroke Pines exceptionally vulnerable to structural termite infestations:

- **The Threat of Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** South Florida is one of the few regions in the United States infested by Asian subterranean termites. This invasive species forms massive colonies containing millions of individuals that consume wood at an alarming rate, producing structural hollows in load-bearing framing within months.
- **CBS First Floors with Wood-Frame Second Stories:** Many two-story single-family homes in Silver Lakes, Pembroke Falls, and Chapel Trail feature concrete block construction on the ground floor with conventional wood-frame construction on the second floor. Subterranean termites travel undetected through interior plumbing chases or electrical conduits from the ground slab directly into upper-level load-bearing wood framing.
- **Monolithic Slabs and Plumbing Penetrations:** Pembroke Pines homes are built on monolithic concrete slab-on-grade foundations. When concrete cures, shrinkage gaps form around copper water pipes, PVC drain stacks, and shower/tub block-outs. Subterranean termites require a gap of only 1/32 of an inch to enter structural partition walls.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Pembroke Pines Neighborhoods:**
  - *Silver Lakes & Chapel Trail:* 1990s master-planned developments built around interconnected canal systems. High ground moisture and dense perimeter landscaping create optimal foraging conditions for subterranean colonies.
  - *Grand Palms & Pembroke Falls:* Gated communities featuring Mediterranean-style homes with barrel tile roofs and decorative stucco. Drywood termites penetrate roof eaves, unsealed gable vents, and fascia boards loosened during seasonal tropical storms.
  - *Eastern Pembroke Pines & Cinnamon Ridge:* 1970s and 1980s single-story ranch homes where mature ficus and oak tree root systems provide direct subterranean transit lines toward residential foundations.

### Pest Biology: Asian Subterranean vs. West Indian Drywood Termites

Pembroke Pines homeowners face concurrent threats from two destructive pest species:
- **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** Ground-nesting insects that build extensive underground tunnel networks and construct thick earthen mud shelter tubes across foundation stems, drywall, and utility conduits to reach structural timbers.
- **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Airborne insects that fly into attic spaces and roof framing during warm spring and summer evenings. They establish colonies directly inside roof trusses, plywood sheathing, and door casings without requiring soil contact, ejecting sand-like hexagonal fecal pellets (frass).

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis and FDACS WDO Reports

Florida real estate law provides robust legal protections for property purchasers through landmark judicial doctrine and standardized state inspection procedures.

### The Landmark Florida Precedent: *Johnson v. Davis* (1985)

In Florida real estate law, the definitive standard regarding property disclosures was established by the Florida Supreme Court in the landmark decision ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**:
- The court held that where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them.
- Subsequent rulings (such as *Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.*) confirmed that an "AS IS" contract (including the standard FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase) does not relieve a seller of their *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure duty or shield them from liability for fraudulent non-disclosure.

### The Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential

The standard Florida Realtors **Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential** requires sellers to detail known property conditions:

- **Section 10 (Wood-Destroying Organisms / Pests):** Specifically requires the seller to disclose:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present infestation or damage caused by wood-destroying organisms (termites, powder post beetles, old house borers, wood-decaying fungi)?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present treatments for wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Is the property currently covered by a transferable termite warranty or treatment contract?"*
  - *"Have any structural repairs been completed to repair damage caused by wood-destroying organisms?"*
- **Section 5 & 8 (Roofing & Structural Conditions):** Mandates disclosure of roof leaks, water intrusion behind stucco, and attic moisture—the primary conditions that sustain Formosan and Asian subterranean carton nests.

### The Official Florida WDO Report (Form FDACS-13645)

Official termite inspections in Florida are regulated by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)** under **Chapter 482 Florida Statutes** and must be recorded on standardized **Form FDACS-13645**:
- **Section 2 (Inspection Findings):** Details visible active infestations, evidence of previous infestations, visible structural wood damage, and past chemical treatment drill marks.
- **Section 3 (Inaccessible Areas):** Highlights obstructed zones, including vaulted ceilings lacking attic crawl spaces, areas behind spray foam attic insulation, enclosed soffits, and walls obstructed by built-in cabinetry.

### What Pembroke Pines Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers in Broward County often use evasive language on the Seller's Property Disclosure:
- *"Local spot treatment completed for drywood termites in 2022."* (Conceals that localized treatment was chosen to avoid the expense of whole-structure tent fumigation).
- *"Second-floor framing reinforced during master bath remodel."* (Downplays the structural sistering of Asian subterranean termite-eaten wall studs).
- *"Transferable termite bond in place."* Fails to reveal that the bond covers only subterranean species, leaving drywood termite infestations completely uncovered.

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Material structural hazards rarely appear as an explicit, single warning. Instead, critical indicators are scattered across separate disclosures:
- The Home Inspector notes on page 42: *"Second-floor master bedroom baseboard shows localized moisture staining and soft wood trim near plumbing wall."*
- The FDACS WDO Pest Inspector writes under Inaccessible Areas: *"Spray foam insulation applied to underside of roof deck prevented visual inspection of attic truss chords."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatment on the disclosure, writing: *"Routine annual pest control service maintained."*

When evaluated separately, each statement seems routine. But when cross-referenced, they reveal a dangerous reality: Asian subterranean termites entered through the ground-floor plumbing chase into the second-story master bedroom wall, hollowed out the structural 2x4 studs and floor framing, and colonized attic trusses hidden behind the spray foam insulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Pembroke Pines

### 1. Are termites common in Pembroke Pines, Florida?
Yes. Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*), Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*), and West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*) are widespread throughout Pembroke Pines and Broward County. Tropical humidity, high water tables, and mixed CBS/wood-frame construction create prime conditions for termite activity.

### 2. What does Florida law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under the Florida Supreme Court ruling in *Johnson v. Davis*, sellers must disclose all known latent material defects affecting property value, including active or past termite infestations, chemical treatment history, and structural wood damage.

### 3. What is an FDACS-13645 WDO report in Florida?
Form FDACS-13645 is the official Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) inspection report mandated by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. It documents visible active infestations, prior damage, past treatment marks, and inaccessible property areas.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite repairs in Florida?
No. Standard Florida homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, wood rot, and wood-destroying insects. Insurance carriers classify insect damage as a preventable maintenance issue, requiring property owners to bear all structural and cosmetic repair costs.
