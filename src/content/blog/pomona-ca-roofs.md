# The Ultimate Guide to Spotting Roof Damage in Pomona Real Estate Disclosures

Positioned strategically at the eastern gateway of Los Angeles County bordering the Inland Empire, Pomona is a historic and dynamic city characterized by diverse residential architecture. From the grand Victorian, Craftsman, and Spanish Colonial Revival residences in the Lincoln Park Historic District and Wilton Heights to mid-century ranches in Ganesha Hills and modern master-planned developments in Phillips Ranch, Pomona offers accessible Southern California living with rich cultural roots.

However, behind Pomona’s sunny valley skies and picturesque mountain backdrops lies an intensely punishing physical environment for residential roofing systems: **fierce seasonal Santa Ana windstorms howling through mountain passes, triple-digit summer thermal baking, seismic micro-fault racking along the active San Jose Fault, and strict California Title 24 energy mandates**.

Under California real estate law—governed by the statutory **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)** codified under California Civil Code § 1102—sellers must disclose known material defects affecting property value and safety. Yet identifying latent wind shear damage, cracked Spanish concrete tiles, or unpermitted roofing layers hidden behind carefully worded disclosures requires an in-depth understanding of Pomona Valley building physics and California statutory disclosure mandates.

## Why Pomona Roofs Fail: Santa Ana Winds, Extreme Heat, and Fault Line Stress

Pomona’s inland valley topography and unique atmospheric patterns create severe, cumulative structural stress on residential roofing systems.

### Severe Santa Ana Wind Events and Cajon Pass Funneling
Pomona sits directly in the path of the ferocious Santa Ana winds that blow out of the Great Basin through the Cajon and San Gorgonio passes:
- **High-Velocity Wind Uplift:** During autumn and winter Santa Ana events, dry desert winds gust between 50 and 70+ mph. As these winds strike windward roof slopes, they create massive negative pressure (uplift) along eaves, hips, and ridges.
- **Shingle Tab Creasing and Tear-Off:** Wind gets underneath brittle shingle tabs, breaking the manufacturer’s thermal adhesive seal. Shingles flap violently, creasing horizontally above the nail line and tearing free, exposing bare felt underlayment.
- **Tile Chattering and Breakage:** In tile-roofed communities like Phillips Ranch, high winds induce "tile chattering," where unfastened clay or concrete tiles vibrate against one another. This causes interlocking lugs to snap off, causing tiles to slide out of position and allowing driving rain to strike deteriorating underlayment directly.

### Triple-Digit Summer Heat and Thermal Fatigue
Situated in the eastern Pomona Valley, Pomona experiences prolonged summer heatwaves with temperatures frequently exceeding 100°F to 105°F:
- **Asphalt Volatilization:** Direct solar exposure elevates dark shingle temperatures to 160°F or more. This extreme heat bakes out volatile hydrocarbons and plasticizing oils from the asphalt substrate, leaving shingles brittle, curled, and prone to cracking.
- **Granule Loss:** As thermal expansion and contraction cycles repeat daily, the bond between the asphalt coating and protective mineral granules weakens. Wind and rain wash away the granules, exposing the underlying fiberglass mat to rapid ultraviolet breakdown.
- **Attic Heat Trapping:** Poorly ventilated attics in older homes trap superheated air, cooking roof sheathing from below and causing plywood decking to delaminate.

### Active Seismic Fault Lines: The San Jose and Chino Faults
Pomona sits in an active seismic corridor bisected by the San Jose Fault and bordered by the Chino Fault and Sierra Madre Fault zones:
- **Structural Racking:** Periodic micro-earthquakes and subtle tectonic creep cause home framing, roof trusses, and heavy masonry chimneys to flex and settle independently.
- **Flashing Rupture:** This structural movement shears brittle roofing mastic and pulls metal counter-flashings and step flashings away from stucco sidewalls and brick chimneys. These hairline ruptures allow water to penetrate interior wall cavities during winter rains without leaving immediate ceiling stains.

### Dry Brush and Wildland-Urban Interface Hazards
With surrounding foothills, windborne embers during wildfire events pose extreme risks to compromised or poorly maintained roofs with cracked tiles or accumulated dry debris in valleys.

## Understanding California Real Estate Disclosure Laws

California enforces comprehensive statutory disclosure requirements designed to ensure transparency and protect homebuyers from undisclosed structural defects.

### The California Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)
Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of residential real estate (1 to 4 units) must provide prospective buyers with the statutory **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement**.

Section II of the TDS mandates specific disclosures concerning the roof:
- **Roof Age and Composition:** The seller must disclose the approximate age of the roof covering and its material (e.g., concrete tile, clay tile, asphalt shingle, built-up gravel).
- **Known Defects or Malfunctions:** The seller must affirmatively declare whether they are aware of any significant defects, ongoing or past leaks, or structural failures.
- **Unpermitted Work:** Sellers must disclose whether any reroofing, skylight installations, or structural framing changes were executed without required City of Pomona building permits.

### The Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)
In standard California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) transactions, sellers must also complete the **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)**, an exhaustive document requiring disclosure of:
- Any past or current water leaks, moisture intrusion, or attic dampness.
- History of insurance claims filed for wind, storm, or roof damage.
- Repair history, chemical moss/algae treatments, or unpermitted handyman patches.

### The Landmark Legal Precedent: *Easton v. Strassburger*
The gold standard for California real estate disclosure litigation is ***Easton v. Strassburger* (152 Cal. App. 3d 90 (1984))**. The court established that sellers and real estate agents have an affirmative duty to conduct a reasonably competent and diligent visual inspection of accessible areas and disclose all material facts affecting the value or desirability of the property.

If a seller in Pomona conceals Santa Ana wind damage, masks active ceiling leaks, or fails to disclose an unpermitted reroof:
- **Compensatory Damages:** Buyers can recover actual damages, including full roof replacement costs, structural framing repairs, and interior remediation.
- **Punitive Damages and Rescission:** Under California Civil Code § 3294, intentional concealment or fraudulent misrepresentation allows buyers to pursue punitive damages or judicial rescission of the purchase agreement.

### California Title 24 "Cool Roof" Requirements (Climate Zone 9)
Under the **California Building Standards Code (Title 24, Part 6)**, Pomona falls into **Climate Zone 9**. Any residential reroofing project altering more than 50% of the roof surface or 1,000 square feet must utilize certified "Cool Roof" materials meeting strict Solar Reflectance Index (SRI) criteria. Sellers who performed unpermitted DIY reroofs often used non-compliant materials, creating code compliance liabilities for the buyer upon future resale.

## The Manual Review Problem: Navigating Pomona Escrow Packets

In a Pomona real estate transaction, closing and disclosure packets regularly span 180 to 240 pages, incorporating:
- The statutory California TDS and C.A.R. SPQ disclosure questionnaires.
- Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD) statements detailing San Jose Fault zones, high-fire-hazard severity zones, and liquefaction areas.
- City of Pomona Development Services historical permit records.
- HOA governing documents, CC&Rs, and reserve studies in planned communities like Phillips Ranch.
- General home inspection reports, WDO pest inspection reports, and roof specialty evaluations.

### Ambiguous Phrases Used by Sellers to Obscure Roof Defects
Sellers seeking to protect their proceeds frequently utilize vague, minimizing phrases in their disclosures:
- *"Roof inspected annually; several cracked tiles replaced after windy season in 2023."* (Conceals that high Santa Ana winds sheared tiles across multiple slopes, exposing aged, sun-rotted 30-pound felt underlayment).
- *"Minor maintenance and resealing completed around chimney flashing in 2024."* (Masks the fact that San Jose Fault micro-settling separated the brick chimney from the roof deck, creating active attic leaks during rain).
- *"Roof age estimated at 12 years; concrete tile roof built to last a lifetime."* (Downplays the reality that while concrete tiles last 50 years, the underlying organic felt paper underlayment deteriorates after 20 years in high desert heat).
- *"Cosmetic ceiling drywall patch in hallway completed during interior repaint."* (Covers an active valley leak where water penetrates during wind-driven winter storms).

Given standard California **17-day buyer contingency timelines**, homebuyers are frequently overwhelmed by voluminous documentation and overlook subtle warnings that signify catastrophic roof failure.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Pomona

### 1. Does the City of Pomona require a building permit for roof replacements?
Yes. The City of Pomona Development Services Department requires a building permit for all residential reroofing projects exceeding 100 square feet. Additionally, because Pomona is in California Climate Zone 9, all roof replacements must comply with California Title 24 Energy Code requirements for Cool Roofs, necessitating certified high-reflectance materials and proper attic ventilation ratios.

### 2. What is a tile "lift and reset," and when is it necessary in Pomona?
A "lift and reset" involves carefully removing existing concrete or clay tiles, stripping away deteriorating underlayment down to the wood deck, replacing rotted decking, installing new high-performance synthetic underlayment and flashings, and reinstalling the original tiles. In Pomona, while tiles can last 50+ years, the organic felt underlayment often fails after 20 to 25 years due to intense valley heat, making a lift-and-reset necessary.

### 3. How do Santa Ana winds damage roofs in Pomona?
Santa Ana winds funnel through the nearby Cajon and San Gorgonio passes at speeds of 50 to 70+ mph. These dry, turbulent gusts create strong uplift along eaves and ridges, unsealing shingle tabs, breaking fiberglass mats, and chattering or dislodging heavy concrete tiles, leaving underlying underlayment vulnerable to subsequent rainstorms.

### 4. Can a Pomona seller sell a property "As-Is" to avoid disclosing past roof leaks?
No. In California, selling real property "As-Is" does not exempt a seller from statutory disclosure requirements under California Civil Code § 1102. An "As-Is" clause simply indicates that the seller will not perform repairs or offer price concessions; the seller remains legally bound to disclose all known material defects, past roof leaks, or unpermitted roofing work on the TDS and SPQ.
