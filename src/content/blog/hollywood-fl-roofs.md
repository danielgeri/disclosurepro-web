# The Ultimate Guide to Spotting Roof Damage in Hollywood Real Estate Disclosures

Nestled between Fort Lauderdale and Miami along the Atlantic coast in Broward County, Hollywood, Florida offers quintessential South Florida living. From the grand 1920s Mediterranean Revival estates flanking the man-made waterways of Hollywood Lakes and the mid-century CBS (concrete block) ranches of Hollywood Hills and Boulevard Heights to luxury golf-course properties in Emerald Hills and beachfront residences, Hollywood represents desirable subtropical real estate.

However, behind the swaying royal palms and ocean breezes lies one of the most punishing environments for residential roofs in North America: **tropical storm and hurricane wind uplift pressures, relentless tropical UV radiation, driving horizontal rains, corrosive salt air that rusts metal fasteners and flashings, and the strict structural mandates of the Florida Building Code**.

In Florida, real estate disclosure is anchored by the historic landmark Florida Supreme Court ruling ***Johnson v. Davis*—a case that was fought specifically over a concealed leaking roof**. Under Florida law, sellers must affirmatively disclose all known material latent defects. Yet in South Florida’s fast-moving property market, spotting vague disclosure caveats, unpermitted reroofing overlays, and aging underlayment beneath concrete tiles requires deep scrutiny.

## Why Hollywood Roofs Fail: Hurricane Forces, Tropical UV, and Salt Corrosion

Positioned directly in South Florida’s hurricane alley, Hollywood residential roofs must withstand relentless mechanical, atmospheric, and chemical stressors.

### Hurricane Wind Uplift and Negative Pressure Vortices
During tropical storms and major hurricanes:
- **Aerodynamic Corner Vortices:** High winds flowing over pitched roofs separate at the eaves, ridges, and hip corners, generating powerful suction vortices. Uplift forces easily exceed 60 to 90 pounds per square foot during Category 2 through 4 storms.
- **Tile Chattering and Mechanical Fastener Shearing:** On barrel clay and concrete S-tile roofs common in Hollywood Lakes and Emerald Hills, wind uplift causes tiles to "chatter" against the deck. In older installations where tiles were secured with galvanized roofing nails rather than stainless steel screws or foam adhesive paddy systems, fasteners shear, launching tiles into airborne projectiles.
- **Asphalt Shingle Sealant Peeling:** On composition shingle homes in Hollywood Hills, tropical gusts break the thermal adhesive sealant strips along the leading edge, creasing the shingles and exposing the vulnerable underlayment below.

### The Breakdown of Underlayment Beneath Tiles
Many buyers mistakenly assume that concrete roof tiles are the waterproof barrier:
- **Tiles Are the Shedding Layer:** Concrete and terra cotta tiles are simply an aesthetic shedding mantle; the true waterproof barrier is the asphalt-saturated underlayment beneath them.
- **Subtropical Thermal Degradation:** Summer temperatures on dark barrel tile roofs in Hollywood reach 160°F to 180°F. The heat bakes organic felt underlayment, drying out asphalt oils and leaving the paper brittle and cracked.
- **Lifespan Discrepancy:** While concrete tiles may remain structurally intact for 40 to 50 years, traditional 30-pound or 90-pound organic felt underlayment in South Florida rarely survives past 15 to 20 years before disintegrating.

### Salt Spray and Atmospheric Galvanic Corrosion
Hollywood’s oceanfront setting and barrier islands bring airborne marine aerosols across the city:
- **Fastener Corrosion:** Salt-laden humidity penetrates roof tile gaps and eave soffits, attacking standard electro-galvanized nails and staples. Corroded fasteners rust through, leaving tiles and decking sheathing unanchored.
- **Flashing and Valley Rust:** Aluminum and light galvanized valley metal, drip edges, and plumbing vent flashings pit and oxidize, opening pinhole leaks that channel water directly into attic trusses.

## Understanding Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws

Florida maintains stringent legal protections regarding property disclosure, driven by landmark judicial precedent rather than just legislative statutes.

### The Landmark Precedent: *Johnson v. Davis*
The cornerstone of all residential real estate disclosure law in Florida is the landmark Florida Supreme Court decision in ***Johnson v. Davis* (480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985))**.

Interestingly, this pivotal case began right here in South Florida when a seller falsely assured a buyer that ceiling stains were merely cosmetic and that the roof was sound, only for the home to suffer massive ceiling leaks during the first torrential rain. 

The Florida Supreme Court established the definitive legal doctrine:
> *"Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under a duty to disclose them to the buyer."*

This rule applies universally across Florida:
- An "As-Is" contract (such as the standard Florida Realtors/Florida Bar "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase) **does not relieve** the seller of their affirmative legal duty to disclose known material latent defects.
- Active concealment—such as applying kilz primer and fresh paint over water-stained acoustic ceilings or applying silicone caulk over cracked tile valleys—exposes the seller to severe liability for fraudulent misrepresentation and contract rescission.

### The Florida Association of Realtors (FAR/BAR) Seller’s Disclosure
In standard South Florida residential sales, sellers complete the **Florida Realtors Seller’s Property Disclosure**:
- **Roof Section Requirements:** The seller must state the approximate age of the roof covering and identify whether it is tile, shingle, metal, or built-up.
- **Known Leaks and Insurance Claims:** The seller must disclose any past or present roof leaks, missing tiles, interior water stains, or historical insurance claims filed for storm or hurricane damage.
- **Permit Verification:** The seller must affirmatively declare whether reroofing work was completed with mandatory Broward County / City of Hollywood building permits and passed final inspection.

### Florida Building Code and the 25% Rule
Under the **Florida Building Code (Existing Building)**, if more than 25% of a roof section is repaired or replaced within a 12-month period, the entire roofing system must be brought up to current Florida Building Code standards. In South Florida, this mandates secondary water barriers (self-adhering modified bitumen underlayment), enhanced ring-shank decking re-nailing (6-inch on center spacing), and wind-mitigation compliant roof-to-wall hurricane clips.

## The Manual Review Problem: Navigating Dense South Florida Disclosures

In a standard Hollywood real estate purchase, the buyer receives an extensive closing dossier often exceeding 180 to 240 pages, encompassing:
- The FAR/BAR Seller’s Property Disclosure.
- City of Hollywood Building Division permit histories and open code violations.
- Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form (OIR-B1-1802) for property insurance discounts.
- Elevation certificates and FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs).
- HOA or condo association estoppel letters and financial reserve studies.
- Comprehensive structural home inspections and wood-destroying organism (WDO) reports.

### Deceptive Language Frequently Used on Florida Disclosures
Sellers aware of deteriorating roofs often use euphemistic language to dodge liability while minimizing red flags:
- *"Roof inspected and touched up following Hurricane Ian; several loose tiles re-secured."* (Conceals that the underlayment beneath the tiles was torn and waterlogged, allowing ongoing seepage).
- *"Minor cosmetic ceiling stain in guest bedroom from prior tropical depression; dry since."* (Downplays an ongoing flashing leak at the chimney chase or AC condenser stand that leaks during high-wind rains).
- *"Roof original to 2005 rebuild; concrete tiles in excellent physical shape."* (Technically true regarding the concrete shells, but conceals that the 20-year-old organic felt underlayment has reached the end of its useful lifespan).
- *"Roof maintenance package completed by licensed handyman in 2023."* (Masks the fact that unpermitted elastomeric sealant was smeared over rusted valley metal without pulling a City of Hollywood permit).

Given standard Florida contract **10-to-15 day inspection contingency periods**, buyers under pressure frequently fail to cross-reference ambiguous disclosure statements against city permit databases and wind mitigation documents.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Hollywood, FL

### 1. Does the City of Hollywood require a permit for roof repairs and replacements?
Yes. The City of Hollywood Building Division requires a building permit for any roof replacement and for roof repairs that exceed $1,000 in value or alter more than 25% of a roof section. All work must strictly comply with the current Florida Building Code, which mandates secondary water barriers (peel-and-stick underlayment) and enhanced deck re-nailing.

### 2. What is the landmark *Johnson v. Davis* rule in Florida real estate?
Under *Johnson v. Davis* (480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)), the Florida Supreme Court established that when a seller of residential real estate knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property that are not readily observable by the buyer, the seller has an affirmative legal duty to disclose them. Selling a home "As-Is" does not shield a seller from liability if they knowingly fail to disclose latent roof defects or leaks.

### 3. What is the Florida 25% roof replacement rule?
Under the Florida Building Code (Existing Building), if more than 25% of an existing roof section is repaired, replaced, or recovered within any 12-month period, the entire roof section must be brought into full compliance with the modern Florida Building Code. In many older South Florida homes, this triggers a mandatory total roof replacement.

### 4. Why can't home inspectors always detect roof leaks on tile roofs in Hollywood?
Home inspectors often cannot walk on barrel clay or concrete tile roofs due to the significant risk of cracking brittle tiles and creating new leaks. Consequently, inspectors frequently conduct inspections from ladder positions at the eave. Because the tiles cover the waterproof underlayment, brittle or rotting felt paper cannot be visually verified without physically lifting tiles, leaving hidden underlayment failures undetected.
