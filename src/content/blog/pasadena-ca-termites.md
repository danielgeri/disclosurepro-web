## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Pasadena and the San Gabriel Valley

Pasadena’s foothill microclimate, historical timber construction, and mature urban forest create ideal conditions for destructive wood-boring insect colonies.

### Why Pasadena Housing Stock and Foothill Microclimates Exacerbate Pest Hazards

Several localized climatic and structural elements heighten termite vulnerability throughout Pasadena:

- **Foothill Moisture & Mountain Runoff:** Nestled beneath the San Gabriel Mountains, Pasadena receives higher annual precipitation and foothill dew than coastal Los Angeles basins. Runoff channeled down the Arroyo Seco keeps subgrade soils moist, creating prime conditions for subterranean colonies to survive warm summer months.
- **Architectural Timber Heritage (The Craftsman Legacy):** Home to the historic American Arts and Crafts movement, Pasadena boasts thousands of homes featuring exposed exterior wood. Deep roof overhangs, decorative timber knee braces, wood shake siding, and open attic vents provide direct aerial entry for swarming drywood termites.
- **Vulnerabilities Across Pasadena Neighborhoods:**
  - *Bungalow Heaven & Historic Highlands:* Early 20th-century Craftsman bungalows built on raised foundations with local Arroyo stone and mortar. Inadequate crawlspace cross-ventilation and wood siding in close contact with garden soil invite subterranean termites directly into timber mudsills.
  - *Madison Heights & Oak Knoll:* Grand Spanish Colonial Revival and Tudor Revival estates with intricate wood roof structures, interior timber lintels, and thick stucco exteriors where hidden moisture leaks facilitate subterranean and drywood feeding behind plaster.
  - *Linda Vista & San Rafael Hills:* Mid-century hillside homes built on stepped concrete foundations and post-and-beam frameworks. Shifting hillside soils and expansive exterior wood view decks present critical pest hazards.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean vs. Drywood Termites in the San Gabriel Valley

Pasadena homebuyers must understand the distinct feeding and reproductive habits of both local species:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Ground-dwelling insects that nest deep in soil to maintain required humidity. They construct protective earthen mud shelter tubes across concrete stem walls, Arroyo stone foundations, and plumbing pipes to access structural floor framing. They hollow out timber along the soft internal grain, destroying load-bearing capacity while leaving an outer shell of paint intact.
- **Western Drywood Termites (*Incisitermes minor*):** Non-soil-dwelling insects that colonize dry structural lumber. During warm, dry Santa Ana wind conditions in late summer and autumn, reproductive winged swarmers take flight and infiltrate attic vents, rafter tails, and window casings. They carve multi-directional galleries and kick out distinctive six-sided fecal pellets (frass) through microscopic exit holes.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 and SPCB Branch 3 Reports

California maintains one of the strictest statutory real estate disclosure frameworks in the United States, yet buyers must navigate subtle legal protections.

### Statutory Disclosures: The TDS and SPQ

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of 1-to-4 unit residential dwellings must provide buyers with the statutory **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)** and the standard California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)**:

- **TDS Section II.C:** Specifically queries sellers regarding known *"settling, slippage, sliding, or other soil problems"* and *"flooding, drainage or grading problems,"* both of which create subterranean moisture traps and entry cracks.
- **SPQ Section V (Pests, Infestations, and Soil):** Explicitly requires the seller to disclose:
  - Any past or current infestations of termites, dry rot, powder-post beetles, or wood-destroying pests.
  - Previous chemical treatments, pest control contracts, or structural repairs.
  - Copies of all structural pest control inspection reports completed during their ownership.
- **Concealment and Fraud:** Under California law, a seller who knowingly conceals known active infestations, paints over termite kickout holes, or conceals prior Branch 3 reports is liable for actual reconstruction damages, emotional distress, and punitive damages for intentional fraud.

### SPCB Branch 3 Structural Pest Control Inspection Reports

In California real estate transactions, standard home inspectors do not inspect for wood-destroying pests. Inspections must be conducted by licensed Branch 3 structural pest inspectors governed by the **Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB)**:

- **Section 1 Findings (Active Infestation & Immediate Damage):** Identifies visible active colonies of subterranean or drywood termites, active fungus/dry rot, and damaged structural lumber requiring immediate extermination and physical structural carpentry repair.
- **Section 2 Conditions (Conditions Conducive to Infestation):** Identifies environmental conditions that do not currently show active pests but are deemed likely to lead to infestation—such as earth-to-wood contact, plumbing leaks, faulty grade clearances, or unsealed exterior joints.
- **Contingency Deadlines:** Under the standard C.A.R. Residential Purchase Agreement (RPA), buyers typically have 17 calendar days (often shortened to 7 to 10 days in competitive Pasadena bidding) to review reports, complete invasive testing, and negotiate repairs.

### What Pasadena Sellers Strategically Reveal vs. Conceal

In high-value Pasadena transactions, sellers frequently minimize pest history through deceptive wording:

- *"Spot treatment completed on attic rafter tails in 2022."* (Conceals that spot treatment was chosen to avoid the expense of whole-house Vikane gas tent fumigation).
- *"Perimeter chemical barrier renewed annually."* (Masks the fact that subterranean termites were found swarming inside the crawlspace access well).
- *"Exterior eaves repainted and touched up prior to listing."* (Hides that hollowed timber corbels were patched with automotive Bondo rather than replaced).

### Fragmented Red Flags Across Disparate Documents

Critical structural red flags are rarely consolidated into one obvious warning. Instead, vital clues are fragmented across separate documents:

- The General Home Inspector writes on page 44: *"Arroyo stone foundation crawlspace exhibits high humidity; minor wood deflection noted at living room floor."*
- The SPCB Branch 3 Pest Report notes under Inaccessible Areas: *"Crawlspace clearance below 12 inches beneath dining room; visual inspection obstructed."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior pest treatments on the SPQ, writing: *"Exterior localized spot treatment completed in 2021; no active problems known."*

Read independently, each notation appears minor. But when analyzed together, they indicate an imminent disaster: high crawlspace moisture combined with inaccessible framing and an exterior spot treatment strongly suggests that subterranean termites have destroyed the uninspected load-bearing floor joists beneath the dining room.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Pasadena

### 1. Are termites common in historic Pasadena homes?
Yes. Pasadena’s climate, rich tree canopy, and architectural history featuring extensive exposed timber framing make wood-destroying organisms exceptionally common. Nearly every historic Craftsman, Spanish Revival, and mid-century home in Pasadena will face both Western Subterranean and Western Drywood Termite activity during its lifespan.

### 2. Can drywood termites be effectively treated with localized spot treatments?
While localized spot treatments (such as chemical injection or heat) can eradicate isolated colonies in accessible wood, they cannot reach termites hidden inside wall framing or inaccessible roof cavities. For comprehensive eradication of drywood termites, whole-house Vikane gas tent fumigation remains the gold standard.

### 3. What are California sellers legally required to disclose regarding termites?
Under California Civil Code § 1102 and the standard C.A.R. Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ), sellers must disclose all known active or past termite infestations, pest damage, prior chemical treatments, and provide copies of all pest inspection reports and warranties in their possession.

### 4. Does California homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs?
No. Standard California homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, insects, or vermin. Insurance carriers treat termite infestation as a preventable property maintenance issue, leaving all structural reconstruction costs to the property owner.
