## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Henderson and Clark County

Henderson's desert climate—characterized by extreme summer heat exceeding 105°F and low ambient humidity—forces termites to seek underground moisture reservoirs created by residential irrigation and plumbing.

### Why Desert Landscaping and Slab Construction Foster Infestations

Several regional construction practices and landscaping elements make Henderson homes vulnerable to wood-destroying pests:
- **Artificial Irrigation Microclimates:** To maintain desert landscaping, Henderson homeowners install automated drip irrigation networks, synthetic turf washdown systems, and decorative shrub beds directly against exterior foundation perimeters. This daily localized watering keeps the underlying soil moist year-round, creating optimal habitats for **Desert Subterranean Termites (*Heterotermes aureus*)** and **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*)**.
- **Post-Tension Concrete Slab Penetrations:** The vast majority of Henderson homes are built on monolithic post-tension concrete slabs. As houses settle or concrete cures, tiny shrinkage cracks form around sewer lines, water supply pipes, and bathtub traps. Foraging subterranean termites easily pass through cracks as narrow as 1/32 of an inch to access internal wood framing.
- **Stucco Weep Screed Obstructions:** Nevada residential building codes require a metal weep screed at the base of exterior stucco walls to allow trapped moisture to escape. When homeowners add decorative landscaping rock, flagstone walkways, or soil above the weep screed level, termites bypass the concrete foundation entirely and tunnel directly behind the stucco into interior wall framing.
- **Concealed Wall Cavities in Stucco-Frame Homes:** Stucco-over-wood-frame construction creates continuous, well-insulated wall cavities. Termites consume the structural bottom plates and interior 2x4 studs behind drywall without leaving any exterior visible traces.

When irrigated soil meets unsealed slab penetrations or covered weep screeds, subterranean termites establish massive colonies beneath luxury residences.

### The Biology and Mechanics of Desert Subterranean Termites in Southern Nevada

Desert Subterranean Termites have adapted specifically to the Mojave Desert environment:
1. **Deep Soil Nesting and Heat Avoidance:** During peak summer heat, worker termites forage deep beneath concrete slabs where temperatures remain cool and moisture from utility lines accumulates.
2. **Narrow Mud Shelter Tubes:** When crossing exposed concrete footings or interior stem walls, termites construct slender, highly durable mud tubes made of digested wood, desert sand particles, and saliva to prevent desiccation.
3. **Internal Timber and Drywall Consumption:** Termites hollow out structural bottom sill plates and eat the paper backing off interior drywall sheets. Often, the first noticeable sign of an infestation is a faint rippling or bubbling in interior wall paint that homeowners mistake for moisture damage.

Because structural destruction occurs inside sealed wall cavities behind drywall, severe load-bearing damage often remains hidden until baseboards buckle or doorframes sag.

## Nevada Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Henderson Buyers Must Know

Residential real estate transfers in Nevada are strictly governed by **Nevada Revised Statutes (NRS) Chapter 113 (NRS § 113.130 – NRS § 113.150)**. Under NRS 113.130, a seller of residential property must deliver a completed Seller's Real Property Disclosure Form (SRPD) to the buyer at least 10 days before the property is conveyed.

### Key Nevada Disclosure Documents

When buying a home in Henderson, prospective buyers must review several statutory and contractual documents:
- **Nevada Real Estate Division (NRED) Seller's Real Property Disclosure Form (Form SRPD):** The state-mandated disclosure statement. Under the section titled *"Systems / Conditions / Structure"*, the seller must explicitly disclose:
  - Any history of pest, insect, or wood-destroying organism infestations (including termites).
  - Any previous chemical treatments, perimeter barrier applications, or bait station installations.
  - Any structural repairs completed to correct insect or moisture damage.
  - Any foundation cracks, soil movement, or settling slab conditions.
- **National Pest Management Association (NPMA-33) Form / Nevada WDI Report:** Required by FHA, VA, and many conventional mortgage lenders in Clark County. A licensed pest control operator with the Nevada Department of Agriculture inspects accessible framing, perimeter weep screeds, and plumbing penetrations.
- **Buyer's Statutory Rescission Rights under NRS 113.150:** If a seller fails to disclose a known material defect, or if an amended SRPD reveals a new defect before closing, the buyer has the statutory right to cancel the purchase contract without penalty and receive a full refund of earnest money.

### What Nevada Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Under NRS 113.130, sellers are legally bound to disclose all known material defects. However, sellers frequently use vague phrasing to downplay past infestations:
- *"Routine preventative pest control service completed on perimeter in 2022"* (omitting that exterminators treated active subterranean mud tubes entering through master bathroom slab penetrations).
- *"Replaced baseboards and patched drywall in laundry room during cosmetic remodel"* (masking that the studs behind the drywall were hollowed out by subterranean termites).
- *"Seller is a corporate trustee/relocation entity with limited direct occupancy"* (while certain corporate sellers claim limited personal knowledge, fraud and intentional concealment remain actionable under Nevada common law).

## The Manual Review Problem: Red Flags in Massive Documentation

In a typical Henderson home purchase, the complete due diligence file routinely spans 150 to 250 pages. This package includes:
- The completed Nevada SRPD Form
- Detailed general home inspection reports with 70+ high-resolution photos
- NPMA-33 pest inspection reports and previous chemical treatment warranties
- Henderson Master Association and Sub-HOA CC&R documents, budgets, and reserve studies
- Clark County Assessor records, parcel maps, and title commitments

### How Critical Warnings Get Overlooked

Crucial indications of termite activity are frequently buried in technical inspection comments:
- An inspector might note: *"Exterior stucco weep screed buried below decorative river rock along east elevation; foundation sill condition cannot be visually verified."* If a buyer ignores this remark, they risk buying a home where termites have bypassed foundation barriers.
- An attached invoice might itemize *"perimeter concrete drilling and termiticide injection along garage slab,"* proving past subterranean termite activity that the seller characterized as routine spraying.

Manually cross-referencing hundreds of dense pages during a short contractual due diligence period creates substantial risk for homebuyers.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Henderson

**1. Are termites common in Henderson, Nevada?**
Yes. Desert Subterranean Termites (*Heterotermes aureus*) and Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) are widespread throughout Henderson and the Las Vegas Valley. Residential irrigation, lush landscaping, and plumbing penetrations through concrete slabs provide moisture and access to structural timber.

**2. Does Nevada law require a termite inspection before buying a house?**
No. Nevada state statute does not mandate a dedicated pest inspection for conventional cash or private mortgage sales. However, FHA and VA mortgage programs require a clean NPMA-33 Wood-Destroying Insect report completed by a licensed Nevada pest control operator.

**3. What does NRS 113.130 require a Nevada seller to disclose regarding termites?**
Under NRS 113.130 and the Nevada SRPD form, sellers are legally obligated to disclose any known active infestations, prior termite treatments, structural repairs made to correct insect damage, and whether an active termite warranty exists on the home.

**4. What is the 10-day cancellation rule under Nevada real estate law?**
Under NRS 113.150, if a seller fails to provide the SRPD within the statutory timeline or delivers an amended disclosure revealing a newly discovered material defect, the buyer has the statutory right to cancel the purchase contract within 10 days of receipt and obtain a full refund of earnest money.
