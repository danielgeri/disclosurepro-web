## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Drywood Termites in Central Florida

Central Florida sits in USDA Hardiness Zone 9b/10a, characterized by long, humid summers, over 53 inches of annual rainfall, and mild winters that allow insect colonies to feed 365 days a year without entering dormancy.

### Why Orlando's Architecture and Environment Create Ideal Infestation Conditions

Several regional and architectural factors make Orlando properties exceptionally vulnerable:
- **Dual Termite Pressure (Subterranean & Drywood):** Unlike northern markets where subterranean termites are the sole threat, Orlando experiences severe pressure from both subterranean colonies in the soil and airborne **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*)** that fly directly into attic louvers, soffits, and fascia boards during spring and summer nuptial swarms.
- **Invasive Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Widely established across Orange County, Formosan subterranean termites are known as "super termites." A single mature colony can contain several million individuals and build aerial "carton nests" inside structural wall cavities using chewed wood, soil, and moisture from roof leaks, bypassing the need for soil contact.
- **Slab-on-Grade Foundation Penetrations:** Modern Orlando homes built on monolithic concrete slabs often have plumbing, electrical conduits, and bath trap rough-ins that penetrate through the slab. As the slab cures and settles, micro-expansion gaps around PVC pipes provide hidden entry pathways directly into interior wall studs.
- **Stucco-to-Grade Framing:** Many Central Florida residences feature stucco exterior cladding that extends down below the soil line. Termites tunnel undetected behind the stucco layer, eating through the underlying plywood sheathing and wood framing without breaking the exterior stucco surface.
- **Historic Heart-Pine and Cypress Framing:** In historic neighborhoods like Thornton Park and College Park, century-old wood-frame bungalows resting on brick or concrete masonry piers create damp crawlspaces that invite subterranean colonies upward into primary floor joists.

When subtropical heat and moisture combine with wood-framed construction, termite colonies can silently hollow out primary structural framing members in a matter of months.

### Biology and Mechanics: How Wood-Destroying Organisms Attack Orlando Homes

Understanding how different termite species inflict damage is essential for evaluating property disclosures:
1. **Eastern & Formosan Subterranean Termites:** Worker termites construct brown, airtight mud shelter tubes along concrete stems, plumbing risers, and crawlspace piers to maintain high humidity while foraging for cellulose. They eat wood along the soft springwood grain, leaving structural members hollowed out from within.
2. **Drywood Termites:** Drywood termites do not require contact with the ground or moisture from the soil. Swarming alates enter attics and eaves, boring directly into structural rafters, ceiling joists, and door frames. They excavate expansive smooth-walled galleries across the wood grain, pushing out distinctive hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) through tiny kick-out holes.

Because both species destroy wood from the inside out, extensive structural degradation frequently remains concealed behind drywall, stucco, and insulation until significant structural deflection occurs.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Orlando Buyers Must Know

Florida real estate law provides strong legal protections for buyers regarding property defects, anchored by landmark state Supreme Court jurisprudence.

### The Landmark *Johnson v. Davis* Doctrine

In the historic case *Johnson v. Davis* (480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)), the Florida Supreme Court established the foundational legal principle for residential real estate disclosures:
> *"Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under a duty to disclose them to the buyer."*

This affirmative duty applies to all residential property sales in Florida, regardless of whether the transaction uses a standard contract or an "AS IS" purchase agreement. A seller cannot hide behind an "AS IS" clause if they had actual knowledge of unobservable latent termite damage or structural decay and failed to disclose it.

### Key Florida Disclosure Documents

When purchasing real estate in Orlando, buyers must review several statutory and contractual documents:
- **Florida Realtors / Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) "AS IS" Residential Contract:** The standard purchase contract in Central Florida. While Section 12 allows buyers an inspection period (typically 10 to 15 days) to cancel for any reason, the seller remains bound by the *Johnson v. Davis* duty to disclose latent defects.
- **Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR):** Promulgated by Florida Realtors. Section 10 specifically covers *"Wood-Destroying Organisms / Termites"*, requiring the seller to disclose:
  - Any current or past infestation by termites, wood-boring beetles, or dry rot fungi.
  - Any past chemical treatments, subterranean bait systems, or whole-structure tent fumigations.
  - Any known structural or cosmetic damage caused by wood-destroying organisms and whether repairs were made.
  - Whether a transferable termite warranty, bond, or subterranean prevention contract is active.
- **Florida WDO Inspection Report (Form FDACS-13645):** Issued under Chapter 482 of the Florida Statutes by a certified pest control operator licensed by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). It details visible evidence of active infestation, prior treatment drill holes, and structural damage in accessible areas.

### What Florida Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Despite clear legal standards, sellers and listing agents frequently use ambiguous phrasing to downplay past termite issues:
- *"Property has an active, transferable termite bond in place with annual renewal"* (omitting that the bond was taken out after exterminators discovered active drywood termites in the attic truss system).
- *"Spot treatment completed in garage wall; no active infestation known"* (minimizing widespread structural framing damage behind drywall).
- *"Repaired exterior fascia and soffit wood rot during recent exterior painting"* (masking that drywood termites had hollowed out structural roof rafter tails).

## The Manual Review Problem: Red Flags in Massive Documentation

In a typical Orlando residential transaction, the complete due diligence documentation frequently spans 175 to 250 pages. This package includes:
- The completed Florida SPDR disclosure form
- Multi-page general home inspection reports with 90+ photos of attics, crawlspaces, and roofs
- Official FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) inspection reports
- Orange County Comptroller property records and City of Orlando Permitting Services histories
- Termite bond contracts, renewable warranty agreements, and chemical application logs

### How Critical Warnings Get Overlooked

Crucial indications of termite damage are frequently buried in inspection disclaimers:
- An inspector or WDO technician might write: *"Attic blown-in fiberglass insulation depth of 12 inches prevents visual inspection of top plates, ceiling joists, and lower truss cords; inaccessible areas not inspected."* If a buyer ignores this note, they assume the entire financial burden of undetected structural decay.
- An attached termite bond certificate might list *"subterranean bait stations installed 2021; spot liquid treatment applied to master bathroom bath trap,"* confirming a prior subterranean breach into the home's structural framing.

Manually cross-referencing hundreds of dense pages within a brief 10-day inspection window creates substantial risk for Central Florida homebuyers.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Orlando

**1. Are termites common in Orlando and Central Florida?**
Yes. Orlando is in one of the highest-risk regions in the United States for wood-destroying organisms. The combination of year-round warmth, high humidity, and heavy rainfall supports both subterranean termites (including destructive Formosan colonies) and West Indian drywood termites.

**2. What is the seller's legal duty under Florida's Johnson v. Davis doctrine?**
Under *Johnson v. Davis*, a residential seller in Florida must disclose all known material facts that affect the value of the property if those facts are not readily observable to the buyer. This legal duty applies even when selling a home under an "AS IS" contract.

**3. Does a Florida WDO inspection report guarantee a home is free of termite damage?**
No. A Florida WDO inspection report (Form FDACS-13645) only covers visible and accessible areas at the specific time of inspection. It does not guarantee the absence of wood-destroying organisms inside concealed wall cavities, beneath slab foundations, or under heavy attic insulation.

**4. What is the difference between a subterranean termite bond and drywood termite protection?**
Most standard Florida termite bonds only cover subterranean termite re-treatment (or limited structural repairs) and completely exclude drywood termites, which require structural tent fumigation. Always read the fine print of any existing termite agreement to confirm the specific species covered and repair guarantees.
