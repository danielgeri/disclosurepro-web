## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Drywood Termites in West Covina, CA

West Covina’s geography in the eastern San Gabriel Valley, combined with its distinct post-war architectural heritage, creates specific risks for prospective homeowners.

### How Inland Valley Heat, Santa Ana Winds, and Mid-Century Framing Harbor Pests

Several regional environmental and structural factors make West Covina properties prime targets for timber pests:

- **Inland Basin Thermal Dynamics:** Located inland from the coastal marine layer, West Covina experiences intense heat and low relative humidity during the summer and fall. This intense heat drives subterranean termites deep into the damp subsoil beneath concrete slab foundations, while creating ideal conditions for drywood termites to colonize heated attics and exposed exterior eaves.
- **The Santa Ana Wind Factor:** In September and October, hot, dry desert winds blow through the Cajon and San Gorgonio passes into the San Gabriel Valley. These wind events trigger massive late-season swarms of Western drywood termites, carrying reproductive alates directly into unscreened attic vents, open patio rafters, and tile roof underlayments across West Covina.
- **Vulnerabilities Across West Covina Neighborhoods:**
  - *South Hills:* Prestigious custom mid-century and ranch-style homes built across rolling terrain in the 1960s and 1970s. Many feature extensive architectural wood overhangs, exposed open-beam ceilings, and raised foundation crawlspaces where low ground clearance and soil-to-wood contact foster subterranean colonies.
  - *Woodside & Shadow Oak:* Classic 1950s and 1960s suburban tract homes built on concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Shifting alluvial clay soils cause hairline settlement cracks through garage slabs and interior floors, allowing subterranean termites direct, hidden access into bottom wall plates.
  - *Cameron & Walnut Creek Corridor:* Properties adjacent to natural washes and seasonal creek drainages where abundant native vegetation and high soil moisture support expansive native subterranean termite populations.

### Pest Biology: Western Subterranean vs. Western Drywood Termites

Unlike many regions that contend with only one dominant pest, West Covina buyers face a coordinated biological assault:

- **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Lives in massive soil-based colonies. They require moisture from the earth and construct earthen mud tubes across concrete foundation stem walls, plumbing chases, and fireplace bricks to reach wood framing. They hollow out structural lumber along the soft annual growth rings.
- **Western Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes minor*):** Requires no contact with the soil and survives on the minimal ambient moisture present in seasoned wood. Colonies live entirely inside structural rafters, ceiling joists, fascia boards, and window frames. They kick out tiny, hexagonal, sand-like fecal pellets (frass) through pinhole "kick-out" exit holes.
- **Distinct Swarming Seasons:** In West Covina, Western subterranean termites swarm on warm, sunny afternoons following winter and spring rains (typically February through April). Western drywood termites swarm in explosive flights during hot, sunny late afternoons in September, October, and November.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 and the TDS

California maintains strict consumer protection laws governing residential real estate transactions, placing affirmative disclosure burdens on sellers.

### The Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and SPQ

Under **California Civil Code § 1102**, sellers of residential real property (1 to 4 units) must deliver a completed Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and the California Association of REALTORS® (C.A.R.) Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):

- **Mandatory Pest and Wood-Destroying Inquiries:** Section II.C of the TDS asks the seller:
  - *"Are you (Seller) aware of any: Interior or exterior pest/termite damage or infestation?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any room additions, structural modifications, or other alterations or repairs made without necessary permits?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any settling, slipping, sliding, or other soil problems?"*
- **The Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ Section V.A):** Further demands whether the seller has knowledge of *"pests, wood-destroying pests or organisms, or pest control inspections or treatments affecting the Property."*
- **The Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) Framework:** In California, termite inspections are officially designated as "Wood Destroying Pests and Organisms Inspection Reports" issued by Branch 3 pest control companies licensed by the California Department of Consumer Affairs. Reports are bifurcated into:
  - *Section 1 Items:* Active infestations or infections (e.g., live termites or wood-decay fungus) requiring immediate eradication and structural repair.
  - *Section 2 Items:* Conditions conducive to infestation (e.g., earth-to-wood contact, faulty plumbing leaks, unsealed stucco weep screeds).
- **Statutory Liability for Concealment:** Under California legal precedent (*Easton v. Strassburger* and *Cal. Civ. Code § 1102.13*), a seller or real estate licensee who willfully or negligently fails to disclose known material defects is subject to compensatory damages, repair costs, and potential punitive damages.

### Deceptive Language in West Covina Disclosures

Sellers often draft vague notations to satisfy the letter of the law while obscuring severity:

- *"Section 1 clearance obtained in 2021 during refinance."* (Hides the fact that only localized spot chemical treatment was performed, leaving extensive hidden colonies in the attic).
- *"Fascia boards repaired and repainted prior to listing."* (Conceals that drywood termites had eaten structural rafter tails).
- *"Minor mud tubes scraped in water heater closet."* (Downplays an ongoing subterranean infestation beneath the slab).

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in West Covina, CA

### 1. Are drywood termites or subterranean termites more common in West Covina?
Both species are exceptionally common in West Covina. Subterranean termites nest in the soil and attack floor sills and bottom plates through concrete slab cracks. Drywood termites enter directly from the air into attic vents, rafters, and eaves during hot autumn Santa Ana wind conditions.

### 2. What is the difference between Section 1 and Section 2 on a California pest report?
In California Branch 3 pest reports, Section 1 items represent active infestations or wood-destroying fungus requiring mandatory immediate eradication and repair. Section 2 items represent conditions conducive to future infestations (such as plumbing leaks, earth-to-wood contact, or lack of ventilation) that should be corrected as preventative maintenance.

### 3. Does a standard home inspection cover termites in West Covina?
No. General home inspectors in California are not licensed by the Structural Pest Control Board to identify or report on wood-destroying organisms. You must order an independent Branch 3 Wood Destroying Pests and Organisms Inspection Report.

### 4. When is whole-structure tenting required versus localized spot treatments?
Localized spot treatments (such as foam or microwave applications) are suitable only for minor, isolated drywood termite colonies with visible boundaries. When drywood termites have penetrated inaccessible structural framing, attic joists, or multiple wall cavities, whole-house structural fumigation (tenting with Vikane gas) is the only proven method to ensure 100% colony elimination.
