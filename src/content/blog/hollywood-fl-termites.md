## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Hollywood and South Florida

Hollywood’s combination of lush tropical landscaping, coastal water bodies, and diverse architectural eras makes termite management an essential component of property due diligence.

### How Hollywood Housing Stock and Subtropical Moisture Accelerate Pest Damage

Several geographic, environmental, and structural conditions heighten termite risks across Hollywood:

- **Subtropical Climate and High Water Table:** Hollywood experiences an average of over 60 inches of annual rainfall, high relative humidity, and a groundwater table that sits within a few feet of the surface in low-lying coastal neighborhoods. This constant ambient dampness prevents timber assemblies from fully drying out, creating the precise moisture gradients required for rapid subterranean termite proliferation.
- **CBS Construction and Hidden Wood Cavities:** While the exterior perimeter walls of most post-WWII Hollywood homes are built with concrete block stucco (CBS), these structures are far from termite-proof. Roof structures consist of wooden pitch trusses or flat rafters, interior drywall is fastened to wood furring strips attached to the masonry, and door frames, baseboards, and kitchen cabinets are composed of soft wood and composite cellulose.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Hollywood Neighborhoods:**
  - *Hollywood Lakes (North & South Lakes):* 1920s Mediterranean Revival homes and 1950s ranch homes featuring complex hip-and-gable rooflines, exposed wood rafter tails, and close proximity to saltwater and freshwater canals where subterranean moisture is ubiquitous.
  - *Hollywood Hills & Emerald Hills:* 1960s–1980s single-family homes with large roof spans, vaulted ceilings, and extensive wooden pool decks, pergolas, and decorative wood fencing that provide direct paths toward structural framing.
  - *Downtown Hollywood Historic District:* 1920s frame-vernacular and stucco bungalows with raised wood crawlspaces, timber subflooring, and crawlspace vents located near damp soil.

### Pest Biology: The Triple Threat in Broward County

Homebuyers in Hollywood must contend with three distinct, destructive wood-destroying insect species:

- **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Often dubbed "super termites," Formosans establish massive underground colonies containing millions of individuals. Unlike native subterranean species, Formosans consume wood at an extraordinary rate. When combined with roof leaks or high attic humidity, they construct spongy internal nests known as "carton" made of digested wood and saliva, allowing them to nest high inside roof trusses without ever returning to the soil.
- **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** Another destructive invasive species widespread in Broward County. Asian subterranean termites swarm earlier in the year (typically February to April) and thrive in South Florida's coastal warmth. They readily infest structural pilings, roof framing, and interior drywall backing.
- **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Non-soil-dwelling insects that colonize structural timber directly. Swarming adults enter through attic vents, fascia joints, or window frames during warm spring and summer evenings. They live entirely inside structural lumber, excavating galleries and expelling sand-like hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) onto windowsills and attic insulation.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: *Johnson v. Davis* and Statutory Mandates

Florida real estate disclosure jurisprudence is anchored by one of the most significant judicial rulings in American property law.

### The Landmark Precedent: *Johnson v. Davis* (1985)

Under the Florida Supreme Court’s landmark ruling in ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**:
- Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them to the buyer.
- This affirmative disclosure duty applies whether or not the buyer directly inquires about the defect.
- Under subsequent case law (*Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.*, 504 So. 2d 1361), an "As-Is" contract clause does **not** relieve the seller of the legal obligation to disclose known latent material defects, including hidden structural termite damage.

### The Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)

In transactions utilizing the standard Florida Realtors / Florida Bar (FR/BAR) contract, sellers typically complete the **Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)** form:

- **Section 8 (Infestations and Wood-Destroying Organisms):** Requires sellers to disclose:
  - Any past or present presence of termites, dry rot, powder-post beetles, or other wood-destroying organisms.
  - Any structural or cosmetic damage caused by wood-destroying organisms.
  - Any chemical treatments, bait station installations, or repairs completed for pest remediation.
  - The existence of any active termite warranty, bait station service bond, or transferable protection contract.
- **Section 5 (Roof and Attic Conditions):** Requires disclosure of roof leaks, pooling water, or attic moisture issues—the primary catalyst for aerial Formosan termite colonies.

### The Official Florida WDO Report: Form FDACS-13645

Under **Chapter 482 of the Florida Statutes**, pest inspections must be performed by certified pest control operators licensed by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)** using the standardized **Form FDACS-13645 (Wood-Destroying Organisms Inspection Report)**:

- **Section 2 & 3 (Findings):** Formally documents evidence of active infestation, dead insects, abandoned mud tubes, frass deposits, and visible structural timber damage.
- **Section 5 (Inaccessible Areas):** Highlights critical zones that could not be visually inspected, such as cathedral ceilings, vaulted roof sections, attics obstructed by air conditioning ductwork or blown-in insulation, and areas behind built-in cabinetry or wall furring strips.

### What Hollywood Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

In high-value South Florida transactions, sellers often craft evasive disclosure answers to minimize property concerns:

- *"Subterranean termite bait stations maintained annually along exterior perimeter."* (Conceals the fact that bait stations were installed in response to an active Formosan swarm emerging from living room baseboards).
- *"Flat roof re-coated and fascia boards repainted in 2023."* (Masks the fact that termite-hollowed rafter tails were filled with Bondo and painted over rather than structurally replaced).
- *"Property sold strictly As-Is with no warranties."* (Sellers frequently believe this absolves them from liability, ignoring their affirmative duty under *Johnson v. Davis*).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Buried Clues in 200-Page Closing Packets

During Florida's standard 10-to-15-day inspection period under the FR/BAR contract, buyers are tasked with evaluating an extensive collection of dense technical documentation:

- FR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase with comprehensive addenda.
- Seller’s Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR) form.
- 50-to-80-page Comprehensive General Home Inspection Report with infrared thermography and photo logs.
- Official FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organisms Inspection Report.
- Broward County building permit histories, flood zone elevation certificates, and title commitments.

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Severe structural pest damage rarely appears as an explicit, unified warning. Instead, crucial clues are dispersed across separate disclosures:

- The General Home Inspector writes on page 42: *"Water staining and elevated moisture meter readings noted on wood ceiling decking adjacent to master bedroom flat roof scupper."*
- The WDO Pest Inspector writes in Section 5 of Form FDACS-13645: *"Low roof clearance and heavy fiberglass insulation in flat roof cavity precluded visual inspection of truss tails and ceiling joists."*
- The Seller answers "No" to known structural damage on the SPDR, noting: *"Routine localized drywood spot treatment performed in 2022."*

In isolation, these seem like routine items for a South Florida home. When evaluated together, however, they indicate that roof drainage moisture allowed an invasive Formosan colony to construct an aerial carton nest in the ceiling cavity, consuming the roof trusses hidden by the insulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Hollywood, FL

### 1. Why are termites so aggressive in Hollywood and South Florida?
Hollywood’s warm, subtropical climate, high humidity, and abundant rainfall create an environment where wood-destroying insects remain active year-round. Furthermore, the presence of invasive Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*) significantly escalates structural risk because they consume wood rapidly and can establish aerial nests without soil contact.

### 2. Can termites damage concrete block (CBS) homes in Hollywood?
Yes. While termites cannot eat concrete block, virtually all CBS homes utilize wood for roof trusses, ceiling joists, fascia boards, soffits, and interior wall furring strips. Subterranean termites enter through expansion joints or hairline foundation cracks, while drywood termites fly directly into attic louvers and eaves.

### 3. What must a Florida home seller disclose regarding termites under *Johnson v. Davis*?
Under *Johnson v. Davis*, sellers have an affirmative legal duty to disclose any known latent defect that materially affects the value of the property and is not readily observable by the buyer. On the SPDR form, sellers must disclose all known past or present termite infestations, damage, chemical treatments, and warranties. Selling a property "As-Is" does not excuse a seller from this duty.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs in Florida?
No. Standard Florida homeowners and hazard insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, wood-destroying organisms, and dry rot. Insurance carriers categorize insect infestations as preventable property maintenance issues, leaving homeowners solely responsible for all structural repair and remediation costs.
