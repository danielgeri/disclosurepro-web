Missing a vague disclosure answer on an **Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Report (765 ILCS 77/35)**—such as downplayed basement seepage, unpermitted two-layer roof overlays, or undisclosed condo capital projects—frequently leaves Illinois homebuyers facing **$7,500 to $48,000+ in unexpected structural and waterproofing repairs**. Under the **Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act (765 ILCS 77/1 et seq.)**, sellers of 1-to-4 unit residential properties, condominiums, and co-ops must answer **24 statutory questions** before you sign a binding purchase contract.

Yet across Chicago neighborhoods, DuPage and Lake County suburbs, and downstate markets, sellers routinely treat the 24-question form as a formality—checking "No" on technicalities or writing softened notes like *"minor dampness during heavy storms."* Knowing how to audit every section of 765 ILCS 77 alongside the **Illinois Radon Awareness Act**, the **5-business-day Attorney Review window**, and **Section 22.1 Condominium Disclosures (765 ILCS 605/22.1)** gives you decisive leverage before contingencies expire.

## Decoding 765 ILCS 77/35: The 24 Statutory Disclosure Questions

Under **765 ILCS 77/35**, sellers must mark **Yes**, **No**, or **N/A** across 24 specific statements covering structural, mechanical, environmental, and title conditions. When an item is marked **Yes**, the seller must provide a written explanation on the form.

### The High-Risk Clusters Inside the 24 Questions
Rather than skimming 24 checkboxes equally, focus your inspection and legal review on the highest-cost clusters:

1. **Occupancy & Prior Knowledge Baseline (Q1):** Confirms whether the seller occupied the property within the past 12 months. Non-resident landlords, estate executors, and house flippers often have limited "actual knowledge"—increasing your need for invasive inspections.
2. **Flooding, Floodplain & Basement/Crawlspace Leakage (Q2–Q4):** Asks whether the seller is aware of flooding or recurring leakage in the crawlspace or basement, location in a floodplain, or flood insurance coverage/claims. In Chicagoland clay soils, basement water intrusion is the #1 source of post-closing litigation.
3. **Roof, Ceilings & Chimneys (Q5):** Covers leaks or material defects in roofs, ceilings, or chimneys. Cross-reference this immediately against our [Illinois Roof Inspection & Disclosure Guide](/blog/illinois-roof-inspection-disclosure-guide/)—because sellers frequently patch interior ceiling drywall after an ice dam leak while leaving rotted roof decking untouched.
4. **Foundation, Walls & Floors (Q6):** Covers settling, bowing basement block walls, or structural slab cracking.
5. **Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC & Sump/Well/Septic (Q7–Q14):** Evaluates material defects in electrical panels (including older 60-amp fuse boxes or Federal Pacific breakers), galvanized or cast-iron plumbing, heating/AC systems, well water potability, and septic systems.
6. **Environmental Hazards & Underground Tanks (Q15–Q19, Q24):** Probes for unsafe concentrations of radon, asbestos, lead paint, toxic substances, underground fuel storage tanks (common in pre-1960 Chicago homes converted from heating oil), and **Question #24**, which specifically asks whether the property was ever used for **methamphetamine manufacturing** under the Methamphetamine Control and Community Protection Act.
7. **Boundaries, Zoning, Violations & Material Defects (Q20–Q23):** Covers boundary/easement disputes, municipal code violations, and any remaining material defects not otherwise listed.

### Critical Legal Rules: "Aware," "Material Defect," and "As-Is" Sales
- **Actual Knowledge Only:** Under **765 ILCS 77/5**, *"aware"* means having **actual notice or actual knowledge without any specific investigation or inquiry**. Sellers are not required to hire a home inspector before completing the form—meaning an honest "No" never guarantees a defect-free house.
- **Reasonably Corrected Loophole:** The statute defines a *"material defect"* as a condition having a substantial adverse effect on property value or occupant health/safety—*unless the seller reasonably believes it has been corrected*. If a seller paid a handyman to caulk a basement crack and truly believed it fixed the leak, they may legally check "No"!
- **Continuing Duty to Update:** Under updated Illinois law (P.A. 102-0765), sellers carry an ongoing obligation to **supplement and update** the disclosure report if they discover an answer was inaccurate or if a new material defect arises before closing.
- **"As-Is" Does Not Exempt Sellers:** Marketing an Illinois home "As-Is" does **not** waive 765 ILCS 77. Sellers must still deliver the completed 24-question report truthfully.

## Illinois Radon Awareness Act & Lead-Based Paint Disclosures

Beyond the 24 questions in 765 ILCS 77, Illinois transactions require two critical environmental disclosures:

### 1. Illinois Radon Awareness Act (420 ILCS 46)
Northern and Central Illinois sit over uranium-rich glacial till deposits that produce high indoor radon levels. Under **420 ILCS 46**, sellers must deliver:
- The **Disclosure of Information on Radon Hazards** form disclosing any known prior radon tests or mitigation systems.
- The Illinois Emergency Management Agency (IEMA) pamphlet, *Radon Testing Guidelines for Real Estate Transactions*.

If your 48-hour continuous radon monitor test registers at or above the **EPA 4.0 pCi/L action level**, buyers routinely demand a **$1,200 to $2,500 seller credit** to install a sealed sub-slab depressurization fan system.

### 2. Federal & Illinois Lead Paint Rules
For any residential building constructed before **1978**, sellers must provide the federal **Lead-Based Paint Disclosure**, disclose any known lead hazards or municipal violation notices, and allow a **10-day inspection window** (unless waived in writing). In older Chicago brick two-flats and bungalows, peeling window-sash friction surfaces and unencapsulated exterior porch trim frequently trigger high abatement quotes.

## The 5-Business-Day Attorney Review Window & Basement Seepage Traps

In Northern Illinois—particularly under the **Multi-Board Residential Real Estate Contract** used throughout Chicagoland—contracts include an automatic **5-business-day Attorney Review and Home Inspection period** beginning the day *after* the Date of Acceptance (excluding weekends and federal holidays).

### How Buyers Leverage the 5-Day Window
During those five business days, your real estate attorney can propose contract modifications, clarify tax prorations, or formally disapprove the contract if deal-breaking issues emerge. Simultaneously, your home inspector and sewer-scope specialist must inspect the home so your attorney can serve a written **Inspection Modification / Repair Credit Demand** before 5:00 PM on Day 5 (or request a brief extension to obtain specialist bids).

### Basement Seepage & Clay Sewer Laterals
Because Illinois homes face extreme freeze-thaw cycles and heavy spring snowmelt, scrutinize every basement clue in the disclosure packet:
- **Euphemisms in Q2–Q4:** Watch for phrases like *"slight dampness in northwest corner during 2023 flood"* or *"installed second sump pump as precaution."* Freshly painted cinderblock walls or brand-new basement drywall over concrete block often hide active hydrostatic seepage or bowed foundation walls.
- **Clay Sewer & Catch Basin Backups:** Pre-1970 Chicago homes use vitrified clay sewer laterals and exterior masonry **catch basins**. Standard home inspectors do **not** camera-scope underground sewer lines; order a $200–$350 video sewer scope during the 5-day window to spot collapsed clay joints or tree root intrusion before signing off on contingencies.

## Chicago Condo Section 22.1 Disclosures (765 ILCS 605/22.1)

If you are purchasing a condominium in Chicago or anywhere in Illinois, **765 ILCS 77/35** only covers the interior of your unit—it tells you almost nothing about the building's roof, masonry tuckpointing, or financial solvency. For that, you must examine the **Section 22.1 Disclosure (765 ILCS 605/22.1)** provided by the condominium association.

### What the 22.1 Statement Must Reveal
Upon written request, the condo board has up to **10 business days** to furnish:
1. The Declaration, Bylaws, and Rules & Regulations.
2. Current reserve replacement fund balance and last financial statement.
3. **Anticipated capital expenditures** approved or expected for the current and next **two fiscal years** (such as masonry parapet rebuilds, elevator modernization, or flat-roof replacement).
4. Pending lawsuits or judgments against the association.
5. Confirmation of insurance coverage and any unpaid assessments or liens on the unit.

**Attorney Review Timing Trap:** Because management companies frequently take 7–10 business days to deliver 22.1 packages—longer than the 5-day Attorney Review period—always ensure your attorney preserves a separate **Condo Document Review Contingency** giving you a clean exit if reserve funds are depleted or a $25,000 special assessment is looming.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Report

### Does an Illinois seller have to complete 765 ILCS 77 if selling "As-Is"?
Yes. Under the Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act, an "As-Is" contract clause only relieves the seller from agreeing to perform repairs—it does not exempt the seller from completing and delivering the 24-question Residential Real Property Disclosure Report truthfully before contract signing.

### What happens if the seller delivers the Illinois disclosure report after we sign the contract?
Under **765 ILCS 77/40**, if the seller fails to deliver the disclosure report prior to signing the purchase agreement, the buyer has the right to terminate the contract at any time prior to closing and receive a full refund of all earnest money. If the report is delivered before closing and discloses a previously unknown material defect, you may have termination rights within the statutory window.

### How long after closing can a buyer sue for fraud on an Illinois disclosure report?
Under **765 ILCS 77/60**, an action for violation of the Act (such as knowingly making false statements or failing to disclose a known material defect) generally carries a **1-year statute of limitations** from the earlier of the date of possession, the date of occupancy, or the date of recording of an instrument of conveyance. Because proving *actual knowledge* in court after closing is expensive, catching discrepancies during your 5-day Attorney Review period is far more effective.

### Are condo 22.1 disclosures required to be delivered within the 5-day attorney review period?
No. Under **765 ILCS 605/22.1**, condominium associations have up to **10 business days** to produce the 22.1 disclosure package. Standard Chicagoland contracts treat condo document review as an independent contingency so buyers retain termination rights if the 22.1 packet arrives after the 5-business-day Attorney Review window and reveals low reserves or pending special assessments.
