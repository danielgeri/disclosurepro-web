## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Eugene and Lane County

Eugene’s unique topography—surrounded by Spencer Butte, the Coburg Hills, and the Willamette River floodplains—creates microclimates with prolonged wood dampness, dense tree canopies, and acidic clay-loam soils that foster robust termite activity.

### Why Eugene Housing Stock and Willamette Valley Climate Elevate Pest Risk

A combination of architectural traditions, local geology, and environmental moisture makes Eugene residences uniquely susceptible to termite infestation:

- **Heavy Annual Precipitation and Crawl Space Moisture:** Eugene's extended rainy season (October through May) saturates soils. The majority of older homes in the Friendly Area, College Hill, and South Hills are built on raised wood foundations with crawl spaces. Insufficient crawl space ventilation, missing polyethylene vapor barriers, and perimeter standing water draw subterranean termites directly into structural timber framing.
- **Douglas Fir Timber Framing and Dense Tree Cover:** Eugene’s lush urban canopy of Douglas firs, bigleaf maples, and cedar trees sheds organic matter that maintains soil moisture around residential perimeters. Aging tree root systems extending beneath homes provide natural subterranean feeding paths straight toward foundation stem walls.
- **Hillside Runoff and Buried Framing in the South Hills:** In the South Hills and College Hill, steep building topography often causes surface groundwater runoff to accumulate against uphill foundation walls. When soil levels rise above foundation concrete, wood framing makes direct earth contact, inviting both subterranean and dampwood termite colonization.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Eugene Neighborhoods:**
  - *Friendly Area & College Hill:* 1920s–1940s craftsman homes featuring unexcavated dirt basements, original concrete form boards left behind in crawl spaces, and direct wood-to-earth contact at porch piers.
  - *Ferry Street Bridge & Cal Young:* 1970s–1980s ranch-style homes built with slab-on-grade foundations where plumbing cutouts and expansion joints allow subterranean termites to enter interior load-bearing partition walls.
  - *South Hills:* Multi-level hillside architecture with extensive cantilevered cedar decks, exterior wood siding in direct contact with mulch, and exposed rim joists.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean vs. Dampwood Termites in Western Oregon

Eugene property buyers must be aware of two primary wood-destroying insect species:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The most economically destructive pest in Western Oregon. Ground-dwelling colonies build intricate mud shelter tubes across foundation stem walls and concrete piers to reach dry floor framing. They feed along the soft grain of structural timber, hollowing out joists while leaving the outer wood shell seemingly intact.
- **Pacific Dampwood Termites (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*):** Significant structural pests that do not require soil contact but thrive in wood with elevated moisture content (typically above 25%). They colonize structural framing compromised by roof leaks, failing window flashing, or poorly ventilated crawl spaces.

## Oregon Real Estate Disclosure Laws: ORS § 105.464 and WDO Standards

Oregon law provides explicit consumer protection mechanisms to ensure homebuyers are notified of known material property defects prior to closing.

### The Oregon Statutory Property Disclosure Mandate

Under **Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS) § 105.464**, residential sellers must deliver a completed statutory **Seller’s Property Disclosure Statement** to prospective buyers:

- **Section 5 (Wood Destroying Organisms / Pest Infestations):** Requires the seller to explicitly answer:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present infestation of termites, wood-boring beetles, or other wood-destroying organisms on the property?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present damage to the property caused by wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Has the property been treated for termites or other wood-destroying organisms? If yes, provide the date and company name."*
  - *"Is there a transferable pest warranty or service contract currently in effect?"*
- **Section 3 & 4 (Roof, Drainage, Moisture, and Foundations):** Mandates disclosure of roof leaks, crawl space dampness, standing water, and basement settling—factors directly linked to pest infestation conditions.

### Buyer Rescission Rights Under ORS § 105.475

Under **ORS § 105.475**, Oregon buyers possess a statutory right to revoke their purchase offer within **five business days** of receiving the completed disclosure statement. If a seller discloses prior termite treatments or unaddressed wood damage late in the transaction, the buyer can terminate the transaction without penalty and recover their earnest money deposit.

### Official Oregon Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Pest Reports

In Oregon, formal pest inspections are carried out by **Oregon Department of Agriculture (ODA)** licensed pesticide applicators and structural pest inspectors. A formal Oregon WDO report evaluates:
- **Active Infestation:** Visual confirmation of live termites, dampwood frass, or active subterranean mud tubes.
- **Prior Damage:** Historical wood destruction, hollowed framing members, or chemical drill patterns in foundations.
- **Conducive Conditions:** Direct earth-to-wood contact, cellulose debris in crawl spaces, unvented crawl spaces, roof flashing failures, and excessive perimeter moisture.

### What Eugene Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers in Lane County frequently downplay past pest activity using ambiguous phrases:
- *"Preventative perimeter barrier treatment completed in 2022."* (Hides the fact that subterranean mud tubes were discovered inside the crawl space).
- *"Floor framing strengthened during bathroom update."* (Downplays the structural sistering of termite-damaged floor joists).
- *"Annual pest inspection service maintained."* (Fails to disclose that bait stations were installed following a winged termite swarm inside the living room).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Crucial Clues in 200-Page Closing Packets

During Oregon's standard 10-to-14-day professional inspection contingency under Oregon Real Estate Forms (OREF) contracts, buyers receive an overwhelming amount of paperwork:
- OREF Residential Sale Agreement, Inspection Addenda, and Counteroffers
- Oregon Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (ORS 105.464)
- 45-to-75-page General Home Inspection Report
- Oregon Licensed WDO Pest & Dry Rot Inspection Report
- Lane County Title Commitments, Property Tax Records, and Environmental Disclosures

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Material structural hazards rarely appear as an explicit, single warning. Instead, critical indicators are scattered across separate disclosures:
- The Home Inspector writes on page 42: *"High moisture reading (30%) and spongy wood detected on crawl space rim joist below southwest deck."*
- The WDO Pest Inspector notes under Inaccessible Zones: *"Fiberglass crawl space batt insulation obscured visual inspection of bottom mudsills."*
- The Seller answers "No" to active infestations on the ORS 105.464 disclosure, but writes under repairs: *"Rebuilt exterior deck ledger and replaced crawl space insulation in 2023."*

In isolation, each note appears benign. But when cross-referenced, the reality becomes apparent: deck ledger water intrusion created high moisture levels in the crawl space, where subterranean termites breached the perimeter mudsills and destroyed floor framing beneath the insulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Eugene

### 1. Are termites a common issue in Eugene, Oregon?
Yes. Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) and Pacific Dampwood Termites (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*) are prevalent throughout Eugene, Lane County, and the Willamette Valley. The combination of heavy winter rainfall, high crawl space humidity, and timber-framed construction creates prime conditions for wood-destroying insect activity.

### 2. What does Oregon law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS) § 105.464, sellers must complete the Seller's Property Disclosure Statement, disclosing all known past or present termite infestations, structural wood damage, and history of pest treatments or active service contracts.

### 3. Does a standard home inspection in Eugene include an official WDO report?
No. Standard home inspections provide a general overview of a property's structural and mechanical condition. A specialized Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection, conducted by an Oregon Department of Agriculture (ODA) licensed structural pest inspector, is required to formally identify termite infestations, dry rot, and conducive conditions.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs in Oregon?
No. Standard Oregon homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, and wood-destroying insects. Insurance companies consider insect damage a preventable maintenance issue, leaving property owners responsible for all structural and cosmetic repair costs.
