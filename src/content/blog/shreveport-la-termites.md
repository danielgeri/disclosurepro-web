## The Hidden Hazard: Formosan and Subterranean Termites in Shreveport

Shreveport’s location in Northwest Louisiana features long, humid summers, abundant rainfall (exceeding 50 inches annually), and rich alluvial soils along the Red River basin. This subtropical environment creates optimal breeding conditions for wood-destroying insects.

### Why Shreveport Housing Stock and Geology Elevate Termite Risk

Several distinct structural and environmental factors contribute to high termite vulnerability across Shreveport:

- **Expansive Red Clay and Alluvial River Soils:** Caddo Parish soils contain heavy clay and river silt that retain significant moisture. When heavy rains alternate with intense summer heat, ground humidity remains high, creating ideal conditions for subterranean foraging.
- **Formosan "Super Termite" Activity:** Shreveport is located within the active zone for Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*), which entered Louisiana through Gulf ports and expanded northward along rail and river routes. Formosan colonies contain millions of insects and consume framing lumber at rapid rates.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities in Historic and Mid-Century Neighborhoods:**
  - *South Highlands Historic District and Pierremont:* Built between the 1920s and 1950s, these historic residences feature massive timber framing, raised pier-and-beam foundations, and low-clearance crawl spaces. Subterranean termites exploit damp crawl spaces and masonry brick piers to reach untreated heart pine floor joists and central carrying beams.
  - *Highland and Fairfield Historic District:* Turn-of-the-century Victorian and Craftsman cottages where wood-to-ground contact, aging porch framing, and damp crawl spaces create direct access for subterranean colonies.
  - *Broadmoor and Spring Lake:* 1950s through 1970s slab-on-grade ranch homes. Settling clay soils cause slab stress fractures, allowing termites to enter through plumbing rough-ins, weep screeds, and expansion joints directly into interior partition walls.

### Pest Biology: Formosan vs. Eastern Subterranean Termites

Homebuyers in Shreveport must contend with two destructive species:

1. **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Builds soil-based colonies and constructs earthen mud shelter tubes across foundation walls to access wooden timbers, hollowing out structural framing along the soft growth rings.
2. **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Aggressive pests that construct secondary aerial nests ("cartons") made of chewed wood, soil, and saliva within wall cavities, allowing them to survive without returning to the ground for moisture. A mature Formosan colony can cause severe structural framing damage within months.

## Louisiana Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Statutory Disclosures and Redhibition

Louisiana real estate law provides specific statutory disclosure mechanisms alongside historic civil code protections.

### The Louisiana Residential Property Disclosure Statement (LRPDS)

Under **Louisiana Revised Statutes (La. R.S.) 9:3196-3200**, sellers of residential property must provide a completed Louisiana Residential Property Disclosure Statement to the buyer:
- **Section 4 (Termites / Wood-Destroying Organisms):** Explicitly asks:
  - *"Has there been any past or present termite or wood-destroying insect infestation or damage?"*
  - *"Has the property been treated for termites or wood-destroying insects?"*
  - *"Is there an active termite contract/warranty currently in effect on the property?"*
  - Requires the seller to identify the pest control company, contract type (retreat vs. retreat-and-repair), and annual renewal cost.
- **Section 1 (Structure):** Inquires about roof leaks, subfloor movement, and foundation settling that often accompany wood-destroying insect infestations.

### Louisiana Redhibition Law (La. C.C. Art. 2520 et seq.)

Under the **Louisiana Civil Code**, buyers have strong legal protections under the law of **redhibition**:
- A redhibitory defect is a hidden defect that renders the property so defective or inconvenient that the buyer would not have purchased it, or would have paid a significantly lower price.
- If the seller knew of the termite defect and failed to disclose it, they are classified as a **bad-faith seller** under La. C.C. Art. 2545, making them liable for rescission of the sale or price reduction, plus damages and attorney fees.
- **Waiver of Redhibition in "As-Is" Sales:** Under Louisiana law (*Shelton v. Standard/700 Associates*), an "as-is" clause with waiver of redhibition is null and void if the buyer can prove the seller had actual knowledge of hidden termite defects and intentionally failed to disclose them.

### Official Louisiana Wood Destroying Insect Report (WDIR)

In Louisiana, pest inspections must be performed by a technician licensed by the **Louisiana Department of Agriculture and Forestry (LDAF)**. The official Louisiana WDIR details:
- Evidence of active or past subterranean/Formosan termites, drywood termites, or powderpost beetles.
- Presence of previous chemical treatment drill holes, bait stations, or visible structural wood hollows.
- Inaccessible crawl spaces, spray-foam insulation, or wall paneling that prevented visual examination.

### What Shreveport Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers frequently use non-committal language on Section 4 of the LRPDS:
- *"Active termite bond in place with local pest company."* (Masks that the bond was issued only as a retreat-only contract because structural damage disqualified the property from a retreat-and-repair warranty).
- *"Previous minor moisture repairs completed under bathroom."* (Conceals that moisture attracted Formosan termites that hollowed out floor joists).
- *"Property sold As-Is with full waiver of redhibition."* (Used by sellers to deter buyers from pursuing claims for hidden defects).

## The Manual Review Problem: Navigating Louisiana Real Estate Documentation

During the standard 10-to-14-day inspection and due diligence period under the Louisiana Real Estate Commission (LREC) purchase contract, Shreveport buyers receive large document sets:
- LREC Promulgated Residential Agreement to Buy or Sell
- Louisiana Residential Property Disclosure Statement (LRPDS) with attached termite warranty contracts
- 40-to-70-page licensed home inspection report with moisture meter readings
- Official LDAF Wood Destroying Insect Report (WDIR)
- Caddo Parish conveyance records, historic district design covenants, and title commitments

### The Risk of Missing Fragmented Evidence

Critical indicators of termite devastation are often split across separate documents:
- The home inspector notes on page 38: *"Crown molding separation and door frame racking in master suite."*
- The pest inspector notes in Section V: *"Spray foam insulation along crawl space rim joists prevents visual inspection of floor framing."*
- The seller writes on the LRPDS: *"Annual termite inspection performed March 2023."*

Without connecting these disparate observations, the buyer will not realize that the crown molding separation is caused by Formosan termites hollowing out the load-bearing wall studs behind the master bedroom drywall.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Shreveport

### 1. Are Formosan termites found in Shreveport, Louisiana?
Yes. Formosan Subterranean Termites are active throughout Northwest Louisiana, alongside native Eastern Subterranean Termites. Formosan colonies are exceptionally large and can cause rapid structural damage.

### 2. What does Louisiana law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under La. R.S. 9:3196-3200, sellers must complete the Louisiana Residential Property Disclosure Statement, specifically disclosing known active infestations, prior damage, past chemical treatments, and the specifics of any active termite warranty contract.

### 3. What is the difference between a retreat-only bond and a retreat-and-repair bond in Louisiana?
A retreat-only bond covers only the cost of applying chemical termiticide if termites return. A retreat-and-repair bond covers both the chemical re-treatment and the structural repair costs of new termite damage up to a specified policy limit (e.g., $250,000 or $1,000,000).

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Louisiana?
No. Standard Louisiana homeowners insurance policies exclude termite and insect damage from coverage, treating wood-destroying insect prevention as an uninsurable homeowner maintenance responsibility.
