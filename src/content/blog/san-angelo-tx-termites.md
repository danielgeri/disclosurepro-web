## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in San Angelo, TX

San Angelo’s unique West Texas geology, river valley hydrology, and residential construction practices create distinct structural vulnerabilities for wood-framed homes.

### How Concho Valley Soils, Slab Plumbing, and Heat Waves Drive Infestations

Several localized environmental and architectural factors make San Angelo homes prime targets for wood-destroying insects:

- **Expansive Caliche-Clay Soils and Slab Cracking:** The Concho Valley is characterized by heavy red clay subsoils underlain by dense caliche limestone hardpan. During prolonged summer droughts, clay soils contract dramatically, pulling away from concrete slabs. When heavy autumn thunderstorms hit, the clay expands rapidly. This cyclic soil movement causes hairline shrinkage cracks and foundation shifts in concrete slabs, creating direct subterranean entryways for foraging termites.
- **Concho River and Lake Nasworthy Hydrology:** Properties situated near the Concho River basin, Lake Nasworthy, or Red Arroyo benefit from shallow alluvial water tables. Subterranean termites require continuous humidity to avoid desiccation; the subterranean moisture preserved in these drainage basins sustains large, resilient colonies that forage outward into surrounding neighborhoods.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across San Angelo Neighborhoods:**
  - *Santa Rita Historic District:* 1910s to 1930s historic bungalows, Spanish Revival, and Tudor homes featuring raised wood-framed pier-and-beam foundations. Aging, uninsulated crawlspaces and untreated old-growth yellow pine mudsills provide immediate cellulose meals for subterranean colonies.
  - *College Hills & Sunset:* 1960s and 1970s single-story brick ranches built on concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Copper and cast-iron plumbing penetrations through the slab for bathtubs, showers, and kitchen sinks provide hidden highways straight from the soil into interior partition walls.
  - *The Bluffs & PaulAnn:* 1990s and 2000s brick veneer homes. Landscaping beds and mulch piled over exterior foundation brick weep holes allow subterranean termites to bypass concrete perimeters and enter directly behind exterior brick veneer walls.

### Pest Biology: Arid-Land and Desert Subterranean Termites

Homeowners in the Concho Valley face pressure from two native subterranean species:

- **Arid-Land Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes tibialis*):** The most common wood-destroying pest in West Texas. They live in large subterranean colonies located three to ten feet underground to escape surface freezing in winter and extreme surface heat in summer. They construct airtight mud shelter tubes composed of soil particles and saliva to traverse foundation concrete. They consume wood along the soft springwood grain, leaving brittle hollowed timbers filled with dirt.
- **Desert Subterranean Termite (*Heterotermes aureus*):** Highly specialized to endure intense heat, this species forages actively during the hottest months of July and August. They build slender, drop-down mud tubes that can descend several feet from ceiling joists into drywall cavities.
- **Swarming Patterns in Tom Green County:** Nuptial swarms in San Angelo occur primarily during two windows: in the spring (March to May) following early rain showers, and in late summer (July to September) during seasonal monsoon storms. Seeing hundreds of discarded insect wings around window sills, garage floors, or bathroom fixtures is definitive proof of an established reproductive colony on the property.

## Texas Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Property Code § 5.008 & Form TXR-1406

In Texas, residential real estate transactions are strictly regulated under state statutory disclosure laws designed to protect buyers from concealed physical property defects.

### The Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice (Property Code § 5.008)

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, sellers of single-family residential properties must deliver a completed Seller's Disclosure Notice (using either Texas REALTORS® Form TXR-1406 or TREC Form OP-H) to the buyer on or before the effective date of the contract:

- **Section 4 (Pests & Structural Conditions):** The disclosure form specifically queries:
  - *"Are you aware of active termites or other wood-destroying insects (WDI)?"*
  - *"Are you aware of termite or WDI damage needing repair?"*
  - *"Are you aware of previous termite damage or previous termite treatment?"*
  - *"Is there a current termite policy or service contract in place? If yes, provide provider name and contract terms."*
- **Section 3 (Foundation & Slab):** Queries whether the seller is aware of any foundation repairs, settling, slab cracking, or structural soil movement. In West Texas, foundation movement and termite intrusion are deeply interconnected.
- **Buyer's Right of Termination:** Under Texas Property Code § 5.008(f), if the seller fails to provide the completed disclosure notice prior to contract execution, the buyer may terminate the contract for any reason within seven days after receiving the notice or up until closing, whichever is earlier, and receive an immediate full refund of earnest money.

### Seller Knowledge vs. The Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA)

While Texas law protects sellers who disclose defects in good faith based on actual knowledge, sellers cannot commit affirmative misrepresentation, fraud, or fraudulent concealment:

- **The Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA):** Under the Texas DTPA (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.46), a seller who knowingly conceals material termite damage or misrepresents prior treatment history to induce a purchase can be held liable for treble (triple) damages, mental anguish, and attorney fees.
- **Deceptive Phrasing in West Texas Disclosures:** Watch for minimizing language on seller notices:
  - *"Preventative perimeter soil treatment completed in 2022; contract expired."* (Frequently masks active termite tunneling discovered in master bath partition walls).
  - *"Repaired cosmetic trim and baseboard in hallway."* (Often conceals structural timber framing that was hollowed out by termites).
  - *"Brick weep hole vents cleaned and sealed."* (Can indicate an attempt to block visible mud tubes climbing into wall cavities).

### The Texas Official WDI Inspection Report

In Texas, pest inspections are conducted by licensed pest control technicians certified by the Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA) Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS). Inspections are documented on the official **Texas Structural Pest Control Service Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report**:
- Identifies active infestations, previous infestations, and previous treatments of termites, carpenter ants, and wood-boring beetles.
- Details conducive structural conditions, such as flowerbeds contacting wood framing, wood-to-ground contact, plumbing leaks, and foundation cracks.
- Outlines inaccessible areas, including sub-slab plumbing traps, insulated attics, and enclosed crawlspaces.

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooked Red Flags in West Texas Escrow Packets

A standard San Angelo real estate closing involves an extensive documentation packet: the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) One to Four Family Residential Contract, title commitments, mineral reservation notices, survey inspection certificates, and 45-page private home inspection reports.

Under standard Texas option periods (typically 5 to 10 days for physical due diligence), buyers must process complex technical data under strict deadlines. A critical warning—such as a single sentence buried on page 34 of a home inspection report stating: *"Evidence of prior pesticide drill holes in garage concrete slab; mud shelter tubes observed inside master bathtub access panel"*—is easily overlooked by a hurried buyer. Travis and Rebecca purchased a 2,200-square-foot mid-century brick ranch on Johnson Street in College Hills for $265,000. On the Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice (TXR-1406), the seller marked "Yes" to previous termite treatment, writing: *"Preventative perimeter chemical barrier applied in 2021 by local exterminator; transferrable warranty."* Under active damage needing repair, the seller marked "No."

During their 7-day option period, the general home inspector commended the home’s newly replaced roof, updated HVAC unit, and freshly tiled bathrooms. The inspector noted minor exterior mortar cracking along the brick veneer, categorizing it as standard West Texas foundation settling. Reassured by the seller’s statement that the 2021 treatment had been "preventative," Travis and Rebecca opted not to order a dedicated TDA-certified WDI inspection.

Seven months after closing, during a wet May storm, Rebecca noticed that the wood paneling in the living room had become soft and spongy near the floorboards, and tapping on the door frame produced a papery, hollow echo. The "warranty" referenced by the seller had lapsed six months before listing and contained zero provisions for structural timber replacement.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in San Angelo, TX

### 1. Are subterranean termites active in San Angelo and the Concho Valley?
Yes. Subterranean termites are active throughout San Angelo and Tom Green County. Despite hot, dry surface weather, termites burrow deep into moist soils along the Concho River basin and enter homes through concrete slab plumbing penetrations and foundation expansion joints.

### 2. How do subterranean termites penetrate concrete slab homes in Texas?
Termites cannot eat concrete, but they can pass through cracks as narrow as 1/32 of an inch. They enter through plumbing rough-ins, expansion joints between the garage and main house, and exterior brick weep holes that sit below ground level.

### 3. What does Texas law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under Texas Property Code § 5.008, sellers must disclose all known active termite infestations, past termite treatments, and unrepaired structural wood damage. Knowingly concealing termite problems can expose sellers to severe liability under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA).

### 4. Is a WDI inspection required when purchasing a home in San Angelo?
While Texas state law does not mandate a WDI report for cash sales, conventional lenders, VA, and FHA mortgage programs almost universally require an official TDA Structural Pest Control Service WDI report before funding. Every buyer should commission an independent WDI inspection during their contract option period.
