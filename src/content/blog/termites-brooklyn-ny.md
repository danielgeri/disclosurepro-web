When purchasing a property in Brooklyn, buyers are handed the New York Property Condition Disclosure Statement alongside a stack of inspection reports. Hidden within those dense pages of legal jargon and handwritten notes can be one of the most destructive and expensive issues a Brooklyn homeowner can face: **termites**.

## The Brooklyn Termite Problem

Brooklyn's beautiful, historic housing stock—especially aging brownstones and century-old townhouses with original wood joists—provides an absolute feast for subterranean termites. Because these pests destroy wood from the inside out, the damage is rarely visible during a standard walkthrough, particularly in finished basements.

The warning signs are usually buried in the paperwork. Sellers are required to disclose known material defects, but the language used is often vague or deliberately minimized:
- *"Evidence of past pest activity in the basement joists, treated by prior owner."*
- *"Minor wood rot near foundation sill plate."*

## Why Manual Review Fails

Homebuyers and even seasoned real estate agents typically spend just 15 to 20 minutes skimming these massive disclosure packets. It is incredibly difficult and risky to manually hunt for subtle mentions of termite damage across pest control invoices, general inspection addendums, and seller questionnaires. 

Missing a brief note about "frass" or "hollow-sounding floor joists" can lead to structural failures requiring tens of thousands of dollars in emergency repairs immediately after closing.

## The Cost of Overlooking the Signs

Termite damage goes beyond just replacing a few boards. It compromises the structural integrity of load-bearing walls and floors. If a seller's disclosure mentions past treatments but fails to provide a structural engineer's clearance, you could be buying a severely compromised home.
