## The Hidden Threat: Dual Termite Infestations Across Inglewood, CA

Inglewood’s coastal basin hydrology, historic raised-foundation framing, and dense urban architectural styles create severe structural liabilities to wood-destroying insects.

### Why Coastal Marine Fog and Raised Foundations Accelerate Infestations

Several localized environmental and architectural dynamics amplify termite risks throughout Inglewood residences:

- **The Pacific Marine Layer and Coastal Humidity:** Inglewood sits approximately four to five miles east of the Pacific Ocean. During late spring and summer (the infamous "May Gray" and "June Gloom"), a dense marine layer blankets the city overnight, keeping exterior stucco, eaves, and shaded perimeter foundations damp well into the afternoon. This continuous coastal humidity prevents wood-destroying insects from desiccating, providing ideal conditions for both ground-dwelling and airborne colonies.
- **Historic Raised Foundations and Damp Crawlspaces:** The vast majority of homes in Morningside Park, Fairview Heights, and Centinela Valley built between 1920 and 1950 sit on raised foundations featuring perimeter concrete footings, cripple walls, and unpoured dirt crawlspaces. Many of these historic crawlspaces lack adequate foundation cross-ventilation or modern vapor barriers. Rising damp from the soil keeps subfloor wood timbers—including perimeter redwood mudsills, floor joists, and tongue-and-groove subflooring—perpetually moist, drawing subterranean termites directly into load-bearing framing.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities in Spanish Revival & Craftsman Homes:**
  - *Morningside Park:* 1920s-1930s Spanish Colonial Revival residences with decorative parapet walls, flat roof sections, and stucco weep screeds buried beneath raised flowerbeds, allowing subterranean termites direct, concealed entry into wall framing.
  - *Fairview Heights & Centinela:* 1920s Craftsman bungalows with exposed exterior rafter tails, wooden eaves, and covered front porches where drywood termites easily establish colonies without soil contact.
  - *Inglewood Knolls:* 1940s-1950s post-war California cottages where aging plumbing pipes in crawlspaces create localized moisture leaks that accelerate subterranean termite feeding along main floor girders.

### Pest Biology: The Dual Threat in the Los Angeles Basin

Homebuyers in Inglewood contend with two distinct wood-destroying species:

- **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** These ground-nesting insects live in colonies containing hundreds of thousands of workers. They construct airtight earthen mud shelter tubes over foundation footings, interior piers, and plumbing lines to reach structural wood without drying out. They feed aggressively along the softer springwood grain, leaving a paper-thin exterior wood shell while completely hollowing out mudsills, floor joists, and wall studs.
- **Western Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes minor*):** Unlike subterranean termites, drywood termites do not require soil moisture. Swarming reproductive pairs land directly on exposed wood, boring into roof trusses, attic framing, and window frames. They live entirely within the wood, excavating broad, smooth galleries across growth rings and ejecting tiny, sand-like hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) through microscopic kick-out holes.
- **Swarming Patterns in Inglewood:** Western Subterranean Termites swarm on warm, sunny spring mornings following rain (February through May). Western Drywood Termites swarm during late summer and early autumn (August through October) on hot, dry afternoons. Observing swarms of winged insects or finding piles of shed translucent wings around windowsills or exterior eaves is conclusive proof of an active infestation.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 & SPCB Reports

Purchasing residential real estate in Los Angeles County requires an exact understanding of California’s disclosure statutes and Structural Pest Control Board regulations.

### The Statutory Framework: Cal. Civ. Code § 1102 et seq.

In California, sellers of residential real property must provide prospective buyers with standardized statutory disclosure documents:

- **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS):** Governed by California Civil Code § 1102.6, the TDS requires sellers to make good-faith disclosures regarding known material defects. Section II.C requires sellers to answer whether they are aware of:
  - *"Any room additions, structural modifications, or other alterations or repairs made without necessary permits?"*
  - *"Any settling, slippage, sliding, or other soil problems?"*
  - *"Flooding, drainage or grading problems?"*
  - *"Major damage to the property or any of the structures from fire, earthquake, floods, or landslides?"*
- **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):** Published by the California Association of Realtors (CAR), the SPQ is standard in Southern California transactions. Section V.A explicitly mandates disclosure of:
  - *"Any pest, animal or insect infestations, or wood-destroying organism damage?"*
  - *"Any past or present chemical treatments or repairs for termites or other pests?"*
- **Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) Inspection Reports (Form WDO):** Regulated under the California Business & Professions Code § 8500 et seq., inspections by licensed Branch 3 pest control companies must categorize findings into:
  - **Section 1 Items:** Active infestations of wood-destroying organisms or active wood rot fungi requiring immediate chemical treatment or structural timber repair. Standard California contracts customarily require the seller to clear Section 1 conditions.
  - **Section 2 Items:** Conditions deemed conducive to pest infestation (such as earth-to-wood contact, faulty perimeter stucco screeds, standing water in crawlspaces, or plumbing leaks). While sellers are not automatically required to pay for Section 2 repairs under standard contracts, unaddressed Section 2 conditions almost inevitably result in active Section 1 infestations within months.

### Common Disclosure Evasions in Inglewood Real Estate

In Inglewood's high-demand market, sellers and listing agents frequently use minimal or ambiguous statements on disclosure forms:

- *"Termite inspection completed in 2021; localized spot treatment applied to garage roof overhang."* (Obscures the fact that widespread subterranean termite damage was present in the main home's crawlspace).
- *"Subfloor framing reinforced during recent bathroom remodel."* (Conceals that subterranean termites had hollowed out structural floor joists beneath the shower pan).
- *"Exterior wood trim freshly painted and recaulked for aesthetic refresh."* (Disguises heavy drywood termite kick-out holes and gallery tunneling across exterior eaves).

- The statutory Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and CAR Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ).
- A 40-to-80-page private home inspection report.
- The certified SPCB Wood Destroying Organism (WDO) report with diagrams.
- City of Inglewood building permits, Certificate of Occupancy records, and Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD) reports.

### The Inherent Limitation of General Home Inspections in California

Many prospective buyers believe that a standard home inspection uncovers all structural and insect issues. However, California law expressly restricts the scope of general home inspectors:

> *"A general home inspection is a non-invasive, visual examination of the physical structure and systems of a building. The inspector is not licensed or required to identify wood-destroying organisms, termites, or fungal decay, nor is the inspector required to dismantle finishes, remove insulation, or enter crawlspaces with inadequate clearance or physical obstructions."*

In Inglewood’s vintage Spanish Revival and Craftsman homes, where low-clearance crawlspaces and enclosed eaves restrict visual access, general home inspectors frequently flag these areas as "inaccessible." If a buyer fails to reconcile the pest inspector's list of inaccessible areas against the seller's SPQ disclosures, massive subterranean and drywood termite damage can remain completely hidden until floors begin to sag or structural framing gives way.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Inglewood, CA

### 1. Is a termite inspection mandatory when buying a home in Inglewood, CA?
California law does not legally mandate a pest inspection for cash sales. However, institutional lenders (including FHA, VA, and conventional loans) almost universally require a clear Section 1 SPCB inspection report prior to funding loan documents. In Inglewood, where both subterranean and drywood termites are prevalent, an independent inspection is an essential buyer safeguard.

### 2. Can drywood termites infest a home in Inglewood without ground contact?
Yes. Unlike subterranean termites, Western Drywood Termites do not need ground contact or soil moisture. Swarming reproductive winged pairs fly directly into attic vents, exposed rafter tails, and window frames during warm late-summer afternoons, establishing colonies directly inside the wood framing.

### 3. What legal remedy exists if an Inglewood seller hid known termite damage?
Under California Civil Code § 1102.13 and established California case law (*Shapiro v. Sutherland*), a seller who knowingly fails to disclose material defects or misrepresents property conditions on the TDS or SPQ can be held liable in Los Angeles County Superior Court for actual repair damages, loss of property value, and potentially punitive damages if intentional fraud is proven.

### 4. What visible warning signs should I watch for during an Inglewood home showing?
Key indicators include:
- Piles of tiny hexagonal fecal pellets (resembling coarse sand or pepper) on windowsills, baseboards, or exterior porches.
- Earthen mud shelter tubes running vertically up concrete foundation footings or plumbing pipes in crawlspaces.
- Spongy or springy hardwood flooring near exterior walls, bathrooms, or front porches.
- Patched circular drill holes spaced 12 inches apart along garage concrete slabs or exterior foundation walls, indicating past pressurized chemical treatments.
