Sunnyvale's unique blend of mid-century architectural design, mild South Bay climate, and alluvial valley soils creates distinct wood-destroying pest hazards.

### Why Sunnyvale Housing Stock and Eichler Architecture Elevate Pest Risk

Several construction practices, soil characteristics, and climatic elements intensify termite hazards in Sunnyvale properties:

- **Eichler Post-and-Beam Vulnerabilities:** Sunnyvale contains one of the largest concentrations of Joseph Eichler mid-century modern homes in Northern California. These properties feature slab-on-grade foundations with hydronic radiant heating pipes embedded in concrete. Warm sub-slab temperatures encourage subterranean termites to nest directly beneath the home, while wood sill plates and exterior groove siding installed in direct soil or concrete contact provide immediate food sources.
- **Exposed Rafter Beams & Drywood Swarms:** The flat and low-sloped roofs characteristic of Sunnyvale mid-century homes lack conventional enclosed attic spaces. Exposed redwood or fir tongue-and-groove ceiling decking, exterior beam overhangs, and fascia boards are directly exposed to late-summer Western Drywood Termite swarms from the Santa Clara Valley.

  - *Heritage District:* Early 1900s Craftsman and Queen Anne homes with raised crawlspaces, wood skirting, and unsealed masonry foundation piers.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean vs. Drywood Termites in Santa Clara County

Sunnyvale homebuyers must understand the two major wood-destroying insect species active in the area:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The most widespread subterranean termite species in Northern California. They live underground and build earthen mud shelter tubes over foundation edges and plumbing lines to reach structural framing, hollowing out the soft interior grain of structural posts, sill plates, and subflooring.
- **Western Drywood Termites (*Incisitermes minor*):** Non-soil-dwelling insects that live entirely within structural timber. They colonize exposed roof beams, eave overhangs, and window frames during warm autumn swarming events, carving extensive chambers across structural roof decking and expelling characteristic six-sided fecal pellets (frass).

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 and SPCB Branch 3 Reports

California enforces rigorous statutory disclosure laws designed to protect homebuyers from undisclosed property defects.

### The Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and SPQ

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers must provide two essential disclosure forms: the statutory **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)** and the California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)**:

- **TDS Section II.C:** Inquires whether the seller is aware of any *"settling, slippage, sliding, or other soil problems"* or *"Flooding, drainage or grading problems,"* both of which frequently correlate with subterranean termite entry points.
- **SPQ Section V (Pests, Infestations, and Soil):** Directly asks the seller to disclose:
  - Known past or present pest infestations, including termites, dry rot, or wood-destroying beetles.
  - Previous pest control treatments, chemical applications, or service contracts.
  - Repairs, alterations, or structural replacements resulting from pest damage.

Under California law, a seller who intentionally fails to disclose known termite swarms, past localized treatments, or pest inspection reports is liable for fraudulent concealment or negligent misrepresentation.

### Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) Branch 3 Reports: Section 1 vs. Section 2

In California, official Wood Destroying Pest and Organisms (WDO) inspection reports are conducted by licensed Branch 3 pest control companies regulated by the **Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB)**:

- **Section 1 Findings (Active Infestations / Direct Damage):** Documents visible active colonies of subterranean or drywood termites, wood-decay fungus, and compromised structural timbers requiring immediate eradication and physical repair.
- **Section 2 Conditions (Conducive Conditions):** Documents conditions that do not show active infestation but are likely to lead to one, such as earth-to-wood contact, buried siding lines, plumbing leaks, or cracked perimeter caulking.

### What Sunnyvale Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

In high-value Silicon Valley transactions, sellers frequently employ vague phrasing on disclosure forms:
- *"Local spot treatment performed on beam overhangs in 2022."* (Conceals that spot treatment was chosen to avoid the expense of whole-home tent fumigation or roof foam removal).
- *"Exterior siding refreshed during painting."* (Masks the fact that termite-hollowed Eichler siding panels were patched with Bondo rather than replaced).
- *"Bait stations maintained along rear patio."* (Downplays that baiting was initiated following an active subterranean swarm into the radiant floor slab).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Crucial Clues in 200-Page Closing Packets

During California's standard 17-day buyer investigation period (often shortened in competitive Silicon Valley multiple-offer environments), buyers must review an extensive volume of technical documentation:
- C.A.R. Residential Purchase Agreement, counteroffers, and contingency addenda.
- Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ) and statutory Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS).
- 50-to-80-page Comprehensive General Home Inspection Report.
- SPCB Branch 3 Structural Pest Control Report with Section 1 and Section 2 repair bids.
- Santa Clara County Natural Hazard Disclosures (NHD), preliminary title reports, and municipal permit histories.

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Critical structural hazards rarely appear as an explicit, single warning. Instead, vital indicators are fragmented across separate disclosures:
- The Home Inspector writes on page 39: *"Planter soil and decorative river rock are piled directly against exterior Eichler grooved siding on the south elevation."*
- The SPCB Pest Inspector records under Inaccessible Areas: *"Tongue-and-groove ceiling insulation and built-up foam roof prevented visual inspection of roof structural decking."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatments on the SPQ, writing: *"Routine perimeter exterior spray completed in 2023."*

Individually, these notes seem minor. But when cross-referenced, the danger becomes clear: buried siding allowed subterranean termites to bypass concrete slab inspection edges, crawl behind the siding, and destroy the structural post-and-beam support columns along the south elevation.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Sunnyvale

### 1. Are termites common in Sunnyvale, California?
Yes. Both Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) and Western Drywood Termites (*Incisitermes minor*) are widespread throughout Sunnyvale and Santa Clara County. Mild South Bay weather, radiant heated slabs, and exposed mid-century architectural timbers create ideal conditions for wood-destroying insect infestations.

### 2. What makes Eichler homes in Sunnyvale particularly vulnerable to termites?
Eichler homes feature slab-on-grade foundations with embedded radiant heating pipes that keep subterranean soils warm year-round. In addition, original post-and-beam construction often places exterior wood siding and structural posts in direct contact with concrete slabs or garden soil, allowing subterranean termites easy access. Exposed roof decking also attracts drywood termite swarms.

### 3. What are California sellers required to disclose regarding termites?
Under California Civil Code § 1102 and the standard Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ), sellers must disclose all known active or past termite infestations, pest damage, previous treatments, and copies of any pest control inspection reports or warranties in their possession.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs in California?
No. Standard California homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, and other wood-destroying organisms. Insurance carriers classify pest damage as a preventable property maintenance issue, leaving property owners responsible for all structural and cosmetic repair costs.
