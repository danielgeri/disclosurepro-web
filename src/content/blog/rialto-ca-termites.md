## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Drywood Termites Across Rialto, CA

Rialto’s foothill topography, extreme thermal dynamics, and diverse residential housing stock combine to create specific vulnerabilities for residential framing.

### How Cajon Pass Winds, Alluvial Soils, and Framing Types Foster Infestations

Several localized environmental and architectural dynamics make Rialto properties prime targets for wood-destroying organisms:

- **Thermal Valley Inversion and Deep Subterranean Tunneling:** With summer temperatures routinely surpassing 105°F, native subterranean termites cannot survive near baked surface soil. They burrow deep beneath concrete slabs to escape surface heat. When residential lawn sprinklers, drip lines, or leaking hose bibbs moisten the soil adjacent to stem walls, termites build vertical mud shelter tubes directly up foundation walls to reach structural wood framing.
- **Cajon Pass Santa Ana Winds and Drywood Swarms:** Positioned directly southwest of the Cajon Pass, Rialto bears the brunt of high-velocity Santa Ana wind events each autumn. These dry, gusting desert winds trigger explosive swarms of Western drywood termites from September through November. Swarmers are blown against rooflines, penetrating unshielded attic gable vents, eave soffits, and exposed rafter tails.
- **Architectural Stock and Construction Vulnerabilities:**
  - *Downtown Rialto & Old Town:* 1920s to 1940s Craftsman bungalows and Spanish cottages built over shallow raised crawlspaces. Low ground clearance, damp uninsulated crawlspaces, and lack of cross-ventilation allow subterranean termites direct access to structural redwood mudsills and subflooring.
  - *Central Rialto & El Rancho Verde:* 1950s and 1960s California ranch homes built on concrete slab foundations. Plumbing block-outs beneath bathtubs and showers, along with perimeter slab shrinkage cracks, allow subterranean termites to bypass perimeter soil barriers and hollow out internal load-bearing wall studs.
  - *North Rialto & Renaissance:* Contemporary 2000s to 2020s two-story stucco homes with complex concrete tile roof systems. Thermal expansion frequently cracks roof tiles, allowing windblown drywood swarmers to penetrate underlayment and colonize attic roof trusses.

### Pest Biology: Western Subterranean vs. Western Drywood Termites

Understanding the biological distinctions between Southern California timber pests is critical when evaluating property disclosures:

- **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The primary timber pest in San Bernardino County. A single mature colony contains hundreds of thousands of individual workers. They construct protective earthen mud tubes over concrete foundations to reach structural lumber, eating along the soft springwood growth rings and leaving a hollow timber shell behind.
- **Arid-land Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes tibialis*):** Extremely prevalent in the dry, rocky alluvial soils of the Inland Empire. Highly adapted to arid environments, they forage deeply beneath foundation footings during dry months and emerge rapidly following spring rain showers.
- **Western Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes minor*):** Independent of soil moisture, drywood termites live entirely within dry structural timber, attic rafters, window jambs, and fascia boards. They produce tiny, grooved, hexagonal fecal pellets that accumulate in distinctive conical piles beneath active infestation sites.
- **Swarming Periods in Rialto:** Subterranean termites swarm on warm, sunny mornings following early spring rains from February through April. Drywood termites swarm in explosive flights during hot, bright, dry afternoons from September through November, particularly during Santa Ana wind conditions.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 and SPCB Standards

California law provides extensive statutory protections for residential property purchasers, placing clear legal burdens on sellers to disclose known property defects.

### California Civil Code § 1102 and the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of one-to-four unit residential properties must provide a completed Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):

- **Section II.C of the TDS Form:** Explicitly mandates that the seller state whether they are aware of any *"Substances, materials, or products which may be an environmental hazard... or Wood Destroying Pests/Organisms."*
- **The Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):** Section V.A of the standard California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) SPQ form directly inquires: *"Are you (Seller) aware of: (1) Any past or present infestation, damage, or treatment of termites, dry rot, or other wood-destroying organisms? (2) Any past or present structural repairs or alterations made to address pest damage?"*
- **Duty of Truthfulness and Good Faith:** Disclosures must be made in good faith. A seller who marks "No" despite having observed termite droppings, obtained exterminator bids, or completed cosmetic cover-ups is liable for intentional misrepresentation or fraudulent concealment.
- **Buyer Cancellation Rights:** Under California Civil Code § 1102.3, if the TDS or any material amendment is delivered after an offer is executed, the buyer has a statutory right to cancel the contract within three days of personal delivery (or five days if delivered by mail or electronic transmission).

### The California Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) WDO Inspection Report

In California, structural pest inspections are performed by licensed Branch 3 Structural Pest Control operators and documented on a standardized Structural Pest Control Inspection Report:

- **Section 1 Items:** Conditions with active infestations or infections (active subterranean termites, drywood termites, or active wood decay fungus) or structural damage resulting from them. In standard purchase negotiations, buyers frequently request sellers to complete and pay for all Section 1 clearances before closing.
- **Section 2 Items:** Conditions deemed conducive to infestation or infection, such as earth-to-wood contact, plumbing leaks, faulty caulking, or lack of subfloor ventilation, where no active damage is yet evident.
- **Notice of Work Completed (Form 43M-44):** If the seller performed pest remediation prior to listing, California law requires them to furnish copies of all previous inspection reports, structural certifications, and official Notices of Work Completed filed with the SPCB within the preceding two years.

### Deceptive Disclosure Phrasing in Inland Empire Transactions

Savvy buyers in Rialto should be alert to ambiguous seller statements:

- *"Section 1 clearance obtained prior to marketing."* (Often masks the fact that the exterminator performed cheap localized chemical injection while leaving structurally hollowed timbers in place).
- *"Fascia boards repaired and repainted prior to listing."* (Frequently disguises structural rafter decay caused by drywood termites without addressing internal attic framing).
- *"Routine localized spot treatment applied in garage wall."* (Fails to disclose whether subterranean termites had migrated into adjacent living room floor framing).

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Rialto, CA

### 1. How do Santa Ana winds affect termite activity in Rialto?
Rialto is situated near the Cajon Pass, making it particularly vulnerable to severe autumn Santa Ana windstorms. These hot, dry winds create the exact climatic conditions that trigger massive Western drywood termite swarms. Swarmers are carried by the wind and penetrate attic gable vents, unshielded soffits, and roof eaves without needing ground contact.

### 2. What is the difference between Section 1 and Section 2 on a California pest report?
In California, a Branch 3 Wood Destroying Pest and Organisms Inspection Report classifies findings into two categories. Section 1 covers conditions with active infestations or infections (live termites, beetles, or active wood decay fungus) and resulting damage that requires immediate treatment or repair. Section 2 covers conditions conducive to infestation (such as plumbing leaks, earth-to-wood contact, or faulty subfloor ventilation) that should be corrected to prevent future pest invasion.

### 3. Can a seller in Rialto legally sell a home "AS IS" without fixing termite damage?
Yes, a home can be sold "AS IS" in California. However, an "AS IS" clause only means the seller is not contractually obligated to pay for repairs; it does not exempt the seller from California Civil Code § 1102 disclosure requirements. The seller is still legally required to disclose all known termite infestations, previous treatments, and structural damage on the TDS and SPQ forms.

### 4. Is whole-house fumigation always necessary for drywood termites in Rialto?
While localized spot treatments (such as chemical injection or heat treatments) can eradicate small, accessible colonies, whole-house fumigation (tenting with sulfuryl fluoride) remains the only proven method to guarantee complete eradication of drywood termites nesting throughout inaccessible roof trusses, wall cavities, and attic framing.
