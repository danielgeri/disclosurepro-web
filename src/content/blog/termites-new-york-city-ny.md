## How Do You Know If Your New York House Has Termite Damage?

Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*) are active across all five New York City boroughs and every Hudson Valley and Long Island suburb. Contrary to popular myth, New York winters do **not** kill subterranean colonies—they simply tunnel below the 42-inch frost line and stay active year-round beside heated basement boilers, radiant steam pipes, and shared party-wall masonry.

Because subterranean termites eat soft springwood from the inside out while preserving a paper-thin painted exterior veneer, structural beams can look intact during a 20-minute open house. Inspect basements, crawlspaces, and inspection photos for these **six definitive warning signs**:

1. **Pencil-Width Earthen Mud Tubes:** Dried dirt tunnels running vertically across rubblestone, fieldstone, brick, or cinder-block foundation walls—especially near boiler rooms, steam risers, sewer stacks, and rear-yard extensions.
2. **Swarmer Wings on Basement Window Sills (March–May):** Hundreds of discarded, equal-length translucent wings clustered along cellar windows, hatchways, or French doors after warm spring rains signal an established colony beneath or inside the house.
3. **Hollow-Sounding Sill Plates, Baseboards & Joists:** Tapping basement rim joists, stair stringers, or ground-floor trim produces a dull, papery hollow sound; pressing a flathead screwdriver gently into darkened timber reveals honeycomb galleries packed with gritty dried soil.
4. **Sagging Floors & Sticking Doors Over Uninspected Cellars:** Noticeable floor deflection in a Brooklyn brownstone parlor, a Queens Tudor living room, or a Rockland County split-level family room often traces back to hollowed carrying joists resting in damp foundation masonry pockets.
5. **Freshly Drywalled Basement Ceilings or New "Sister" Joists:** Newly installed sheetrock over basement joists—or shiny modern 2x8 lumber bolted beside century-old darkened beams without a municipal permit—is a classic sign of partial cosmetic concealment after prior termite feeding.
6. **Blistered Paint, Pinholes & Bubbling Plaster:** Pinholes or bubbling drywall along lower-level exterior walls frequently indicate termites chewing immediately beneath the paper backing of sheetrock.

### High-Risk New York Housing Types
- **Brooklyn, Manhattan & Bronx Brownstones / Rowhouses (1870–1930):** Timber floor joists mortised directly into porous brick or brownstone party walls wick capillary groundwater, creating concealed feeding corridors across neighboring lots.
- **Queens & Staten Island 1-to-4 Family Homes (1920–1960):** Tudors, colonials, and semi-attached homes with finished ground-floor apartments where drywall conceals foundation mudsills and rear porch ledgers.
- **Suburban Downstate NY (New City / Rockland, Westchester & Long Island):** Mid-century split-levels, Cape Cods, and colonials built on multi-level concrete slabs where termites bypass visual detection through interior slab expansion cold joints and wooded yard root systems.

## New York Termite Disclosure Law: Mandatory 56-Question PCDS (No More $500 Opt-Out)

For decades, New York real estate operated under practical *caveat emptor* because sellers routinely checked nothing and paid buyers a **$500 credit at closing** to skip the Property Condition Disclosure Statement. **That loophole is gone.**

Under the amended **New York Property Condition Disclosure Act (N.Y. Real Prop. Law §§ 460–467)** effective March 20, 2024:
- **Mandatory Delivery Before Binding Contract:** Sellers of 1-to-4 family residential properties must deliver a completed **56-question PCDS** to the buyer *before* the buyer signs a binding purchase contract.
- **Explicit Pest, Rot & Flood Disclosures:** Sellers must answer **Yes**, **No**, **Unknown**, or **Not Applicable** regarding known termite or wood-destroying insect infestations, prior pest treatments, structural rot, foundation water seepage, and expanded flood-history questions.
- **Critical NY Exemptions to Watch:** Condominiums, co-ops, newly constructed homes never previously occupied, foreclosures, and certain fiduciary/estate transfers remain **exempt** from the PCDS—meaning buyers of co-ops, condos, or estate-sale brownstones receive zero statutory pest questionnaire and must rely entirely on independent inspection and board-minute review.

### Companion Documents You Must Cross-Check
1. **NPMA-33 Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) Report:** Conducted by a **New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (NYDEC)** certified pesticide technician. Required by FHA, VA, and most conventional lenders, this form lists active termites, evidence of prior infestation, concrete drill holes from past treatments, and **inaccessible areas**.
2. **Prior Treatment Diagrams & Repair Invoices:** If a seller marks *"Yes"* to past treatment, never accept a verbal reassurance—review our guide on [buying a house with a history of termites](/blog/buying-house-with-termite-history-disclosure-guide/) to verify whether structural carpentry was ever completed alongside chemical extermination.
3. **NYC DOB / Local Building Department Permit History:** Confirm whether sistered joists, structural shoring, or basement finishing were signed off by a licensed engineer or done unpermitted.

Unlike states where buyers inspect *after* signing a contract, New York metro custom requires buyers to complete home and WDI inspections **during the frantic 3-to-7-day window between accepted offer and formal contract execution**. Once your attorney delivers the signed contract and 10% down payment to escrow, renegotiating for hidden defects becomes extraordinarily difficult.

Dangerous red flags almost never sit on page 1—they hide in **cross-PDF contradictions** similar to the Western New York patterns documented in our [Buffalo, NY termite disclosure guide](/blog/buffalo-ny-termites/):

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termites in NYC & New York Homes

### How do I know if my house has termite damage in New York?
Look for **pencil-thin earthen mud tubes** along basement stone, brick, or concrete walls; **piles of discarded translucent wings** near cellar windows or baseboards in spring (March–May); **hollow-sounding wooden joists, sill plates, or trim** when tapped; sagging ground-floor hallways; and unexplained bubbling paint or pinholes in lower-level drywall. Because subterranean termites feed inside timber behind finished basement walls, schedule an NYDEC-certified NPMA-33 WDI inspection before signing your purchase contract.

### Can a New York home seller still pay a $500 credit instead of filling out the PCDS?
No. Effective March 20, 2024, New York amended Real Property Law Article 14 to eliminate the $500 opt-out credit completely. Sellers of 1-to-4 family homes in NYC, Rockland County, Westchester, Long Island, and statewide must deliver the completed 56-question PCDS before contract signing (narrow statutory exemptions remain for condos, co-ops, new construction, and certain estate transfers).

### Do NYC high-rise condos, co-ops, and brownstones get termites?
Yes—especially **brownstones, townhouses, and low-rise masonry buildings** where wooden floor joists bear inside damp brick or stone foundation pockets. Ground-floor and parlor-level condo or co-op units can also suffer subterranean infiltration through basement masonry cracks or rear garden decks. Always review building maintenance logs and board meeting minutes for recurring pest-control invoices.

### Can I sue a New York seller if I discover termite damage after closing?
Proving post-closing nondisclosure in New York is costly and difficult. Because the PCDS is an **actual-knowledge** disclosure, you must prove the seller knowingly answered falsely or actively concealed structural damage (for example, hiding contractor invoices or covering active mud tubes with fresh drywall). If the seller wrote *"past activity treated"* or *"Unknown,"* courts frequently rule the buyer was on inquiry notice—making pre-contract AI disclosure review and an NPMA-33 inspection vastly cheaper than litigation.
