When purchasing a property in New York City, buyers are often handed a massive stack of paperwork, including the New York Property Condition Disclosure Statement. Hidden within those 100+ pages of dense legal jargon and handwritten inspector notes can be one of the most destructive and expensive issues a homeowner can face: **termites**.

## The NYC Termite Problem

New York City's diverse housing stock—from historic Brooklyn brownstones to aging Queens colonials—provides the perfect environment for subterranean termites. Because these pests destroy wood from the inside out, the damage is rarely visible during a standard walkthrough.

Instead, the warning signs are usually buried in the paperwork. Sellers are legally required to disclose known material defects, but the language used is often vague or deliberately minimized:
- *"Evidence of past pest activity in the basement joists, treated by prior owner."*
- *"Minor wood rot near foundation sill plate."*

## Why Manual Review Fails

Homebuyers and even seasoned real estate agents typically spend just 15 to 20 minutes skimming these massive disclosure packets. It is incredibly difficult and risky to manually hunt for subtle mentions of termite damage across pest control invoices, general inspection addendums, and seller questionnaires. 

Missing a brief note about "frass" or "hollow-sounding beams" can lead to structural failures requiring tens of thousands of dollars in emergency repairs immediately after you receive the keys.

## The Cost of Overlooking the Signs

Termite damage goes beyond just replacing a few boards. It compromises the structural integrity of load-bearing walls and floors. If a seller's disclosure mentions past treatments but fails to provide a structural engineer's clearance, you could be buying a compromised home.
