## Yes, Termites Thrive in Utah—Especially Along the Wasatch Front

Many relocating buyers—and even longtime Utah residents—assume freezing snowstorms and low humidity eliminate structural pest risks. In reality, subterranean termites live **below the frost line inside soil microclimates** sustained by automated sprinkler systems, foundation grading runoff, and heated concrete basements.

### Primary Termite Species Threatening Utah Homes

1. **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Active throughout northern and central Utah—especially across irrigated valleys in **Salt Lake County, Utah County, Davis County, and Weber County**. Colonies tunnel through hairline foundation shrinkage cracks, basement window wells, and unsealed utility penetrations to build earthen mud shelter tubes straight into wooden sill plates and floor joists.
2. **Arid-Land Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes tibialis*):** Uniquely adapted to Utah's alkaline, sandy, and rocky desert soils. Unlike species that require swamp-like moisture, *R. tibialis* survives in far drier soil profiles across the **Sandy foothills, Draper, Tooele, and Southern Utah (St. George / Washington County)**, foraging upward wherever landscape irrigation, drip lines, or leaky hose bibs dampen perimeter footings.
3. **Desert Subterranean Termites (*Heterotermes aureus*):** Concentrated primarily in warm **Washington County and St. George** subdivisions, where thin, drop-like mud tubes hang from garage ceilings or crawl through stucco expansion joints year-round.

## Utah Real Estate Disclosure Law: UAR SPCD & *Mitchell v. Christensen*

Utah does not rely on a single state-mandated statutory checklist form for all sellers; instead, most residential transactions use the standardized **Utah Association of Realtors (UAR) Seller's Property Condition Disclosure (SPCD)** required under the **Utah Real Estate Purchase Contract (REPC)**. Crucially, Utah's legal disclosure baseline is enforced by Utah Supreme Court common law.

### The *Mitchell v. Christensen* (2001 UT 80) Latent Defect Doctrine
In **_Mitchell v. Christensen_ (2001 UT 80)**, the Utah Supreme Court held that a residential seller has an **affirmative legal duty to disclose known material defects that cannot be discovered by a buyer through a reasonable inspection**—even when the property is sold "As-Is." Concealed subterranean termite damage behind finished basement drywall, hollowed load-bearing floor joists, or prior active swarms known to the seller qualify as latent material defects.

### What to Audit on the Utah UAR Seller's Property Condition Disclosure (SPCD)
Under the standard Utah REPC, the seller must deliver the SPCD by the **Seller Disclosure Deadline** (typically 3–7 days after contract acceptance). Buyers should scrutinize three overlapping sections:
- **Section 12 (Pests / Wood-Destroying Organisms):** Asks whether the seller is aware of any past or present infestation or damage from termites, carpenter ants, wood-boring insects, or rodents—plus whether the home has ever been treated, when treatment occurred, and whether any warranty remains in effect.
- **Section 4 & 5 (Roof, Exterior, Foundation & Moisture):** Requires disclosing basement water intrusion, sump pumps, foundation cracking, and exterior stucco or drainage defects—the exact conducive conditions that sustain Wasatch Front subterranean colonies.
- **Section 14 (Past Inspections & Reports):** Buyers should verify whether the seller holds prior pest control invoices, treatment diagrams, or earlier inspection reports from fallen-through escrows.

### Official Utah UDAF NPMA-33 WDI Inspections
General home inspectors in Utah are **not** required to hold a structural pest applicator license, and standard home inspection contracts disclaim formal termite certifications. Always hire a pest inspector licensed in structural pest control by the **Utah Department of Agriculture and Food (UDAF)** to issue an official **NPMA-33 Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) Report** during your Due Diligence window. ## How Cross-PDF Contradictions Slip Past Utah Buyers Before Due Diligence Ends

Under the Utah REPC, your **Due Diligence Deadline** (commonly 10 to 14 calendar days) is an unforgiving cutoff: once 5:00 PM passes without a written cancellation or mutually signed **Resolution of Due Diligence Addendum**, your earnest money is typically at risk. During those two weeks, buyers receive 120 to 200+ pages across the UAR SPCD, an 80-page general home inspection, a radon test, a sewer scope, HOA documents, and an NPMA-33 WDI report.

The most expensive termite traps rarely sit in bold print—they emerge when separate PDFs contradict one another:

- **UAR SPCD checks "No" to active termites and "Yes" to past treatment ("spot treated garage framing in 2021").**
  **NPMA-33 Section IV (Obstructed Areas) reveals:** *"Finished basement drywall and spray-foamed rim joists along south and west walls inaccessible for visual inspection."*
- **General Home Inspection Report (Page 46) notes:** *"Elevated moisture readings (24%) along basement window well framing; sprinklers spraying directly against stucco below grade; minor soft wood noted at exterior door jamb."*
- **Seller's 2021 Pest Invoice** shows a $650 spot-foam application along one garage wall rather than a full perimeter liquid barrier or bait system—and **zero structural contractor inspection** of the adjacent load-bearing sill beam.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termites & Disclosures in Utah

### Are termites actually common in Utah homes?
Yes. Both **Western Subterranean Termites** (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) and **Arid-Land Subterranean Termites** (*Reticulitermes tibialis*) are widespread along the Wasatch Front—including Salt Lake City, Sandy, West Valley City, Provo, and Ogden—as well as **St. George** in Southern Utah. Irrigated lawns and heated basements create ideal soil moisture conditions year-round.

### Must a Utah home seller disclose past or active termite damage?
Yes. Under the Utah Supreme Court's **_Mitchell v. Christensen_** latent-defect rule and the **UAR Seller's Property Condition Disclosure (SPCD)**, sellers must truthfully disclose known past or active termite infestations, chemical treatments, and structural wood damage that a buyer would not discover through a reasonable visual inspection—even in an "As-Is" sale.

### Who pays for the termite inspection and treatment when buying a house in Utah?
Under the standard Utah REPC, the **buyer** pays the $85–$175 NPMA-33 WDI inspection fee as part of Due Diligence. If active termites or wood damage are uncovered, **eradication treatment and structural repair credits are fully negotiable** prior to the Due Diligence Deadline—and sellers commonly pay for active treatment when required for VA or FHA loan clearance.

### How can I tell if a Utah home has subterranean termites during a showing?
Look for pencil-width dirt **mud shelter tubes** climbing concrete foundation walls or basement window wells, blistering paint or hollow-sounding baseboards near exterior walls, piles of discarded translucent wings in spring (March–May), and stucco or wood siding touching irrigated mulch beds. Because finished Utah basements conceal framing, only a licensed NPMA-33 WDI inspection provides reliable verification.
