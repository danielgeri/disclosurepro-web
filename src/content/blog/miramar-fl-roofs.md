Under Florida real estate law—anchored by the landmark Florida Supreme Court ruling in ***Johnson v. But in a fast-moving South Florida market where cosmetic patching frequently disguises rotted underlayment and failed hurricane straps, identifying hidden moisture penetration and evasive disclosure statements requires thorough technical scrutiny and legal diligence.

## Why Miramar Roofs Fail: HVHZ Wind Uplift, Solar Desiccation, and Failed Underlayment

Miramar’s subtropical maritime environment subjects residential roofing assemblies to severe physical and mechanical stresses.

### High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) Aerodynamic Pressures
Miramar is situated within Florida’s designated High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, requiring the strictest wind-resistance building standards in the United States:
- **Severe Negative Pressure Uplift:** When hurricanes strike South Florida, high-velocity winds accelerate over roof ridges, eaves, and corners. This creates intense aerodynamic suction that easily lifts unfastened barrel tiles, peels back architectural shingles, and shears fastener heads.
- **Tile Lug and Mortar Failure:** On the Spanish S-tile and barrel tile roofs common across SilverLakes and Riviera Isles, years of wind vibration fracture the ceramic attachment lugs and crumble old mortar bedding along hip and ridge caps. During major storms, loose tiles become airborne projectiles, breaking adjacent tiles and puncturing underlying waterproofing layers.
- **Fastener Corrosion from Humid Marine Air:** South Florida’s high humidity and subtropical air corrode non-stainless steel mechanical fasteners and roof hurricane clips, reducing their load capacity right when hurricane-force gusts strike.

### Extreme Solar Ultraviolet (UV) Exposure and Underlayment Disintegration
Miramar enjoys more than 240 days of intense subtropical sunshine each year, but this solar radiation takes a severe toll:
- **Bitumen Desiccation Under Tiles:** While concrete and clay tiles can withstand decades of weather, the organic asphalt felt or modified bitumen underlayment installed beneath them has a much shorter functional lifespan. Attic temperatures in Miramar regularly exceed 150°F, cooking the underlayment from above and below.
- **Underlayment Powdering:** Over 15 to 20 years, volatile petroleum plasticizers evaporate from the underlayment, leaving it brittle and full of hairline cracks. When wind-driven tropical rains push water beneath the interlocking tiles, the degraded underlayment fails completely, allowing rainwater to pour directly onto structural roof sheathing.

### Flat Roof Seam Failure and Ponding on Patios
Many Miramar residences feature low-slope or dead-flat roof sections over covered lanais, patios, and master suite balconies:
- **Water Ponding:** Intense tropical cloudbursts deliver water faster than standard gravity drains can shed it. Standing water ponds for days, accelerating the breakdown of adhesive seams in modified bitumen membranes and creating persistent interior leaks.

### The Florida Homeowner's Insurance Crisis
In today’s Florida market, roof age and condition dictate insurance availability:
- Insurance carriers operating in Broward County routinely require a **4-Point Inspection** and a **Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form (OIR-B1-1802)**.
- Most private carriers will **refuse to issue a policy** or will issue a notice of cancellation on any home with an asphalt shingle roof older than 10 to 12 years or a tile roof older than 15 to 20 years, regardless of whether the roof is actively leaking.
- A buyer who purchases a home with an aging roof may find themselves completely unable to secure property insurance or forced onto Florida’s state-backed Citizens Property Insurance Corporation with high deductibles and restricted coverage.

## Understanding Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws

Florida maintains stringent legal standards regarding seller disclosures, establishing landmark consumer protections that hold sellers liable for concealing roof defects.

### The Landmark Precedent of *Johnson v. Davis* (1985)
In the historic decision ***Johnson v. Davis* (480 So. 2d 625)**, the Florida Supreme Court established the modern legal standard for property disclosure:
- The court ruled: *"Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under a duty to disclose them to the buyer."*
- This duty applies to all residential property sales in Florida, including transactions using the standard "AS IS" contract.
- Sellers **cannot** avoid liability for known roof leaks or structural rot by inserting an "as-is" clause into the purchase agreement.

### The Florida Realtors/Florida Bar (FR/BAR) Seller's Property Disclosure
In virtually all Broward County transactions, sellers complete the standard **Florida Realtors/Florida Bar Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)**:
- **Section 4 (Roof):** The seller must state the **Roof Type**, **Approximate Age**, and date of last replacement.
- **Question 4(c):** Explicitly asks: *"Has the roof leaked during your ownership?"* If yes, the seller must specify whether the leak was corrected.
- **Question 4(d):** Demands disclosure of any structural repairs, restoration work, or insurance claims filed for wind, hail, or storm damage.
- **Building Permits:** The seller must affirmatively declare whether all reroofing work was performed with mandatory building permits from the local building municipality.

### Florida Building Code (HVHZ) and Product Approval Standards
The **City of Miramar Building Division** strictly enforces the Florida Building Code, incorporating High-Velocity Hurricane Zone requirements:
- **Mandatory Reroofing Permits:** A municipal building permit is legally required for any roof replacement or structural repair.
- **Product Approval (NOA):** All roofing components installed in Miramar—including shingles, tiles, underlayments, and fasteners—must possess a valid **Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA)** or Florida Product Approval certifying that the product has passed rigorous missile-impact and high-wind laboratory testing.
- **Secondary Water Barrier Mandate:** Florida Building Code requires the installation of a sealed roof deck or self-adhering secondary water barrier (such as peel-and-stick underlayment) across the entire deck surface to prevent water intrusion even if exterior tiles or shingles are blown off.

During this short window, the buyer must analyze an overwhelming volume of documentation:

### Vague Phrasing Sellers Use to Disguise Roof Deficiencies
In South Florida, sellers frequently employ strategic euphemisms to avoid disclosing the imminent failure of aging roofs:
- *"Spanish tile roof inspected annually; routine maintenance performed and loose tiles secured."* (Masks the reality that the 22-year-old underlayment beneath the tiles has completely decomposed).
- *"Minor ceiling discoloration in master closet from prior tropical storm; repainted and dry."* (Disguises an active flashing failure that leaks whenever sustained winds blow from the east).
- *"Flat roof section over lanai serviced with commercial silicone coating in 2024."* (Conceals rotten plywood sheathing and chronic water ponding).
- *"Roof original and functional; buyer to rely on their own inspector."* (Avoids revealing that multiple insurance carriers have already refused to renew coverage due to roof age).

Buyers under time pressure often fail to connect these subtle disclosures to Florida’s strict insurance underwriting requirements, closing on properties that become uninsurable within weeks.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Miramar, FL

### 1. Does the City of Miramar require a building permit for residential roof replacement?
Yes. The City of Miramar Building Division requires a building permit for all residential reroofing projects. Because Miramar is located in Broward County's High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ), all roofing materials and installation methods must comply with the Florida Building Code and possess a valid Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) or Florida Product Approval. City building inspectors conduct mandatory inspections of the dry-in/underlayment and final roof installation.

### 2. How does the landmark ruling *Johnson v. Davis* protect Miramar homebuyers?
Under the Florida Supreme Court decision *Johnson v. Davis*, a residential seller has an affirmative legal duty to disclose any known facts or defects materially affecting the value of the property that are not readily observable to the buyer. This duty applies even when buying a property "AS IS." If a seller fails to disclose past roof leaks, rotted roof decking, or unpermitted repairs, the buyer can sue for fraudulent concealment and recover full compensatory repair costs.

### 3. How does roof age affect homeowner's insurance in Broward County?
Due to Florida’s property insurance market conditions, insurance carriers enforce strict age limits on roofs. In Miramar, many private insurance companies will not write or renew policies on homes with asphalt shingle roofs over 10 to 15 years old, or tile roofs over 15 to 20 years old, regardless of visual condition. Buyers should review a 4-Point Inspection and Wind Mitigation Inspection before purchasing to verify insurance eligibility.

### 4. What is a "secondary water barrier," and why is it mandatory in Miramar?
A secondary water barrier (often called a sealed roof deck) is a waterproof membrane—typically a self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet ("peel-and-stick")—applied directly to the structural wood roof deck before shingles or tiles are installed. Under the Florida Building Code for High-Velocity Hurricane Zones, this secondary barrier is required to prevent water intrusion into the home in the event that hurricane-force winds blow off the outer shingles or tiles.
