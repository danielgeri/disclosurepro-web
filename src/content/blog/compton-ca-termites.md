## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean & Drywood Termites in Compton, CA

Compton’s alluvial soil conditions, coastal marine layer humidity, and prevalent post-war wood-framed construction create sustained pressure from two distinct termite species.

### How Los Angeles River Hydrology, Alluvial Soils, and Crawlspaces Drive Infestations

Several localized environmental and architectural factors make Compton homes prime targets for wood-destroying organisms:

- **Alluvial Basin Hydrology & Compton Creek:** Compton is situated within the alluvial drainage plain of the Los Angeles River and Compton Creek. Shallow groundwater tables along these waterways keep subsoils damp throughout the year. Because subterranean termites require high relative humidity (near 100%) within their underground colonies to prevent desiccation, Compton's alluvial soils provide an endless subterranean moisture reservoir.
- **Post-War Raised Foundation Crawlspaces:** The vast majority of residences constructed in Compton during the building boom of the 1940s and 1950s feature raised perimeter concrete stem wall foundations with wood-framed crawlspaces. Ground clearance beneath floor joists is frequently under 18 inches, and homes were built prior to modern requirements for continuous plastic ground vapor barriers. Unvented crawlspaces trap moisture, creating damp environments where subterranean termites tunnel up foundation stem walls directly into structural timber framing.
- **Architectural Stock and Construction Vulnerabilities:**
  - *Sunny Cove & Rosecrans Bungalows:* 1940s and 1950s single-story bungalows constructed with untreated Douglas fir and old-growth redwood mudsills. Exterior stucco applied below grade or landscaping soil piled against exterior foundation walls bridges foundation concrete, allowing termites to bypass perimeter barriers.
  - *Richland Farms Ranchettes:* Properties featuring detached wooden barns, outbuildings, corrals, and mature fruit trees. Decaying wood fences and tree stumps in yards sustain massive subterranean colonies that forage underground into main residential foundations.
  - *Spanish Revival & Stucco Cottages:* Homes with flat or parapet roofs and exposed exterior rafter tails. Wind-driven rain saturates roof eaves, creating prime nesting sites for airborne drywood termites.

### Pest Biology: Western Subterranean vs. Western Drywood Termites

Homeowners in Compton face pressure from two distinct termite species:

- **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The primary subterranean pest in Southern California. These insects live in underground colonies containing several hundred thousand workers. They construct airtight earthen mud shelter tubes across concrete foundation walls to reach wooden subflooring. They consume timber along the softer springwood grain, leaving hollowed wood structural shells packed with soil and feces.
- **Western Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes minor*):** Unlike subterranean termites, drywood termites require no contact with soil or groundwater. Reproductive swarmers enter homes directly from the air through attic gable vents, unsealed exterior window joints, and exposed roof rafter tails. They establish colonies entirely inside dry structural timber, carving out smooth galleries across both springwood and summerwood rings and expelling distinctive, sand-like hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) through microscopic kickout holes.
- **Swarming Patterns in Los Angeles County:** Western subterranean termites swarm on warm, sunny days in late winter and spring (typically February through May) following seasonal rains. Western drywood termites swarm during hot, sunny late-summer and autumn afternoons (typically September through November), particularly during dry Santa Ana wind conditions. Discovering discarded wings on window sills or small piles of sand-like pellets beneath baseboards is definitive evidence of an active infestation.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 & The TDS

In California, residential real estate transactions are strictly regulated under state statutory disclosure mandates designed to protect purchasers from latent defects and deceptive sales practices.

### The California Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of residential real property (1 to 4 units) are legally obligated to provide prospective buyers with a completed statutory Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):

- **Section II.C (Pests & Structural Conditions):** The statutory disclosure form specifically queries sellers regarding:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present wood-destroying pests or organisms (termites, beetles, fungus)?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present damage to the property caused by wood-destroying pests or organisms?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past chemical treatments for wood-destroying pests? If yes, provide details and documentation."*
  - *"Are you aware of any settling, slipping, sliding, or other structural problems with foundations, floors, or framing?"*
- **Buyer's Statutory Right of Rescission:** Under Cal. Civ. Code § 1102.3, if the TDS is delivered after the execution of an offer, the buyer retains the unilateral statutory right to cancel the contract within three days of personal delivery (or five days if delivered by mail) and receive an immediate, full refund of their earnest money deposit.

### Seller Knowledge, Easton v. Strassburger, and Section 1 vs. Section 2 WDO Reports

Under California law, sellers have a legal duty to disclose all known material facts affecting the value or desirability of the property. Failing to disclose known termite damage constitutes fraudulent concealment and negligent misrepresentation under California Civil Code § 1710.

Crucially, in California real estate practice, pest inspections are governed by the **California Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB)**. A licensed Branch 3 pest inspector issues the official **Wood Destroying Pests and Organisms (WDO) Inspection Report**, which separates findings into two critical categories:
- **Section 1 Items:** Conditions with active infestations or infections (e.g., active subterranean mud tubes, live drywood colonies, or dry rot fungal decay) where structural damage is occurring. These conditions must typically be eradicated and repaired prior to mortgage funding.
- **Section 2 Items:** Conditions deemed likely to lead to infestation or infection (e.g., earth-to-wood contact, cellulose debris in the crawlspace, faulty foundation grade levels, or plumbing leaks), but where no active infestation is yet visible.
- **Deceptive Minimizing Phrases in Southern California Disclosures:**
  - *"Routine preventative pest spray completed in 2022; no active warranty."* (Frequently masks active drywood pellets found in attic framing).
  - *"Cosmetic stucco crack patched along exterior foundation."* (Often conceals subterranean mud tubes entering behind weep screeds).
  - *"Subfloor shoring installed to correct historical floor bounce."* (Can hide structural mudsills hollowed out by subterranean colonies).

During their 14-day contingency period, the general home inspector praised the home’s newly refinished hardwood floors, updated electrical panel, and freshly painted stucco exterior. ## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Compton, CA

### 1. Are termites common in Compton and the Gateway Cities?
Yes. Termite pressure across Compton, Long Beach, and the Los Angeles Basin is among the highest in the country. The region's warm Mediterranean climate, combined with alluvial soils along Compton Creek and the marine layer humidity, creates an optimal environment for both subterranean and drywood termites.

### 2. What is the difference between Section 1 and Section 2 items on a California WDO report?
Under the California Structural Pest Control Board, **Section 1** items identify active infestations of wood-destroying pests or dry rot fungus requiring immediate eradication and repair. **Section 2** items identify conditions likely to lead to infestation (such as earth-to-wood contact or plumbing leaks), but where no active infestation is currently present.

### 3. Does a standard California home inspection include a certified termite report?
No. General home inspectors perform non-invasive visual examinations of accessible building components, and standard California inspection contracts explicitly disclaim pest warranties. Buyers should always hire a licensed Branch 3 pest control company to perform an official Wood Destroying Pests and Organisms (WDO) inspection.

### 4. What legal recourse does a buyer have if a Compton seller concealed termite damage?
Under California Civil Code § 1102 and § 1710, a seller who intentionally conceals known termite damage or misrepresents property condition on the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) is liable for fraudulent concealment, negligent misrepresentation, or breach of contract. Buyers can seek compensatory damages for repair costs or, in severe cases, contract rescission.
