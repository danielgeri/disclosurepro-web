## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Savannah and Chatham County

Savannah’s subtropical climate, coastal water tables, and antique timber framing create an ecosystem where wood-destroying organisms thrive year-round.

### Why Savannah Housing Stock and Lowcountry Climate Magnify Pest Hazards

Several localized environmental and structural factors make Savannah homes uniquely vulnerable to severe termite infestations:

- **Subtropical Climate & Coastal Marsh Humidity:** Savannah experiences long, humid summers, mild winters, and heavy seasonal precipitation. Ambient moisture in crawlspaces rarely drops below 70%, keeping structural mudsills, floor joists, and porch headers perpetually damp and appetizing to foraging insects.
- **Antique Heart-Pine Framing & Brick Pier Foundations:** Many of Savannah’s historic homes rest on brick foundation piers. Over the centuries, settling mortar joints, damp unpaved crawlspaces, and enclosed crawlspace skirts provide sheltered, dark runways where subterranean termites travel directly into heart-pine and cypress sills.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Savannah Neighborhoods:**
  - *Historic & Victorian Districts:* Multi-story 19th-century residences featuring exterior wooden double piazzas, decorative corbels, and ground-floor garden levels. Wood framing touching damp brick coursing creates prime access for Formosan invaders.
  - *Ardsley Park & Chatham Crescent:* 1910s–1940s classic colonials and Craftsman homes with mature live oak canopies. Decaying tree roots beneath root-pruned yards provide underground nesting reservoirs, while tree branches touching roofs allow aerial swarmers to bypass foundation chemical barriers.
  - *Isle of Hope & Wilmington Island:* Tidal marshfront properties built with expansive wooden boardwalks, pilings, and screened porches exposed to constant coastal dampness.

### Biology: Eastern Subterranean vs. Formosan "Super-Termites"

Savannah homebuyers must recognize the distinct threats posed by these two wood-destroying insect species:

- **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Native ground-nesting insects that rely on continuous soil contact for moisture. They construct earthen mud shelter tubes across foundation piers and enter structural lumber from below.
- **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Dubbed "super-termites," a single Formosan colony can contain several million individuals. Formosan termites build spongy "carton" nests out of soil, chewed wood, and excrement inside wall cavities, allowing them to nest above ground as long as moisture from a roof leak or window seal is present.
- **May–June Evening Swarms:** During warm, humid evenings in late spring, Formosan alates swarm in massive clouds attracted to outdoor porch lights and indoor lamps. Discovering hundreds of translucent wings trapped in window screens or floating in outdoor light fixtures is an urgent signal of nearby colony activity.

## Georgia Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Caveat Emptor, the GAR SPDS, and the Official WIIR

Georgia real estate law maintains strong adherence to traditional *caveat emptor*, making the contract review and inspection phase critical.

### The Doctrine of Caveat Emptor and the GAR Seller's Disclosure

Unlike states with rigid statutory disclosure statutes, Georgia law does not legally mandate that a seller complete a disclosure form. However, standard practice uses the **Georgia Association of Realtors (GAR) Seller’s Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS)**:

- **Section 2 (Structural Items, Termites, Dry Rot, and Pests):** Asks the seller:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present termite, wood-destroying insect, or dry rot infestation or damage?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any structural repairs made to address past infestation?"*
  - *"Is the property currently covered by a transferable termite warranty or bond?"*
- **The Seller's Legal Duty to Disclose:** Under Georgia common law, an exception to *caveat emptor* applies to **latent material defects**. A seller has a legal affirmative duty to disclose serious structural defects that they know about which could not be discovered by the buyer through ordinary, reasonable due diligence. Intentionally painting over active Formosan mud tubes or concealing extermination records constitutes actionable fraud.

### The Official Georgia Wood Infestation Inspection Report (WIIR)

In Georgia real estate transactions, standard home inspections explicitly disclaim pest analysis. The purchase agreement should require the seller to deliver the **Official Georgia Wood Infestation Inspection Report (WIIR)**—commonly known as the "Georgia Termite Letter":

- **Part 10 (Findings):** Documents whether there is visible evidence of active or prior infestation of subterranean termites, Formosan termites, powder-post beetles, wood-boring beetles, or wood-decaying fungus.
- **Part 11 (Obstructions & Inaccessible Areas):** Itemizes blocked locations—including crawlspaces with less than 18 inches of clearance, crawlspace insulation batts, and finished drywall walls.
- **Transferable Termite Warranty Bonds:** In Chatham County, a property must have an active, transferable termite bond (Retreatment and Damage Repair Bond). Buyers must confirm whether the bond covers Formosan termites, as many basic bonds strictly exclude Formosan damage or only cover re-treatment, leaving repair costs to the homeowner.

### How Savannah Sellers Strategically Downplay Pest Histories

Savannah property sellers frequently utilize misleading language:

- *"Transferable booster bond in place with local pest company."* (Conceals that the bond is "re-treatment only" and does not cover structural timber repairs).
- *"Previous minor spot treatment performed on rear porch in 2022."* (Hides that Formosan termites were found nesting inside the rear wall cavity).
- *"Property sold As-Is with right to inspect."* (An 'as-is' provision in Georgia places an even greater evidentiary burden on the buyer if latent fraud is later alleged).

Critical termite hazards are often split across separate documents, masking the true severity of the situation:

- The Home Inspector writes on page 29: *"Wood rot and high moisture readings observed along rear band joist beneath the sunroom."*
- The Seller’s Disclosure marks "Yes" to prior treatment, adding: *"Routine annual inspection completed; home protected by Sentricon stations."*
- The Termite Bond Document fine print states in Section 4(c): *"Coverage excludes Formosan subterranean termite damage repairs; liability strictly capped at chemical re-treatment."*

When synthesized, these entries reveal a catastrophic hazard: the high moisture under the sunroom indicates active wood rot attracting Formosans, and the existing warranty will not pay a single dollar toward the tens of thousands needed for structural repairs.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Savannah

### 1. Are Formosan termites worse than regular termites in Savannah?
Yes. Formosan subterranean termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*) are substantially more destructive than native subterranean termites. Their colonies can number several million workers, they consume wood at up to ten times the rate of native termites, and they can construct aerial "carton" nests inside walls without returning to the soil for moisture.

### 2. Does a clean Official Georgia WIIR guarantee a house has no termites?
No. An Official Georgia Wood Infestation Inspection Report (WIIR) only reflects conditions visible on the date of inspection in accessible areas. It does not inspect behind finished plaster, enclosed crawlspaces, or inside structural timber framing.

### 3. What is the difference between a "Repair" and "Retreatment" termite bond in Georgia?
A "Retreatment" bond only obligates the pest company to spray or inject additional chemical termiticide if termites return. A "Repair" bond (or Warranty Bond) legally commits the company to cover the cost of repairing structural framing damaged by termites after the bond’s effective date. Always verify that a bond includes damage repair and covers Formosan species.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Savannah?
No. Homeowners insurance policies across Georgia strictly exclude damage caused by termites, wood-destroying organisms, or dry rot. Insurers classify insect infestations as preventable homeowner maintenance, leaving all reconstruction expenses entirely to the property owner.
