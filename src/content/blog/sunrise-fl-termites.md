## South Florida Structural Hazards: Subterranean and Drywood Termites in Sunrise

Acquiring a home in Sunrise requires understanding how regional Everglades hydrology, tropical weather cycles, and Florida concrete block construction interact with destructive pest species.

### Biology and Mechanics of South Florida Termite Species

Residential properties in Broward County face aggressive pressure from three primary termite varieties:

- **Asian and Formosan Subterranean Termites:** South Florida is one of the few regions in the United States where invasive Asian and Formosan subterranean termites overlap and hybridize. These aggressive subterranean colonies can reach several million individuals. They feed voraciously, consuming timber framing at three to five times the rate of native species and creating massive carton nests inside wall voids and attics.
- **West Indian Drywood Termites:** Unlike subterranean species that require soil contact for moisture, drywood termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*) infest dry structural timber directly. Swarming alates fly into attic vents, eaves, and window casings, boring directly into roof trusses, ceiling joists, and door frames without needing mud shelter tubes.
- **Concealed Wood Destruction Behind Concrete Block and Furring Strips:** Most single-family homes in Sunrise feature Concrete Block Structure (CBS) exterior walls. However, interior walls are framed with drywall fastened to wooden furring strips anchored directly to the concrete block. Subterranean termites enter through slab cracks and feed silently on these furring strips, wooden baseboards, and interior partition studs behind finished plaster and drywall.
- **Attic Truss Degradation:** Drywood and subterranean termites frequently attack the prefabricated wooden roof trusses that bridge CBS walls. Because attics in South Florida reach temperatures above 130°F with high humidity, structural wood undergoes thermal stress, making it highly susceptible to progressive termite hollow-out.

### Sunrise Architectural and Environmental Accelerators

Specific environmental features and construction characteristics in Sunrise accelerate structural pest vulnerability:

- **Proximity to the Everglades and High Water Tables:** Sunrise directly borders the Everglades wildlife management basins. The municipal water table sits only a few feet beneath residential ground level. This saturated soil profile provides subterranean termite colonies with an endless water source, enabling colonies to thrive right against building slabs.
- **Widespread Retention Lakes and Canal Networks:** Many neighborhoods in Sunrise, including Welleby and Springtree, are planned around artificial retention lakes and drainage canals. High ground moisture along lakefront lots creates ideal moisture corridors that attract foraging subterranean colonies to foundation slab perimeters.
- **Finished Enclosed Florida Rooms and Patios:** Many mid-century and 1970s homes in Sunrise feature converted Florida rooms or enclosed carports where wooden framing was constructed over exterior concrete slabs without adequate capillary moisture breaks or subterranean barriers.
- **Barrel Tile and Shingle Roof Cavities:** Mediterranean-style barrel tile roofs create warm, sheltered interstitial spaces along eave overhangs and fascia boards. Drywood swarmers easily enter beneath tile edges to establish colonies in structural decking.

### Seasonal Swarming Cycles and Physical Red Flags

Homebuyers touring Sunrise homes should watch closely for physical signs of termite infestation:

- **Year-Round Swarming Windows:** In South Florida, subterranean termites swarm primarily from February through May, while Formosan and Asian termites produce massive evening swarms around porch lights from April through July. Drywood termites swarm in late spring and throughout warm summer evenings.
- **Frass (Drywood Termite Droppings):** Small, clean piles of hexagonal fecal pellets resembling sand or ground coffee accumulating beneath window sills, door frames, or baseboards.
- **Mud Shelter Tubes on Stucco and Stem Walls:** Earthen tubes climbing exterior foundation walls, visible around pool pump plumbing penetrations, or ascending garage wall footings.
- **Rippled or Bubbling Wall Paint:** Paint or wallpaper that appears blistered or water-damaged, often caused by termites tunneling right up to the paint surface.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Legal Obligations in Broward County

Residential acquisitions in Sunrise are governed by established Florida statutory mandates and landmark Supreme Court common law precedents regarding property disclosures.

### The Landmark Johnson v. Davis Disclosure Standard

Under Florida common law, property disclosure is anchored by the Florida Supreme Court's ruling in ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**:
- **Affirmative Duty to Disclose:** A seller of residential property who knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property, which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, has an affirmative legal duty to disclose those facts to the buyer.
- **Abrogation of Strict Caveat Emptor:** *Johnson v. Davis* eliminated strict "buyer beware" rules for residential sales in Florida. A seller cannot remain silent regarding known latent defects such as past uncorrected structural termite damage, hidden roof truss decay, or recurrent subterranean infestations.
- **The Florida Realtors SPDR Disclosure Form:** Sellers typically execute the standard Florida Association of Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR) form:
  - *Section 11 (Wood-Destroying Organisms):* Specifically asks: "Are you aware of any past or present wood-destroying insect or organism infestation or damage?" and "Are you aware of any past or present treatments for wood-destroying insects?"
  - *Contractual Addenda:* When using the standard Florida Realtors/Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase, sellers remain legally bound by the *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure requirement regardless of the "as-is" contract language.

### Limitations of Florida Disclosures and Legal Remedies

Despite Florida's buyer-protective case law, critical legal complexities arise in practice:

- **Actual Knowledge vs. Constructive Knowledge:** The seller’s duty applies strictly to defects within their *actual knowledge*. If an elderly or absentee seller genuinely had no knowledge that drywood termites had hollowed out attic trusses behind ceiling insulation, they cannot be held liable under *Johnson v. Davis*.
- **The Burden of Proof in Fraudulent Misrepresentation:** If a buyer discovers severe damage post-closing, the buyer must prove that the seller possessed actual prior knowledge of the defect and intentionally failed to disclose it. If a seller simply marked "No" based on an absence of recent visible swarmers, establishing fraud requires uncovering contractor invoices, prior inspection reports, or witness testimony.

### Deciphering Deceptive Seller Phrases

Sellers and listing agents in South Florida frequently use understated language on disclosure riders that downplays structural danger:

- **"Property covered under active annual pest control service contract":** In South Florida, having a "service contract" often simply means an exterminator sprays for cockroaches and ants, or that a termite bond exists with numerous contractual exclusions that do not cover structural wood replacement.
- **"Fascia board and soffit repair completed prior to exterior painting in 2022":** Painting contractors routinely patch rot or termite-eaten fascia boards with plastic putty or thin wood patches before repainting, concealing deeper truss tail destruction in the attic.
- **"Inspector unable to access crawl spaces or perimeter attic due to blown insulation or ductwork obstructions":** General home inspectors explicitly exempt confined attic spaces from full inspection, leaving the highest-risk structural truss components unexamined.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Sunrise, FL

### 1. Are termites common in Sunrise and Broward County?
Yes. Broward County is located in an extreme termite hazard zone. Because of South Florida's warm climate, tropical humidity, and proximity to the Everglades, Sunrise homes regularly encounter native subterranean termites, West Indian drywood termites, and aggressive invasive Formosan and Asian subterranean termites.

### 2. What is the difference between a subterranean termite and a drywood termite in Florida?
Subterranean termites nest underground or in damp carton nests, requiring soil moisture or internal plumbing leaks, and travel via earthen mud tubes. Drywood termites live entirely inside dry structural wood (like roof trusses and door frames) without soil contact and leave characteristic sand-like fecal pellets (frass).

### 3. What legal obligations do Florida sellers have under Johnson v. Davis?
Under *Johnson v. Davis*, a Florida residential seller must disclose any known facts materially affecting the value or desirability of the property that are not readily observable by the buyer. Knowingly concealing past termite damage or structural decay violates Florida law and exposes the seller to lawsuits for rescission or damages.

### 4. What is a Florida WDO inspection, and is it mandatory in Sunrise?
A Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection is a specialized inspection conducted under Florida Chapter 482 regulations (documented on FDACS Form 13645). While Florida law does not require it for every transaction, virtually all mortgage lenders require a clean WDO report prior to funding. Homebuyers should always make transactions contingent on an independent WDO inspection.
