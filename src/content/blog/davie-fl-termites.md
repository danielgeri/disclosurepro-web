## The Hidden Threat: Invasive Super-Termites Across Davie, FL

Davie’s proximity to Everglades wetlands, extensive drainage canals, and lush landscaping create an ideal breeding ground for invasive wood-destroying organisms.

### How Canal Hydrology, CBS Construction, and Equestrian Acreage Fuel Infestations

Several localized factors make Davie homes exceptionally vulnerable to wood-destroying pests:

- **Canal Networks and High Groundwater Tables:** Properties situated along the C-11 canal, Orange Drive, or Griffin Road contend with a year-round shallow water table. Subterranean termites require constant moisture to survive and expand their colonies; Davie's damp subsoils provide an inexhaustible water reservoir that allows underground colonies to forage continuously.
- **CBS Construction and Hidden Wood Roof Trusses:** Most South Florida homes are built using Concrete Block Structure (CBS) for exterior walls. Homebuyers frequently assume CBS homes are immune to termites. However, CBS homes utilize extensive wood framing for interior partition walls, furring strips, and massive wood roof truss networks. Subterranean termites tunnel through foundation expansion joints, plumbing chases, and hollow concrete block cores, reaching wood roof trusses without ever being visible on exterior walls.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Davie Neighborhoods:**
  - *Forest Ridge & Shenandoah:* 1980s and 1990s single-family homes situated amidst dense oak groves. Decaying root systems, heavy wood mulch beds, and overhanging tree branches provide direct aerial bridges to roof fascias.
  - *Long Lake Ranches & Imagination Farms:* Sprawling custom estates featuring detached guest houses, horse barns, and extensive perimeter wood fencing. Wood fencing embedded directly into damp soil serves as an ideal incubator for Formosan colonies to grow before migrating into the main residence.
  - *Ivanhoe & Playland Isles:* 1970s single-story ranches built with low slab clearances. Unsealed plumbing penetrations beneath kitchen islands and master bathtubs offer hidden subterranean entryways.

### Pest Biology: Formosan and Asian Subterranean Termites

Purchasers in Davie face structural pressure from the most destructive termite species on earth:

- **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Known as the "super-termite," a mature Formosan colony can contain over five to ten million workers. They consume wood at up to ten times the rate of native subterranean species, capable of hollowing out load-bearing timber framing in months. Crucially, Formosans construct secondary "carton nests" made of soil, saliva, and excrement inside wall voids and roof assemblies, allowing them to survive independently of ground soil contact if a roof or plumbing leak exists.
- **Asian Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** A tropical relative of the Formosan termite that swarms earlier in the year. Like Formosans, Asian subterranean termites produce immense colonies that cause rapid, catastrophic structural damage.
- **West Indian Drywood Termite (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** In addition to subterranean pests, Davie properties face drywood termites that live entirely within structural rafters and decorative wood trim, kicking out distinctive six-sided fecal frass pellets from tiny pinholes.
- **Swarming Patterns in Broward County:** Asian subterranean termites swarm on warm, humid evenings in March and April, while Formosan subterranean termites swarm from late April through June, particularly following heavy tropical thunderstorms. Seeing thousands of large, winged termites swarming outdoor patio lighting or finding piles of shed wings inside window tracks is a clear sign of an established infestation.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis and FDACS Form 13645

Florida jurisprudence places a strict, non-waivable affirmative disclosure duty on residential sellers to protect purchasers from latent property defects.

### The Landmark Johnson v. Davis Doctrine

In the seminal 1985 case *Johnson v. Davis* (480 So. 2d 625), the Florida Supreme Court fundamentally reshaped residential real estate transactions:

- **The Affirmative Duty to Disclose:** The Court held that where a seller knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them.
- **Application to Wood-Destroying Organisms:** Active termite infestations, hidden carton nests inside wall cavities, or unaddressed roof truss damage qualify as latent material defects under *Johnson v. Davis*. A seller cannot hide behind an "AS-IS" contract clause to escape liability for failing to disclose known termite activity.
- **Legal Remedies:** A seller who conceals material termite defects can be sued for contract rescission, refund of earnest money deposits, and actual compensatory damages for structural remediation.

### The Florida Realtors Property Disclosure Statement

While Florida law does not mandate a specific statutory form, sellers commonly execute the **Florida Realtors Seller’s Property Disclosure (SPDC)**:

- **Section 8 (Wood-Destroying Organisms):** Explicitly asks:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present termite, wood-destroying insect, or rot infestations affecting the Property?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any damage to the Property caused by termites or other wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Has the Property ever been treated for termites or wood-destroying organisms? If yes, provide dates, treating companies, and documentation."*
  - *"Is there an active termite bond or service contract currently in effect? Is it transferable?"*

### Ambiguous Seller Tactics in Broward County Real Estate

When reviewing disclosures for Davie homes, watch out for evasive statements:

- *"Home covered under annual preventative subterranean termite bond."* (Frequently conceals that the bond was issued only after active Formosan termites were discovered in the roof rafters).
- *"Fascia board wood replaced during routine exterior painting."* (Often obscures that drywood or subterranean termites had hollowed out the structural eave framing).
- *"Spot treatment completed in attic over garage."* (Conceals widespread drywood or Formosan carton nesting hidden behind attic insulation).

### The Florida WDO Inspection (FDACS Form 13645)

Pest inspections in Florida are governed by **Chapter 482 of the Florida Statutes** and conducted by licensed pest control operators certified through the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). Inspections must be documented on the state-mandated **FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Inspection Report**:
- Identifies visible evidence of live infestations, previous infestations, and previous treatments for subterranean termites, drywood termites, wood-decay fungi, and wood-boring beetles.
- Details structural conditions conducive to wood-destroying organisms, such as wood-to-ground contact, improper exterior stucco clearance, and roof leaks.
- Outlines inaccessible areas, including vaulted ceiling spaces, insulation-covered roof trusses, and sub-slab plumbing conduits.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Davie, FL

### 1. Are Formosan super-termites common in Davie and Broward County?
Yes. Davie and Broward County are among the most active corridors for invasive Formosan and Asian subterranean termites in North America. The high water table, extensive canal system, and mature tree canopies create optimal conditions for multi-million-member supercolonies.

### 2. Can termites damage a Concrete Block Structure (CBS) home in Florida?
Yes. While termites do not eat concrete, CBS homes feature extensive wooden roof trusses, interior wood-framed partition walls, and wooden furring strips. Subterranean termites tunnel through foundation cracks, plumbing penetrations, and hollow concrete block cores straight into roof framing.

### 3. What does Florida law require sellers to disclose regarding termites?
Under the landmark Florida Supreme Court doctrine in *Johnson v. Davis*, sellers must disclose all known latent material defects affecting property value, including active termite infestations, past treatments, and structural timber damage. An "AS-IS" contract clause does not protect a seller who fails to disclose known termite problems.

### 4. What is the difference between a "retreatment-only" termite bond and a "repair" bond?
A "retreatment-only" bond covers only the cost of reapplying chemicals if termites return, leaving the homeowner responsible for thousands in timber repair costs. A "damage repair" bond covers both chemical eradication and structural wood repair. Homebuyers should always inspect the exact terms of any transferable termite warranty.
