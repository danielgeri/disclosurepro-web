## Why Do Homes in Albuquerque Have Termites in a Dry Desert Climate?

Albuquerque's housing stock—spanning 1920s–1950s Pueblo Revival adobe and wood-frame bungalows, Territorial homes with exposed roof vigas, and mid-century concrete-slab ranches—presents unique structural entry points for two distinct termite species:

### 1. Arid-Land & Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes* & *Heterotermes*)
Subterranean termites escape New Mexico's intense surface heat by nesting several feet beneath sun-baked caliche and sandy soil. Rather than drying out, colonies exploit human-made moisture pockets around residential foundations:
- **Drip Irrigation & Xeriscape Landscaping:** Even drought-tolerant Albuquerque yards rely on drip emitters placed directly along stem walls, keeping perimeter soil damp year-round.
- **Concrete Slab & Plumbing Penetrations:** Subterranean workers squeeze through **1/32-inch shrinkage cracks** in monolithic concrete slabs, expansion joints under converted garages, and poorly sealed bathtub or shower trap blockouts.
- **Evaporative ("Swamp") Cooler Runoff:** Roof-mounted or window evaporative coolers discharge continuous condensation and bleed-off water down stucco parapets and exterior foundation lines—creating a direct moisture highway into wall studs.
- **North Valley & South Valley High Water Tables:** Homes near the Rio Grande bosque, acequias, and flood-irrigated lots sit over elevated groundwater that sustains massive underground colonies beneath pier-and-beam crawlspaces.

### 2. Western Drywood Termites in Roof Vigas, Latillas & Parapets
Unlike subterranean species, **western drywood termites** require zero soil contact. After summer monsoon storms in July and August, winged reproductives fly into unscreened attic vents, cracked stucco parapet caps, and exposed **wooden vigas and fascia boards**. Once inside, they hollow out structural beams from the interior while pushing out tiny hexagonal fecal pellets (*frass*) that are easily mistaken for windblown desert dust or sawdust.

## Can Termites Damage My House Structure in One Year in Albuquerque?

Buyers often assume that an inspection misses only minor surface issues that take a decade to worsen. In reality, **termites can compromise load-bearing framing within 12 months** under three common Albuquerque conditions:

1. **Undetected Mature Colonies Behind Stucco:** A newly founded colony takes 3 to 5 years to grow large enough to cause severe structural failure. However, many older Albuquerque homes harbor mature colonies of 300,000+ workers that have already spent years feeding silently inside exterior wall cavities behind wire-lath stucco or foam EIFS. Once those colonies spread into load-bearing top plates or floor joists, structural deflection can occur in **6 to 12 months**.
2. **Roof Parapet & Viga Water Intrusion:** Flat-roof Pueblo homes frequently develop hairline parapet cracks during freeze-thaw winter nights. Rain and snowmelt seep down into ceiling vigas, softening the timber and triggering simultaneous fungal wood rot and accelerated termite consumption within a single season.
3. **Cosmetic Flipper Patching:** Unscrupulous sellers or short-term flippers sometimes scrape away mud tubes, pack wood putty into hollowed trim, and apply fresh elastomeric stucco paint prior to listing. Inside the sealed wall cavity, the untouched colony continues destroying framing during the first year of ownership.

## New Mexico Real Estate Disclosure Laws: NMAR Form 1110 & WDI Rules

Under New Mexico common law and the **New Mexico Real Estate License Law (NMSA 1978, § 61-29-1 et seq.)**, sellers and brokers must disclose known **adverse material facts** affecting the property's value or structural integrity. Most residential transactions use the **NMAR Form 1110 (Adverse Material Facts Disclosure Statement)**.

### Why Sellers Phrase Termite Clues Vaguely in Disclosure Packets
Even on a multi-page disclosure packet, buyers rarely see a plain confession reading *"Active termites in living room wall."* Instead, the DisclosurePro Real Estate Research Team routinely observes four evasive disclosure patterns buried alongside 100+ pages of HOA rules, title commitments, and contractor invoices:

1. **The "Routine Pest Control" Euphemism:** Sellers check *"Yes"* to pest treatment or mention *"monthly bug spray"* while omitting that a pest company trenched the perimeter and drilled concrete slabs for subterranean termites three years earlier.
2. **Unexplained Wood & Stucco Patching:** Notes such as *"replaced bedroom baseboards in 2023"* or *"repaired fascia and re-stuccoed west parapet"* often mask localized termite feeding discovered during a minor repair.
3. **"Unknown" Checkboxes by Investors & Estate Sales:** Out-of-state landlords or short-term flippers routinely check *"No Knowledge"* or *"Unknown"* across NMAR Form 1110. Walk the concrete patio, front porch, and garage perimeter yourself: evenly spaced **1/2-inch patched mortar circles every 12 to 18 inches** are physical proof of prior sub-slab chemical termiticide injection.
4. **Treatment Without Structural Inspection:** A seller who writes *"Treated for termites in 2021; issue resolved"* is only describing chemical eradication—not structural repair. Pest control technicians apply termiticide; they do not open stucco walls or sister hollowed studs.

### NMDA WDI Inspections & VA/FHA Loan Requirements
Because Albuquerque is home to Kirtland Air Force Base and Sandia National Laboratories, a significant share of local transactions utilize **VA or FHA financing**:
- **VA Loan Rules:** The Department of Veterans Affairs generally requires a clean **NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect Inspection Report** completed by an **NMDA-certified pest technician**. Any active infestation, visible mud tubes, or wood rot caused by WDI activity must be treated—and structural damage repaired by a licensed contractor—before the lender clears the loan to close.
- **NMAR Form 2104 Notice of Objection:** Under standard New Mexico purchase agreements, buyers must submit written inspection objections and supporting estimates before the inspection period expires. Always schedule your WDI inspection alongside your general home inspection on day one of escrow.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termites in Albuquerque, NM

### Why do homes in Albuquerque have termites in a dry desert climate?
Albuquerque has both subterranean termites and western drywood termites. Subterranean termites thrive beneath the desert surface by seeking moisture from lawn sprinklers, drip irrigation lines, leaking plumbing under concrete slabs, and evaporative cooler drains, entering homes through foundation cracks as narrow as 1/32 inch. Drywood termites require no soil moisture at all and fly directly into attic vents, eaves, and exposed roof vigas during late-summer monsoon swarms.

### Can termites damage my house structure in one year in Albuquerque?
Yes. While a brand-new colony takes several years to mature, older Albuquerque homes—especially pre-1970 stucco properties in Nob Hill or the North Valley—often conceal mature colonies of hundreds of thousands of termites inside enclosed wall cavities. If an existing colony is active alongside a parapet or plumbing leak, it can severely compromise sill plates, wall studs, or roof vigas within 6 to 12 months.

### What is a termite letter (WDI report) and who pays for it in New Mexico?
A "termite letter" in New Mexico is an official **NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Inspection Report** issued by a pest control operator licensed by the New Mexico Department of Agriculture (NMDA). Who pays for the inspection and any required treatment is negotiable in the NMAR Form 2104 Purchase Agreement; buyers commonly pay the $95–$185 inspection fee during due diligence (though VA loans have specific rules regarding veteran-paid pest fees in certain structures), while sellers frequently pay for required active-infestation treatment or offer closing credits.

### Are sellers legally required to disclose past termite treatments in New Mexico?
Yes—if the seller has actual knowledge of past or active termite infestations, structural wood damage, or prior chemical treatments, they must disclose those adverse material facts on **NMAR Form 1110**. However, sellers who claim they never inspected a crawlspace or inherited a property without records may check "Unknown," making an independent WDI inspection essential before you waive contingencies.
