## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Fontana and the Inland Empire

Fontana is positioned directly at the base of the Jurupa Hills and the Cajon Pass in the San Bernardino Valley. This geographic setting creates distinct environmental pressures that fuel aggressive termite activity year-round.

### Why Fontana's Climate and Housing Stock Accelerate Infestations

Several environmental and construction factors make Fontana a hotbed for wood-destroying pests:

- **Santa Ana Wind Corridors & Extreme Heat:** Fontana regularly experiences fierce Santa Ana winds pushing hot, dry desert air through the Cajon Pass. During late summer and autumn, temperatures regularly exceed 100°F. These conditions accelerate the swarming cycles of drywood termites, which ride high-velocity thermal wind currents directly onto residential roofs and eaves.
- **Foothill and Alluvial Soils:** Fontana sits on extensive alluvial fan deposits and coarse soils originating from the San Gabriel and San Bernardino Mountains. These porous, well-aerated soils allow subterranean termites to burrow deep during dry spells and surge upward toward foundations during rainy winter months.
- **Divergent Housing Stock:** 
  - *South Fontana & Downtown:* Older neighborhoods around Arrow Boulevard, Citrus Avenue, and Sierra Avenue feature post-WWII ranch homes and mid-century properties built over raised crawl spaces with untreated Douglas fir framing, subfloor lumber resting close to bare earth, and aging stucco.
  - *North Fontana (Hunter’s Ridge, Sierra Lakes, Heritage, Summit Heights):* Modern master-planned developments feature concrete slab-on-grade construction with stucco exteriors. When soil settles or seismic tremors cause micro-fissures in concrete slabs, subterranean termites exploit plumbing penetrations, electrical conduits, and expansion joints to access interior wall studs.
- **Stucco Weep Screed Defects:** On modern Fontana homes, exterior stucco often extends below grade or buries the metal weep screed into landscaped planters and mulch beds. This creates a concealed moisture highway that lets subterranean termites enter structural sill plates undetected.

### The Biology: Western Subterranean vs. Western Drywood Termites

Homebuyers in Fontana must defend against two distinct species:

1. **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):**
   - **Habitat:** Lives in subterranean colonies containing hundreds of thousands of individual workers.
   - **Mechanics:** They require constant ground contact and moisture. To reach wood above ground, they build vertical earthen mud tubes across concrete footings and stem walls.
   - **Destruction Pattern:** They eat soft springwood along the grain, leaving hollowed layers packed with mud and fecal matter.
2. **Western Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes minor*):**
   - **Habitat:** Lives directly inside dry, sound structural timber, roof rafters, fascia boards, and window headers without needing soil contact.
   - **Mechanics:** Swarmers enter attic vents, exposed rafter tails, and roof fascia during September and October heatwaves.
   - **Destruction Pattern:** They carve large, smooth chambers across the wood grain, kicking out tiny hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) through pinhole exit holes.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Fontana Sellers Must Disclose

California maintains some of the most robust statutory disclosure frameworks in the United States, yet buyers routinely misunderstand how liability is allocated.

### Statutory Requirements under California Civil Code § 1102

Under California law, sellers of residential 1-to-4 unit properties must provide prospective buyers with standardized disclosure documents:

- **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS):** Mandated by Civil Code § 1102.6. Section II asks the seller to disclose whether they are aware of *"Pest damage, or any condition which may result in pest damage"* or *"Room additions, structural modifications, or other alterations or repairs made without necessary permits."*
- **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):** A detailed supplemental questionnaire published by the California Association of Realtors (CAR). Section V specifically inquires about wood-destroying pests, past treatments, structural repairs, moisture intrusion, and existing inspection reports.
- **Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD) Statement:** Identifies high fire severity zones (common in North Fontana near the foothills), earthquake fault zones, and flood zones that impact building maintenance.

### Understanding the Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) WDO Report

In California, pest inspections are conducted by licensed Branch 3 Structural Pest Control operators who issue a standard **Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) Inspection Report**. This document divides findings into two critical categories:

- **Section 1 Findings (Active Infestation / Damage):** Conditions where active subterranean termites, drywood termites, fungus, or wood rot are currently present and causing damage. Lenders (especially FHA and VA loans) generally mandate Section 1 clearance prior to funding.
- **Section 2 Findings (Conducive Conditions):** Conditions deemed likely to lead to future infestation or decay, such as earth-to-wood contact, plumbing leaks, faulty stucco weep screeds, or excessive cellulose debris under a crawl space. While sellers are rarely contractually obligated to pay for Section 2 items unless negotiated, ignoring them guarantees future structural failure.

### What Fontana Sellers Conceal vs. Legally Disclose

Under California's "actual knowledge" standard, sellers are not legally required to inspect areas they have never accessed. This creates loopholes:
- **Evasive Language:** Phrasing such as *"Termite spot treatment completed in 2023 for general maintenance"* downplays extensive localized infestation.
- **The "As-Is" Misconception:** Sellers often market properties "As-Is," believing it exempts them from disclosure. Under California law (*Shapiro v. Sutherland*), selling "as-is" relieves the seller from making repairs, but does **not** exempt them from disclosing known latent material defects.
- **Investor & Flipper Disclaimers:** Investors who purchase Fontana homes at auction frequently execute an SPQ stating *"Seller has never occupied property and has no personal knowledge of defects,"* despite cosmetically painting over termite-damaged eaves.

## The Manual Review Problem: Why Critical Clues Get Missed

A standard Fontana real estate escrow package contains 200 to 450 pages of dense documentation:

- CAR purchase contracts and counteroffers
- 12-page TDS and SPQ forms filled out in handwritten script
- 30-to-60 page general home inspection reports
- 10-to-20 page Branch 3 WDO pest reports with diagrammatic blueprints
- Preliminary title reports, CC&Rs, HOA documents (if buying in Sierra Lakes or Heritage)
- City of Fontana permit histories and zoning disclosures

### Where Red Flags Hide

Buyers under strict California contract contingency timelines (often reduced to 7–10 days) easily miss subtle cross-references:

- A home inspector writes on page 42: *"Cosmetic wood filler observed on exterior eaves and window trim."*
- The pest inspector notes in Section 5 (Inaccessible Areas): *"Portions of attic framing inaccessible due to blown-in fiberglass insulation; inspection limited."*
- The seller writes on the SPQ: *"Annual pest control contract with local exterminator."*

Without cross-referencing these three items, a buyer will not realize that the seller was actively managing an aggressive drywood colony, patched over damaged rafter tails before listing, and the pest inspector was unable to verify the attic framing.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Fontana

### 1. Are termites common in Fontana, California?
Yes. Fontana’s Inland Empire climate—marked by scorching summers, mild winters, and strong Santa Ana winds—creates ideal breeding grounds for both Western Subterranean and Western Drywood termites. Almost every home over 15 years old in Fontana will encounter wood-destroying organisms at some point.

### 2. Who pays for termite repairs during a Fontana home sale?
Under the standard CAR Residential Purchase Agreement (RPA), repairs are negotiable. Historically, buyers routinely requested sellers to pay for Section 1 clearance. In competitive markets, sellers often refuse repairs, transferring responsibility to the buyer. However, VA and FHA lenders still require Section 1 clearance before approving a loan.

### 3. What is the difference between Section 1 and Section 2 on a California WDO report?
Section 1 identifies active infestations or active fungal rot currently causing structural damage that requires immediate eradication and repair. Section 2 identifies conducive conditions (such as faulty plumbing, direct earth-to-wood contact, or poor drainage) that do not currently have termites but are likely to cause an infestation if left uncorrected.

### 4. Can tent fumigation kill subterranean termites in Fontana?
No. Structural tent fumigation (using sulfuryl fluoride gas) is designed exclusively for Drywood termites nesting inside exposed structural wood above ground. Subterranean termites live deep in the soil beneath your foundation; treating them requires perimeter trenching, sub-slab liquid termiticide barriers, or localized baiting systems.
