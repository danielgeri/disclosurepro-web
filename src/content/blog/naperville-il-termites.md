### How Naperville Housing Stock and River Valley Hydrology Accelerate Pest Hazards

Several localized environmental and construction factors heighten termite vulnerability across Naperville:

- **DuPage River Basin Hydrology & Glacial Clay:** The West Branch of the DuPage River meanders directly through Naperville, sustaining high groundwater tables. DuPage County’s heavy glacial till clay retains extensive moisture during spring rains and winter snowmelts. ### Biology of the Eastern Subterranean Termite in DuPage County

Homebuyers in Naperville must understand the feeding and colonization habits of **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)**:

- **Subterranean Colonies and Mud Shelter Tubes:** Subterranean termites cannot tolerate exposure to ambient air and dry environments. When traveling across exposed concrete stem walls or plumbing chases, they construct distinct brown mud shelter tubes made of soil, digested cellulose, and saliva.
- **Internal Structural Consumption:** Termites feed exclusively on the soft springwood along the timber grain, leaving an exterior veneer of wood and paint untouched. A structural load-bearing sill plate, floor joist, or carrying beam can be hollowed out and functionally destroyed while looking completely sound on the surface.
- **Spring Swarming Cues:** In northern Illinois, the first warm, sunny afternoons following heavy spring rains (typically late March through May) trigger nuptial swarming. Winged reproductive termites (alates) swarm briefly, shed their wings, and search for moist foundation entry points. Piles of shed insect wings on basement window sills or near floor drains are a critical warning sign.

## Illinois Real Estate Disclosure Laws: 765 ILCS 77/ and NPMA-33 Reports

Illinois statutory law governs the seller’s disclosure obligations, but buyers must understand the legal limits of these protections.

### The Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act

Under **765 ILCS 77/**, sellers of residential property in Illinois must complete and deliver the statutory **Residential Real Property Disclosure Report**:

- **Line Item 19 (Current Infestations):** Sellers must declare whether they are aware of current active infestations of termites or other wood-boring insects.
- **Line Item 20 (Structural Defects from Prior Infestations):** Sellers must state whether they are aware of structural defects resulting from previous termite infestations.
- **Actual Knowledge Defense:** Under Illinois law, the seller's legal duty is limited to *actual knowledge*. A seller is not obligated to perform destructive testing, pull down basement drop ceilings, or remove perimeter insulation.
- **Liability for Concealment:** Under Section 55 of the Act, if a seller knowingly provides false or misleading information—such as patching termite mud tubes or concealing exterminator invoices—they are liable for actual damages, court costs, and attorney fees.

### NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Reports in Illinois

General home inspectors licensed by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR) are explicitly not required to identify wood-destroying organisms. Standard inspection contracts include sweeping disclaimers excluding termite identification. Buyers must hire an independent pest control professional licensed by the Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) who issues a standardized **NPMA-33** report:

- **Section II Findings:** Discloses active infestations, signs of prior non-active infestation, or evidence of past chemical drilling in concrete slabs.
- **Section III Obstructions:** Documents areas where visual inspection was impossible—including fully drywalled finished basements, built-in cabinetry, insulation batts along rim joists, and low-clearance crawlspaces.
- **Contingency Deadlines:** Under the standard Multi-Board Residential Real Estate Contract 7.0, buyers have only 5 business days after contract acceptance to conduct inspections and raise written repair requests.

### How Naperville Sellers Strategically Minimize Pest History

In high-value Naperville transactions, sellers frequently minimize prior pest history:

- *"Perimeter baiting system installed as routine preventive maintenance."* (Conceals that bait stations were installed after an active colony attacked the basement staircase).
- *"Previous minor infestation treated in 2019; zero issues since."* (Downplays the fact that no structural timber inspection was performed behind finished basement walls).
- *"Property sold 'As-Is' with all faults."* (Illinois case law confirms that an 'as-is' provision does not insulate a seller from liability for fraudulent concealment of known latent structural defects).

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Naperville

### 1. Are termites common in Naperville, Illinois?
Yes. Subterranean termites are active throughout DuPage and Will Counties. The presence of the DuPage River, high water tables, and expansive clay soils provide ideal moisture conditions for Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*).

### 2. Can modern homes with poured concrete basements get termites?
Yes. Termites do not eat concrete, but they readily enter homes through microscopic settling cracks, expansion joints around egress windows, and gaps around plumbing penetrations. Once inside, they attack untreated structural mudsills and floor joists.

### 3. What legal rights do buyers have if a Naperville seller concealed termite damage?
Under the Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act (765 ILCS 77/55), if a seller had actual knowledge of an active infestation or structural termite defect and intentionally failed to disclose it, the buyer can sue for actual repair costs, court costs, and reasonable attorney fees.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite repairs in DuPage County?
No. Homeowners insurance policies throughout Illinois strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, and wood-destroying insects. Insurance carriers classify insect damage as preventable property maintenance, leaving all structural repair expenses to the homeowner.
