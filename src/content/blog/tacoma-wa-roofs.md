## Why Tacoma Roofs Fail: Constant Marine Moisture, Aggressive Moss, and Salt Air Corrosion

Evaluating the structural condition of a residential roof in Tacoma requires understanding how the South Puget Sound climate interacts with Pacific Northwest building methods.

### Puget Sound Moisture and Chronic Wetness
Tacoma experiences an extended rainy season spanning October through May, characterized by persistent drizzle, overcast skies, and high humidity:
- **Failure of the Drying Cycle:** Unlike climates that experience intermittent heavy rain followed by hot sunshine, Tacoma roofs remain damp for weeks or months at a time. This continuous moisture keeps roofing materials saturated, accelerating the breakdown of asphalt adhesives and promoting dry rot in wooden sheathing.
- **Salt Spray Atmospheric Degradation:** In waterfront neighborhoods like Old Town, Ruston, and along Marine View Drive, salt-laden marine air accelerates the corrosion of galvanized valley flashings, chimney saddles, and roofing nails.

### Aggressive Moss and Lichen Colonies
The combination of constant moisture and dense shade from Douglas firs and Bigleaf maples creates ideal conditions for moss:
- **Capillary Water Wicking:** As moss grows across asphalt shingles, it forms thick spongy mats that retain water like a reservoir. The rhizoids (root-like structures) burrow beneath shingle edges, lifting the tabs and breaking the factory sealant bond. Rainwater is then drawn backward beneath the shingles via capillary action.
- **Granule Loss from Aggressive Cleaning:** Homeowners attempting to prepare a home for listing frequently hire unlicensed cleaners who pressure-wash roofs. Pressure washing strips the protective ceramic granules from asphalt shingles, drastically shortening the roof's remaining lifespan.

### Aging Cedar Shake Breakdown and Skip Sheathing Rot
Many homes built in Tacoma prior to the 1970s originally featured Western red cedar shake roofs:
- **Cedar Shake Splitting and Cupping:** Unmaintained cedar shakes curl, split, and suffer severe wood rot after 25 to 30 years in the Pacific Northwest dampness.
- **Unsupported Open Skip Sheathing:** Historic homes were framed with 1-inch spaced "skip sheathing" to allow cedar shakes to breathe. When subsequent contractors overlaid asphalt shingles without first installing a continuous solid plywood deck, the unsupported shingles sag between the gaps, cracking under foot traffic or heavy wind loads.

## Washington Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Form 17 and Seller Duties

Homebuyers in Pierce County are protected by clear statutory disclosure mechanisms under Washington state law.

### The Washington Seller Disclosure Statement (RCW 64.06)
Under RCW 64.06.020, sellers of residential real property must deliver the statutory **Washington Form 17 (Seller Disclosure Statement)** within five business days of mutual contract acceptance:
- **Section 4: Structural and Roof Inquiries:** Form 17 contains direct, mandatory questions regarding the roofing system:
  - *"How old is the roof?"* (The seller must provide an age or indicate "Don't Know").
  - *"Has the roof leaked within the last 5 years?"* (Requires Yes, No, or Don't Know).
  - *"If yes, has it been repaired?"* (Mandates a detailed explanation of repairs).
  - *"Has there been any damage to the roof or other structure caused by fire, wind, flood, or other catastrophe?"*
- **Section 5: Systems and Fixtures:** Requires disclosure of any known defects in drainage, gutters, or downspouts.

### The Three-Day Rescission Right (RCW 64.06.030)
Washington law provides buyers with a powerful statutory protection:
- **Absolute Right of Cancellation:** Once the buyer receives the completed Form 17, they have three business days to examine the disclosures and cancel the contract for any reason, obtaining a 100% refund of their earnest money deposit.
- **Failure to Deliver:** If the seller fails to deliver Form 17, the buyer's right to rescind remains active up until the deed is recorded at closing.

### Limitations and Legal Remedies for Misrepresentation
Under Washington case law (*Alejandre v. Bull*, 159 Wn.2d 674), Form 17 is a disclosure document, not a warranty. If a seller answers "Don't Know" to roof age or leak history, the buyer cannot sue on the contract alone. 

However, if a seller actively conceals water damage—such as repainting water-damaged ceiling drywall or scraping moss off rotted sheathing without disclosing known active leaks—the buyer can pursue claims for **fraudulent concealment** and **negligent misrepresentation** to recover repair costs.

## The Manual Review Problem: Navigating Tacoma Disclosure Files

In standard Pierce County transactions using the **Northwest Multiple Listing Service (NWMLS)** contract, buyers operate under an **Inspection Contingency (NWMLS Form 35)** that typically allows 10 calendar days to complete inspections.

During this brief period, buyers must evaluate a substantial volume of technical documentation:
1. The completed Washington Form 17 disclosure questionnaire.
2. A 50-to-70-page private home inspection report featuring common rainy-weather disclaimers (e.g., *"Roof surface wet and slippery; inspected from ladder at eave line only; heavy moss accumulation obscures shingle surface"*).
3. City of Tacoma Planning and Development Services permit archives.
4. Wood Destroying Organism (WDO) pest reports checking for moisture ants and subterranean rot.
5. Invoices from roofing contractors, moss-removal companies, or gutter service technicians.

Reviewing these files under tight contingency deadlines is difficult. A home inspector might note *"heavy moss growth along north valley,"* while the seller notes on Form 17: *"Roof cleaned annually; no leaks."* That innocent statement frequently conceals years of trapped moisture that has rotted out the underlying structural plywood and fascia boards.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Tacoma

### 1. Does the City of Tacoma require a building permit for residential re-roofing?
Yes. The City of Tacoma Planning and Development Services department requires a building permit for residential roof replacements when old roofing is stripped to the sheathing or when structural decking is repaired. The work must comply with the International Residential Code (IRC) and Washington State energy code amendments.

### 2. Why is pressure-washing a roof dangerous in Tacoma?
Pressure-washing is one of the most destructive things you can do to an asphalt shingle roof. The high-pressure water stream strips away the ceramic granules that protect the asphalt from UV light, loosens the shingle adhesive strips, and drives water backward underneath the laps directly onto the wooden sheathing, creating hidden dry rot.

### 3. What are moisture ants, and why are they associated with roof leaks in Tacoma?
Moisture ants (*Lasius* species) are common throughout the Pacific Northwest. Unlike carpenter ants, moisture ants exclusively infest wood that is chronically wet and decaying. When a roof leak goes unaddressed in Tacoma, moisture ants frequently colonize the saturated roof rafters and sheathing, accelerating the structural destruction of the timber.

### 4. What is the best method for managing moss on a Tacoma roof?
The safest and most effective way to manage roof moss in Tacoma is a combination of gentle physical removal using a soft-bristle brush, followed by an application of an eco-friendly moss-killing treatment (such as zinc sulfate or sodium percarbonate). Additionally, trimming overhanging evergreen branches to increase sunlight and airflow helps keep the roof deck dry.
