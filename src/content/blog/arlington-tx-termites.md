## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Arlington and Tarrant County

Arlington features a mixture of established mid-century neighborhoods, custom lakeside properties, and modern master-planned communities. However, virtually every residential foundation in Tarrant County is exposed to subterranean termite pressure.

### Why Arlington's Climate and Soils Accelerate Termite Activity

Several regional factors make Arlington homes susceptible to subterranean wood-destroying pests:
- **Expansive Eagle Ford and Blackland Prairie Clay Soils:** North Texas soils expand when wet and contract drastically during dry summer heat waves. This cyclic movement causes concrete slab foundations to shift, creating hairline stress cracks and separating plumbing penetrations. Subterranean termites exploit these sub-millimeter gaps to enter wall framing undetected.
- **Slab-on-Grade Construction:** The vast majority of Arlington homes are built on monolithic or post-tension concrete slabs. When exterior landscaping, flower beds, or mulch are placed above the slab brick ledge, it creates hidden subterranean bridges into exterior wall framing.
- **Established Neighborhoods:** Communities in North Arlington, Interlochen, and near Lake Arlington feature mature landscaping, high shade, and sprinkler irrigation that keep perimeter soils moist throughout hot summer months.

When warm spring rains arrive, subterranean termite colonies produce large swarms of winged reproductive alates that establish new nesting sites around home foundations.

### The Biology and Mechanics of Texas Subterranean Termites

The primary pest infesting Arlington homes is the **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)**, along with the **Dark Southeastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes virginicus*)**.

These pests operate through specialized mechanisms:
- **Underground Colonies:** Colonies nesting deep in moist clay soil can contain up to a million workers foraging across large territories.
- **Shelter Mud Tubes:** Because subterranean termites have soft bodies that quickly dehydrate, they build earthen mud tubes across exposed concrete foundation perimeters to access wood framing.
- **Cellulose Digestion:** Termites feed on softwood framing studs, baseboards, drywall paper, and roof rafters. Because they feed from the inside out, extensive structural damage is often concealed behind intact exterior paint.

When termites enter through bathtub traps or plumbing block-outs beneath slab foundations, they can infest interior load-bearing walls without leaving visible mud tubes on the exterior perimeter of the home.

## Texas Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Buyers Must Understand

Texas enforces strict statutory disclosure requirements governed by **Texas Property Code § 5.008**. Sellers of single-family residential property must provide buyers with a written disclosure notice before the execution of a binding contract.

### Key Texas Disclosure Documents

In an Arlington real estate transaction, buyers typically review:
- **Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice (TREC Form OP-H or Texas REALTORS® Form TXR 1406):** A multi-page statutory form where sellers must disclose known defects, including specific questions on *"Termites or other wood destroying insects,"* *"Previous Termite Treatment,"* *"Previous Termite Damage,"* and *"Termite or WDI damage needing repair."*
- **Texas Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS) Official Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report:** Issued by a certified pest control applicator licensed by the Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA). This report documents active infestations, previous treatments, visible damage, and conditions conducive to infestation.
- **Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA):** Texas Business & Commerce Code § 17.46, which provides severe civil remedies for buyers if a seller or licensee intentionally misrepresents or conceals known material defects.

### What Texas Sellers Disclose vs. What They Conceal

Under Texas Property Code § 5.008, sellers are only required to disclose defects within their *actual knowledge*. This standard can result in vague or evasive disclosures:
- *"Previous treatment performed in 2022; transferable service warranty on file"* (avoiding mention of why treatment occurred or where structural damage was found).
- *"Repaired minor drywall crack in master bath"* (masking that the wall studs had been hollowed out by subterranean termites entering via the plumbing rough-in).
- *"Seller has limited knowledge; property inherited"* (common in estate sales, shifting the burden of investigation entirely to the buyer).

If a seller disclaims knowledge on the Seller's Disclosure Notice, proving intentional misrepresentation post-closing requires clear proof of prior knowledge.

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Critical Red Flags

In a typical Arlington real estate transaction, disclosure binders frequently exceed 200 pages, combining:
- The Texas REALTORS® Seller's Disclosure Notice
- Multi-page general home inspection reports with photo logs
- TDA SPCS Official WDI reports
- Foundation elevation surveys and plumbing hydrostatic pressure test results
- HOA rules, municipal permits, and title commitments

### How Critical Warnings Get Missed

Important findings are frequently buried in technical appendices:
- An SPCS WDI inspector might check Section 5: *"Conducive Condition: Soil-to-wood contact at rear patio deck and flower beds over brick line."* If a buyer ignores this notation, they may purchase a home where termites are actively bypassing the concrete foundation ledge.
- A general inspection report might include a photo captioned: *"Minor insect shelter tubes visible behind water heater closet."* Without careful cross-referencing, a buyer may mistake this for inactive historic residue.

Navigating hundreds of pages during a standard 7- to 10-day option period can lead to critical oversights.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Arlington

**1. Is a WDI inspection mandatory for purchasing a home in Arlington?**
While Texas law does not mandate a pest inspection for cash transactions, FHA, VA, and many conventional lenders require an official TDA SPCS Wood Destroying Insect report before approving a mortgage. Regardless of financing, a WDI inspection is an essential due diligence step.

**2. What are "conducive conditions" on a Texas WDI report?**
Conducive conditions are property characteristics that facilitate termite intrusion, such as mulch piled above the foundation brick line, wood-to-ground contact at fence posts or decks, leaky outdoor hose bibbs, or poor drainage around the slab perimeter.

**3. Does homeowner's insurance in Texas cover termite damage?**
No. Standard Texas residential insurance policies (HO-A and HO-B forms) explicitly exclude damage caused by termites, insects, or rot, considering it preventable homeowner maintenance.

**4. How does foundation settling in Arlington relate to termite infestations?**
When expansive clay soils cause foundation slabs to crack or shift, the resulting fractures provide direct subterranean access points through the concrete slab into interior walls, allowing termites to bypass perimeter chemical barriers.
