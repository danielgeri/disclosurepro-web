## The Elements Destroying San Antonio Roofs

To accurately evaluate a roof in San Antonio, a homebuyer must understand the unique combination of climate factors and housing styles across Bexar County.

### The Hail Alley Hazard: Micro-Fractures and Granule Loss
San Antonio is situated in the infamous "Hail Alley" corridor of Central and South Texas. Spring cold fronts colliding with humid Gulf air regularly spawn severe supercells producing golf-ball to baseball-sized hail:
- **Fiberglass Mat Fractures:** Hail impacts rarely puncture completely through a shingle. Instead, the kinetic impact pulverizes the protective mineral granules into the asphalt and fractures the internal fiberglass mat.
- **Delayed Water Infiltration:** Immediately after a hailstorm, a roof may not leak. But without mineral granules, the exposed asphalt quickly degrades under ultraviolet light. Within 6 to 12 months, the bruised asphalt cracks open, allowing subsequent rains to penetrate directly into the decking.
- **Metal Valleys and Flashing Dents:** Hail impacts dent and bend copper or galvanized steel valley flashings, pulling them away from adjacent shingles and creating hidden channels for water intrusion.

### South Texas Heat and Thermal Shock
San Antonio routinely endures prolonged summer stretches with temperatures exceeding 100°F:
- **Asphalt Volatilization:** Rooftop surface temperatures on dark architectural shingles easily reach 150°F to 170°F. This extreme heat bakes out essential petroleum plasticizers, causing shingles to lose flexibility, curl, and blister.
- **Thermal Shock:** When a sudden late-afternoon thunderstorm drops heavy, cold rain onto a 160°F roof, the assembly experiences extreme thermal shock. The sudden contraction cracks brittle asphalt and breaks adhesive sealant strips.
- **Wind Lift:** Once thermal adhesive strips lose their bond, typical 40-to-60 mph thunderstorm gusts easily lift the shingles, allowing wind-driven rain to blow beneath the courses.

### Inadequate Attic Ventilation and Wood-Decay Fungi
Many older homes in historic San Antonio neighborhoods—as well as rapidly constructed suburban developments—suffer from inadequate ridge and soffit ventilation. During humid summer months, superheated, moist air becomes trapped in the attic space:
- Condensation forms on the underside of plywood or OSB roof decking.
- This creates an ideal environment for wood-decay fungi, which rots the structural roof sheathing and framing from the inside out while the shingles on top appear intact to an untrained eye.

## Navigating Texas Real Estate Disclosure Laws in San Antonio

Texas statutory law establishes clear disclosure obligations, but understanding how sellers use ambiguous phrasing to protect themselves is vital for homebuyer protection.

### Texas Property Code Section 5.008
Under Section 5.008 of the Texas Property Code, sellers of single-family residential properties must deliver the standardized **Seller's Disclosure Notice** (using TREC form OP-H or Texas REALTORS® form TXR-1406):
- **Section 4:** Specifically requires the seller to disclose the **Type of Roof**, the **Approximate Age of the Roof**, and whether there are any known **Defects or Malfunctions** in the roof, ceilings, or chimney.
- **Section 7:** Demands disclosure of any **Unresolved Insurance Claims** or previous claims filed for property damage (such as wind or hail claims).
- **Water Penetration:** Inquires whether the home has experienced past roof leaks, ceiling stains, or moisture problems.

### The "Pocketed Insurance Claim" Red Flag
One of the most widespread issues in San Antonio real estate involves insurance claims following major hailstorms:
A homeowner files a claim after a severe hailstorm. An insurance adjuster inspects the property, determines the roof is a total loss, and cuts an insurance settlement check for $25,000. Rather than replacing the roof, the seller spends $1,000 on minor cosmetic shingle repairs and pockets the remaining $24,000.

When putting the home on the market, the seller checks "No" to active leaks, knowing that the roof has not yet leaked into the living room. However, the insurance carrier has already officially recorded the roof as totaled in insurance databases (such as C.L.U.E.). 

If you purchase this home, you will face severe problems:
1. When you apply for homeowner's insurance, the carrier may exclude the roof from coverage or demand an immediate complete replacement as a condition of issuing a policy.
2. If another storm hits, any claim you file will be denied because the prior damage was never officially repaired.

### Deceptive Language in San Antonio Disclosures
Sellers and investors frequently craft ambiguous statements on the Seller's Disclosure Notice to dodge liability:
- *"Roof inspected after 2023 spring storm; minor maintenance performed"* (concealing that an adjuster totaled the roof).
- *"No known leaks during seller's ownership"* (technically true during dry summer months, but hiding failing underlayment).
- *"Architectural composite shingle; age unknown but appears in good condition"* (deflecting inquiry from an 18-year-old shingle system).
- *"Seller is an investor/estate and has never occupied the property"* (used to check 'Unknown' across all questions).

## The Manual Review Problem: Racing the Texas Option Period

In Texas real estate, buyers typically negotiate a contractual **Option Period** (under Paragraph 23 of the standard TREC One-to-Four Family Residential Contract). In San Antonio's competitive neighborhoods, this option period usually lasts only **5 to 7 days**, during which the buyer has the unrestricted right to terminate the contract.

During this compressed timeframe, buyers must review an overwhelming amount of documentation:
1. The Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice.
2. The Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange (C.L.U.E.) report.
3. Municipal permit records from the City of San Antonio Development Services Department.
4. Professional home inspection, termite (WDI), and foundation elevation reports.
5. HOA restrictive covenants, bylaws, and financial statements.

In the rush to negotiate repairs before the option deadline expires at 5:00 PM, buyers frequently miss critical red flags. For example, a buyer might notice that the seller listed the roof age as "unknown," but fail to notice that the home inspector noted extensive hail bruising across the rear roof slope, or miss an unsettled $20,000 storm claim listed on page 3 of the C.L.U.E. report.

Manually cross-referencing these technical files under extreme time pressure leaves buyers vulnerable to catastrophic financial surprises.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in San Antonio

### 1. How often do hailstorms damage roofs in San Antonio?
San Antonio experiences severe convective hailstorms almost every spring. According to meteorological data, Bexar County experiences an average of 4 to 8 hail events per year, with severe hailstorms producing damaging hail (exceeding 1 inch in diameter) every 2 to 3 years. Because hail damage is often invisible from the ground, having a certified roofing professional physically inspect the roof after any hail event is critical.

### 2. What are Class 4 impact-resistant shingles and are they worth it in San Antonio?
Class 4 impact-resistant shingles are specially engineered shingles tested to withstand the impact of a 2-inch steel ball dropped from 20 feet without cracking the fiberglass mat (under UL 2218 testing standards). In San Antonio, installing Class 4 shingles significantly reduces future hail damage risk and often qualifies homeowners for substantial annual discounts (up to 20% to 30%) on their homeowner's insurance premiums.

### 3. Can I sue a seller in Texas under the DTPA for hiding roof damage?
Yes. Under the **Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA)** and Section 27.01 of the Texas Business and Commerce Code (Statutory Fraud in Real Estate), a seller who knowingly conceals material roof defects, hides pocketed insurance claims, or makes false statements on the Seller's Disclosure Notice can be held liable. If the court finds the seller acted "knowingly" or "intentionally," the buyer may be awarded treble damages (up to three times economic damages) plus attorney's fees.

### 4. What is a C.L.U.E. report, and why is it essential when buying in San Antonio?
A Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange (C.L.U.E.) report is a five-to-seven-year history of insurance claims filed on a property. It reveals whether prior owners filed claims for hail or wind damage, how much the insurance company paid, and whether repairs were certified. In San Antonio, always request that the seller provide a C.L.U.E. report during your option period to verify whether the seller pocketed hail claim payouts.
