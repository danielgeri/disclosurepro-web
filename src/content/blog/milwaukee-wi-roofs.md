## The Anatomy of Milwaukee Roof Degradation: Lake Michigan Snow, Ice Dams, and Aging Decking

Understanding how residential roofs deteriorate in Milwaukee County requires analyzing the city’s architectural styles alongside severe Upper Midwest winter weather patterns.

### The Physics of Catastrophic Ice Damming
Ice damming is the single greatest cause of winter roof failure in southeastern Wisconsin:
1. **Thermal Heat Bypass:** In Milwaukee’s older housing stock—such as 1910s Craftsman bungalows and 1920s duplexes—attic insulation is frequently inadequate or compromised by unsealed bypasses (around attic hatches, chimneys, and recessed lighting). Warm interior air escapes into the unconditioned attic space.
2. **Uneven Snow Melt:** As the warm attic air heats the underside of the roof sheathing, the snow sitting on the upper roof slope melts, even when outside temperatures are well below 20°F.
3. **Refreezing at the Eaves:** Meltwater runs down the roof slope until it reaches the cold eave or overhang, which extends beyond the heated exterior wall. Here, the water refreezes into a solid ridge of ice.
4. **Water Back-Up:** Over days of sub-freezing weather, the ice dam grows, creating a frozen reservoir. Liquid water pools behind the ice dam and, unable to drain into gutters, backs up under asphalt shingles. It penetrates through nail holes, saturates roof decking, drenches attic insulation, and pours down interior wall cavities.

### Freeze-Thaw Thermal Shock on Historic Materials
Milwaukee routinely experiences rapid temperature swings where winter temperatures climb from 10°F to 45°F during a mid-winter thaw, only to plummet back to single digits overnight:
- **Asphalt Shingle Micro-Cracking:** Water trapped in microscopic surface fissures of aging asphalt shingles freezes and expands by approximately 9% in volume. This relentless mechanical force fractures shingle fiberglass cores, strips protective ceramic granules, and curls shingle tabs.
- **Historic Slate and Clay Tile Spalling:** On historic mansions in the East Side and North Shore enclaves like Shorewood and Whitefish Bay, 100-year-old slate roofs are vulnerable to moisture infiltration into delaminating stone layers. Repeated freezing causes the slate tiles to flake, crack, and slide from their rusted copper nails.

### Multi-Layer Shingle Installations and Decking Decay
In many older Milwaukee neighborhoods, past homeowners sought to avoid costly tear-off fees by applying new architectural shingles directly over existing layers:
- **Hidden Double and Triple Layers:** Milwaukee building codes historically allowed a second layer of shingles. However, adding a second layer traps heat, reduces shingle lifespan by 30%, and prevents visual inspection of the underlying roof decking. Underneath two layers of shingles, tongue-and-groove boards or original cedar shake decking can rot completely undetected until rafters sag under heavy snow loads.
- **Rotted Fascia and Soffits:** Blocked gutters weighted down with hundreds of pounds of solid ice pull away from rotted fascia boards, tearing eaves open to driving rain, raccoons, and squirrels.

## Wisconsin Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Wis. Stat. Chapter 709

Wisconsin real estate law imposes formal statutory disclosure obligations on residential property sellers, providing strong legal remedies for buyers who understand the rules.

### The Real Estate Condition Report (RECR)
Under **Wis. Stat. § 709.02**, sellers of residential property (1 to 4 units) must provide the buyer with a completed **Real Estate Condition Report (RECR)** within 10 days of an accepted offer:
- **Section B1 – Roof Defects:** The seller must affirmatively declare whether they are aware of any defects in the roof, including its covering, flashing, chimneys, skylights, gutters, or downspouts.
- **Section B2 – Water and Moisture Infiltration:** The seller must disclose known water, moisture, or dampness issues in the roof, ceilings, walls, or attic.
- **Ice Damming History:** If a property has experienced recurring ice damming that forced water into living areas or required heat cables, the seller is legally obligated to declare this as a material defect.

### The Statutory Definition of a "Defect"
Under **Wis. Stat. § 709.03**, Wisconsin defines a defect as:
> *"A condition that would have a significant adverse effect on the value of the property; that would significantly impair the health or safety of future occupants of the property; or that if not repaired, removed or replaced would significantly shorten or adversely affect the normal life of the premises."*

If a seller knows that their roof leaks during spring thaws or that an ice dam caused water to stain an upstairs ceiling, hiding that information behind a fresh coat of primer is a direct statutory violation.

### The Buyer’s Statutory Right of Rescission
Under **Wis. Stat. § 709.05**, Wisconsin grants buyers an extraordinary protection:
- If the seller delivers a RECR that reveals a defect, or if the seller fails to deliver the report within 10 days of an accepted offer, the buyer has the legal right to **rescind the contract in writing within two business days** and receive a full, prompt return of all earnest money.
- However, if the seller falsely checks "No" to roof defects and the buyer closes without discovering the lie, the buyer must file a formal lawsuit under **Wis. Stat. § 100.18 (Fraudulent Representations)** or common law misrepresentation to recover damages—a process requiring costly expert testimony and substantial attorney fees.

### Deceptive Phrasing in Milwaukee Disclosures
Sellers and listing agents frequently deploy euphemistic language to obscure serious roof defects on the RECR:
- *"Minor ice damming experienced during historic 2024 freeze; heat tape installed"* — Often masks structural decking rot and chronic heat loss in unventilated attics.
- *"Roof inspected annually by local handyman; all shingles secured"* — Conceals that a licensed roofer condemned the roof and advised complete replacement.
- *"Cosmetic ceiling touch-ups completed in upper unit"* — Hides recurring winter water leaks dripping down through rental units.
- *"Roof age unknown; functioning as intended"* — The standard evasion when an owner knows the multi-layered asphalt shingles are over 25 years old.

## The Manual Review Problem: Navigating the Wisconsin Inspection Window

In the standard Wisconsin REALTORS® Association (WRA) WB-11 Residential Offer to Purchase, buyers typically negotiate an **Inspection Contingency window of 15 to 20 days** (often compressed to 7 to 10 days in competitive situations).

During this critical window, a buyer must digest an overwhelming volume of documentation:
1. The completed statutory Real Estate Condition Report (RECR).
2. The 60-to-90-page comprehensive home inspection report.
3. City of Milwaukee Department of Neighborhood Services (DNS) public records to check for outstanding building code violations, orders to repair, or historic preservation citations.
4. Lead-based paint and asbestos disclosures (ubiquitous in pre-1978 Milwaukee homes).
5. Rental leases, tenant estoppel certificates, and utility records (if purchasing a duplex or investment property).

Because Milwaukee home inspections during winter and early spring often take place when roofs are blanketed with snow or ice, home inspectors include strict disclaimers: *"Roof surface covered in 4 inches of packed snow; roofing materials, flashings, and gutters could not be visually verified."* If an unwary buyer fails to negotiate an escrow holdback, seller warranty, or re-inspection contingency for spring thaw, they assume 100% of the financial risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Milwaukee

### 1. Does homeowner’s insurance cover ice dam damage in Milwaukee?
Typically, standard homeowner’s insurance covers resulting interior water damage caused by ice dams (such as soaked drywall, ruined carpeting, and water-damaged ceilings). However, insurance policies almost never pay for the replacement of the aging roof, the cost of installing proper attic insulation, or the structural repair of rotted rafter tails that caused the ice dam in the first place. Furthermore, filing recurring ice dam claims can result in non-renewal or steep deductible increases.

### 2. What is an Ice and Water Shield, and is it required in Milwaukee?
An Ice and Water Shield is a self-adhering rubberized asphalt membrane applied directly to roof decking before shingles are installed. Under the Wisconsin Uniform Dwelling Code (UDC), building codes require an ice barrier extending from the roof eave edge to a point at least 24 inches inside the interior heated wall line on all heated structures. In Milwaukee’s climate, top-tier contractors strongly recommend extending this barrier 6 feet up the roof slope in valleys and eaves to prevent severe ice dam leaks.

### 3. How many layers of shingles are permitted on a Milwaukee home?
Under Wisconsin building codes, a maximum of **two layers** of asphalt shingles is legally permitted. However, adding a second layer over an existing roof is rarely advisable in Milwaukee. It prevents inspection of rotted decking, traps excessive summer heat, adds substantial dead weight during heavy winter snowstorms, and voids many manufacturer shingle warranties.

### 4. What should I do if a Milwaukee home inspection takes place with snow on the roof?
If snow or ice prevents your home inspector from physically walking or viewing the roof, never simply waive the inspection contingency. Request an addendum to the purchase contract that includes:
- A specific seller warranty certifying the roof has had no active leaks or ice dam backups in the past 24 months,
- An escrow holdback of $5,000 to $10,000 pending a professional spring roof inspection, or
- An extension of the roof inspection contingency until weather conditions allow a thorough visual evaluation by a licensed Wisconsin roofing contractor.
