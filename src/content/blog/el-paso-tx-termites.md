Many homebuyers relocating to **El Paso, Texas**—from Fort Bliss families house-hunting in the Northeast and Eastlake to buyers eyeing historic brick homes in **Sunset Heights, Kern Place, or Coronado**—assume that 9 inches of annual Chihuahuan Desert rainfall means wood-destroying insects aren't a serious threat.

In reality, El Paso sits in a **moderate-to-heavy subterranean and desert drywood termite zone**. Because desert termites nest deep beneath concrete slabs to tap artificial landscape irrigation, plumbing condensation, and monsoon runoff around Franklin Mountains foothills, infestations frequently go undetected for years—until a buyer inherits **$8,000 to $25,000+ in hidden sill-plate, roof-rafter, or drywall-stud damage**.

Understanding how Texas disclosure law works—and how to cross-check the seller's paperwork against a licensed **Texas Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report** before your short Option Period expires—is essential to protecting your purchase.

## Why Termites Thrive Inside El Paso's Stucco & Brick Homes

El Paso's housing stock combines mid-century masonry/stucco ranches (Cielo Vista, Eastwood, Ridgecrest), 1910s–1930s wood-framed bungalows (Manhattan Heights, Austin Terrace), and newer monolithic post-tension slab builds in Horizon City, Socorro, and the Upper Valley. Three local conditions accelerate concealed termite damage:

### 1. Desert Subterranean & Arid-Land Termites Beneath Slabs
Unlike humid Gulf Coast species that leave obvious exterior mud trails, **arid-land and western subterranean termites** (*Reticulitermes* and *Heterotermes* species) tunnel several feet below the sun-baked caliche and sandy-loam soil. They enter slab-on-grade homes through:
- **1/32-inch shrinkage cracks** around cast-iron or PVC bath trap penetrations
- Expansion joints between garage slabs, stem walls, and converted patio rooms
- Moisture pockets created by drip irrigation lines, evaporative ("swamp") cooler overflow drains, or slow slab plumbing leaks

### 2. Western Drywood Termites in Attic Vigas, Fascia & Roof Trusses
Flat-roof Pueblo-style stucco homes and pitched-shingle roofs across West El Paso frequently harbor **western drywood termites**, which require **zero soil contact**. Alate swarmers fly directly into unscreened soffit vents or cracked stucco parapets after late-summer monsoon storms, hollowing out roof rafters and window headers while dropping tiny hexagonal fecal pellets (*frass*) behind finished drywall.

### 3. Upper Valley High Water Tables & Flood Irrigation
In El Paso's **Upper Valley and Lower Valley**, Rio Grande canal irrigation and higher seasonal soil moisture create humid microclimates around older pier-and-beam and slab foundations—accelerating both subterranean termite colonies and wood-decaying fungi.

## What Texas Law Requires Sellers to Disclose (TREC OP-H vs. TXR-1406)

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, residential sellers in El Paso County must provide a written Seller's Disclosure Notice—typically either the state **TREC OP-H** form or the more detailed **Texas Realtors TXR-1406** form (see our complete [Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice Guide](/blog/texas-trec-sellers-disclosure-guide/)).

Both forms ask the seller to check **Yes**, **No**, or **Unknown** for:
- * Active Termites (includes drywood)* and *Termite or Wood Rot Damage Needing Repair*
- *Previous Termite or Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) Treatment*

### Where El Paso Buyers Get Burned in the Fine Print
1. **Drilled Concrete Holes Without Disclosure:** Walk the perimeter patio, front porch, and garage stem wall. Evenly spaced **1/2-inch patched mortar dots every 12 to 18 inches** in concrete prove the property was previously drilled and sub-slab injected for subterranean termites—even if a short-tenure seller or flipper checked *"Unknown"* on the disclosure!
2. **VA Loan Mandatory WDI Rules Near Fort Bliss:** Because Fort Bliss drives a huge share of El Paso VA home loans, note that **the Department of Veterans Affairs requires a clean Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA) Wood Destroying Insect Report (`TDA Form SPCB T-4`)** on Texas properties. Any active infestation or conducive wood-rot condition flagged on the TDA WDI report must be treated and cleared prior to VA loan closing.
3. **Unpermitted Evaporative-to-Refrigerated Air Retrofits & Patio Enclosures:** Many older El Paso homes were retrofitted from roof-mounted swamp coolers to refrigerated HVAC—leaving poorly sealed roof deck penetrations that leak monsoon water into ceiling joists and attract termites.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termites in El Paso, TX

### Does a standard Texas home inspection include a termite (WDI) report?
No. Unless your home inspector holds a dual **Texas Department of Agriculture Certified Applicator / Technician license** in the Wood Destroying Insect category and you explicitly order the **SPCB T-4 WDI Report** add-on, a general TREC home inspection will not satisfy lender WDI requirements or probe specifically for hidden termite galleries.

### How long do I have to negotiate termite repairs in El Paso?
In Texas, your leverage lives inside your **Option Period**—often just **5 to 10 calendar days** from the effective contract date. Schedule both your general home inspection and your TDA WDI inspection within the **first 48–72 hours** so you have written contractor estimates in hand before 5:00 PM on your final Option day.

### Can I cancel if the seller delivers the Texas Seller's Disclosure late?
Yes. Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008(f)**, if the seller fails to deliver the statutory Seller's Disclosure Notice before you sign the contract, you have **7 days after receiving the notice** to terminate for any reason and receive a full refund of your earnest money.
