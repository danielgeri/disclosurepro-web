## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Drywood Termites in Lakeland, FL

Lakeland's geography, hydrology, and diverse architectural stock create distinct vulnerabilities for wood-framed structures and slab foundations.

### How Freshwater Lakes, Sandy Soils, and Historic Framing Accelerate Infestations

Several localized environmental and construction factors make Lakeland properties particularly susceptible to wood-destroying pests:

- **Lake Basin Hydrology and Shallow Aquifers:** With dozens of lakes scattered across city limits—including Lake Hollingsworth, Lake Mirror, Lake Morton, and Lake Parker—the surrounding soil retains high ambient moisture. Subterranean termites require continuous humidity to prevent their soft bodies from desiccating; the persistent moisture near Lakeland's shorelines provides an optimal subterranean habitat.
- **Sandy, Acidic Polk County Soil:** The sandy soils characteristic of the Central Florida ridge allow termites to tunnel effortlessly through the ground. Unlike heavy clay that expands and contracts, loose sandy soil enables subterranean workers to construct extensive underground gallery networks radiating tens of yards from their central nest.
- **Architectural Stock and Construction Vulnerabilities:**
  - *Dixieland Historic District:* 1920s Craftsman bungalows constructed on raised brick piers or concrete stem walls with uninsulated crawlspaces. Moist soil beneath shaded crawlspaces combined with low ground clearance allows native subterranean termites to construct mud tubes directly up pier footings into structural floor sills.
  - *Lake Morton & Beacon Hill:* Historic Mediterranean Revival and Colonial homes featuring heavy lumber framing, exterior stucco finishes, and vintage heart pine timbers. When roof valleys or stucco parapets develop minor water leaks, moisture accumulates inside wall cavities, creating ideal conditions for secondary aerial nests.
  - *South Lakeland & Grasslands:* Post-1980s concrete block (CBS) and wood-frame tract homes built on monolithic concrete slabs. While concrete blocks resist insect penetration, subterranean termites enter through plumbing penetrations (bathtub traps, toilet flanges, expansion joints) to devour interior partition studs, baseboards, and roof trusses.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean Invaders vs. Drywood Termites

Understanding the biological profiles of Central Florida termite species is essential when evaluating disclosure documentation:

- **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** The most widespread native timber pest in Polk County. A mature colony can house hundreds of thousands of workers living in subterranean nests. They construct protective mud tubes across foundation stems to reach wood framing, feeding along the softer springwood grain.
- **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Rapidly expanding inward from the Tampa Bay coastline into Polk County, this invasive "super-termite" forms colonies numbering several million individuals. Formosan termites forage aggressively and can build "carton nests" inside walls using chewed wood and moisture, allowing them to destroy timber structures up to five times faster than native species.
- **West Indian Drywood Termite (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Unlike subterranean termites, drywood termites do not require soil contact or mud tubes. They colonize dry structural timber, attic rafters, fascias, and door jambs directly from mating flights. Their presence is betrayed by small, distinct hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) pushed out of microscopic "kick-out" holes.
- **Swarming Patterns in Lakeland:** Eastern subterranean termites swarm on warm mornings following early spring rains (February through April). Formosan termites swarm in massive twilight flights on warm, humid evenings in May and June. Drywood termites swarm primarily during humid summer nights from May through August, drawn toward porch lights and window lamps.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis and the SPDR

Florida real estate jurisprudence places clear legal duties on sellers, rejecting pure *caveat emptor* ("buyer beware") principles for residential transactions.

### The Landmark Johnson v. Davis Precedent

In the landmark case *Johnson v. Davis* (480 So. 2d 625), the Florida Supreme Court established the definitive legal standard for residential real estate sales:

- **The Affirmative Duty to Disclose:** Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property that are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them.
- **Application to Termites and Wood Decay:** Active termite infestations, concealed structural framing damage, or recurring wood-destroying organism attacks qualify as latent material defects. If a seller knows the property has suffered termite damage, they cannot remain silent.
- **"AS IS" Contracts Do Not Eliminate Liability:** The vast majority of Lakeland residential sales use the Florida Realtors/Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) "AS IS" contract. Many sellers mistakenly believe that checking the "AS IS" box absolves them of disclosure obligations. Florida courts have repeatedly confirmed that an "AS IS" clause never shields a seller from fraud or failure to disclose known latent material defects under *Johnson v. Davis*.

### The Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)

In residential transactions, sellers typically complete the Florida Association of Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR). Section 8 directly addresses wood-destroying organisms:

- **Past and Present Infestations:** Demands disclosure of any past or current infestations of termites, dry rot, or other wood-destroying organisms.
- **Damage and Repairs:** Requires sellers to specify whether any structural damage has occurred and whether repairs have been made.
- **Bonds and Warranties:** Inquires whether the property is under an active termite warranty, the name of the pest control company, and whether the bond is transferable to the purchaser. Buyers must verify whether the policy is a full structural repair bond or merely a "re-treatment only" agreement.

### The Florida Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Inspection Report

Structural pest inspections in Florida must be conducted by licensed pest control operators under Florida Statutes Chapter 482 and documented on Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) **Form DACS 13645**:

- Identifies live infestations of subterranean termites, drywood termites, powder post beetles, and wood-decay fungi.
- Documents visible evidence of past infestation and previous chemical treatments.
- Notes conditions conducive to infestation, including wood-to-ground contact, improper drainage, plumbing leaks, and excessive vegetation against exterior walls.

### Misleading Termite Disclosure Language in Lakeland

Sellers often use euphemistic language to minimize past pest problems:

- *"Annual preventative termite bond maintained."* (Often masks the fact that a major chemical drill-and-treat was executed following extensive interior damage).
- *"Previous cosmetic trim repair completed on patio framing."* (Frequently disguises structural joist compromise caused by drywood termites).
- *"Periodic swarmers observed outside during spring; no active infestation known."* (Dismisses swarmers that actually originated from hidden wall voids).

## The Manual Review Problem: Concealed Risks in Florida Real Estate Documents

Relying on superficial document reviews exposes buyers to enormous financial risks.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Lakeland, FL

### 1. Are Formosan termites active in Lakeland, FL?
Yes. While historically concentrated along coastal ports like Tampa and St. Petersburg, Formosan subterranean termites have steadily migrated inland along the I-4 corridor into Lakeland and Polk County. Because they establish colonies of several million workers and consume wood at five times the rate of native subterranean termites, early detection through specialized inspections is critical.

### 2. Does a Florida "AS IS" contract protect a seller from failing to disclose termite damage?
No. Under Florida Supreme Court precedent in *Johnson v. Davis*, an "AS IS" contract does not relieve a seller of their affirmative legal duty to disclose known latent defects that materially affect the value of the property and are not readily observable. If a seller knows about termite damage or prior structural infestations and fails to disclose it, they can be held legally liable for fraudulent concealment.

### 3. What is the difference between a treatment-only bond and a repair warranty in Lakeland?
A treatment-only bond (re-treatment agreement) requires the pest control company to re-spray or re-bait the property if live termites return, but provides $0 for fixing structural framing or flooring damage. A full repair warranty commits the pest company to pay for structural repairs caused by new infestations. When reviewing disclosures, buyers must verify the exact terms of any existing pest bond.

### 4. Is a WDO inspection mandatory when purchasing a home in Lakeland?
While Florida state law does not legally mandate a Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection for cash transactions, most mortgage lenders (including VA, FHA, and conventional underwriters) require a clear FDACS Form DACS 13645 before approving financing. Given Lakeland’s high termite pressure, every buyer should commission an independent WDO inspection regardless of loan requirements.
