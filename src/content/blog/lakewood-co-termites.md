## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Lakewood and the Front Range Foothills

Lakewood's position along the base of the Rocky Mountain foothills, combined with unique geotechnical soil conditions, creates distinct subterranean pest pathways.

### Why Lakewood Housing Stock and Expansive Soils Elevate Pest Risk

Several geological, architectural, and climatic elements intensify termite hazards across Lakewood homes:

- **Expansive Bentonite Clay Soils and Slab Fissures:** Jefferson County is famous for "heaving bedrock" and swelling clay soils. As these clays expand and contract with seasonal snowmelt and summer drought, concrete basement slabs and foundation footings develop stress fractures. Subterranean termites utilize these foundation cracks as subterranean highways into interior structural timber.
- **Heated Basement Slabs & Year-Round Pest Activity:** Subterranean termites avoid freezing Colorado winters by nesting below the frost line and congregating beneath heated basement floors, utility rooms, and slab-on-grade additions. Radiant indoor warmth allows colonies to feed continuously on structural framing throughout winter.
- **Walkout Basements & Earth-to-Wood Grade Transitions:** In hillside neighborhoods like Green Mountain and Applewood, walkout basements and terraced landscaping often result in exterior soil, planter mulch, or timber retaining walls directly contacting main floor framing, bridging the gap between soil and wood.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Lakewood Neighborhoods:**
  - *Applewood & Morse Park:* 1950s–1960s custom sprawling ranch homes with finished basements, wood siding, and extensive garden beds abutting exterior foundations.
  - *Green Mountain & Eiber:* 1960s–1970s bi-levels and split-levels with half-buried lower levels and crawlspaces where unsealed dirt floors elevate moisture levels.
  - *Belmar & Kendrick Lake:* 1980s–2000s two-story homes with floating basement slab construction where perimeter expansion joints offer hidden termite entry points.

### Pest Biology: Arid-Land and Eastern Subterranean Termites in Colorado

Lakewood homebuyers must be familiar with the two primary wood-destroying insect species active along the Front Range:

- **Arid-Land Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes tibialis*):** The most common termite species in Colorado's high-altitude, semi-arid environment. They nest deep in rocky, clay soils and construct hard, durable earthen mud tubes to cross foundation walls and reach framing lumber.
- **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Prevalent in irrigated suburban subdivisions and established older neighborhoods. These insects require constant moisture, congregating around basement plumbing penetrations, sprinkler overspray zones, and sump pump basins to feed on soft interior wood grain.

## Colorado Real Estate Disclosure Laws: The CREC Seller's Property Disclosure (SPD19)

Colorado real estate transactions operate under state-mandated disclosure rules overseen by the Colorado Real Estate Commission (CREC).

### CREC Form SPD19 (Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential)

Under Colorado standard contract forms, sellers complete the approved **Seller's Property Disclosure (Residential) (Form SPD19)**:

- **Section K (Structural Items & Foundation):** Sellers must disclose known conditions regarding:
  - *"Settlement or heaving of foundation slabs, stem walls, or crawlspace posts"*
  - *"Cracking or shifting in foundation walls, basement floors, or exterior masonry"*
  - *"Subsoil drain issues, sump pump history, or standing basement moisture"*
- **Section L (Environmental & Pest Conditions):** Inquires directly:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present damage or infestation from wood-destroying insects (termites, carpenter ants, powderpost beetles)?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present chemical treatments or pest control service contracts?"*

Under Colorado common law and the doctrine of latent defect disclosure, sellers have a legal obligation to disclose known material latent defects that are not readily discoverable by a standard buyer walkthrough. A seller who knowingly conceals active infestations or past framing damage can be held liable for fraudulent concealment and intentional misrepresentation.

### Wood Destroying Insect Reports in Colorado

In Colorado, Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) inspections are typically documented using the national **NPMA-33 WDI Report** or specialized inspection riders by licensed applicators under the **Colorado Department of Agriculture (CDA) Division of Plant Industry**:

- **Section II (Inspection Findings):** Details active infestations, evidence of previous infestations (frass, abandoned mud tubes, damaged structural timber), or previous chemical treatments (visible drill holes in basement slabs or exterior concrete flatwork).
- **Section III & IV (Conducive Conditions & Inaccessible Areas):** Notes crucial risk factors such as soil contact with siding, basement floor cracks, uninsulated crawlspaces, or areas blocked by finished drywall.

### What Lakewood Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

In competitive Denver-area transactions, sellers often draft vague descriptions on Form SPD19:
- *"Basement perimeter treated historically as preventative measure."* (Downplays an ongoing active subterranean infestation).
- *"Basement slab cracks sealed with epoxy."* (Masks the fact that sealant was applied to block active termite mud tubes emerging from foundation fractures).
- *"Lower-level baseboards replaced during remodel."* (Conceals that rotted and termite-hollowed stud plates were covered rather than structurally repaired).

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Major structural hazards in Front Range properties rarely appear in a single obvious summary. Instead, critical indicators are fragmented across separate forms:
- The Home Inspector writes on page 38: *"Heaving and 1/4-inch hairline cracking noted in finished basement slab near furnace room."*
- The Pest Inspector writes on the NPMA-33: *"Inaccessible sill plates behind finished basement framing; previous chemical drill marks noted on front patio slab."*
- The Seller checks "No" to current active pests on Form SPD19, noting: *"Routine perimeter exterior spray completed in 2022."*

Individually, these notes may seem like standard Front Range soil settling. But when synthesized, they reveal a severe hazard: expansive soil cracked the basement slab, allowing subterranean termites to enter the furnace room and destroy the structural load-bearing sill plates and floor joists hidden behind finished basement drywall.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Lakewood

### 1. Are termites common in Lakewood, Colorado?
Yes. Both Arid-Land Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes tibialis*) and Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*) are common along the Colorado Front Range, including Lakewood. Subterranean colonies thrive beneath heated basement slabs and exploit foundation cracks caused by expansive clay soils.

### 2. What are Colorado home sellers legally required to disclose regarding termites?
Under Colorado law and the standard CREC Seller's Property Disclosure (Form SPD19), sellers must disclose all known past or present wood-destroying insect infestations, structural pest damage, and previous chemical treatments. Sellers are legally obligated to disclose known material latent defects.

### 3. What is an NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect Report?
An NPMA-33 is the standardized Wood Destroying Insect Report used by licensed pest control operators in Colorado. It details visible active or previous infestations of termites, carpenter ants, and wood-boring beetles, previous treatments, and structural conducive conditions like earth-to-wood contact and basement slab cracking.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs in Colorado?
No. Standard Colorado homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, or other wood-destroying pests. Insurance providers treat pest damage as a preventable maintenance issue, leaving homeowners responsible for all repair and remediation expenses.
