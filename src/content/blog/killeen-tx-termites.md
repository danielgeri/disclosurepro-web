## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Killeen and Bell County

Killeen's geographical placement in the Texas Hill Country transition zone creates extreme seasonal climatic shifts and soil conditions that encourage aggressive termite activity.

### How Central Texas Soils, Weather, and Housing Stock Exacerbate Pest Hazards

Several localized factors make Killeen homes exceptionally vulnerable to subterranean termite damage:

- **Expansive Houston Black Clay Soil:** Much of Killeen rests upon heavy, expansive clay soils that exhibit dramatic swell-shrink cycles. During brutal Texas summer droughts, the clay shrinks and pulls away from the exterior concrete foundation, creating subterranean fissures. When heavy spring or autumn downpours arrive, the soil expands rapidly. This continuous movement causes hair-line settlement cracks in monolithic concrete slabs. Subterranean termites utilize these foundation cracks as subterranean highways directly into your home's wall framing.
- **Slab-on-Grade Construction and Weep Holes:** Nearly all residential homes in Killeen built after 1970 utilize monolithic slab-on-grade concrete foundations with brick veneer exteriors. State building codes require open "weep holes" in the bottom brick courses to allow trapped wall cavity moisture to escape. Unfortunately, when landscaping mulch, sod, or garden beds rise above the concrete foundation line, subterranean termites gain direct, uninspected entry through weep holes into interior wall framing.
- **Plumbing Penetrations and Bath Traps:** Modern Texas slab homes feature recessed plumbing cutouts (bath traps) beneath fiberglass tubs and walk-in showers. Because these slab penetrations contain exposed soil beneath the concrete pour, subterranean termites frequently tunnel up directly around drainage pipes, infesting interior bathroom wall studs without ever building visible mud tubes on the exterior perimeter.
- **High Military & Rental Turnover:** Killeen's proximity to Fort Cavazos means homes frequently transition between tenant occupants and absentee owners. Properties may sit with unaddressed plumbing leaks or neglected landscape grading for years, allowing colonies to multiply undisturbed inside wall voids.
- **Humid Central Texas Summers:** High humidity combined with long stretches of 95°F to 105°F temperatures accelerates termite foraging activity. Subterranean colonies construct extensive underground networks searching for cellulose material.

### Biology of Central Texas Termites: Eastern Subterranean vs. Formosan Species

Killeen homebuyers must be familiar with the biological characteristics of local subterranean termites:

1. **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** The most widespread wood-destroying pest in Texas, Eastern Subterranean Termites nest deep in the ground where they maintain required humidity. When foraging above ground, they construct earthen mud shelter tubes composed of soil, saliva, and wood particles to shield themselves from dry air and light. They consume structural wood along the softer springwood grain, leaving hollowed timber filled with dried dirt.
2. **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Known colloquially as "super termites," Formosan colonies can number several million individuals. They consume structural framing at an alarming rate—up to several pounds of wood per week in mature colonies. Crucially, Formosan termites can construct secondary above-ground nests called "carton nests" inside wall cavities using saliva, chewed wood, and frass. If a roof leak or plumbing condensation provides moisture, Formosan colonies can survive completely detached from the soil.

## Texas Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Texas Property Code § 5.008 and WDI Reports

Texas law imposes strict statutory disclosure requirements on residential home sellers, but buyers must understand the exact forms and legal thresholds.

### The Legal Framework: Texas Seller's Disclosure Notice

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, a seller of a single-unit residential real property must deliver a completed **Seller's Disclosure Notice** to the buyer on or before the effective date of the purchase contract. Sellers typically use the official **Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) Form OP-H** or the Texas REALTORS® (TXR) Form 1406:

- **Section 4 of the Seller's Disclosure:** Specifically queries the seller regarding known structural and environmental defects. Sellers must explicitly check "Yes" or "No" regarding:
  - Active termite infestation or wood-destroying insect activity.
  - Previous termite or wood-destroying insect damage.
  - Previous termite treatment or ongoing preventative chemical contracts.
  - Previous repairs made to address termite damage.
- **Legal Ramifications of Non-Disclosure:** If a seller fails to provide the notice prior to contract execution, Texas Property Code § 5.008(f) gives the buyer the statutory right to terminate the contract for any reason within seven days after receiving the notice. Furthermore, if a seller knowingly misrepresents or conceals known termite damage, they are liable under the **Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA)** (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.41 et seq.), which allows defrauded buyers to recover treble (triple) damages and mandatory attorney's fees for intentional misrepresentation.

### Texas Official Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Reports

In Texas, general real estate inspectors licensed by TREC do not perform official pest inspections unless they hold a separate license issued by the **Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA) Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS)**.

A licensed pest inspector issues the official **Texas SPCS Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report**:
- **Section 4 (Findings):** Notes whether there is visible active infestation, visible evidence of prior infestation (such as old mud tubes or hollowed wood), or visible evidence of prior treatment.
- **Section 5 (Conditions Conducive):** Details environmental hazards that encourage termites—including wood-to-ground contact, soil piled above foundation grade, wood mulch against brick weep holes, plumbing leaks, and inaccessible subareas.
- **VA Financing Mandate:** In military markets like Killeen, a large percentage of transactions utilize VA financing. Under VA loan guidelines, a clear Texas WDI report is mandatory. By regulation, the buyer cannot pay for the WDI inspection; the fee must be covered by the seller or another third party.

### What Killeen Sellers Strategically Reveal vs. Conceal

In fast-paced transactions, sellers and absentee landlords often minimize past termite issues:
- *"Routine perimeter pest spray completed in 2023."* (Masks the fact that subterranean termites were found tunneling up the garage expansion joint).
- *"Previous termite treatment on file; no active infestation known."* (Conceals that the seller did a localized spot drill rather than a comprehensive whole-home liquid barrier, leaving internal framing vulnerable).
- *"Minor sheetrock repair completed behind bathroom baseboard."* (Hides that workers scraped away termite mud tubes and repainted drywall without replacing hollowed bottom wall plates).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Clues in 180-Page Closing Packets

During a Texas purchase transaction, buyers must navigate a dense, complex documentation package within a tight Option Period (often only 5 to 7 days under Paragraph 5 of the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract).

A standard Killeen closing dossier includes:
- The TREC One to Four Family Contract and military/VA loan financing addenda.
- The Texas Seller’s Disclosure Notice (TXR 1406 / TREC OP-H).
- A 50-to-80-page TREC general property inspection report.
- The official TDA SPCS Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report.
- Bell County survey plats, subdivision deed restrictions, HOA bylaws, and preliminary title commitments.

### The Hazard of Inaccessible Area Disclaimers

Critical liabilities are often hidden in Section 3 of the Texas WDI report:
- *"Bath trap access obstructed; could not visually inspect behind master garden tub."*
- *"Weep holes on south elevation obstructed by decorative stone planter beds."*

When buyers fail to scrutinize these fine-print disclaimers, they mistakenly assume a clean checkmark in Section 4 means the home is completely pest-free. In reality, colonies frequently enter through the exact obstructed weep holes and bath traps that the inspector was unable to see. Manually cross-checking these notations against inspection photos while managing loan deadlines creates serious risks.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Killeen

### 1. Is a WDI termite inspection mandatory when buying a home in Killeen, TX?
While Texas state law does not mandate a WDI inspection for cash buyers, virtually all major mortgage lenders require one. For military buyers using VA loans—which represent a substantial portion of Killeen home purchases—a clear Texas SPCS WDI inspection report is strictly mandatory. By VA regulation, the buyer cannot pay for this inspection; the cost must be paid by the seller or negotiated as a lender credit.

### 2. What are "conducive conditions" on a Texas WDI report, and must the seller repair them?
On a Texas WDI report, "conducive conditions" are environmental factors that make a home susceptible to future termite infestations. Common examples in Killeen include wood-to-ground contact, flower beds or mulch covering brick weep holes, plumbing leaks around slabs, and tree limbs touching the roof. Under the standard TREC contract, sellers are not legally obligated to repair conducive conditions unless specifically negotiated in the contract during the buyer's Option Period.

### 3. How do subterranean termites penetrate slab-on-grade concrete foundations?
Subterranean termites do not eat through solid concrete. Instead, they enter through microscopic settlement cracks (as narrow as 1/32 of an inch), expansion joints between concrete pours, and cutouts around plumbing pipes (such as bathtub traps and commode drains). In brick homes, they also enter through exterior weep holes if the soil or mulch level rises above the concrete foundation slab.

### 4. What red flag disclosures should Killeen homebuyers look out for?
Watch out for vague disclosures such as: *"prior perimeter pest treatment,"* *"minor cosmetic patch behind baseboards,"* *"annual termite contract in place,"* *"obstructed weep holes on WDI report,"* and *"inaccessible bath trap area."* Whenever prior treatment or damage is disclosed, demand copies of the original treatment contract, chemical application records, and confirmation of whether structural carpentry repairs were completed by a licensed contractor.
