## How the March 20, 2024 Amendment Eliminated New York's $500 PCDS Opt-Out Credit

For more than two decades after the **Property Condition Disclosure Act (PCDA)** took effect in 2002, almost no seller in Downstate New York (NYC, Long Island, Westchester) and many Upstate sellers ever filled out the PCDS. Under former **RPL § 465**, sellers could legally skip the entire questionnaire simply by giving the buyer a **$500 closing credit**—an easy trade to avoid post-closing misrepresentation lawsuits.

Effective **March 20, 2024**, New York amended **RPL §§ 462, 465, and 467** to end that loophole permanently:
- **No More $500 Buyout:** Non-exempt sellers of **1-to-4 family residential real property** can no longer pay $500 to avoid completing the PCDS.
- **Delivery Before Binding Contract:** The completed, signed PCDS must be delivered to the buyer (or buyer's agent) **before** the buyer signs a binding contract of sale, and a copy must be attached to the purchase contract.
- **Actual Damages Liability:** Because the $500 credit cap is gone, a seller who willfully fails to disclose known material defects or knowingly submits false answers on the PCDS faces direct liability for the buyer's **actual damages**—plus traditional common-law fraud remedies for active concealment.
- **Real Estate Broker Mandate:** Under **RPL § 466**, licensed New York real estate agents are statutorily required to inform sellers of their mandatory PCDS duty and timely deliver the completed statement to buyers.

## Auditing the Expanded 56-Question New York PCDS: What Buyers Must Check

The updated **56-question New York PCDS** is divided into four major sections. Even though sellers answer based on **actual knowledge** (meaning "Yes," "No," "Unknown," or "N/A"), experienced buyers treat *"Unknown"* checkboxes by long-term homeowners as immediate inspection targets rather than neutral answers.

### 1. General Information & Ownership History (Questions 1–9)
Establishes how long the seller owned and occupied the property, shared driveways, easements, municipal certificates of occupancy (COs), and whether any unpermitted additions or structural alterations exist. In NYC, Long Island, and Westchester, an open **Department of Buildings (DOB) violation** or missing **Certificate of Occupancy (CO)** for a finished basement or deck can block mortgage closing entirely.

### 2. Environmental & The Expanded 2024 Flood Risk Questions (Questions 10–29)
The March 2024 legislation added **new and expanded flood, insurance, and disaster-assistance questions** directly into the Environmental section, alongside toxic hazard items:
- **FEMA 100-Year & 500-Year Floodplains:** Whether any portion of the property sits inside a designated federal floodplain.
- **Mandatory Federal Flood Insurance & Active Policies:** Whether federal law requires flood insurance on the home and whether the seller currently carries a flood insurance policy.
- **Flood Claims & FEMA Disaster Assistance:** Whether the seller has ever filed an NFIP/private flood insurance claim or received federal/state disaster aid (FEMA/SBA) for flood damage.
- **Standing Water, Seepage & Mold:** Whether the property has experienced recurring basement/crawlspace seepage, standing water, or indoor mold growth.
- **Lead, Asbestos, Radon & Fuel Storage Tanks:** Covers pre-1978 lead plumbing/paint, asbestos insulation or floor tiles, radon test results, and **buried underground heating oil tanks (USTs)**—a notorious $15,000–$45,000+ environmental liability across Long Island, Westchester, and Hudson Valley homes.

### 3. Structural Systems: Roofs, Rot, Foundations & Termites (Questions 30–40)
Requires actual-knowledge disclosures regarding:
- **Roof Material, Age & Leaks:** Flat modified bitumen/EPDM brownstone roofs in NYC boroughs and heavy snow-load roofs Upstate require close scrutiny—compare against our [Brooklyn, NY roof inspection & disclosure guide](/blog/brooklyn-ny-roofs/).
- **Wood-Destroying Insects (Termites, Carpenter Ants, Powderpost Beetles):** Sellers must disclose known past or present infestations, chemical treatments, and structural wood damage. Across both Downstate masonry rowhomes and Upstate wood-frame homes, never rely solely on a "No" or "Unknown" checkbox—see our [New York City termite disclosure guide](/blog/termites-new-york-city-ny/) and [Buffalo, NY termite disclosure guide](/blog/buffalo-ny-termites/).
- **Foundation, Exterior Walls, Chimneys & Fire/Smoke Damage:** Covers bowed stone/block walls, masonry parapet cracks, and past fire remediation.

### 4. Mechanical Systems, Plumbing, Sewer & Electrical (Questions 41–56)
Audits public water vs. private wells, public sewer vs. septic/cesspool systems (critical in Suffolk County and rural Upstate), electrical panel capacity (60-amp fuse boxes, FPE/Zinsco panels, cloth/knob-and-tube wiring), heating/boiler systems, and air conditioning.

## Who Is Still Exempt From the New York PCDS? (Co-Ops, Condos & Estates)

Even after the March 2024 repeal of the $500 credit, **RPL § 463** still exempts several major New York residential transaction categories from delivering a PCDS:

| Property / Transfer Type | PCDS Required? | Mandatory Alternative Buyer Due Diligence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1-to-4 Family Houses & Townhouses (Standard Sale)** | **YES (Mandatory)** | Audit all 56 PCDS questions against your independent inspection, WDI termite report, sewer scope, and municipal CO/permit search. |
| **Cooperative Apartments (Co-Ops)** | **EXEMPT** | Your NY real estate attorney must review the **Offering Plan**, **2–3 years of Board Meeting Minutes**, audited financial statements, flip tax, and underlying building mortgage. |
| **Condominium Units (Condos)** | **EXEMPT** | Review Condo Offering Plan, reserve balances, **NYC Local Law 11 (FISP)** facade inspection reports, Local Law 97 carbon penalty exposure, and pending special assessments. |
| **Estates / Fiduciaries (Executors, Trustees)** | **EXEMPT** | Treat as full *caveat emptor*; order comprehensive structural, sewer-scope, oil-tank sweep, and NPMA-33 WDI inspections before signing. |
| **New Construction (Never Inhabited)** | **EXEMPT** | Rely on NY General Business Law **Article 36-B Statutory Housing Merchant Implied Warranty** (1-year workmanship, 2-year systems, 6-year structural) + independent phase inspections. |

## Pre-Contract Attorney Review: Downstate vs. Upstate Negotiation Strategy

How you use a 56-question PCDS contradiction to negotiate credits depends heavily on where in New York you are buying:

- **Downstate New York (NYC, Long Island, Westchester, Rockland):** Deals follow a **pre-contract due diligence model**. Once an offer is accepted, you typically have **5 to 10 days before signing the binding Contract of Sale** to review the PCDS, run your home inspection, oil-tank sweep, and WDI termite inspection, and negotiate price adjustments or credits directly through your real estate attorney. Once both parties countersign the contract, there is usually **no general inspection contingency**—making pre-signing PDF verification critical.
- **Upstate & Western New York (Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse, Albany, Hudson Valley):** Buyers generally sign a standard purchase contract subject to a **3-to-5-business-day Attorney Approval Period** and formal **Inspection Contingency** (typically 7–10 days), allowing written credit requests or cancellation after contract signing.

## Frequently Asked Questions About the New York Property Condition Disclosure Statement

### Can a New York home seller still pay a $500 credit instead of filling out the PCDS?
No. Effective **March 20, 2024**, New York amended **Real Property Law Article 14** to completely repeal the $500 opt-out credit. Non-exempt sellers of 1-to-4 family residential properties must now complete and deliver the mandatory **56-question Property Condition Disclosure Statement** before a binding purchase contract is signed.

### What new questions were added to the 56-question New York PCDS?
The March 2024 revision expanded the form from 48 to **56 questions**, primarily adding detailed **flood risk and insurance disclosures**—including whether the property sits in a FEMA 100-year or 500-year floodplain, whether federal flood insurance is required or currently held, whether the seller has ever filed a flood insurance claim or received FEMA/government disaster aid, and whether the home has suffered flooding, seepage, or mold.

### Do New York condo and co-op sellers have to complete the PCDS?
No. Under **NY Real Property Law § 463**, sales of **condominium units and cooperative apartments (co-ops)** remain exempt from the PCDS—along with estate/fiduciary sales and never-inhabited new construction. Condo and co-op buyers rely instead on attorney review of the Offering Plan, audited building financials, Local Law 11 facade reports, and 2+ years of board meeting minutes.

### What if a New York seller checks "Unknown" on almost every PCDS question?
Checking **"Unknown"** is permitted when a seller genuinely lacks actual knowledge, and some attorneys advise cautious answers to minimize liability. However, heavy use of "Unknown" by a long-term owner-occupant is a major buyer red flag—never treat it as a clean bill of health. Order a full general inspection, NPMA-33 WDI termite inspection, sewer scope, and oil-tank sweep before signing the binding Contract of Sale, and note that a seller who falsely marks "Unknown" to hide documented past flood claims or contractor bids remains liable for actual damages and fraudulent concealment.
