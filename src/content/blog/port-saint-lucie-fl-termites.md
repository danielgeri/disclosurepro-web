## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Port Saint Lucie and St. Lucie County

Port Saint Lucie is situated along the Treasure Coast of Florida, bordered by the North Fork of the St. Lucie River and the Atlantic coastal plain. Our local environment features a humid subtropical-to-tropical climate, wet summer rainy seasons, sandy acidic soils, and seasonal tropical storms—conditions that provide an optimal habitat for destructive wood-destroying insect colonies.

### Why Port Saint Lucie Housing Stock and Coastal Climate Elevate Pest Risk

Several architectural, geological, and climate factors make residential properties across Port Saint Lucie particularly vulnerable to structural termite infestations:

- **Proliferation of Asian and Formosan Subterranean Termites:** The Treasure Coast is increasingly impacted by both Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*) and Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*). These aggressive species construct massive underground colonies that can consume structural wood framing at up to ten times the rate of native subterranean species.
- **Monolithic Slabs and Plumbing Penetrations:** Modern homes across Tradition, Torino, and St. Lucie West are constructed with monolithic slab-on-grade foundations. As concrete cures, shrinkage gaps form around copper water lines, PVC drain stacks, and shower/tub block-outs. Subterranean termites require a gap of only 1/32 of an inch to enter interior structural partition walls.
- **Landscape Mulch and Buried Foundation Stems:** In suburban developments, homeowners frequently install thick layers of cypress or pine bark mulch directly against exterior stucco walls. Mulch retains ground moisture and cools the soil, creating a continuous, sheltered corridor for subterranean termites to bypass exterior concrete stems.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Port Saint Lucie Neighborhoods:**
  - *Sandpiper Bay & River Park:* 1970s and 1980s CBS homes located near waterways. Mature oak and palm root systems, along with high ground moisture, create elevated subterranean termite pressure.
  - *St. Lucie West & Torino:* 1990s and 2000s single-family homes featuring vaulted ceilings and wood-framed gable ends where drywood termites penetrate unsealed roof vents and hurricane-loosened soffits.
  - *Tradition & Becker Biltmore:* Rapidly expanding master-planned communities where soil grading and decorative flower beds frequently cover exterior foundation weep holes.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean vs. Drywood Termites

Port Saint Lucie homeowners face simultaneous threats from two distinct wood-destroying insect groups:
- **Subterranean Termites (Asian, Formosan & Eastern):** Soil-nesting insects that construct protective earthen mud shelter tubes over foundation stem walls, plumbing pipes, and concrete blocks to reach wood framing, consuming the soft springwood along the grain.
- **Drywood Termites (West Indian & Western):** Airborne insects that fly into structures during warm late-spring and summer swarms. They infest attic roof trusses, fascia boards, and door frames without needing soil contact, expelling distinctive sand-like hexagonal fecal pellets (frass).

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis and FDACS WDO Reports

Florida real estate transactions are governed by strong consumer protection doctrines established by the Florida Supreme Court and standardized state inspection regulations.

### The Landmark Florida Precedent: *Johnson v. Davis* (1985)

In Florida real estate law, seller disclosure obligations are governed by the landmark Florida Supreme Court decision ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**:
- The court established that where a seller of residential real estate knows of facts materially affecting the value or desirability of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them.
- Subsequent appellate decisions (such as *Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.*) established that an "AS IS" contract (including the standard Florida Realtors/Florida Bar FAR/BAR "AS IS" contract) does not relieve a seller of their *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure duty or protect them from liability for fraudulent non-disclosure.

### The Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential

The standard Florida Realtors **Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential** requires sellers to provide detailed information regarding property conditions:

- **Section 10 (Wood-Destroying Organisms / Pests):** Specifically requires the seller to disclose:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present infestation or damage caused by wood-destroying organisms (termites, powder post beetles, old house borers, wood-decaying fungi)?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present treatments for wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Is the property currently covered by a transferable termite warranty or treatment contract?"*
  - *"Have any structural repairs been completed to repair damage caused by wood-destroying organisms?"*
- **Section 5 & 8 (Roofing & Structural Components):** Mandates disclosure of roof leaks, water intrusion behind stucco, and attic moisture—conditions that accelerate Formosan and subterranean carton nest formation.

### The Official Florida WDO Report (Form FDACS-13645)

Official termite inspections in Florida are regulated by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)** under **Chapter 482 Florida Statutes** and must be completed on standardized **Form FDACS-13645**:
- **Section 2 (Inspection Findings):** Identifies visible active infestations, evidence of previous infestations, visible structural wood damage, and past chemical treatment drill marks.
- **Section 3 (Inaccessible Areas):** Documents obstructed zones, including vaulted ceilings lacking attic access, areas behind spray foam attic insulation, enclosed soffits, and walls covered by built-in cabinets.

### What Port Saint Lucie Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers in St. Lucie County often use evasive phrasing on the Seller's Property Disclosure:
- *"Local spot treatment completed for drywood termites in 2022."* (Conceals that spot treatment was chosen to avoid the expense of whole-structure tent fumigation).
- *"Roof framing reinforced during minor attic repair."* (Downplays the structural sistering of Formosan termite-hollowed roof truss chords).
- *"Transferable termite bond active."* Fails to clarify that the bond covers only subterranean species, leaving drywood termite infestations completely uncovered.

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Crucial Clues in 200-Page Closing Packets

During the standard 10-to-15-day inspection contingency under the FAR/BAR "AS IS" contract, buyers receive a substantial stack of complex closing documents:
- Florida Realtors/Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) AS IS Contract and Addenda
- Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential
- 40-to-75-page Comprehensive General Home Inspection Report
- Official Form FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) Report
- St. Lucie County Property Appraiser records, Wind Mitigation Reports, and Title Commitments

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Material structural hazards rarely appear as an explicit, single warning. Instead, critical indicators are scattered across separate disclosures:
- The Home Inspector notes on page 41: *"Minor water staining and soft timber observed on roof truss web members near south attic gable vent."*
- The FDACS WDO Pest Inspector writes under Inaccessible Areas: *"Blown-in fiberglass attic insulation prevented visual inspection of bottom truss chords."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatment on the disclosure, writing: *"Routine annual pest control service maintained."*

When evaluated separately, each statement seems routine. But when cross-referenced, they reveal a dangerous reality: tropical storm rains entered through the gable vent, drywood and Formosan termites colonized the damp attic framing, and hollowed out structural truss chords hidden beneath the blown-in insulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Port Saint Lucie

### 1. Are termites common in Port Saint Lucie, Florida?
Yes. Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*), Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*), and West Indian Drywood Termites are highly prevalent throughout Port Saint Lucie and St. Lucie County. Subtropical warmth, sandy soils, high water tables, and hurricane-loosened soffits create ideal conditions for termite colonization.

### 2. What does Florida law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under the Florida Supreme Court ruling in *Johnson v. Davis*, sellers must disclose all known latent material defects affecting property value, including active or past termite infestations, chemical treatment history, and structural wood damage.

### 3. What is an FDACS-13645 WDO report in Florida?
Form FDACS-13645 is the official Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) inspection report mandated by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. It documents visible active infestations, prior damage, past treatment marks, and inaccessible property areas.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite repairs in Florida?
No. Standard Florida homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, wood rot, and wood-destroying insects. Insurance carriers classify insect damage as a preventable property maintenance issue, requiring property owners to bear all structural and cosmetic repair costs.
