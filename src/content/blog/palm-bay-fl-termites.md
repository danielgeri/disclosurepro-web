## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean and Drywood Termites in Palm Bay, FL

Palm Bay’s Space Coast geography, extensive drainage canal network, and typical building practices create distinct vulnerabilities to wood-destroying organisms.

### How Canal Hydrology, Sandy Soils, and Concrete Block Construction Fuel Infestations

Several environmental and construction factors make Palm Bay properties prime targets for timber pests:

- **Canal Hydrology and High Water Tables:** Palm Bay is crisscrossed by hundreds of miles of freshwater drainage canals and bordered by Turkey Creek and the Indian River Lagoon. Saturated sub-grade soils provide subterranean termites with perpetual ambient moisture. Subterranean termites require constant moisture to avoid desiccation, and Palm Bay's shallow water table provides an inexhaustible reservoir.
- **The "CBS Myth" in Florida Construction:** Most homes in Palm Bay are built with Concrete Block Structure (CBS) exterior walls on monolithic concrete slab foundations. However, these homes are framed internally with wood stud partitions, wood furring strips attached to exterior block walls, and extensive engineered wood roof truss systems. Subterranean termites easily climb the interior cavities of hollow concrete blocks to reach roof framing, while drywood termites enter attics directly through exterior gable and soffit vents.
- **Vulnerabilities Across Palm Bay Neighborhoods:**
  - *Port Malabar:* Expansive subdivisions developed between the 1960s and 1990s. Mature pine and oak trees frequently harbor subterranean colonies whose foraging tunnels extend beneath slab foundations into plumbing sleeves.
  - *Lockmar:* Residential properties featuring sprawling single-family homes where patio additions, screen enclosures, and wood privacy fences directly contact exterior stucco walls, creating concealed termite bridges.
  - *Bayside Lakes:* Master-planned developments built in the 2000s and 2010s. In-ground automatic lawn irrigation systems frequently spray directly against exterior stucco weep lines, keeping slab perimeters saturated year-round.

### Pest Biology: Asian vs. Formosan vs. Drywood Termites

Palm Bay is unique because it sits in an active transition zone where multiple invasive and native species overlap:

- **Asian Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** A tropical super-termite introduced through South Florida seaports, now established in Brevard County. They build massive underground colonies of several million workers, forage aggressively, and cause rapid, devastating structural damage to roof trusses and framing.
- **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Capable of creating secondary "carton" nests inside attic cavities and double walls, allowing colonies to survive above ground without returning to the soil if an air conditioning condensation drip or minor roof leak provides moisture.
- **West Indian Drywood Termite (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Lives entirely within seasoned structural lumber without any soil contact. They colonize attic roof trusses, ceiling joists, and door frames, expelling tiny, hard, hexagonal fecal pellets (frass) through pinhole exit holes.
- **Swarming Timing in Central Florida:** In Palm Bay, subterranean termites swarm on warm, humid evenings between March and June following rain events. Drywood termites swarm in explosive flights during hot, humid late-spring and summer evenings. Finding shed translucent wings around window sills or piles of sand-like pellets along baseboards confirms an active infestation.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis and the SPDR

Residential transactions in Florida are governed by strong legal doctrines regarding property condition disclosures.

### The Landmark Johnson v. Davis Doctrine

Under the landmark Florida Supreme Court decision in ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**, where a seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them to the buyer:

- **Application to Termites and Structural Damage:** Florida courts have repeatedly held that active termite infestations, previous unaddressed wood-destroying insect damage, and compromised structural trusses constitute latent material defects falling squarely under the *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure rule.
- **The Florida Realtors/Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) Contract:** Most transactions use either the standard FAR/BAR Residential Contract or the "AS IS" Residential Contract. In both versions, the seller is obligated by common law to disclose known material latent defects. Crucially, selling a home "AS IS" does not eliminate or waive the seller’s duty under *Johnson v. Davis*.
- **The Seller’s Property Disclosure – Residential (SPDR):** Standard Florida disclosure forms ask explicit questions regarding wood-destroying organisms:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present termite or other wood-destroying organism infestations or damage?"*
  - *"Has the property been treated for termites or other wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Is the property currently covered by a transferable termite guarantee or service contract?"*
- **The Florida WDO Inspection Report (Form FDACS-13645):** Termite inspections in Florida are conducted by licensed pest control businesses regulated by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS). The official report documents live organisms, previous infestations, and visible wood damage.

### Deceptive Language in Palm Bay Disclosures

Sellers often draft minimal disclosure notations designed to technically satisfy the law while minimizing buyer alarm:

- *"Transferable pest warranty in place with local exterminator."* (Conceals that the warranty was opened after active Asian subterranean termites were discovered destroying attic trusses).
- *"Previous cosmetic repair to fascia board."* (Masks the fact that drywood termites had hollowed out structural roof rafters).
- *"Routine preventative perimeter soil injection completed in 2022."* (Hides an active subterranean colony discovered inside interior drywall partitions).

## The Manual Review Problem: Concealed Red Flags in Florida Closings

Closing a home in Palm Bay involves reviewing an extensive collection of legal documents: the FAR/BAR Contract, municipal lien searches, HOA and deed restriction packages, title commitments, flood elevation certificates, and 60-page general inspection reports.

Under Florida licensing rules, general home inspectors conduct non-invasive visual examinations and explicitly disclaim wood-destroying organism evaluations. When a buyer receives a separate Florida WDO report on Form FDACS-13645, critical nuances are frequently lost in translation. A WDO inspector may note "evidence of previous wood-destroying organisms observed in attic; no live insects seen at time of inspection," while the general inspector’s report notes "minor roof truss deflection above garage."

Without automated intelligent document synthesis, prospective buyers fail to cross-reference these clues before the expiration of the inspection contingency period, leaving them financially vulnerable to major structural timber failures.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Palm Bay, FL

### 1. Are concrete block (CBS) homes in Palm Bay safe from termites?
No. While termites cannot consume concrete blocks or cement slabs, CBS homes contain significant structural wood framing, including engineered roof trusses, interior partition studs, furring strips, and door jambs. Subterranean termites travel through hollow block cores, while drywood termites enter attics directly from the air.

### 2. Does Florida law require sellers to disclose past termite treatments?
Yes. Under the Florida Supreme Court’s *Johnson v. Davis* ruling, sellers are legally required to disclose known latent material defects affecting the property’s value, which includes active or past termite infestations and structural damage.

### 3. What is the difference between subterranean termites and drywood termites in Florida?
Subterranean termites nest in the ground and construct earthen mud tubes to reach wood framing. Drywood termites live entirely within dry structural lumber without soil contact, producing characteristic hexagonal sand-like fecal pellets (frass).

### 4. What are common warning signs of termites in a Palm Bay home?
Look for earthen mud tubes along foundation slabs or inside garage walls, fine piles of hexagonal fecal pellets beneath window sills or on countertops, bubbling or wavy drywall paint, sagging ceilings, and flights of winged swarmers around exterior lights in spring and summer.
