## San Francisco Peninsula Structural Hazards: Termite Pressures in Redwood City

Safeguarding your real estate investment when purchasing a Redwood City property requires understanding how regional microclimates, geological soil variations, and distinct building styles intersect with wood-destroying organisms.

### Biology and Mechanics of Peninsula Termite Species

In San Mateo County, structural framing is vulnerable to three distinct termite species:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Subterranean termites nest several feet underground in moist soil. Because they desiccate quickly in open air, workers build narrow earthen mud shelter tubes composed of clay, saliva, and masticated cellulose across concrete stem walls, foundation footings, and crawlspace piers. They feed along the softer springwood grain of structural timber, completely hollowing out mudsills, floor joists, and wall studs while leaving the exterior surface paint or wood shell intact.
- **Western Drywood Termites (*Incisitermes minor*):** Unlike subterranean species, drywood termites do not require soil contact. During warm late-summer and autumn afternoons, reproductive alates fly into attic louvers, exposed rafter tails, and roof eaves. They burrow directly into sound framing lumber, carving extensive galleries and pushing diagnostic six-sided fecal pellets (frass) through microscopic kick-out holes.
- **Pacific Dampwood Termites (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*):** The largest termite species in North America, dampwood termites thrive in high-moisture environments. In Redwood City’s wooded hillside pockets and coastal areas, dampwood termites establish colonies in structural lumber that has absorbed persistent moisture from roof leaks, unvented crawl spaces, or exterior earth-to-wood contact.
- **Hidden Framing Consumption:** Because mid-century and vintage Peninsula residences feature closed wall finishes, insulation, and hardwood flooring, active infestations can advance for years before hollow sounds, sagging floors, or trim separation alert homeowners to catastrophic framing loss.

### Redwood City Architectural and Environmental Accelerators

Several specific architectural elements and environmental characteristics in Redwood City significantly elevate structural pest risk:

- **Mid-Century Post-and-Beam and Eichler-Style Architecture:** A significant number of homes in Mount Carmel, Roosevelt, and surrounding Peninsula neighborhoods feature mid-century post-and-beam construction. These homes often utilize low-clearance crawl spaces or concrete slab foundations with radiant heating pipes. Gaps around subterranean plumbing sleeves and foundation cold joints offer subterranean termites direct access to structural support posts and tongue-and-groove roof decking.
- **Redwood Shores Marine Silt and Engineered Fill:** Properties in Redwood Shores are built over engineered fill and marine clay near San Francisco Bay lagoons. High groundwater tables, salt air moisture, and localized slab settlement create elevated humidity and hairline slab cracks, allowing subterranean termites to invade ground-level wall framing.
- **Emerald Hills Wooded Lots and Microclimates:** Hillside residences in Emerald Hills are shaded by dense coastal redwoods, Douglas firs, and oak trees. Deep leaf litter, natural runoff, and thick shade prevent soil around foundations from drying out, maintaining continuous moisture reservoirs that support massive subterranean termite colonies.
- **1920s Raised Foundation Crawl Spaces with Cripple Walls:** Classic bungalows in Mount Carmel and Edgewood Park feature raised concrete perimeter foundations with uninsulated, low-clearance crawlspaces. Vintage untreated mudsills and unreinforced wooden cripple walls often sit inches from damp soil, lacking modern metal termite shields or chemical barriers.

### Seasonal Swarming Cycles and Physical Red Flags

Homebuyers touring Redwood City properties should watch for physical signs of termite infestation:

- **Subterranean Spring Swarms:** In San Mateo County, subterranean termites typically swarm during warm, sunny mornings immediately following early spring rains between February and April.
- **Drywood Autumn Swarms:** Drywood termites swarm on warm, dry afternoons between September and November, frequently coinciding with offshore Bay Area warm spells.
- **Accumulations of Fecal Frass:** Tiny, hard, hexagonal pellets accumulating beneath attic rafters, window casings, or built-in cabinetry, varying from cream to dark reddish-brown.
- **Mud Tubes in Subareas:** Slender, dark-brown mud tubes climbing concrete stem walls, ascending brick fireplace bases, or bridging crawlspace floor piers.
- **Sagging Hardwood Floors and Spongy Trim:** Baseboards that crush under light thumb pressure or floorboards that bounce near exterior foundation walls.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Legal Obligations in Redwood City Escrows

Residential purchases in Redwood City are governed by California statutory disclosure laws designed to protect buyers from concealed defects.

### Statutory Disclosures Under California Civil Code § 1102

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of residential property must complete and deliver the standardized **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)** and the California Association of Realtors **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ)**:

- **Mandatory Inquiries Regarding Wood-Destroying Organisms:**
  - *TDS Section II.C.3:* Requires the seller to disclose known defects in "subflooring, foundation, or other structural components."
  - *SPQ Section V.A:* Requires the seller to disclose whether they are aware of "any pest or animal infestations, or damage to the Property from termites, dry rot, pests, or vermin."
  - *SPQ Section V.B:* Compels disclosure of "any past or current pest control treatments, pest control contracts, or warranties."
- **The Affirmative Disclosure Standard:** California law imposes an affirmative legal duty on sellers to disclose all known material facts affecting the property's value, desirability, or safety. A seller cannot downplay structural termite damage as minor cosmetic wear.
- **Statutory Right of Cancellation:** Under California Civil Code § 1102.3, if the TDS or SPQ is delivered after the buyer’s purchase offer has been accepted, the buyer possesses an unconditional statutory right to cancel the purchase contract within three days (if delivered in person) or five days (if delivered electronically).

### Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) Reports: Section 1 vs. Section 2

In San Mateo County escrows, certified termite reports are performed by licensed operators governed by the California Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB). Reports categorize findings into two distinct sections:

- **Section 1 Conditions:** Active infestations of wood-destroying pests (termites, beetles, or wood-decay fungus) or visible existing structural damage resulting from an infestation. Section 1 items require prompt chemical eradication, fumigation, or structural repair.
- **Section 2 Conditions:** Conditions deemed conducive to future infestations (e.g., faulty earth-to-wood clearance, subterranean drainage issues, buried stucco weep screeds, or plumbing leaks) where no active pest infestation is yet visible.
- **Legal Recourse for Fraudulent Concealment:** Under California common law (*Shapiro v. Sutherland*, *Lingsch v. Savage*), an "as-is" clause does not protect a seller or listing broker from liability for active concealment or intentional failure to disclose known material defects. If a seller patched hollowed sill plates with cosmetic wood filler and answered "No" on the SPQ, the buyer may pursue damages for repair costs, loss in property value, and attorney fees.

### Deciphering Deceptive Seller Phrases

Sellers and listing agents frequently employ understated terminology on disclosure addenda that obscures significant structural damage:

- **"Exterior dry rot repaired at roof eaves; preventative treatment in 2021":** Sellers frequently attribute wood damage to surface dry rot while omitting that the underlying cause was an active drywood or dampwood termite infestation that extended into interior structural roof rafters.
- **"Minor hairline slab crack patched near sliding glass door":** Concrete slab cracks adjacent to exterior patios frequently serve as primary entry points for subterranean termites accessing baseplates and wall framing.
- **"Inspector unable to access subarea framing due to low crawlspace clearance and insulation":** General home inspectors explicitly disclaim responsibility for concealed components. Unwary buyers assume the home was thoroughly vetted, when in reality the most vulnerable sill plates and floor joists were never inspected.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Redwood City, CA

### 1. Are termites common in Redwood City and the San Francisco Peninsula?
Yes. Redwood City and San Mateo County are designated as high-risk termite zones. Homes face ongoing threats from Western subterranean termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) living in moist clay soils, Western drywood termites (*Incisitermes minor*) infesting attic framing and exposed eaves, and Pacific dampwood termites (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*) attacking damp architectural timber.

### 2. Can termites attack mid-century post-and-beam homes in Redwood City?
Yes. Mid-century post-and-beam construction relies heavily on large wooden carrying girders and vertical posts resting on low-clearance crawl spaces or concrete piers. Subterranean termites construct mud tubes up concrete footings to enter these critical structural members, causing serious structural deflection.

### 3. What legal obligations do California home sellers have regarding termite disclosures?
Under California Civil Code § 1102, sellers must disclose all known past and present termite infestations, pest control treatments, and structural defects on the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ). Intentionally concealing termite damage or providing misleading statements exposes sellers to legal liability for repair damages and attorney fees under *Shapiro v. Sutherland*.

### 4. Does a standard Redwood City home inspection include a certified termite report?
No. Standard home inspections evaluate general mechanical, electrical, and cosmetic components, but standard inspection agreements explicitly exclude certified wood-destroying organism (WDO) evaluations. Homebuyers in Redwood City should always hire a licensed Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) operator to conduct a dedicated WDO inspection prior to removing contingencies.
