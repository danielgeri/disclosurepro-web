## The Hidden Threat: Termites in Tampa and Hillsborough County

Tampa Bay sits at the intersection of several wood-destroying pest habitats. The region is home to subterranean termites, drywood termites, and aggressive invasive species.

### Why Tampa's Climate and Housing Stock Foster Severe Infestations

Several specific factors contribute to Tampa's high termite vulnerability:
- **Subtropical Coastal Climate:** Tampa experiences high humidity and frequent summer thunderstorms. This sustained moisture keeps structural wood framing at elevated moisture levels that attract wood-destroying insects.
- **Historic Bungalows in Seminole Heights, Tampa Heights, and Hyde Park:** Built in the 1910s through 1930s, these iconic Craftsman bungalows and Victorian homes feature wood framing resting on brick pier-and-beam crawl spaces. Inadequate ground clearances and humid crawl spaces provide subterranean termites with easy access to structural sill plates and floor joists.
- **Concrete Block Construction in South Tampa and Davis Islands:** While exterior walls may be constructed of concrete block (cinder block), interior partition walls, furring strips, second-story additions, and roof trusses are framed with wood. Subterranean termites travel through hollow block cells to reach roof framing undetected.

Warm spring weather triggers massive swarms of reproductive alates, which disperse across residential neighborhoods to establish new colonies.

### The Biology and Mechanics of Tampa Bay's Wood-Destroying Termites

Tampa homebuyers face threats from multiple destructive termite species:
1. **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Native ground-dwelling termites that build earthen mud shelter tubes across concrete stem walls and brick piers to reach structural wood.
2. **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Highly invasive "super-termites" capable of building massive colonies numbering in the millions. Formosans can construct aerial **carton nests** inside wall cavities and attics, thriving without soil contact if a roof or plumbing leak is present.
3. **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** Another destructive invasive species active in South Florida and Tampa Bay, capable of cross-breeding with Formosans and establishing large colonies.
4. **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Inhabit dry structural wood, roof rafters, and door frames without requiring soil moisture, pushing distinctive sand-like fecal pellets (frass) through exit holes.

Because Tampa homes can be attacked by both subterranean and drywood species simultaneously, a comprehensive property evaluation must examine foundations, crawl spaces, and attic framing.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Tampa Buyers Must Understand

Florida real estate disclosure law is rooted in the landmark Florida Supreme Court decision **_Johnson v. Davis_ (480 So. 2d 625)**. Under this doctrine, where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under a duty to disclose them.

### Key Florida Disclosure Documents

When purchasing a home in Tampa, you will review:
- **Florida Realtors / Florida Bar (FR/BAR) Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR):** A comprehensive disclosure form where sellers must disclose known material defects, including specific questions regarding *"infestation or damage by wood-destroying organisms (termites, powder post beetles, wood rot),"* past treatments, structural repairs, and existing warranty contracts.
- **FDACS Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) Inspection Report (Form 13645):** Mandated by Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services under Chapter 482, Florida Statutes. Completed by a certified pest control inspector, this report details:
  - Live wood-destroying organisms observed.
  - Evidence of past infestations (mud tubes, frass, chemical drill holes).
  - Observed structural damage caused by WDOs.
  - Inaccessible areas not inspected.
- **Transferable Termite Bond Documentation:** Detailing whether the home is protected by an active "repair and retreat" or "retreat-only" warranty contract.

### What Tampa Sellers Disclose vs. What They Conceal

Despite the *Johnson v. Davis* standard, sellers often use vague phrasing to downplay past infestations:
- *"Annual pest control contract maintained; no active issues to seller's knowledge"* (failing to disclose that the contract was renewed specifically because subterranean termites were previously treated in the crawl space).
- *"Repaired minor cosmetic wood rot along front porch framing"* (masking extensive structural hollowing of load-bearing sills).
- *"Property sold in As-Is condition"* (in Florida, an As-Is clause does NOT relieve a seller of their statutory duty to disclose known latent material defects under *Johnson v. Davis*).

If a seller conceals known termite damage, they can be held liable for damages, rescission of the contract, and attorney's fees.

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Red Flags in Massive Packets

In a typical Tampa real estate transaction, disclosure binders frequently exceed 250 pages, combining:
- The FR/BAR Seller's Property Disclosure
- Multi-page general home inspection reports with extensive photo logs
- FDACS Form 13645 WDO inspection reports
- Flood elevation certificates and 4-Point Insurance Inspection reports
- Title commitments and HOA/CDD governance documents

### How Critical Warnings Slip Through the Cracks

Crucial details regarding termite activity are often buried in technical appendices:
- An inspector might note on the FDACS WDO report: *"Attic eaves obstructed by blown-in cellulose insulation; flat roof overhangs inaccessible for visual inspection."* Overlooking this comment leaves you unaware of potential drywood colonies in the roof rafters.
- A 4-Point Inspection report prepared for homeowners insurance might mention *"evidence of historic WDO damage to floor joists in sub-area,"* directly contradicting a seller's clean disclosure assertion.

Manually cross-referencing hundreds of pages under tight contingency deadlines creates significant risk of missing crucial warnings.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Tampa

**1. Is a WDO inspection mandatory when buying a home in Tampa?**
While Florida law does not legally require a WDO inspection for cash transactions, nearly all conventional, FHA, and VA mortgage lenders require a clear FDACS Form 13645 WDO report before approving financing. A professional WDO inspection is an indispensable due diligence step.

**2. What is the difference between a "repair and retreat" and "retreat-only" termite bond in Florida?**
A "repair and retreat" bond requires the pest control company to pay for both chemical eradication and structural repairs if termites cause new damage while the bond is active. A "retreat-only" bond only covers the cost of applying chemicals to kill the insects, leaving all structural repair costs to the homeowner.

**3. Does Florida's *Johnson v. Davis* rule apply to "As-Is" real estate contracts?**
Yes. The Florida Supreme Court has firmly established that an "As-Is" contract provision does not waive or override a seller's duty to disclose known latent defects that materially affect the value of the property.

**4. How can I tell if a Tampa home has Formosan subterranean termites?**
Formosan termites construct large, spongy aerial carton nests inside walls or attics, produce larger mud tubes than native subterranean termites, and swarm in massive numbers around exterior lights on warm, humid evenings between May and July.
