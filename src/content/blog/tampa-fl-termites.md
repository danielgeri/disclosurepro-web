## Why Tampa & Hillsborough County Homes Face Dual Termite Pressure

Unlike northern metros threatened by a single soil-dwelling termite species, Tampa Bay properties can be attacked from **both below the foundation and inside the attic rafters simultaneously**. Subtropical humidity, sandy coastal soils, and seasonal afternoon downpours keep structural lumber above the moisture thresholds that attract multiple destructive species.

### Four Termite Species Threatening Tampa Real Estate

1. **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Native ground-dwelling colonies that build pencil-width earthen mud shelter tubes across masonry piers, concrete block stem walls, and expansion joints to reach wood sill plates and subfloors.
2. **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Aggressive invasive "super-termites" capable of forming colonies in the millions. If a roof leak, AC condensate line, or shower pan leak supplies moisture, Formosan termites can construct spongy **aerial carton nests** inside wall cavities or attics without continuous ground contact.
3. **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** An invasive tropical species expanding north through Florida coastal counties that rivals Formosan colonies in structural destruction speed and swarms heavily on humid spring evenings.
4. **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Colonize dry roof trusses, tongue-and-groove ceilings, fascia boards, and window frames with zero soil contact. Buyers spot them by piles of uniform, six-sided sand-like fecal pellets (**frass**) kicked out of pin-sized exit holes.

## Florida Disclosure Law: *Johnson v. Davis*, FR/BAR SPDR & FDACS-13645

Florida residential disclosure rules are governed by the landmark Florida Supreme Court decision **_Johnson v. Davis_ (480 So. 2d 625)**, which requires any residential seller who knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property—facts not readily observable and not known to the buyer—to disclose them affirmatively. Crucially, **signing an FR/BAR "As-Is" Contract does NOT waive *Johnson v. Davis***; sellers still cannot conceal known termite activity, prior structural rot, or failed treatments.

For a deep dive into every Florida statutory disclosure trap—including roof insurance cutoffs and condo reserve rules—read our [Florida Seller Property Disclosure and *Johnson v. Davis* guide](/blog/florida-johnson-v-davis-sellers-disclosure-guide/).

### Key Documents Every Tampa Buyer Must Audit Before Contingencies Expire

- **FR/BAR Seller's Property Disclosure – Residential (SPDR):** Check the **Termites; Other Wood-Destroying Organisms; Pests** section carefully. Sellers must answer whether they know of any current or past infestation or damage by WDOs (termites, powderpost beetles, wood-decaying fungi), whether the home has been treated, and whether an active transferable termite bond exists.
- **FDACS Form 13645 (Wood-Destroying Organisms Inspection Report):** Regulated under **Chapter 482, Florida Statutes**, and completed only by a Florida-licensed pest control inspector. Unlike a general home inspection, FDACS-13645 documents:
  - **Live WDOs observed** (subterranean termites, drywood termites, wood-boring beetles, or active wood-decaying fungi).
  - **Evidence of previous infestation or damage** (mud tubes, drywood frass, damaged lumber, prior drill holes).
  - **Obstructed and Inaccessible Areas:** Every section blocked by blown-in insulation, finished drywall, tiled bath traps, or stored clutter.
  - **Mandatory Notice of Inspection Sticker:** Florida law requires the inspector to affix a dated notice sticker (typically inside the electrical panel, under the kitchen sink, or inside the attic access hatch).
- **Termite Bond Contract ("Repair & Retreat" vs. "Retreat-Only"):** A "preventative warranty" mentioned casually on a disclosure is often a **retreat-only bond** that pays $0 toward structural lumber reconstruction. Always verify exact contract language using our [buying a house with a history of termites disclosure guide](/blog/buying-house-with-termite-history-disclosure-guide/).

### Evasive Disclosure Language Frequently Seen in Tampa Packets

- *"Annual pest service maintained; no active issues to seller's knowledge."* Often masks that the service plan started after a severe crawlspace or attic infestation.
- *"Minor exterior wood rot repaired on front porch and fascia."* Frequently conceals deeper drywood frass or subterranean damage extending into load-bearing headers.
- *"No known termite damage; sold As-Is."* Does not protect a seller who received an undisclosed WDO report or contractor quote within the last several years.

## How Cross-PDF Contradictions Hide Inside Tampa Closing Packets

In a typical Hillsborough County closing, buyers receive **200 to 300+ pages** during a tight 10-to-15-day inspection window: the FR/BAR SPDR, an 80-page general home inspection, a Florida **4-Point Insurance Inspection**, a Wind Mitigation report, an FDACS-13645 WDO report, and HOA or CDD disclosures. The costliest defects almost always sit at the intersection of two separate PDFs:

- **FDACS-13645 WDO Report checks "No Visible Live WDOs,"** while small print under *Inaccessible Areas* notes *"Attic trusses above flat roof addition and center crawlspace piers obstructed by low clearance and ductwork."*
- **Florida 4-Point Insurance Inspection (Page 14)** flags *"elevated moisture staining under master shower subfloor and patched wood framing near pier #4,"* contradicting the seller's clean SPDR checkbox.
- **Prior Pest Control Invoice** shows a 2022 exterior perimeter liquid treatment around the foundation wall—**with zero trenching or drilling around interior crawlspace masonry piers**—leaving an active subterranean colony isolated under the center of the house.

### Real-World Cost Scenario: A 1925 Seminole Heights Bungalow

Consider a typical **$545,000** restored 1925 Craftsman bungalow in Seminole Heights. The seller checks "No" to active termites on the FR/BAR SPDR and notes: *"Preventative subterranean perimeter treatment performed in 2022; active bond with local pest company."* Because the buyer's WDO inspector could not inspect behind low ductwork near the center masonry pier—and the buyer never audited the original 2022 treatment diagram—subterranean termites remained active inside the untreated center pier. Nine months after closing, bathroom remodeling exposed 18 linear feet of hollowed heart-pine sill beam, four compromised floor joists, and damaged stud bases. Because the bond was **retreat-only**, homeowners insurance denied the claim, leaving the buyer facing **$34,700** out of pocket.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termite Disclosures & Inspections in Tampa, FL

### Is a WDO termite inspection required when buying a house in Tampa?
Florida statutes do not mandate a WDO inspection for cash purchases, but VA lenders, many FHA underwriters, and prudent conventional buyers require an official **FDACS Form 13645 WDO report** before closing. Given Tampa's simultaneous subterranean, Formosan, and drywood pressure, skipping a $85–$175 WDO inspection is one of the riskiest shortcuts a buyer can make.

### What is the difference between a "repair and retreat" and "retreat-only" termite bond in Florida?
A **repair-and-retreat bond** obligates the pest control company to pay for both chemical eradication and qualifying structural wood repairs if termites cause new damage while the bond is active. A **retreat-only bond** covers only chemical re-application, leaving the homeowner to pay 100% of structural framing, shoring, and drywall repairs out of pocket.

### Does Florida's *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure rule apply to "As-Is" real estate contracts?
Yes. Under the Florida Supreme Court's ruling in **_Johnson v. Davis_**, an "As-Is" clause waives a seller's obligation to make contract repairs, but it **never** excuses a seller from disclosing known latent material defects—including past or active termite infestations and structural wood damage.

### Can termites damage a concrete block (CBS) home in Tampa?
Yes. While termites do not eat concrete block, subterranean and Formosan termites travel vertically inside hollow block cores and behind exterior stucco to reach wood roof trusses, interior partition walls, furring strips, door frames, and second-story wood framing—while drywood termites infest roof rafters directly through attic vents.
