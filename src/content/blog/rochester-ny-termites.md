## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Rochester and the Genesee Valley

Many Upstate New York buyers believe that Rochester’s cold winters protect homes from insect damage. In reality, Eastern subterranean termites thrive throughout the Genesee River basin, retreating deep underground during winter freezes and emerging to feed on damp residential framing in spring and summer.

### Why Rochester's Lakeside Climate and Historic Architecture Accelerate Infestations

Several geographic, environmental, and structural factors make residential properties in Rochester particularly vulnerable to subterranean termite attacks:

- **Aggressive Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** The primary wood-destroying insect in Monroe County is the Eastern Subterranean Termite. Living in large subterranean colonies beneath the frost line, worker termites forage continuously through damp soils in search of structural wood cellulose.
- **Lake Ontario Moisture and Saturated Soils:** Rochester receives significant annual precipitation and lake-effect snow. High groundwater tables and persistent soil moisture surrounding residential foundations create an ideal foraging habitat for subterranean colonies.
- **Historic Medina Sandstone and Fieldstone Foundations:** Homes built between 1880 and 1930 in neighborhoods like Park Avenue, South Wedge, and Corn Hill frequently feature Medina sandstone, rubble stone, or brick foundations. Over a century of freeze-thaw cycles, lime-based mortar deteriorates, creating subterranean cavities that termites use to enter wooden sills undetected.
- **Late-19th-Century Balloon Framing:** Many historic Rochester homes were constructed using balloon framing, where exterior wall studs run continuously from the foundation sill to the roof rafters without firestops. Once subterranean termites breach the foundation sill, they travel unimpeded up internal wall cavities behind horsehair plaster.
- **Damp Cellars and Unfinished Crawlspaces:** Older homes in the 19th Ward and historic districts often feature unsealed stone basements or dirt-floor crawlspaces beneath rear porches and kitchen bump-outs. Relative humidity levels exceeding 75% create ideal microclimates for both wood-decay fungi and subterranean termites.

### Biology and Mechanics: How Subterranean Termites Destroy Rochester Homes

Subterranean termites require continuous moisture to survive. To travel across stone foundation walls, concrete footings, or cast-iron drainpipes without desiccating, worker termites build protective earthen shelter tubes (mud tubes) made of soil, saliva, and digested cellulose.

Inside the framing, worker termites feed parallel to the soft springwood grain of structural fir, pine, and hemlock framing, leaving the harder summerwood rings and exterior paint intact. A structural sill plate, floor joist, or load-bearing wall stud can be hollowed out while appearing completely solid on the surface. Homebuyers often only discover the infestation when hardwood floors begin to slope, door frames warp, or winged swarmers emerge inside living rooms during warm spring days.

## New York Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Rochester Buyers Must Know

The legal framework governing residential property sales in New York State has undergone historic changes that significantly increase seller disclosure obligations.

### The New York Property Condition Disclosure Act (PCDA)

Historically, New York was a strict *caveat emptor* ("buyer beware") state where sellers routinely opted out of providing a disclosure form by granting a nominal $500 credit to the buyer at closing under New York Real Property Law § 465.

However, landmark legislative amendments eliminated the $500 opt-out credit. **Sellers of residential real property in New York are now legally mandated to deliver a completed 48-question Property Condition Disclosure Statement (PCDS) to the buyer prior to contract execution.**

On the mandatory New York PCDS:
- **Question 34 specifically asks:** *"Are there any past or present insect, rodent, or other pest infestations or conditions on the property?"*
- **Question 35 asks:** *"Has any insect, rodent, or other pest control work been done on the property? If yes, explain."*
- **Question 36 asks:** *"Are there any structural defects or rot in the foundation, framing, floors, or roof?"*

Under Article 14 of the NY Real Property Law, a seller who willfully fails to disclose known material defects or provides false representations can be held liable in New York courts for actual damages sustained by the buyer.

### The Official NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Inspection Report

In Upstate New York, mortgage lenders (including conventional conforming loans, FHA, and VA programs) require an independent **NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Inspection Report** completed by a licensed New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) certified commercial pesticide applicator.

The NPMA-33 report documents:
- **Section II (Findings):** Evidence of active infestations, previous infestations, or insect-related structural wood damage.
- **Section III (Conducive Conditions):** Structural vulnerabilities that promote termite colonies, such as earth-to-wood contact at basement staircases, high soil grade against siding, or unvented crawlspaces.
- **Section IV (Inaccessible Areas):** Critical caveats detailing areas that could not be visually inspected, such as finished basement walls, drywalled ceilings, or insulated rim joist pockets.

### What Rochester Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Even with mandatory PCDS disclosures, sellers frequently use vague or minimizing language on their forms:
- *"Basement perimeter treated preventatively in 2021."* (Concealing that an exterminator treated active mud tubes along the rear sandstone wall).
- *"Previous minor wood rot repaired near rear porch entry."* (Failing to disclose that termites had tunneled into the main structural band joist).
- *"Seller is an estate and has not occupied the property."* (Using non-occupancy to disclaim knowledge of known past pest issues).

### Technical Euphemisms That Conceal Severe Damage

Inspectors often use cautious technical language that downplays serious structural risks:
- An inspector might write: *"Probed softening and minor insect tracking observed along east foundation sill plate."* A buyer might assume this is cosmetic aging, unaware that "probed softening" indicates hollowed structural wood caused by subterranean termites.
- A pest inspection report might note: *"Finished drywall in basement recreation room precluded visual inspection of 85% of foundation mudsills."* This disclaimer shields the inspector while leaving the buyer fully responsible for hidden damage.

Manually cross-referencing hundreds of pages of technical reports against seller statements under tight contractual deadlines creates significant financial vulnerabilities.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Rochester, NY

**1. Are sellers in Rochester legally required to disclose past termite infestations?**
Yes. Under the amended New York Property Condition Disclosure Act (PCDA), sellers can no longer opt out with a $500 credit. They must answer specific questions regarding past and present pest infestations, chemical treatments, and structural wood rot on the mandatory 48-question PCDS form.

**2. Can subterranean termites survive Rochester's cold winters?**
Yes. Eastern subterranean termites survive freezing Upstate New York winters by retreating deep into the soil beneath the regional frost line. When soil warms in the spring, they tunnel upward into residential framing.

**3. Why are historic homes in Park Avenue and South Wedge vulnerable to termites?**
Historic homes in Rochester often feature Medina sandstone or fieldstone foundations with vintage lime mortar. Over decades of freeze-thaw cycles, mortar voids develop, allowing subterranean termites to travel through internal masonry channels directly into wooden mudsills and balloon-framed walls unseen.

**4. What is the difference between a general home inspection and a WDI inspection in New York?**
A general home inspection evaluates the overall condition of a home's primary systems (roof, plumbing, heating, electrical). A Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) inspection is a specialized evaluation performed by a DEC-licensed exterminator using the official NPMA-33 form to identify active infestations, previous damage, and conducive moisture conditions.
