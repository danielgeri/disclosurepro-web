## The Hidden Hazard: Formosan and Subterranean Termites in Tallahassee

Tallahassee is located in the rolling Red Hills region of North Florida. Our climate features long, humid summers, mild winters, high humidity, and annual rainfall exceeding 59 inches. Dense canopies of live oaks, pines, and magnolias combined with red clay soils create optimal environmental conditions for wood-destroying insects.

### Why Tallahassee Housing Stock and Geology Elevate Termite Risk

Several distinct regional and structural factors contribute to extreme termite vulnerability across Tallahassee:

- **Red Clay Soils and Heavy Canopy Shading:** Leon County’s dense red clay soils retain high moisture levels. Heavy tree canopies prevent sunlight from drying foundation soils, creating continuous subterranean moisture reserves where termite colonies thrive.
- **Formosan "Super Termite" Populations:** Tallahassee has established populations of Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*), which spread rapidly through North Florida. Formosan colonies contain millions of foragers and consume structural framing lumber at rapid rates.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities in Historic and Mid-Century Neighborhoods:**
  - *Midtown, Los Robles, and Old Town:* 1920s to 1940s Craftsman bungalows, Colonial Revival homes, and Spanish cottages built on raised brick piers. Heavy crawl space humidity, heart pine framing, and low ground clearance create direct entryways for subterranean termites through masonry foundation piers.
  - *Betton Hills and Waverly Hills:* 1950s through 1970s mid-century ranch homes nestled among massive oak trees. Expansive oak tree root systems penetrate beneath concrete slabs, creating root-void entry channels through which termites access interior wall framing.
  - *Killearn Estates and SouthWood:* 1970s to modern master-planned communities built on slab-on-grade foundations. Heavy landscape irrigation systems and mulch beds installed against exterior stucco or fiber-cement siding allow subterranean termites to enter behind wall cladding undetected.

### Pest Biology: Formosan vs. Subterranean and Drywood Termites

Homebuyers in Tallahassee must contend with three distinct wood-destroying species:

1. **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Aggressive insects that construct secondary aerial nests ("cartons") inside wall cavities, allowing them to survive without returning to the ground for moisture.
2. **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Forages through soil and builds mud shelter tubes over foundation walls to access structural sills and floor joists.
3. **Southeastern Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes snyderi*):** Infests dry structural framing and roof rafters directly without requiring soil contact, producing distinctive sand-like fecal pellets (frass).

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Johnson v. Davis and Seller Obligations

Florida real estate transactions are governed by strong legal disclosure precedents that favor buyer protection.

### The Landmark Doctrine of Johnson v. Davis (1985)

Under the Florida Supreme Court landmark ruling in **Johnson v. Davis (480 So. 2d 625)**:
- Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them to the buyer.
- This duty applies to all residential transactions in Florida and cannot be waived by an "as-is" contract clause (*Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.*).

### The Florida Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)

In standard Tallahassee transactions, sellers complete the **Florida Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)** form:
- **Section 10 (Wood-Destroying Organisms):** Explicitly asks:
  - *"Are you aware of any active or past termite or other wood-destroying organism infestations or damage?"*
  - *"Has the property been treated for termites or other wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Is the property currently covered by a transferable termite warranty, bond, or maintenance agreement?"*
  - Requires the seller to identify the pest control company, treatment dates, bond type, and attach copies of all renewal contracts.

### Official Florida FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Report

In Florida, pest inspections must be performed by an applicator licensed by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)**. The official **FDACS Form 13645** details:
- Evidence of active or past subterranean termites, Formosan termites, drywood termites, powderpost beetles, or wood-decay fungi.
- Visible structural timber damage, previous drill holes in concrete slabs, or bait stations.
- Obstructions and inaccessible areas in crawl spaces, attics, or finished walls.

### What Tallahassee Sellers Disclose vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers frequently use ambiguous language on Section 10 of the SPDR:
- *"Active termite bond in place with local pest company since 2019."* (Conceals that the bond is a retreat-only contract because structural damage disqualified the property from a retreat-and-repair warranty).
- *"Minor wood rot around exterior eaves repaired in 2022."* (Masks drywood termite tunneling in structural roof trusses).
- *"Property sold As-Is with right to inspect."* Under Florida law (*Johnson v. Davis*), selling "as-is" relieves the seller from making repairs, but does **not** exempt them from liability for failure to disclose known material latent defects.

## The Manual Review Problem: Sifting Through Florida Real Estate Packets

During the standard 10-to-15-day inspection period under a FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase, Tallahassee buyers receive large document sets:
- FAR/BAR "AS IS" Residential Contract for Sale and Purchase
- Florida Realtors Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)
- 40-to-70-page licensed home inspection report with infrared thermography
- Official FDACS-13645 Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Inspection Report
- Leon County property appraiser records, canopy road preservation rules, and title commitments

### The Danger of Disconnected Document Clues

Critical signs of severe termite activity are often split across separate documents:
- The home inspector notes on page 38: *"Crown molding separation and door frame racking in master suite."*
- The pest inspector notes on FDACS-13645: *"Insulation along crawl space perimeter prevents visual examination of sill plates."*
- The seller writes on the SPDR: *"Annual termite inspection completed February 2023."*

Without connecting these observations, the buyer will not realize that the crown molding separation is caused by Formosan termites hollowing out the load-bearing wall studs behind the master bedroom drywall.

During escrow, Robert and Jennifer hired an FDACS-licensed pest inspector. The inspector provided an FDACS-13645 report stating no active swarmers were seen in open areas, noting that crawl space clearance beneath the rear family room was under 16 inches and inaccessible. Reassured by the seller’s active "termite bond," Robert and Jennifer completed the purchase.

The seller's "termite bond" was actually a retreat-only contract issued after swarmers emerged in 2021, and the seller had covered the compromised wall framing with new paneling before listing.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Tallahassee

### 1. Are Formosan termites found in Tallahassee, Florida?
Yes. Formosan Subterranean Termites are well-established throughout Leon County and the Big Bend region, alongside native Eastern Subterranean and Drywood Termites. Formosan colonies are massive and cause rapid structural damage.

### 2. What does Florida law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under the landmark Florida Supreme Court ruling in *Johnson v. Davis*, sellers must disclose all known material facts affecting property value that are not readily observable to the buyer, including active infestations, past damage, and chemical treatments.

### 3. What is an FDACS-13645 report in Florida?
The FDACS-13645 is the official Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) inspection report issued by Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services licensed pest control operators. It details visible evidence of termites, beetles, and wood-decaying fungi.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Florida?
No. Standard Florida homeowners insurance policies exclude termite and insect damage from coverage, treating wood-destroying insect prevention as an uninsurable homeowner maintenance responsibility.
