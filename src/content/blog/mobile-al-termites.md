## The Hidden Hazard: Formosan and Subterranean Termites in Mobile and the Gulf Coast

Mobile is one of the wettest cities in the continental United States, receiving over 65 inches of rainfall annually. This environment creates ideal breeding conditions for wood-destroying organisms.

### Why Mobile Housing Stock and Geology Elevate Termite Risk

Several distinct regional and structural factors contribute to extreme termite vulnerability across Mobile:

- **Heavy Rainfall and High Water Tables:** Frequent subtropical downpours and low-lying coastal elevations maintain continuous moisture in sandy-clay topsoils. Subterranean termites require constant moisture, and saturated crawl spaces provide endless hydration for massive colonies.
- **Mobile as a Historic Formosan Termite Epicenter:** Mobile was one of the earliest United States ports infested by Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*), arriving via military cargo ships following World War II. Formosan termites have colonized the entire Mobile metropolitan area. Their colonies contain millions of insects and consume structural timber at rapid rates.

  - *Oakleigh Garden District, Church Street East, and De Tonti Square:* 19th-century Victorian, Greek Revival, and Creole cottages built on raised brick piers. High humidity in shallow crawl spaces, heart pine floor framing, and wood-to-ground contact at porch steps create direct entryways for subterranean and Formosan termites.
  - *Midtown and Ashland Place:* 1920s to 1940s Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Revival homes with partial crawl spaces or slab additions where moisture accumulation behind stucco or brick veneer attracts foraging colonies.
  - *Spring Hill and Llanfair:* 1950s through 1980s ranch and estate homes built on concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Expansive live oak tree root systems penetrate beneath slabs, creating root-void entry channels through which termites access interior wall framing.

### Pest Biology: Formosan vs. Subterranean and Drywood Termites

Homebuyers in Mobile must contend with three distinct wood-destroying species:

1. **Formosan Subterranean Termite (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** The most destructive termite in North America. Formosans build aerial carton nests inside walls, allowing them to thrive above ground without returning to the soil for moisture. They hollow out structural timbers rapidly.
2. **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** Forages through soil and builds mud shelter tubes over foundation piers to access structural sills and floor joists.
3. **Southeastern Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes snyderi*):** Infests dry structural framing and roof rafters directly without requiring soil contact, producing distinctive sand-like fecal pellets (frass).

## Alabama Real Estate Disclosure Laws: The Strict Doctrine of Caveat Emptor

Alabama real estate transactions are governed by common law principles that place heavy responsibility on the buyer.

### The Alabama Doctrine of Caveat Emptor

Under Alabama law (*Nenon v. Brewer* and *Ray v. Montgomery*), the doctrine of **caveat emptor** ("let the buyer beware") strictly applies to used residential home sales:
- In the absence of a fiduciary relationship, a seller has no general affirmative duty to disclose defects in the property.
- **Exceptions to Caveat Emptor:** A seller is legally liable only if:
  1. The buyer makes a specific direct inquiry and the seller provides a fraudulent misrepresentation;
  2. The seller actively conceals a defect through affirmative action (e.g., concealing damaged timbers with fresh drywall or paneling to prevent discovery); or
  3. The seller has knowledge of a defect affecting health or safety that is not discoverable upon reasonable inspection (*Fennell Realty Co. v. Martin*).

### Standard Alabama Association of Realtors Disclosures & Termite Bonds

While Alabama law does not mandate a statutory disclosure form, the **Alabama Association of Realtors (AAR)** provides standard Seller's Property Condition Disclosure statements used in most Mobile transactions. When completed, sellers must answer truthfully regarding:
- Active or past termite infestations and wood rot.
- Past structural repairs and pest control treatments.
- The existence of an active **Termite Bond** (Official Wood Infestation Inspection Report / WDIR).

### Official Alabama Wood Infestation Inspection Report (WDIR)

In Alabama, pest inspections are regulated by the **Alabama Department of Agriculture and Industries (ADAI)**. An official WDIR (Form DGI-1) details:
- Evidence of active or previous subterranean termites, Formosan termites, drywood termites, powderpost beetles, or wood-decay fungi.
- Areas of previous chemical treatment, bait stations, or visible structural wood damage.
- Obstructions and inaccessible areas in crawl spaces, attics, or finished walls.

### What Mobile Sellers Disclose vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers frequently use evasive language regarding pest history:
- *"Active transferable termite bond in place with local pest company."* (Conceals that the bond is a retreat-only contract because structural damage disqualified the property from a retreat-and-repair warranty).
- *"Previous minor dampness in crawl space addressed."* (Masks extensive wood-decay fungus and Formosan termite tunneling in floor framing).
- *"Property sold As-Is, Where-Is."* Under Alabama law, selling "as-is" reinforces caveat emptor, making it nearly impossible for a buyer to recover damages unless intentional active fraud can be proven.

## The Manual Review Problem: Deciphering Mobile Real Estate Documentation

During the standard 10-to-14-day inspection period under an AAR purchase contract, Mobile buyers receive large transaction packages:
- AAR Residential Purchase Agreement with inspection addenda
- Seller's Property Condition Disclosure statement
- 40-to-70-page licensed home inspection report with thermal imaging
- Official ADAI Wood Infestation Inspection Report (WDIR) and termite bond contract
- Mobile County probate records, historic district guidelines, and title commitments

### The Danger of Disconnected Document Clues

Critical signs of severe termite activity are often split across separate documents:
- The home inspector notes on page 38: *"Minor settlement cracks and door binding in master bedroom wing."*
- The pest inspector notes in Section V: *"Spray foam insulation on crawl space subfloor prevents visual examination of floor framing."*
- The termite bond document indicates: *"Bond classification: Re-treatment Only; Repair Coverage Excluded."*

Without connecting these observations, the buyer will not realize that the repair exclusion on the termite bond was triggered by past Formosan termite damage hidden beneath the crawl space spray foam.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Mobile

### 1. Are Formosan termites common in Mobile, Alabama?
Yes. Mobile is an epicenter for Formosan Subterranean Termites along the Gulf Coast. Formosan colonies are massive and can cause severe structural framing damage in a matter of months.

### 2. Is a seller required to disclose termite damage under Alabama law?
Alabama is a strict *caveat emptor* state. Sellers have no general affirmative duty to disclose defects unless directly asked, there is a fiduciary relationship, or health/safety hazards exist. However, if a seller completes a disclosure form, they cannot make fraudulent misrepresentations or actively conceal defects.

### 3. What is the difference between a retreat-only bond and a repair bond in Alabama?
A retreat-only bond covers only the application of chemical termiticide if termites return. A retreat-and-repair bond covers both the chemical treatment and the structural repair costs of new termite damage up to a specified policy limit.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Alabama?
No. Standard Alabama homeowners insurance policies exclude termite and insect damage from coverage, treating wood-destroying insect prevention as an uninsurable homeowner maintenance responsibility.
