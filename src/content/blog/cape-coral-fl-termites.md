## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean "Super Termites" in Cape Coral and Lee County

Cape Coral is situated on a low-lying peninsula surrounded by the Caloosahatchee River and Matlacha Pass. Our local ecosystem is defined by a high water table, extensive brackish and freshwater canal networks, intense summer heat, and annual precipitation exceeding 54 inches during the subtropical rainy season. These conditions create an ideal breeding ground for invasive wood-destroying organisms.

### Why Canal-Front Architecture and Tropical Climate Accelerate Termite Destruction

Several unique regional building methods, aquatic geography, and climate factors contribute to extreme termite vulnerability across Cape Coral:

- **Canal Proximity and Saturated Soil Tables:** Cape Coral’s extensive canal system maintains high soil moisture levels directly beneath residential foundation slabs. High subterranean moisture allows termite colonies to forage continuously without the risk of drying out, even during hot spring dry spells.
- **CBS Construction with Concealed Wood Trusses and Furring Strips:** Standard Cape Coral residential construction utilizes concrete block stucco (CBS) exterior walls. However, wood 1x2 and 2x2 furring strips are attached to the interior block faces to hang drywall, and complex gang-nail Douglas fir or Southern yellow pine roof truss systems bear directly onto the top concrete tie beam. Subterranean termites climb interior block cells undetected to consume furring strips and structural roof trusses.
- **Aerial Carton Nests:** Invasive subterranean species in Southwest Florida can build aerial nests (known as "carton") inside wall cavities, soffits, and attic spaces using a mixture of chewed wood pulp, saliva, and soil. Once established, these colonies no longer need to return to the ground for moisture, allowing them to survive independently and bypass conventional ground soil barrier treatments.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Cape Coral Neighborhoods:**
  - *Pelican and Yacht Club:* 1960s to 1980s ranch-style waterfront homes with older plumbing knockouts, mature ficus and palm trees whose roots penetrate foundation footings, and wood-framed sunrooms.
  - *Surfside and Southwest Cape / Cape Harbour:* 1990s to 2000s Mediterranean-style custom homes with multi-story wood-framed roof lines, decorative stucco mouldings, and unsealed pool cage tie-ins that channel rainwater against exterior stem walls.
  - *Coral Oaks and North Cape:* Single-family homes with large acreage and pine flatwoods surroundings where natural subterranean termite colonies migrate into residential structures.

### Pest Biology: Formosan (*Coptotermes formosanus*) and Asian Subterranean (*Coptotermes gestroi*)

Southwest Florida is one of the few regions in the United States where two of the world's most destructive invasive termite species overlap:
- **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Known as "super termites," a single mature Formosan colony can contain up to 10 million individuals. They consume structural wood at up to ten times the rate of native subterranean species and can cause catastrophic framing failure within months.
- **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** A tropical invasive species highly prevalent in South Florida that swarms earlier in the spring (February to April). They exhibit aggressive foraging behavior and readily form aerial carton nests in attics.
- **Cryptic Interior Wood Excavation:** Both species hollow out structural roof trusses, interior wall studs, and door headers from the inside out, leaving a fragile shell of painted drywall or veneer intact until severe deflection or structural collapse occurs.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: *Johnson v. Davis* and Statutory Mandates

Florida real estate disclosure law is anchored by one of the nation's most famous legal precedents governing property condition disclosures.

### The Landmark Precedent: *Johnson v. Davis* (1985)

Under the Florida Supreme Court’s landmark ruling in ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**:
- Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them to the buyer.
- This duty applies regardless of whether the buyer asks directly and cannot be waived by an "As-Is" contract clause (*Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.*, 1987).

### Florida Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)

In Florida transactions utilizing the standard Florida Realtors / Florida Bar (FR/BAR) contract, sellers complete the **Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)** form:

- **Section 8: Infestations & Wood-Destroying Organisms:** The seller must disclose:
  - Any past or present presence of termites, dry rot, powder post beetles, or other wood-destroying organisms.
  - Any past or present structural damage caused by wood-destroying organisms.
  - Any past chemical treatments or repairs completed for pest remediation.
  - The existence of any active termite warranty, bait station contract, or transferable protection bond.
- **Section 5: Roof and Attic Conditions:** Requires disclosure of roof leaks, truss damage, or attic moisture that creates conducive conditions for Formosan aerial nesting.

### The Florida Official WDO Inspection Report (Form FDACS-13645)

In Florida, pest inspections are regulated under **Florida Statutes Chapter 482** by the **Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS)**. Licensed pest inspectors complete the official **Form FDACS-13645 (Wood-Destroying Organisms Inspection Report)**:
- **Section 2 & 3 (Findings):** Documents live organisms, dead organisms, evidence of previous infestation (frass, mud tubes, carton nests), visible damage, and evidence of previous treatments.
- **Section 5 (Inaccessible Areas):** Highlights obstructed zones, including cathedral ceilings, fully insulated attics with blown-in cellulose, areas blocked by air handler ducts, locked sheds, and areas behind interior wall furring strips.

### What Cape Coral Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Sellers in Lee County frequently downplay historical termite issues on disclosure documents:
- *"Preventative subterranean termite bait stations maintained along exterior perimeter."* (Conceals that Formosan swarmers were discovered inside the master bedroom attic).
- *"Replaced ceiling drywall after minor roof repair."* (Hides the fact that structural roof trusses were hollowed out by Asian subterranean termites).
- *"Property sold As-Is with no warranties."* Under *Johnson v. Davis*, selling a home "As-Is" does not excuse a seller from liability for failing to disclose known latent termite damage.

### The Risk of Disconnected Clues

Material termite hazards rarely appear as an obvious, single warning in real estate closing packets. Instead, critical indicators are fragmented across independent reports:
- The Home Inspector writes on page 37: *"Minor water staining and soft drywall observed around master bathroom ceiling exhaust fan."*
- The FDACS Pest Inspector notes in Section 5: *"Deep blown-in fiberglass insulation and HVAC flex-ducts prevented direct visual inspection of rear roof trusses."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatment on the SPDR, writing: *"Annual Sentricon baiting contract maintained with local pest control provider."*

When evaluated separately, each statement seems routine. But when cross-referenced, they reveal a dangerous reality: Formosan subterranean termites entered through the roof soffit, established an aerial carton nest inside the attic framing, and hollowed out the roof trusses above the master suite while the homeowner maintained only ground-level perimeter bait stations.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Cape Coral

### 1. Are termites common in Cape Coral, Florida?
Yes. Cape Coral experiences intense pressure from invasive Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*), Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*), and native drywood species. The city's extensive canal network, high soil moisture, subtropical heat, and CBS construction with concealed wood roof framing create ideal conditions for severe infestations.

### 2. What are Florida sellers legally required to disclose regarding termites?
Under the Florida Supreme Court doctrine *Johnson v. Davis*, sellers must disclose all known material latent defects affecting property value that are not readily observable to the buyer. This includes active infestations, prior structural damage, historical chemical treatments, and aerial carton nests. Selling a home "As-Is" does not exempt a seller from this disclosure duty.

### 3. What is an FDACS-13645 WDO inspection report?
Form FDACS-13645 is the official Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) inspection report mandated by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. It documents visible evidence of active or prior wood-destroying organisms (termites, beetles, wood-decaying fungi), past treatment marks, and inaccessible structural zones.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Florida?
No. Standard Florida homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, and wood-destroying organisms. Insurance providers classify insect damage as a preventable maintenance responsibility, requiring homeowners to pay for all structural, framing, and cosmetic repairs out of pocket.
