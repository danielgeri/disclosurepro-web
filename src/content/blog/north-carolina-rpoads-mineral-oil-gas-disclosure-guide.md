Signing a North Carolina Offer to Purchase and Contract (**NCAR/NCBA Form 2-T**) without auditing the **Residential Property and Owners' Association Disclosure Statement (RPOADS)** and **Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights Mandatory Disclosure Statement (MOGS)** puts **$5,000 to $50,000+ directly on the line from Day One**. Unlike almost every other state, North Carolina buyers wire a **non-refundable Due Diligence Fee (DDF)**—commonly **$3,000 to $25,000+** in Raleigh-Durham, Charlotte, and Asheville—straight to the seller the moment the contract is executed.

Even more dangerous, **N.C.G.S. Chapter 47E** legally allows sellers to check **"No Representation"** down the entire RPOADS questionnaire. If you miss hidden red flags, flood-disclosure omissions, or broker-known defects before handing over a non-refundable Due Diligence Fee—or fail to cross-check your inspection PDF and **NC WDIR 100** termite report before the Due Diligence Period expires—you either walk away losing thousands in cash or close on tens of thousands in unbudgeted structural repairs.

## Why North Carolina's "No Representation" Rule Traps Out-of-State Buyers

Under the **North Carolina Residential Property Disclosure Act (N.C.G.S. § 47E)**, sellers of 1-to-4 unit residential properties must deliver two mandatory NCREC forms before or at the time a buyer makes an offer:
1. **RPOADS (NCREC Form REC 4.22):** The Residential Property and Owners' Association Disclosure Statement.
2. **MOGS (NCREC Form REC 4.25):** The Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights Mandatory Disclosure Statement.

### The Legal Power (and Trap) of Checking "No Representation"
In mandatory-disclosure states like California or Texas (compare our [Texas TREC OP-H seller's disclosure guide](/blog/texas-trec-sellers-disclosure-guide/)), sellers must answer based on actual knowledge. North Carolina takes a sharply different hybrid *caveat emptor* approach under **N.C.G.S. § 47E-4**:
- **Sellers Can Stay Silent:** A North Carolina homeowner can check **"No Representation" (`NR`)** on *every single question* on the RPOADS—even if they know the roof leaks or the crawlspace joists are rotting—without violating Chapter 47E, so long as they do not make an affirmative false statement or actively conceal a defect (such as painting over active mold or fabricating repair receipts).
- **Brokers CANNOT Hide Behind "No Representation":** Here is the crucial legal lever many buyers overlook. Under **N.C.G.S. § 93A-6(a)(1)**, licensed North Carolina real estate brokers have an **independent, non-waivable statutory duty to discover and disclose all material facts** they know or reasonably should know—even if their seller checked "No Representation" on every box of the RPOADS. If a prior buyer terminated a contract after a failed septic test or structural inspection and shared that report with the listing agent, the listing agent **must** disclose those material defects to you regardless of the seller's `NR` checkboxes.
- **3-Calendar-Day Statutory Rescission Right (N.C.G.S. § 47E-5):** If the seller fails to deliver the RPOADS or MOGS prior to or at the time you submit your offer, you gain a statutory right to **cancel the contract within 3 calendar days** of receiving the missing form (or within 3 calendar days of contract formation, whichever occurs first) and recover **both** your Earnest Money Deposit and your Due Diligence Fee in full.

## Decoding the May 2024 RPOADS Flood Revisions & HOA Traps

Effective **May 1, 2024**, the North Carolina Real Estate Commission (NCREC) substantially overhauled the **RPOADS (REC 4.22)** to give buyers clearer visibility into catastrophic water, structural, and HOA liabilities. When reading an active NC disclosure packet, scrutinize these sections first:

### 1. Expanded Flood & Water-Intrusion Questions
Triggered by severe inland and coastal flooding from hurricanes and tropical remnants across both eastern NC and western Appalachian river valleys, the revised RPOADS broke out dedicated flood questions asking whether:
- The property sits in a **FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA)** or designated floodway.
- The home has ever experienced **flooding, standing water, or water intrusion** from natural events or drainage failures.
- Any **NFIP or private flood insurance claims** have been filed on the structure, or whether the property ever received **FEMA or federal disaster assistance** (which can trigger mandatory flood-insurance covenants that run with the land).

**The `NR` Loophole Still Applies:** A seller can still mark "No Representation" on these new flood questions. If you see `NR` on flood history in a Piedmont creek basin, Wilmington coastal tract, or Buncombe County valley, pull the FEMA Flood Map Service Center layer and request a **CLUE insurance loss-history report** before submitting a high Due Diligence Fee.

### 2. Owners' Association (HOA / Condo) Financial Disclosures
The final section of RPOADS covers mandatory HOA governance: regular dues, transfer/capital contribution fees, **approved or pending special assessments**, and active lawsuits against the association. Because NC sellers frequently leave HOA lines marked `NR` or "Unknown," always verify the HOA's reserve balance, master insurance deductibles, and board meeting minutes during your Due Diligence Period—never rely on the MLS fee estimate alone.

### 3. Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights (MOGS — REC 4.25)
Unlike the RPOADS, the **MOGS form** restricts where a seller may check "No Representation":
- **Questions 1 & 2 (Current Seller Severance):** Sellers **must** answer **Yes** or **No**—`"No Representation"` is **not an option**—regarding whether the *current seller* has severed or intends to sever mineral, oil, or gas rights prior to closing.
- **Questions 3 & 4 (Prior Owner Severance):** Only prior-owner severances allow a "No Representation" checkbox.

If mineral rights were severed by a developer or prior tract owner—a real risk in parts of the Triad, Deep River Basin (Lee, Chatham, Moore counties), and Western NC—a mineral-rights holder can hold surface-access rights that complicate title insurance, future resale, or rural well protection.

## Protecting Your Non-Refundable Due Diligence Fee (NC Form 2-T)

Understanding **NCAR/NCBA Form 2-T** is critical because North Carolina splits buyer deposits into two completely different buckets:

- **Due Diligence Fee (DDF):** Paid directly to the seller upon contract execution as consideration for your unilateral right to terminate for any reason or no reason before **5:00 PM on the Due Diligence Date**. If you walk away after uncovering a $30,000 foundation defect or failed septic field, the **seller keeps 100% of your Due Diligence Fee**.
- **Earnest Money Deposit (EMD):** Held in escrow and refunded to you if you terminate in writing before 5:00 PM on the Due Diligence Date.

Because your DDF is sunk the moment the seller signs, every defect you miss *before* offering—or fail to quantify before your Due Diligence deadline—destroys your negotiation leverage. If a seller refuses repair credits on Day 21, you must choose between forfeiting a $10,000+ DDF or closing on an impaired house.

### Why the NC WDIR 100 Termite Report Is Non-Negotiable
North Carolina sits squarely inside the heavy **Eastern Subterranean Termite** and **crawlspace wood-decay fungus** zone (with aggressive **Formosan termites** established along the coastal plain). Just like the damp crawlspace traps documented in our [Murfreesboro, TN termite disclosure guide](/blog/murfreesboro-tn-termites/), NC homes on vented brick crawlspaces frequently suffer silent sill-plate decay and elevated wood moisture (>20%) that sellers mask behind `NR` checkboxes.

Always order an official **North Carolina Wood-Destroying Insect Information Report (WDIR 100)** from a licensed structural pest control inspector on Day 1 of Due Diligence. Look closely at:
- **Section 2 Obstructions / Inaccessible Areas:** Crawlspaces with low clearance (<18 inches), taped poly sheeting, or fiberglass batts stapled between joists often hide active mud tubes.
- **Active vs. Previous Infestation & Conducive Conditions:** Verify whether any existing "termite bond" is a **retreat-only warranty** (which pays $0 for lumber replacement) or a true **retreat-and-repair bond**.

### Spotting Red Flags Before You Wire the Due Diligence Fee
Before locking in an aggressive DDF, verify three items upfront:
1. **Ask the Listing Agent Directly About Material Facts:** Have your buyer's agent ask in writing whether any prior buyer terminated under contract or shared a prior inspection, septic, or structural engineer report. Remember—under N.C.G.S. § 93A-6, the listing broker **must** disclose known material facts even when the seller checks `NR`.
2. **Pull County Septic & Building Permits Early:** In Wake, Mecklenburg, Durham, and Buncombe counties, mismatching a 4-bedroom MLS listing against a 3-bedroom county septic permit is a notorious resale and lender killer.
3. **Check `Yes` Answers and Blank Lines Carefully:** A seller who checks `No` on 25 items and switches to `No Representation` on only 2 items (such as Roof Leakage or Crawlspace Moisture) is handing you a bright neon roadmap showing exactly where your inspector should probe first.

## Frequently Asked Questions About North Carolina RPOADS & MOGS Disclosures

### Why do so many North Carolina sellers check "No Representation" on the RPOADS?
Under **N.C.G.S. § 47E-4**, North Carolina law explicitly grants sellers the option to check "No Representation" so they avoid making affirmative legal warranties about property systems they have not professionally inspected. While checking `NR` is common—especially among investors, estates, and cautious sellers—it never guarantees the home is defect-free, and it shifts 100% of discovery responsibility onto your inspections during the Due Diligence Period.

### Can I get my North Carolina Due Diligence Fee back if the inspection finds a major undisclosed defect?
In most cases, **no**—if the seller checked "No Representation" and did not actively conceal the defect or commit fraud, walking away during the Due Diligence Period forfeits your Due Diligence Fee (though you recover your Earnest Money Deposit if you terminate before 5:00 PM on the Due Diligence Date). Exceptions exist if the seller breached the contract, committed actionable fraud/active concealment, or failed to deliver the mandatory RPOADS/MOGS prior to your offer (triggering the 3-day rescission right under N.C.G.S. § 47E-5).

### Does the seller have to answer "Yes" or "No" on the Mineral and Oil and Gas Rights (MOGS) form?
Yes, for Questions 1 and 2. Unlike the RPOADS, **NCREC Form REC 4.25 (MOGS)** does **not** allow a seller to check "No Representation" regarding whether the *current seller* has severed or intends to sever mineral, oil, or gas rights from the property. Only Questions 3 and 4 (covering whether *previous* owners severed those rights) permit a "No Representation" response.

### What is an NC WDIR 100 report and who pays for it?
The **WDIR 100** is North Carolina's official **Wood-Destroying Insect Information Report**, completed by a licensed pest control professional to document visible active or prior termite/beetle infestations, wood-decay fungus, structural damage, and inaccessible crawlspace zones. In North Carolina, the **buyer** normally pays for the WDIR 100 during the Due Diligence Period, while any resulting chemical treatment or structural wood repairs are negotiated with the seller on Form 310-T before the Due Diligence deadline expires.
