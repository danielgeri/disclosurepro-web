## The Hidden Threat: Termites Across Midland and the Permian Basin

Midland sits at an elevation of 2,780 feet on the southern edge of the Llano Estacado plains in West Texas. While the region is characterized by scorching summer temperatures exceeding 100°F and low relative humidity, subterranean termites survive by dwelling deep within sub-slab soils and seeking artificial sources of moisture.

### How Permian Basin Caliche Soils and Concrete Slabs Exacerbate Termite Infiltration

Several unique environmental and structural conditions make Midland properties susceptible to subterranean termite invasions:

- **Caliche Hardpan Soils and Sub-Slab Moisture Traps:** Much of Midland is underlain by caliche—a dense, hardened layer of calcium carbonate. When residential monolithic concrete slabs are poured over caliche, irrigation water, plumbing condensation, and sewer line drainage become trapped beneath the slab, creating permanent underground moisture reservoirs in an otherwise arid landscape. Subterranean termites thrive in these moist sub-slab environments.
- **Monolithic Concrete Slab Plumbing Penetrations:** Nearly all Midland homes constructed since 1950 are built on monolithic concrete slab-on-grade foundations. During construction, plumbing blockouts for sewer drains, bathtub traps, and water supply lines penetrate directly through the slab. In dry desert environments, subterranean termites do not need to build visible mud tubes up exterior foundation walls; they travel straight up through plumbing pipe penetrations directly into interior bathroom and kitchen framing.
- **AC Condensate Drainage and Foundation Soakers:** During intense West Texas summer heatwaves, central air conditioning units run constantly, producing gallons of condensate water daily that often drains directly against exterior foundation walls. In addition, homeowners use automated foundation soaker hoses to prevent concrete slabs from cracking during droughts. This constant perimeter moisture creates an ideal, localized habitat for subterranean colonies.
- **Aging Douglas Fir Framing in Mid-Century Ranches:** Thousands of homes in Old Midland were built during the 1950s and 1960s oil booms using dimensional Douglas fir framing. After decades in the dry desert climate, this wood is seasoned and brittle. When subterranean termites penetrate wall framing, they hollow out load-bearing studs rapidly, often leaving only a fragile shell of paint.

### Pest Biology: Arid-Land and Western Subterranean Termites in West Texas

Midland homebuyers contend with two primary subterranean wood-destroying pest species:

1. **Arid-Land Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes tibialis*):** The most prevalent wood-destroying organism in the Permian Basin. Adapted to semi-arid desert soils, these insects nest several feet underground beneath the caliche hardpan to escape intense summer surface heat and winter freezes. In early spring, sudden rainfall triggers nuptial swarms of dark-bodied winged reproductives (alates).
2. **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Frequently found in heavily irrigated, mature residential neighborhoods in Midland. They construct protective earthen mud tubes over concrete stem walls and plumbing lines to shield their soft bodies from the arid West Texas air while foraging for cellulose in wall studs and baseboards.

## Texas Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Texas Property Code § 5.008 and the WDI Report

Texas maintains strict statutory disclosure requirements designed to protect residential real estate purchasers from undisclosed property defects.

### The Statutory Framework: Texas Property Code § 5.008

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, sellers of single-family residential properties must deliver a completed **Seller's Disclosure Notice** (standardized as TREC Form OP-H or Texas REALTORS® Form TXR-1406) on or before the effective date of the contract:

- **Section: Wood-Destroying Insects and Pests:** The disclosure notice explicitly requires the seller to disclose whether they are aware of:
  - Active termites or other wood-destroying insects.
  - Termite or wood rot damage needing repair.
  - Previous termite damage.
  - Previous termite treatment.
  - Any current or past termite warranties, treatment bonds, or service contracts.
- **Texas Deceptive Trade Practices-Consumer Protection Act (DTPA, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 17.41 et seq.):** If a seller knowingly makes false representations, fails to disclose known termite infestations, or conceals prior damage to induce a buyer to purchase, the seller is liable under the Texas DTPA. Successful DTPA claims can result in treble (triple) damages, economic compensation for repair costs, and statutory attorney's fees.
- **The Limits of "As-Is" Provisions under Texas Law:** In Texas (*Prudential Ins. Co. of America v. Jefferson Associates, Ltd.*, 896 S.W.2d 156), an "as-is" clause is legally enforceable only when the seller makes a full, honest disclosure of known defects and does not engage in fraudulent concealment. An "as-is" addendum provides zero legal immunity if a seller concealed active termite damage or misrepresented treatment history.

### The Official Texas Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report

Governed by the **Texas Department of Agriculture Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS)**, the official **Texas Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report** is typically ordered during the contractual Option Period.

The WDI report categorizes findings into specific sections:
- **Part 2: Inspection Findings:** Identifies whether active termites, prior termite damage, or other wood-destroying insects are present.
- **Part 3: Conditions Conducive to Infestation:** Flags critical architectural and environmental risks, including soil-to-wood contact, covered weep holes, excessive moisture around foundations, and plumbing leaks.

### Deceptive Seller Wording to Watch for in Midland Transactions

Sellers in Permian Basin transactions frequently downplay termite history using evasive wording:
- *"Routine pest maintenance performed annually by local exterminator."* (Conceals that the exterminator was called specifically to drill through the concrete slab to treat active termites in the master bath).
- *"Minor plumbing leak repaired beneath master bathroom slab in 2022."* (Masks the fact that subterranean termites were found infesting the wet soil beneath the slab cutout).
- *"Property updated with fresh interior paint and new luxury vinyl plank flooring."* (Frequently hides that termite kick-out holes and hollowed baseboards were spackled and covered).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Critical Red Flags in 150-Page Closings

In Texas transactions, buyers negotiate an "Option Period" (typically 5 to 7 days under Section 5 of the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract). During this brief window, buyers are inundated with documents:

- The TREC contract, third-party financing addenda, and mineral rights reservations.
- The multi-page TREC Seller's Disclosure Notice.
- A 50-to-80-page general home inspection report.
- The Texas SPCS Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report.
- Midland County plat maps, title commitments, and boundary surveys.

### The Critical Limitation of General Home Inspection Reports

General home inspectors in Texas are regulated under the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC), which explicitly excludes pest inspections from standard home inspection standards of practice:
- *"The inspector is NOT required to inspect for wood-destroying insects, environmental hazards, or hidden defects behind finished walls, slabs, or cabinetry."*

When buyers skim past these disclaimers late at night, they assume a clean general inspection report guarantees a sound structure. In Midland homes built on concrete slabs, subterranean termites frequently feast on framing completely hidden behind drywall, entirely undetected until structural wall studs collapse.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Midland

### 1. Do desert climates like Midland really have termite problems?
Yes. Subterranean termites are highly adaptable and prolific across West Texas desert environments. They survive by nesting deep underground beneath the caliche hardpan where soil moisture is retained. In residential areas, they are strongly attracted to moisture produced by foundation soaker hoses, lawn sprinkler systems, plumbing slab penetrations, and air conditioning condensate drains, tunneling directly into structural framing.

### 2. What does Texas law require sellers to disclose about wood-destroying insects in Midland?
Under Texas Property Code § 5.008, sellers must complete the TREC Seller's Disclosure Notice and explicitly disclose any known active termite infestations, previous termite damage, prior chemical treatments, and any existing pest warranties or service contracts. A seller who knowingly conceals known termite activity or previous structural damage faces civil liability for fraud and deceptive practices under the Texas DTPA.

### 3. How do termites enter homes built on concrete slabs in West Texas?
In monolithic concrete slab homes common to Midland, subterranean termites typically enter through unsealed plumbing penetrations in the slab (such as bathtub traps, shower drains, and toilet flanges) or through hairline shrinkage cracks and expansion joints. Because they enter from directly underneath the slab, they invade wall framing without ever constructing visible mud tubes on exterior foundation walls.

### 4. Can a buyer terminate a Texas purchase contract if termites are discovered during the option period?
Yes. Under Section 5 of the standard TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract, a buyer who purchases an unrestricted Option Period has the absolute legal right to terminate the contract for any reason—including the discovery of termite activity or damage on a WDI report—by providing written notice to the seller before the Option Period expires, receiving a full refund of their earnest money deposit.
