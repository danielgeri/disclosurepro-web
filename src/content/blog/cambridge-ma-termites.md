## How to Identify Termite Damage in Historic Cambridge, MA Homes

Unlike southern coastal states threatened by drywood termites, Massachusetts properties are attacked almost exclusively by the cold-hardy **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)**. Rather than dying off during harsh New England winters, mature colonies burrow beneath the 3-to-4-foot frost line—and in Cambridge homes with heated cellars, steam radiators, or finished garden-level apartments, colonies feed actively inside structural framing **365 days a year**.

### Six Local Warning Signs Buyers & Inspectors Must Check

1. **Earthen Mud Shelter Tubes on Fieldstone & Brick Foundations:** Pencil-thin dirt tunnels climbing mortared granite rubble walls, brick chimney piers, or cast-iron sewer stacks. Because lime-sand mortar crumbles after 90–140 years, termites routinely travel *inside* hollow voids between foundation stones directly into the timber sill without ever appearing on the interior cellar face.
2. **Soft, Spongy Perimeter Mudsills & Carrying Beams:** Probing accessible 6x8 hand-hewn oak or pine sills resting on damp rubble ledges reveals springwood hollowed into layered honeycomb galleries while exterior timber surfaces look intact.
3. **Spring Swarming Wings on Basement Windowsills:** On warm, sunny mornings after rain between **March and late May**, thousands of winged reproductive alates emerge indoors. Piles of translucent, equal-length wings along basement sills or garden-level baseboards signal a mature, established colony inside the structure.
4. **Sagging First-Floor Hallways & Sticking Doors:** When subterranean colonies compromise bearing sill plates and first-floor joist ends, interior partitions settle unevenly—often misattributed by sellers to "typical 1900s New England settling."
5. **Finished Basement Drywall or Beadboard Blocking Perimeter Sills:** Newly installed basement paneling, storage closets, or recent garden-unit buildouts along street-facing foundation walls are the #1 blind spot in Cambridge triple-deckers.
6. **Vague "Preventive Pest Service" Invoices:** A single $350–$500 receipt for "exterior perimeter treatment" or "crawlspace spray" without a full structural diagram often indicates prior active discovery that was never structurally repaired.

## Massachusetts Disclosure Laws: Caveat Emptor, Voluntary MAR Forms & M.G.L. c. 93A

Buying in Cambridge carries far higher due-diligence risk than purchasing in mandatory-disclosure states like California or New York because Massachusetts places almost the entire discovery burden on the buyer.

### What Massachusetts Sellers Must (and Don't Have to) Disclose

- **No Mandatory General Property Condition Form:** Massachusetts statutes do **not** require residential sellers to complete a general property disclosure questionnaire. Sellers can legally remain silent about past termite treatments, historic sill rot, or cellar seepage so long as they avoid affirmative lies or active physical concealment.
- **Narrow Statutory Mandates Only:** Massachusetts law mandates disclosure strictly for **Lead Paint** on pre-1978 homes (**M.G.L. c. 111, § 197A**), **Title 5 Septic Systems** (**310 CMR 15.000**, rare inside sewered Cambridge), and smoke/carbon monoxide compliance certificates.
- **Voluntary MAR Seller's Statement of Property Condition:** Some Cambridge sellers complete the Massachusetts Association of Realtors (MAR) voluntary disclosure form, which includes Section 4 (*Pest / Termite Conditions*). Once a seller chooses to answer, they assume a legal duty to tell the **whole truth**—half-truths such as *"preventive spraying only"* while concealing hollowed joists constitute actionable misrepresentation.

### Where Buyer Protections Still Bite: Chapter 93A & Active Concealment

- **Licensed Brokers Cannot Hide Known Defects (M.G.L. c. 93A & 254 CMR 3.00):** Unlike private sellers, licensed Massachusetts real estate brokers and listing agents are governed by the Consumer Protection Act (**M.G.L. c. 93A**). Withholding known material defects—such as a prior buyer's cancelled WDI report documenting live termites or compromised framing—exposes the brokerage to **double or treble damages plus attorney's fees**.
- **Active Concealment Is Illegal (*Kannavos v. Annino*):** Scraping away active mud tubes, painting over damaged sill beams, or nailing fresh drywall over hollowed studs right before open houses crosses the line from legal silence into fraudulent concealment.
- **Standard Home Inspectors Exclude Termites (266 CMR 6.00):** Massachusetts home inspectors are regulated under **266 CMR 6.00** and routinely exclude wood-destroying insect evaluations from general inspection contracts. Always order a separate **NPMA-33 WDI inspection** from a Massachusetts-licensed pest control operator (MDAR certified) before your OTP inspection contingency expires.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termite Inspections & Disclosures in Cambridge, MA

### Does Massachusetts law require a home seller to disclose termite damage?
No. Because Massachusetts follows *caveat emptor* ("buyer beware"), private residential sellers are not required by statute to complete a general property disclosure form or volunteer past termite history—provided they do not lie or actively conceal damage. However, licensed listing agents are bound by **M.G.L. c. 93A** and must disclose known material structural or pest defects.

### Can subterranean termites survive freezing Cambridge winters?
Yes. Eastern Subterranean Termites retreat below New England's 3-to-4-foot frost line in unheated ground and continue feeding uninterrupted throughout winter inside heated Cambridge basements, steam-pipe chases, and finished garden-level apartments.

### Does a standard Massachusetts home inspection include a termite inspection?
No. General home inspectors licensed under **266 CMR 6.00** perform a visual evaluation of accessible building components and explicitly exclude formal wood-destroying insect certifications. Buyers in Cambridge should always order a dedicated **NPMA-33 WDI inspection** from a licensed pest control professional alongside their general inspection.

### How can Cambridge buyers protect themselves when buying a condo in a historic triple-decker?
Request at least **24 months of condo association meeting minutes**, the master insurance policy, the current reserve budget, and all recent maintenance invoices during your OTP review period. Watch for references to basement moisture, masonry repointing, or exterminator visits—because foundation sill repairs on a three-unit condo are typically funded via special assessments split among unit owners.
