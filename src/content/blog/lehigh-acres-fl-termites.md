## Southwest Florida Pest Threats: Formosan and Drywood Termites in Lehigh Acres

Navigating a home purchase in Lehigh Acres requires understanding how local geography, regional weather patterns, and Florida construction styles interact with invasive termite species.

### Biology and Mechanics of Lee County Wood-Destroying Organisms

Unlike northern regions where single subterranean species predominate, Lehigh Acres is an active transition zone where multiple destructive species overlap:

- **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Often dubbed "super termites," Formosan colonies contain up to several million individuals. They forage up to 300 feet through sandy soil and consume timber at up to ten times the rate of native subterranean termites. Formosan termites also construct aerial carton nests within wall cavities using chewed wood, soil, and saliva, allowing them to survive without ground contact if a roof or plumbing leak provides moisture.
- **Asian Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes gestroi*):** Southwest Florida is one of the few regions in the United States where Formosan and Asian subterranean termites coexist. Asian subterranean termites thrive in tropical heat and frequently attack structural wood trusses and interior framing.
- **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Drywood termites do not require soil contact. Reproductive swarmers enter homes aerially through soffit vents, attic louvers, and window frames, establishing colonies directly inside structural roof trusses, ceiling joists, and door jambs.

### Lehigh Acres Construction and Climatic Vulnerabilities

Several distinctive building characteristics and environmental factors in Lehigh Acres accelerate termite infestations:

- **Concrete Block Structure (CBS) with Interior Wood Framing:** The vast majority of Lehigh Acres homes are built using concrete block construction on concrete slab foundations. While exterior CBS walls are impervious to insects, the interior walls are framed with 1x2 or 2x2 wooden furring strips anchored to the block, covered with drywall. Subterranean termites enter through microscopic expansion joints or plumbing slab penetrations and consume these wooden furring strips, hollowing out the interior framing without leaving visible signs on exterior block walls.
- **Engineered Wood Roof Trusses:** Virtually all single-family homes in Lehigh Acres utilize prefabricated wood roof trusses constructed of southern yellow pine. Because roof pitches are often low and attic temperatures exceed 130°F in summer, moisture from condensation or tropical storms softens the wood, creating an ideal habitat for both drywood swarmers and Formosan carton nests.
- **Sandy, Porous Soils and Canal Water Tables:** Lehigh Acres features sandy soils and an extensive network of drainage canals. The high water table and porous sandy ground make it exceptionally easy for subterranean colonies to tunnel extensive distances beneath residential concrete driveways and foundation slabs.
- **Post-Hurricane Moisture Intrusion:** Major tropical events, such as Hurricane Ian, caused widespread soffit damage, roof tile displacement, and flashing leaks throughout Lee County. Even minor wind-driven rain penetration creates pockets of high wood moisture within wall cavities, allowing aerial Formosan colonies to establish permanent nesting sites.

### Seasonal Swarming Cycles and Physical Red Flags

Prospective buyers in Lehigh Acres should be vigilant for telltale signs of termite presence during showings:

- **Spring and Summer Swarms:** Formosan termites swarm in massive numbers at dusk on warm, humid evenings between May and July, frequently drawn to porch lights and exterior windows. Drywood termites typically swarm during sunny spring and summer afternoons.
- **Shed Alate Wings:** Piles of translucent insect wings accumulating on window sills, beneath ceiling fans, or along sliding glass patio door tracks.
- **Drywood Frass (Fecal Pellets):** Piles of tiny, hard, oval droppings resembling sand grains or ground pepper pushed out of "kickout holes" beneath baseboards or from attic truss junctions.
- **Blistering Paint and Rippled Drywall:** Drywall that appears rippled, blistered, or paper-thin, indicating that subterranean termites have consumed the cardboard backing and interior furring strips.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Legal Obligations in Lee County

Florida real estate transactions are governed by established common law principles and standardized association disclosure forms that place strict transparency duties on property sellers.

### The Landmark Doctrine of *Johnson v. Davis*

Unlike many states that strictly enforce *caveat emptor* ("let the buyer beware"), Florida disclosure law is defined by the Supreme Court of Florida's landmark ruling in *Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985):

- **Affirmative Duty to Disclose:** Where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property which are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them.
- **Inapplicability of "As-Is" Clauses:** Florida courts have repeatedly held (e.g., *Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.*) that an "as-is" provision in a FAR/BAR contract does not relieve a seller of their *Johnson v. Davis* disclosure duty. If a seller knows about active or past termite infestation or unaddressed truss damage, failing to reveal it constitutes actionable fraudulent concealment.

### The Florida Realtors/Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) Disclosures

In typical Lehigh Acres transactions, sellers complete the standard **Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR)**:

- **Section 10 (Wood-Destroying Organisms):** The disclosure explicitly asks:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present termite or wood-destroying organism infestations or damage?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any treatments or repairs made due to termites or other wood-destroying organisms?"*
  - *"Is the property currently under a termite warranty or service contract? If yes, is it transferable?"*
- **Attachment of Official Documentation:** If the seller answers affirmatively, they are obligated to provide copies of all past inspection reports, warranty transfer certificates, and remediation invoices.

### Chapter 482 Wood-Destroying Organism (WDO) Inspection Reports

In Florida, structural pest inspections are strictly regulated under **Florida Statutes Chapter 482**:

- **Standardized FDACS-13645 Form:** Licensed pest control companies must issue their findings on the official Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) Form 13645.
- **Strict Distinction Between Organisms:** The report documents live infestation, previous infestation, and visible structural damage caused by subterranean termites, drywood termites, and wood-decaying fungi.

### Deciphering Deceptive Seller Disclaimers

Sellers and listing agents often draft vague notations on disclosure riders that downplay substantial structural hazards:

- **"Termite bond maintained by seller; annual renewal lapsed in 2022":** When a termite bond lapses in Southwest Florida, it is frequently because the pest company discovered active Formosan activity or structural alterations that rendered the home uninsurable without costly remediation.
- **"Minor drywall patch completed in guest bedroom near baseboard":** In CBS homes, drywall patches near the floor often conceal subterranean termite tunnels entering through plumbing rough-ins.
- **"Attic crawl space partially inaccessible due to blown-in fiberglass insulation and HVAC trunks":** WDO inspectors are only required to inspect accessible areas. If the inspector disclaims access to the perimeter roof truss tails, the buyer may remain completely unaware of widespread drywood termite hollows.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Lehigh Acres, FL

### 1. Why are termites so prevalent in Lehigh Acres concrete block homes?
While concrete block (CBS) exterior walls are resistant to termites, the interior walls rely on wooden furring strips to support drywall, and roofs are framed with soft pine wood trusses. Subterranean termites enter through slab expansion joints or plumbing penetrations to consume furring strips, while drywood and Formosan termites invade attics through soffit vents and damaged roof flashing.

### 2. Is a WDO inspection legally required when buying a home in Florida?
While Florida state law does not mandate a WDO inspection for all transactions, conventional, FHA, and VA mortgage lenders routinely require a clean Wood-Destroying Organisms report (Form FDACS-13645) as a condition of loan approval. Even for cash purchases, skipping a WDO inspection in Southwest Florida poses enormous financial risk.

### 3. What constitutes actionable seller fraud under Florida's *Johnson v. Davis* rule?
Under *Johnson v. Davis*, a seller is liable for fraudulent concealment if they knew of active termite infestations or structural wood damage that materially impacts the property's value, failed to disclose it on the seller disclosure statement, and the defect was not readily apparent to the buyer upon an ordinary visual inspection.

### 4. How much does professional termite treatment cost in Lehigh Acres?
Treatment costs vary depending on the pest species. A perimeter liquid barrier (e.g., Termidor) or Sentricon baiting system for subterranean termites typically costs between $1,200 and $2,500. Whole-home tent fumigation with Vikane gas for drywood termites generally costs between $2,500 and $5,000 depending on home volume. If structural roof trusses or floor joists require rebuilding, reconstruction costs can easily exceed $25,000 to $45,000.
