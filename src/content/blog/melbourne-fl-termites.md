## Space Coast Structural Hazards: Termite Dynamics in Melbourne

Purchasing a home in Melbourne requires an understanding of how coastal humidity, sandy soil strata, and local termite biology intersect with residential construction.

### Biology and Mechanics of Local Termite Species

Melbourne residences face relentless attacks from three distinct termite species:

- **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** These native ground-dwelling pests establish subterranean colonies numbering in the hundreds of thousands. Requiring constant moisture, they forage through Brevard County’s sandy soils and enter structures through hairline slab cracks, expansion joints, and plumbing chases. They construct earthen mud shelter tubes to travel vertically, hollowing out structural framing along the soft annual growth rings.
- **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Often dubbed "super termites," Formosan termites establish massive colonies containing several million individuals. They forage aggressively and consume wood at up to ten times the rate of native subterranean species. Unlike native species, Formosans can construct aerial carton nests within wall cavities out of chewed wood, soil, and saliva, allowing them to thrive above ground without returning to the soil if an internal moisture source (such as roof condensation or an AC leak) is available.
- **West Indian Drywood Termites (*Cryptotermes brevis*):** Requiring no connection to the soil or ground moisture, drywood termites extract hydration entirely from the atmospheric humidity and the cellulose they consume. They enter structures aerially during warm evening swarming flights, colonizing attic rafters, truss gusset plates, and exposed fascia, carving smooth galleries across the wood grain.
- **Seasonal Swarming Patterns Across Brevard County:** Subterranean termites swarm on warm, humid spring mornings following rain (typically February through April). Formosan termites swarm in massive airborne clouds during warm, balmy evenings in late spring and early summer (May through June), often attracted to porch lights. Drywood termites swarm during hot, humid summer evenings (June through August).
- **Concealed Timber Excavation:** Because subterranean termites hollow out interior framing while leaving an outer paint veneer intact, and drywood termites seal their kick-out holes after pushing out frass, structural timber damage often remains hidden for years until rooflines sag or doorframes collapse.

### Melbourne Architectural and Environmental Risk Factors

Melbourne’s diverse housing stock and coastal geography heighten property vulnerability to wood-destroying organisms:

- **Concrete Block Structure (CBS) Homes with Wooden Roof Trusses:** While the exterior walls of mid-century CBS homes in Eau Gallie and South Melbourne are impervious to termites, the interior partition walls, furring strips, and entire roof truss systems are built of wood. Subterranean termites enter through slab plumbing penetrations to hollow out interior wall studs, while drywood termites attack roof trusses directly through vented soffits.
- **Historic Wood-Frame Cottages (Downtown Melbourne & Eau Gallie):** Pre-1950 coastal bungalows feature raised pier-and-beam crawlspaces with low ground clearance. High humidity along the Indian River Lagoon softens untreated pine sills, providing an open entryway for subterranean colonies.
- **Sandy Coastal Soils and High Water Tables:** Brevard County's sandy soil allows subterranean termites to forage rapidly in search of wood, while shallow water tables keep subterranean nesting sites continuously hydrated.
- **Air Conditioning Condensation and Roof Leaks:** Subtropical humidity causes significant condensation along HVAC ductwork and roof valleys, creating hidden moisture reservoirs that enable Formosan termites to establish aerial carton nests inside walls without soil contact.

### Physical Indicators Melbourne Buyers Must Inspect

During property walkthroughs in Melbourne, prospective purchasers should look closely for these critical warning signs:

- **Hexagonal Drywood Termite Frass:** Small, hard, sand-like fecal pellets with distinct six-sided concave grooves collecting on window sills, along baseboard ledges, or beneath attic trusses.
- **Earthen Shelter Tubes on Foundation Slabs:** Slender mud tubes extending along exterior stem walls, inside garage slab perimeters, or climbing plumbing waste stacks beneath sinks.
- **Discarded Swarmer Wings:** Piles of translucent insect wings accumulated along window sills, around exterior patio lighting, or inside sliding door tracks following seasonal swarms.
- **Buckling Baseboards and Spongy Door Frames:** Interior door casings, baseboards, or drywall surfaces that sound papery and hollow or cave in under light pressure from an awl or screwdriver.

## Florida Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Legal Obligations in Brevard County

Residential property sales in Melbourne are governed by mandatory disclosure doctrines established by Florida courts and state statutes.

### The Landmark Johnson v. Davis Doctrine

Florida property condition disclosure law is grounded in the seminal Florida Supreme Court ruling in ***Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So. 2d 625 (Fla. 1985)**:

- **Affirmative Legal Duty to Disclose:** The Florida Supreme Court established that where the seller of a home knows of facts materially affecting the value of the property that are not readily observable and are not known to the buyer, the seller is under an affirmative legal duty to disclose them.
- **"As-Is" Contracts Do Not Shield Sellers:** The duty to disclose material latent defects cannot be waived or eliminated by an "as-is" contract provision. In *Rayner v. Wise Realty Co.* (1987), Florida appellate courts confirmed that *Johnson v. Davis* applies directly to concealed termite infestations and structural timber damage.
- **Actionable Fraudulent Concealment:** A seller who conceals active infestations or paints over structural timber damage without disclosure can be held liable for fraudulent concealment, allowing the buyer to sue for actual damages, contract rescission, and attorney fees.

### Standard Florida Contract Disclosures and WDO Inspections

In Brevard County transactions, standard contracts and pest inspections follow established protocols:

- **The Florida FAR/BAR Contract & Seller Property Disclosure:** Under the standard Florida Realtors®/Florida Bar (FAR/BAR) contract, sellers typically complete the Seller's Property Disclosure - Residential (SPDR). Section 8 specifically requires disclosure of:
  - Any past or present infestation of termites, dry rot, or wood-destroying organisms.
  - Any past or present damage to the property caused by termites or wood-destroying pests.
  - All past treatments, active pest control warranties, and transferable repair bonds.
- **The Florida WDO Report (Form FDACS-13645):** In Florida, pest inspections are regulated under **Chapter 482 of the Florida Statutes** and performed by licensed pest control operators using the standardized State of Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) **Form 13645**. The WDO report differentiates findings into visible active infestation, visible prior infestation, and visible structural timber damage resulting from wood-destroying organisms.
- **Visual Limitations:** The FDACS-13645 inspection is visual and non-destructive. It does not inspect framing hidden behind finished drywall, insulated attic cavities, or enclosed soffits.

### The Reality of Florida "Termite Bonds"

Many homes in Melbourne are marketed as having an "active termite bond." However, buyers must be vigilant:

- **Treatment-Only vs. Repair Bonds:** A "treatment-only" bond covers only the cost of reapplying pesticide if termites return; it provides zero coverage for repairing structural framing damage. Only a comprehensive "repair bond" covers structural timber restoration, and even these policies often contain low liability caps and strict exclusions for Formosan carton nests or moisture-related damage.
- **Lapsed Contracts:** Sellers frequently let termite bond renewals lapse or fail to complete the required annual reinspection, voiding warranty protection right before listing the property for sale.

- Termite bond service contracts, annual renewal certificates, and pesticide treatment logs
- Brevard County Property Appraiser records and City of Melbourne building permits

### Misleading Terminology and Euphemisms

Sellers and listing agents frequently use vague language on disclosure forms that obscures serious structural timber damage:

- **"Property covered under active subterranean termite bond":** Having a bond does not mean the house is free from existing damage. Many bonded homes harbor extensive past damage that was never repaired.
- **"Localized spot treatment completed on garage framing in 2023":** Spot treatments do not address wider colony activity or Formosan carton nests thriving behind interior walls.
- **"Attic inspection limited due to blown-in insulation and low roof pitch":** Standard WDO inspection caveats often conceal compromised roof trusses and decayed gusset plates.
- **"Minor wood rot repaired on exterior fascia for cosmetic upkeep":** Freshly patched trim or PVC cladding often conceals hollowed rafter tails and structural trusses.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Melbourne, FL

### 1. Are termites common in Melbourne and Brevard County?
Yes. Brevard County’s warm subtropical climate, sandy soils, and high humidity make it a prime habitat for Eastern subterranean, invasive Formosan subterranean, and West Indian drywood termites. Virtually every wood-framed and CBS home in the region faces termite exposure.

### 2. Can concrete block (CBS) homes in Melbourne get termite damage?
Yes. While concrete block exterior walls are immune to termites, the interior framing, wood furring strips, baseboards, and roof truss systems are constructed of wood. Subterranean termites enter through slab cracks and plumbing pipes to attack interior framing, while drywood termites attack roof trusses directly from the air.

### 3. What does a Florida WDO inspection report cover?
A Florida WDO inspection (FDACS Form 13645) identifies visible active infestations, visible prior infestations, and visible structural timber damage from wood-destroying organisms. However, it is strictly visual and non-destructive, meaning framing hidden behind drywall, under insulation, or in inaccessible attics is not inspected.

### 4. What legal recourse does a buyer have under Johnson v. Davis for undisclosed termite damage?
Under *Johnson v. Davis*, a seller is legally obligated to disclose known material defects affecting property value that are not readily observable. If a seller conceals active termite infestations or structural damage, the buyer can sue for fraudulent concealment, seeking actual repair costs, contract rescission, and legal fees.
