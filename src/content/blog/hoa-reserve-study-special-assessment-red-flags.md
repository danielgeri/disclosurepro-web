When you buy a condo or townhome, inspecting the interior walls of your unit is only half of due diligence. You are also buying a fractional share of the building's **roof, elevators, balconies, siding, plumbing risers, parking garage, and master insurance policy**.

Yet most buyers receive a **300- to 600-page HOA resale disclosure packet**—containing CC&Rs, bylaws, budgets, financial statements, reserve studies, and 12 months of board meeting minutes—with just **3 to 5 days** to review it. Buried inside those PDFs are the warning signs of **$10,000 to $50,000+ special assessments**.

---

## 1. The Single Most Important Number: "Percent Funded" in the Reserve Study

An **HOA Reserve Study** measures how much cash the association currently holds in its reserve bank account compared to the accumulated depreciation of every shared component (roofs, asphalt, boilers, elevators, exterior paint, decks).

Jump directly to the **Executive Summary** of the Reserve Study and locate the **Percent Funded** metric:

- **70% – 100%+ Funded (Strong):** Low risk of surprise special assessments; routine replacements are covered by existing reserves.
- **30% – 69% Funded (Fair / Caution):** Moderate special-assessment or sharp dues-increase risk whenever major components (roofing, siding, elevators) reach year 0 of remaining useful life.
- **0% – 29% Funded (Critical Danger Zone):** High statistical likelihood of **multi-thousand-dollar special assessments** or deferred structural maintenance. Also note: **Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac tightening rules** can render severely underfunded or structurally deferred condos **"non-warrantable,"** making units much harder for future buyers to finance!

## 2. Master Insurance Deductibles & Premium Explosions

Across coastal Florida, California wildfire zones, Colorado hail corridors, and older multifamily buildings nationwide, HOA master property insurance premiums have doubled or tripled in recent years.

Check two places in the HOA budget and insurance certificate:
1. **Year-over-year insurance line item vs. budgeted dues:** Is the association running an operating deficit because insurance spiked 80% mid-year? That shortfall almost always becomes next January's 25% HOA fee hike.
2. **Wind/Hail or Water-Damage Master Deductible:** Many associations quietly raised master policy deductibles from $10,000 to **5% of building value ($100,000–$250,000+)**—requiring unit owners to carry much higher **HO-6 Loss Assessment coverage**.

## 3. Reading 12 Months of Board Meeting Minutes Like a Forensic Auditor

Sellers only have to disclose special assessments that have been **formally levied or noticed**—not assessments the board has been *debating* for eight months! That makes the **last 12–24 months of HOA Board Meeting Minutes** the single highest-signal document in the entire resale package.

Scan the minutes for these exact trigger phrases:
- *"Engineering proposal" / "structural envelope study" / "SB 326 balcony inspection"*
- *"Deferred until next fiscal year"*
- *"Loan options" / "SBA or bank line of credit" / "special assessment workshop"*
- *"Water intrusion in Units..." / "cast-iron stack leaks" / "galvanized re-pipe"*
- *"Executive session — pending litigation"* or *"construction defect counsel"*
- *"Delinquency rate"* (if >15% of unit owners are past due on monthly HOA fees, conventional mortgage lenders may reject the building!)

---

## 4. Rental Caps, Pet Rules & Short-Term Rental Bans in the CC&Rs

Never rely on verbal assurances from an agent (*"Yes, owners rent units here"* or *"Big dogs are fine"*). Verify the latest **recorded Amendments to the CC&Rs and Rules & Regulations** (often located at the *end* of a 200-page scanned PDF, overriding the original 1988 CC&Rs at the front):
- **Rental Cap Waitlists:** If the building caps rentals at 20% and is currently at 20% with a 4-year waitlist, your fallback plan to rent out the condo if relocated is blocked.
- **Breed / Weight / Pet-Count Limits:** E.g., 1 dog under 35 lbs.
- **Hardwood Flooring / Acoustic Underlayment Rules:** Upper-floor condos frequently prohibit removing carpet without expensive IIC/STC-rated soundproofing approval.

## Frequently Asked Questions About HOA Disclosure Packets

### What if the HOA hasn't conducted a Reserve Study in 5+ years?
Treat a missing or outdated (>3–5 years old) Reserve Study as a major red flag, especially in buildings over 15 years old. Construction costs have risen sharply, meaning older cost projections almost always underestimate actual roof, elevator, and facade replacement costs by 30%–50%.

### Who pays a special assessment if it is approved right before closing?
Check your state's purchase contract (for example, California C.A.R. RPA or Texas Condominium Resale Certificate addendum). Often, assessments **levied prior to close** can be negotiated onto the seller (or split), whereas assessments voted on *after* closing become 100% the buyer's responsibility—unless you negotiate an escrow holdback or closing credit during your HOA review contingency!
