## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Green Bay, WI

Green Bay's waterfront geography, alluvial soils along the Fox River, and historic construction methods create unique microclimates where subterranean termites thrive.

### How River Basin Moisture, Heated Basements, and Historic Foundations Harbor Pests

Several environmental and construction dynamics allow subterranean termites to survive and inflict severe damage in Green Bay:

- **Fox River Alluvial Moisture and Soil Hydrology:** Green Bay is bisected by the lower Fox River and flanked by the East River. Low-lying alluvial soils near the waterways maintain high subsoil moisture levels year-round. Subterranean termites require continuous ambient moisture to prevent desiccation; the river basin provides an inexhaustible subterranean reservoir.
- **The Heated Basement Survival Mechanism:** Subterranean termites cannot survive freezing body temperatures. In natural forests, winter freezes push colonies deep underground or kill off northern populations. However, in Green Bay's residential neighborhoods, heated basements, subterranean furnace rooms, and underground radiant heating lines create an artificial perpetual spring. Subterranean termites establish perennial colonies directly beneath basement concrete slabs, feeding on structural mudsills and floor joists 365 days a year.
- **Vulnerabilities in Green Bay Housing Stock:**
  - *Astor Park & Navarino Historic Districts:* Late 19th- and early 20th-century Victorian, Craftsman, and American Foursquare homes featuring stone rubble or early hollow-block foundations. Over decades, weathered lime mortar develops internal channels that allow subterranean termites to construct concealed mud tubes inside foundation walls, reaching massive first-floor carrying timbers without exposing themselves to view.
  - *Preble & Allouez Border:* 1950s and 1960s brick ranches with full basements where finished wood paneling and stud furring walls conceal perimeter foundation stem walls and moisture cracks from visual inspection.
  - *Downtown & Broadway District:* Mixed-use and residential frame properties where earth-to-wood contact at cellar entrances, historic bulkheads, and exterior porches provides direct access for subterranean mud tubes.

### Pest Biology: The Resilient Eastern Subterranean Termite

Understanding the biological profile of Wisconsin's primary wood-destroying pest is essential for evaluating property disclosures:

- **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** The only native subterranean termite species capable of establishing permanent colonies in Wisconsin. They live in subterranean colonies containing hundreds of thousands of workers. Because cold, dry air causes fatal dehydration, they build dense earthen mud shelter tubes composed of soil particles, saliva, and digested cellulose across concrete block, stone, and brick foundation walls.
- **Structural Feeding Habits:** Subterranean termites target the softer springwood fibers inside structural dimensional lumber, leaving the outer timber surface deceptively sound. A carrying beam or floor joist may appear intact on the outside while being entirely hollowed out within.
- **Indoor vs. Outdoor Swarming:** While outdoor swarms in Wisconsin typically occur on warm spring days in May or June, colonies living beneath heated residential basements frequently produce "false swarms" indoors during the winter months (January through March). Discovering hundreds of discarded insect wings around basement window wells, floor drains, or furnace rooms is conclusive evidence of an active, heated colony.

## Wisconsin Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Chapter 709 and the RECR

Wisconsin maintains strong statutory protections for residential real estate purchasers, codified under Wisconsin Statutes Chapter 709.

### The Wisconsin Real Estate Condition Report (RECR)

Under **Wis. Stat. § 709.02**, an owner selling residential real property consisting of one to four dwelling units must complete and deliver a statutory Real Estate Condition Report (RECR) to prospective buyers:

- **Mandatory Property Condition Questions (Wis. Stat. § 709.03):** The RECR requires the seller to disclose known "defects"—defined under Wisconsin law as conditions that have a significant adverse effect on the property's value, significantly impair health or safety, or significantly shorten the normal life of the premises.
- **Specific Structural and Pest Inquiries:**
  - *Section B.1:* Asks whether the seller is aware of defects in the foundation or foundation walls, including settling, cracking, or water infiltration.
  - *Section B.4:* Asks whether the seller is aware of defects in the structure, specifically including *"termites, carpenter ants, or other wood-destroying insects or organisms."*
  - *Section B.13:* Inquires about structural remodeling or repairs completed without required municipal building permits.
- **The Seller's Duty of Honesty:** Under Wisconsin common law and statutory disclosure rules, a seller cannot conceal known latent material defects. An owner who answers "No" to knowledge of wood-destroying insects despite having observed mud tubes, treated the foundation, or received past contractor warnings commits actionable statutory misrepresentation.
- **Buyer's Right of Rescission (Wis. Stat. § 709.05):** If a seller delivers an incomplete or untimely RECR, the prospective buyer has an absolute statutory right to rescind the purchase contract within two business days and receive a full return of their earnest money deposit.

### Deceptive Language in Green Bay Real Estate Disclosures

When reviewing disclosure packets in Green Bay, buyers should watch for ambiguous language designed to downplay structural pest histories:

- *"Basement stone foundation tuckpointed in 2021; preventative maintenance."* (Often conceals that mortar joints were repointed to cover up active subterranean termite mud tubes).
- *"Prior exterminator service called for seasonal basement insects."* (May disguise professional chemical treatments specifically targeting subterranean termites).
- *"Minor floor joist sistering completed near furnace during boiler update."* (Frequently hides the structural replacement of wood sills hollowed out by subterranean feeding).

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Green Bay, WI

### 1. Do termites really survive the freezing winters of Green Bay, Wisconsin?
Yes. While Wisconsin's subzero temperatures prevent subterranean termites from surviving in open, exposed topsoil, they survive in Green Bay by retreating deep underground below the frost line. In residential areas, they thrive year-round by exploiting the constant artificial warmth of heated basements, boiler systems, and sub-slab heating pipes.

### 2. What are a seller's legal disclosure obligations on the Wisconsin Real Estate Condition Report (RECR)?
Under Wis. Stat. § 709.03, sellers must disclose all known "defects" affecting the property. This explicitly includes known past or present infestations of termites, carpenter ants, or other wood-destroying organisms, as well as structural foundation damage. Willful misrepresentation or failure to disclose known defects exposes the seller to legal liability for actual repair damages.

### 3. How do subterranean termites enter homes with stone or concrete block basements in Green Bay?
Subterranean termites construct narrow earthen mud tubes to bridge non-wood materials. In older Green Bay homes with stone rubble or hollow concrete block foundations, weathered mortar creates interior hollow channels. Termites travel invisibly through the core of the foundation wall directly into the structural wooden sill plates.

### 4. Does a standard home inspection in Wisconsin include a certified termite inspection?
No. Standard Wisconsin home inspections are non-invasive visual examinations of accessible structural and mechanical systems. Home inspectors are not legally licensed pest control operators and standard inspection agreements expressly disclaim liability for concealed wood-destroying insect damage. Buyers must independently order a dedicated Wood-Destroying Insect (WDI) inspection.
