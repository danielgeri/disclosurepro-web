## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Aurora and the Fox River Valley

Aurora is bisected by the Fox River, creating an environment characterized by alluvial river soils, seasonal flooding risks, and high summer humidity. While freezing Illinois winters kill off many surface pests, subterranean termites thrive in this microclimate.

### Why Aurora's Geography and Housing Stock Elevate Termite Vulnerability

Several local factors create significant termite hazards across Aurora:

- **Fox River Moisture & Alluvial Soils:** Neighborhoods adjacent to the Fox River—such as the Near East Side, Near West Side, and Riverfront—sit on moisture-rich alluvial soils. High ground moisture allows subterranean termite colonies to expand and forage aggressively during spring and summer.
- **Winter Survival Beneath Heated Slabs:** During sub-zero prairie winters, subterranean termites migrate beneath the frost line or take refuge directly under warm basement slabs, radiant-heated floors, and boiler foundations, remaining active year-round.
- **Architectural Diversity & Timber Framing:**
  - *Tanner Historic District & Near East Side:* Built between the 1880s and 1920s, these historic Queen Anne Victorians, Italianates, and Craftsman homes feature rough-sawn dimensional pine framing, untreated sill plates resting on limestone or brick foundations, and damp stone basements. Termites exploit deteriorating mortar joints to access structural sills.
  - *Pigeon Hill & Boulevard District:* Post-WWII brick bungalows and cape cods featuring finished basements where wood paneling, carpet, and drywall conceal subterranean mud tubes from standard visual inspections.
  - *Far East Side & Waubonsie Valley:* 1970s through 2000s subdivisions built on concrete slab-on-grade foundations. Settling slabs allow subterranean termites to enter through plumbing penetrations around sewer pipes and expansion joints directly into interior partition walls.

### The Biology and Destruction Pattern of Eastern Subterranean Termites

The primary wood-destroying pest in Aurora is the **Eastern Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes flavipes*)**:

- **Colony Structure:** Mature colonies contain hundreds of thousands of workers living underground.
- **Foraging Dynamics:** Workers build earthen mud shelter tubes over foundation stone and concrete piers to protect themselves from low humidity.
- **Destruction Pattern:** They feed along the soft springwood grain of framing lumber, leaving the outer layer of wood paper-thin and intact. This masks catastrophic structural hollows in carrying beams, floor joists, and sill plates from casual walkthroughs.

## Illinois Real Estate Disclosure Laws: What Aurora Sellers Must Disclose

Illinois regulates seller property disclosures under the **Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act (765 ILCS 77/)**.

### The Statutory Illinois Disclosure Report

Under Illinois law, sellers of residential real estate must complete a mandatory statutory disclosure report containing 24 specific questions:

- **Item 19 (Wood-Destroying Insects):** Specifically asks the seller: *"I am aware of structural defects caused by previous infestations of termites or other wood boring insects."*
- **Item 18 (Current Infestations):** Asks the seller: *"I am aware of current infestations of termites or other wood boring insects."*
- **Item 1 (Flooding and Moisture):** Asks the seller: *"I am aware of flooding or recurring leakage problems in the crawlspace or basement."*

### The Official NPMA-33 Pest Inspection Report

In Illinois, pest inspections are conducted by technicians licensed by the **Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH)**. They issue the standardized **NPMA-33 Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Inspection Report**:

- **Section II (Inspection Findings):** Identifies active infestations, previous chemical drill holes, and visible structural timber damage.
- **Section IV (Inaccessible Areas):** Notes areas blocked from inspection by finished basement walls, drop ceilings, insulation, or stored personal items.

### What Aurora Sellers Legally Disclose vs. Strategically Conceal

Under 765 ILCS 77/25, a seller is liable only for defects of which they have *actual knowledge*. This creates opportunities for evasive phrasing:
- *"Basement perimeter treated in 2022 as part of regular preventative maintenance."* (Downplaying an active subterranean infestation that caused structural damage behind finished walls).
- *"Finished basement remodeled with new drywall and flooring in 2021."* (Masking that framing was installed over damaged, untreated foundation timbers without permits).
- *"Property sold As-Is by estate/investor."* Under Illinois law (*Zimmerman v. Northfield Real Estate*), selling "as-is" relieves the seller from making repairs, but does **not** exempt them from liability for fraudulent concealment of known material defects.

### The Risk of Missing Cryptic Notations

Without synthesizing these entries, a buyer will not realize that the springiness in the living room floor is caused by subterranean termites hollowing out the primary center carrying girder behind the basement ceiling.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Aurora

### 1. Are termites common in Aurora, Illinois?
Yes. The Fox River Valley is a prime habitat for Eastern Subterranean Termites due to sandy alluvial soils and river moisture. Subterranean termites remain active throughout cold Illinois winters by nesting beneath heated basement slabs.

### 2. What is an Illinois seller legally obligated to disclose regarding termites?
Under the Illinois Residential Real Property Disclosure Act (765 ILCS 77/), sellers must complete a 24-question disclosure form. Items 18 and 19 specifically require sellers to disclose known active infestations and known structural defects caused by previous wood-destroying insect infestations.

### 3. What is an NPMA-33 report in Illinois?
The NPMA-33 is the official Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) inspection form used by Illinois Department of Public Health licensed pest control operators. It details visible evidence of active or past wood-destroying insects (termites, carpenter ants, powderpost beetles) and inaccessible areas of the home.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Illinois?
No. Standard homeowner insurance policies in Illinois treat termite damage as a preventable maintenance issue, completely excluding structural termite repair costs from coverage.
