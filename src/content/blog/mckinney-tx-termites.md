## The Hidden Threat: Termites in McKinney and Collin County

McKinney’s geographical position in North Texas—characterized by dense, expansive clay soils, high summer humidity, and heavy spring storm systems—provides an optimal habitat for wood-destroying insect colonies.

### Why McKinney Housing Stock and North Texas Climate Elevate Pest Risk

Several architectural characteristics, soil mechanics, and regional climate patterns elevate termite vulnerability across McKinney:

- **Expansive Blackland Clay Soils and Slab Fractures:** McKinney's soil contains high concentrations of montmorillonite clay, which swells drastically during spring rains and shrinks during 100-degree summer heat. This constant soil expansion and contraction causes micro-fissures in post-tension concrete slabs, allowing subterranean termites to bypass exterior perimeter barriers and enter directly into interior structural walls.
- **Brick Veneer Weepholes and Concealed Air Gaps:** The vast majority of homes across Stonebridge Ranch, Craig Ranch, and Hardin Lake feature brick or stone veneer facades over timber framing. If exterior grading, landscaping mulch, or flower beds cover the bottom foundation weep holes, subterranean termites enter behind the brick facade and travel undetected into wall framing.
- **In-Slab Plumbing Block-Outs and Expansion Joints:** Monolithic slabs have cutouts around copper water supply lines, PVC sewer pipes, and bathtub traps. Subterranean termites require a gap of only 1/32 of an inch to migrate from the moist sub-slab soil directly into bathroom partition framing.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across McKinney Neighborhoods:**
  - *Historic Downtown McKinney & Tucker Hill:* 1890s–1930s historic pier-and-beam homes with crawl spaces, direct wood-to-earth contact at porch steps, and mature oak tree roots beneath foundations.
  - *Stonebridge Ranch & Craig Ranch:* 1990s–2010s large two-story homes featuring brick-and-stucco exteriors where extensive flower bed irrigation keeps soil continuously moist around foundation stems.
  - *Adriatica Village & Hardin Lake:* High-density residential developments where zero-lot-line construction and patio slabs frequently bridge exterior foundation inspection lines.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean vs. Formosan Termites in North Texas

McKinney property buyers must watch for two primary wood-destroying insect species:

- **Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*):** The most common wood-destroying organism in North Texas. These ground-dwelling insects live in large subterranean colonies and build mud shelter tubes over foundation stems to reach framing timber. They feed along the grain of the wood, eating the soft springwood while leaving the harder summerwood behind.
- **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** An extremely aggressive subterranean species expanding northward through Texas. Formosan colonies are significantly larger than native subterranean species and can construct secondary above-ground nests ("carton nests") inside wall cavities without returning to the soil if a moisture source (such as an AC condensation leak) is present.

## Texas Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Property Code § 5.008 and TREC WDI Reports

In Texas, residential real estate transactions are governed by comprehensive statutory disclosure mandates designed to inform prospective buyers of known material property defects.

### Texas Property Code § 5.008 and the TREC Seller's Disclosure Notice

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, residential sellers must deliver a completed statutory **Seller’s Disclosure Notice** (standardized by the Texas Real Estate Commission as TREC Form OP-H or Texas REALTORS® Form TXR-1406):

- **Section 4 (Pests & Termites):** Explicitly requires the seller to disclose:
  - *"Are you aware of any active termites or other wood-destroying insects (WDI)?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any termite or WDI damage needing repair?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any previous termite or WDI damage which has been repaired?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any previous termite or WDI treatment?"*
  - *"Is the property covered by a transferable termite warranty or service agreement?"*
- **Section 3 (Structural Components & Foundations):** Inquires into known foundation repairs, slab settlement, and plumbing leaks beneath slabs—factors that directly correlate with termite infestation risk.

Under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA), a seller who intentionally conceals known termite activity, prior treatments, or unaddressed structural timber damage faces severe liability, including treble damages and attorney fees.

### The Official Texas WDI Report (Form TDIR-1080)

In Texas, pest inspections are regulated by the **Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS)** under the **Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA)** and must be completed on standardized **Form TDIR-1080**:
- **Section 5 (Inspection Findings):** Documents visible active infestations, evidence of previous infestations, visible wood damage, and evidence of previous treatments (such as chemical drill holes along perimeter slabs).
- **Section 6 (Inaccessible Areas):** Identifies areas obstructed from view, including wall cavities covered by insulation, crawl spaces with low clearance, and slab perimeters blocked by heavy landscaping.
- **Section 7 (Conditions Conducive to Infestation):** Notes excessive moisture, wood-to-ground contact, form boards left in place, and buried weepholes.

### What McKinney Sellers Reveal vs. Strategically Conceal

Sellers in Collin County frequently use ambiguous statements on the Seller’s Disclosure Notice:
- *"Routine perimeter pest treatment applied in 2022."* (Conceals that perimeter trenching was done after termites were discovered in the kitchen wall).
- *"Sheetrock patched after minor settlement crack."* (Downplays the structural sistering of termite-hollowed wall studs behind the drywall).
- *"Transferable termite bond active."* (Fails to disclose that the bond only covers re-treatment and excludes structural damage repairs).

## The Manual Review Problem: Overlooking Crucial Clues in 200-Page Closing Packets

During Texas's standard 7-to-10-day Option Period under the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract, buyers must analyze a massive collection of documents:
- TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract and Option Period Addenda
- Texas Seller’s Disclosure Notice (TREC OP-H / TXR-1406)
- 40-to-75-page General Home Inspection Report
- Official Texas SPCS Wood Destroying Insect (WDI) Report (TDIR-1080)
- Collin County Property Tax Records, HOA Documents, and Title Commitments

### The Danger of Disconnected Clues

Material structural hazards rarely appear as an explicit, single warning. Instead, critical indicators are scattered across separate disclosures:
- The Home Inspector notes on page 46: *"Landscape mulch covers foundation brick weepholes along east exterior wall."*
- The WDI Pest Inspector records under Inaccessible Areas: *"Spray foam insulation in attic and finished walls prevented inspection of structural wall framing."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatments on the Seller's Disclosure, writing: *"Preventative perimeter barrier installed in 2023."*

When evaluated separately, each statement seems routine. But when cross-referenced, they reveal a dangerous reality: buried weepholes allowed subterranean termites to enter behind the brick veneer, tunnel upward into attic framing, and cause severe structural damage hidden behind spray foam insulation.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in McKinney

### 1. Are termites common in McKinney, Texas?
Yes. Eastern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes*) and Formosan Subterranean Termites are highly prevalent throughout McKinney and Collin County. The region's hot humid climate, expansive clay soils that cause slab settling cracks, and extensive brick veneer construction create prime opportunities for termite colonization.

### 2. What does Texas law require sellers to disclose about termites?
Under Texas Property Code § 5.008 and the TREC Seller’s Disclosure Notice, sellers must disclose all known active or past termite infestations, previous pest treatments, history of structural damage repairs, and whether an active transferable termite warranty exists.

### 3. What is an official Texas WDI Report (Form TDIR-1080)?
Form TDIR-1080 is the standardized Wood Destroying Insect inspection report regulated by the Texas Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control Service. It details active infestations, previous damage, treatment drill marks, inaccessible property areas, and conditions conducive to termite activity.

### 4. Does Texas homeowners insurance cover termite damage repairs?
No. Standard Texas homeowners insurance policies strictly exclude damage caused by termites and other wood-destroying insects. Insurance carriers classify pest damage as a preventable homeowner maintenance issue, leaving property owners responsible for all structural and cosmetic repair costs.
