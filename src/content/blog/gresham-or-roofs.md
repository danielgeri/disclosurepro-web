# The Ultimate Guide to Spotting Roof Damage in Gresham Real Estate Disclosures

Positioned dramatically at the eastern edge of the Portland metropolitan area in Multnomah County, Gresham serves as the vibrant gateway to the world-renowned Columbia River Gorge and Mount Hood National Forest. As Oregon’s fourth-largest city, Gresham combines thriving urban amenities, light rail connectivity to downtown Portland via the MAX Blue Line, and immediate access to outdoor recreation along the Springwater Corridor. The city’s residential neighborhoods reflect decades of Pacific Northwest architectural design: from the established 1970s and 1980s split-levels and Northwest contemporary homes nestled beneath towering mature Douglas firs in Kelly Creek, Holly Brook, and Powell Valley, to classic mid-century ranches in Mount Hood and Rockwood, to newer master-planned communities near Persimmon Country Club.

Yet behind Gresham’s lush green landscapes, snow-capped mountain views, and quiet suburban cul-de-sacs lies one of the most punishing roofing environments in the Pacific Northwest: **continuous, months-long rainfall exceeding 45 inches annually, heavy Douglas fir tree canopies fostering pervasive moss and lichen growth that lifts shingle tabs, hurricane-force winter winds funneling through the Columbia River Gorge gusting over 75 mph, and catastrophic freezing rain and ice storm events that overload roof eaves and collapse gutters**.

Under Oregon real estate law—codified strictly in **Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS 105.462 through 105.490)**—sellers of residential real property have a mandatory statutory duty to complete and deliver an official five-page **Seller's Property Disclosure Statement**. In Gresham’s competitive housing market, however, sellers routinely attempt to disguise chronic moisture damage with aggressive pressure-washing, mask rotted cedar-shake conversions behind architectural overlays, or use vague language on statutory disclosure forms to evade costly reroofing obligations. Protecting your investment before your statutory right of revocation expires demands sharp technical discernment and an in-depth understanding of Oregon real property law.

## Why Gresham Roofs Fail: Gorge Winds, Rampant Moss, and Winter Ice Storms

Gresham's unique geographic position at the mouth of the Columbia River Gorge exposes residential roof systems to extreme meteorological forces distinct from the rest of the Willamette Valley.

### Fierce Columbia River Gorge Winds and Tab Delamination
Gresham sits directly in the crosshairs of the Columbia River Gorge wind funnel:
- **The Gorge Gap Wind Phenomenon:** In autumn and winter, dense cold high-pressure air masses over the Columbia Basin in eastern Oregon and Washington seek escape through the narrow sea-level breach of the Columbia River Gorge. As this air accelerates through the gap, it strikes Gresham with sustained gale-force winds and localized gusts exceeding 65 to 80 mph.
- **Aerodynamic Uplift Forces:** High-velocity winds create immense negative pressure (uplift) across roof ridges, hips, and eave overhangs. Once persistent dampness and cold weather have degraded the shingle's self-sealing adhesive strip, high winds easily peel the unbonded shingle tabs upward.
- **Fatigue and Fastener Blow-Off:** Repeated wind flexing bends shingles back and forth along the nail line, fatiguing the brittle fiberglass mat until entire shingle tabs tear free, exposing underlying roof sheathing to driving horizontal rain.

### Relentless Rainfall and Pervasive Moss Infestation
Gresham receives over 45 inches of precipitation annually, characterized by months of low-intensity drizzle and high atmospheric humidity:
- **The Tree Canopy Microclimate:** Neighborhoods like Kelly Creek and Powell Valley are blanketed by dense stands of towering Douglas firs, western red cedars, and bigleaf maples. These trees deposit continuous organic litter and block sunlight from reaching roof surfaces.
- **Biological Wedging:** Moss (*Dicranum* and *Bryum* species), lichen, and algae thrive in this damp, shaded environment. Moss acts like a dense structural sponge, retaining moisture directly against the shingle surface for weeks. As moss colonies grow, their root-like rhizoids wedge beneath shingle edges, physically lifting the shingles and breaking their factory seal strips.
- **The Destructive Pressure-Washing Cover-Up:** When homeowners prepare to sell, they often hire unlicensed handymen to pressure-wash heavy moss off the roof. High-pressure water blasts away the protective ceramic mineral granules, gouges the asphalt compound, and drives pressurized water backward beneath shingle laps, saturating roof decking right before open houses.

### Catastrophic Winter Ice Storms and Eave Overloading
Gresham's proximity to the Gorge makes it the epicenter for severe Pacific Northwest freezing rain events (known locally as "silver thaws"):
- **Sub-Freezing Gorge Air Inflow:** Cold easterly surface air pouring out of the Gorge undercuts warm, moist Pacific storms. Rain falling from the warm upper atmosphere freezes instantly upon contact with cold rooftops.
- **Extreme Ice Accumulation:** Ice storms can deposit 1 to 2 inches of solid radial glaze ice across roof decks, gutters, and overhangs within hours. This adds thousands of pounds of dead load to structural rafter tails.
- **Gutters Torn from Fascia:** Saturated, ice-weighted gutters pull away from fascia boards, tearing perimeter drip edge flashing and allowing melting ice water to back up directly into soffits and exterior wall cavities.

### Cedar Shake Conversion Deficiencies
Many homes built in Gresham during the 1970s and 1980s originally featured western red cedar shake roofs:
- **Skip Sheathing Transitions:** Cedar shakes were historically installed over 1x4 or 1x6 spaced ("skip") sheathing to allow the wood shakes to breathe. When converted to modern asphalt composition shingles, contractors were required to install continuous 1/2-inch CDX plywood or OSB over the spaced boards.
- **Unpermitted Overlay Shortcuts:** Unscrupulous contractors frequently nailed thin 3/8-inch or 7/16-inch OSB directly over old, sagging skip sheathing without proper structural fasteners, or failed to replace dry-rotted skip boards along eaves and valleys. These improper conversions create soft, spongy roof decks that deflect under foot traffic and pull roofing nails loose over time.

## Navigating Oregon Real Estate Disclosure Laws in Gresham

Oregon maintains strict statutory disclosure requirements designed to protect residential real estate purchasers, codified in **Oregon Revised Statutes § 105.462 through § 105.490**.

### The Oregon Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (ORS 105.464)
Under Oregon law, a seller of single-family residential property must deliver a completed statutory **Seller's Property Disclosure Statement** to every prospective buyer:
- **Section 2 (Roof):** The statutory form explicitly requires the seller to answer:
  - *"Type of roof."*
  - *"Approximate age of roof."*
  - *"Has the roof leaked?"* (Yes / No / Unknown). If yes, the seller must specify whether it has been repaired.
  - *"Are there any known problems with the roof?"*
  - *"Has the roof been replaced?"* If yes, the seller must state the date of replacement and whether a building permit was obtained.
  - *"Have any roof repairs been made during your ownership?"*
- **Material Inaccuracies:** The seller must complete the disclosure based on their actual personal knowledge. Providing inaccurate, evasive, or intentionally misleading answers violates Oregon statutory law.

### The Buyer's Mandatory 5-Day Right of Statutory Revocation (ORS 105.475)
Oregon law provides buyers with an extraordinary statutory protection:
- Under **ORS 105.475**, if a buyer receives the Seller's Property Disclosure Statement, they have the absolute statutory right to revoke their offer and terminate the transaction within **five (5) business days** after delivery of the disclosure statement.
- If the seller fails or refuses to deliver the disclosure statement, the buyer's right of revocation continues all the way until closing. Upon exercising this statutory revocation, the buyer is entitled to the immediate return of their entire earnest money deposit without penalty.

### Oregon Common Law Fraud and Landmark Caselaw (*Heverly v. Kirkendall*)
Oregon courts strictly enforce honesty in real estate transactions and reject attempts to hide behind boilerplate contract language:
- In the landmark Oregon Supreme Court decision ***Heverly v. Kirkendall*, 257 Or. 232, 478 P.2d 381 (1970)**, the court established that a seller who makes an affirmative representation regarding property condition or conceals a known defect is liable for common law fraud, and an "as-is" clause will not protect the seller from fraudulent nondisclosure.
- In ***Bodenhamer v. Patterson*, 278 Or. 367, 563 P.2d 1212 (1977)**, Oregon courts confirmed that active concealment—such as pressure-washing moss to hide disintegrated shingles, painting over ceiling water stains, or concealing unpermitted reroofing—constitutes actionable fraud that entitles the buyer to rescission of the contract or monetary damages.

### City of Gresham Building Codes and Permitting Regulations
The **City of Gresham Building Division** enforces the 2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC, based on the International Residential Code):
- **Mandatory Building Permits:** A residential building permit is legally mandatory for any reroofing project in Gresham that involves replacing structural roof sheathing, adding a second layer of roofing, or altering roof framing.
- **Maximum Overlay Layers (ORSC R908.3.1.1):** Oregon building code permits a maximum of two layers of roof coverings. However, installing a second layer over an existing wood shake roof or over an asphalt roof that is water-soaked, curled, or severely degraded is strictly prohibited. If a home already has two layers of roofing, a complete tear-off down to the bare structural deck is mandatory.
- **High-Wind Fastening Requirements:** Due to the severe Gorge wind hazard, Gresham building code requires shingles installed in designated high-wind exposure zones to be rated under ASTM D7158 (Class H, tested to 150 mph) or ASTM D3161 (Class F, tested to 110 mph) and fastened with six corrosion-resistant roofing nails per shingle rather than the standard four.

## The Manual Review Problem: Drowning in Oregon Due Diligence

Under the standard Oregon Real Estate Forms (OREF) purchase contract, buyers typically negotiate a **Professional Inspection Contingency Period of ten (10) business days**.

During this narrow window, prospective purchasers must review and cross-examine extensive technical documentation:
- The completed five-page Oregon Seller's Property Disclosure Statement.
- City of Gresham municipal building permit records to confirm whether past reroofing or additions were permitted and inspected.
- The property's Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange (C.L.U.E.) report detailing past storm and water claims.
- General home inspection reports spanning 40 to 65 pages that routinely disclaim comprehensive evaluation of steep or wet roofs.

### The Wet Weather Inspection Hazard
In Gresham, a large percentage of home transactions take place during autumn, winter, or spring when roofs are wet, covered in pine needles, or slick with algae:
- Professional home inspectors in Oregon are restricted by safety standards from walking steep, wet composition shingle roofs due to severe slipping hazards.
- Inspection reports routinely contain standard disclaimers such as: *"Roof covering was wet and moss-covered at time of inspection; inspected from eave ladder positions and ground level only. Condition of unbonded shingle tabs, underlayment, and valley flashings could not be determined."*
- When the physical inspection is limited by Pacific Northwest rain, the buyer's financial protection relies heavily on the honesty of the seller's written disclosure statement.

### Evasive Phrasing Sellers Use in Gresham Disclosures
Sellers in Gresham frequently employ ambiguous language on disclosure forms to avoid funding expensive roof replacements before closing:
- *"Roof treated annually with zinc sulphate moss control; normal Pacific Northwest weathering noted."* (Conceals that heavy moss growth has already lifted shingle laps and rotted the plywood sheathing).
- *"Roof evaluated following 2023 Gorge wind event; loose shingles resealed with mastic."* (Hides widespread tab delamination and uncompleted insurance claims).
- *"Minor ceiling discoloration in vaulted dining room attributed to past resolved skylight condensation."* (Masks chronic water intrusion from a rusted valley pan).
- *"Roof converted from cedar shakes to composition shingles prior to ownership; solid condition."* (Disguises an unpermitted overlay installed over rotted skip sheathing).

Deciphering these subtle evasions across dozens of disclosure pages is critical to avoiding catastrophic post-closing restoration expenses.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Gresham, OR

### 1. Does the City of Gresham require a building permit for roof replacement?
Yes. The City of Gresham Building Division requires a residential building permit for reroofing projects that involve replacing structural roof sheathing, installing an overlay, or making alterations to roof framing. Permitted work must comply with the 2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC). City building inspectors verify structural roof decking integrity, inspect flashing assemblies, and confirm proper high-wind fastening before issuing final approval.

### 2. What is the statutory 5-day right of revocation under Oregon disclosure law?
Under Oregon Revised Statutes (ORS 105.475), a homebuyer has the unconditional statutory right to revoke their offer and terminate the transaction within five (5) business days after the delivery of the Seller's Property Disclosure Statement. If a buyer exercises this right within the statutory timeframe, they are entitled to the immediate return of their entire earnest money deposit without penalty.

### 3. Why is pressure-washing a roof dangerous in the Pacific Northwest?
While pressure-washing quickly removes visible moss, commercial pressure washers use intense hydraulic force that strips away the protective mineral granules embedded in asphalt composition shingles. Granule loss leaves the bare asphalt compound directly exposed to solar UV deterioration, cracks the internal fiberglass mat, and drives pressurized water backward beneath shingle laps, leading to premature roof failure and deck rot.

### 4. Why are high-wind rated shingles necessary in Gresham?
Due to Gresham’s proximity to the Columbia River Gorge, winter storms frequently generate sustained winds and gusts exceeding 65 to 80 mph. Standard four-nail shingle installations are highly susceptible to wind uplift and tab tear-off under these conditions. Building codes and roofing best practices in Gresham mandate shingles rated under ASTM D7158 (Class H, 150 mph) or ASTM D3161 (Class F, 110 mph), installed with an enhanced six-nail fastening pattern.
