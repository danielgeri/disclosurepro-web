## Why Dallas Homes Face Severe Subterranean & Formosan Termite Pressure

Dallas real estate combines three structural risk multipliers that make surface-only walk-throughs unreliable: expansive clay soil movement, a massive inventory of historic pier-and-beam bungalows, and aggressive Gulf Coast pest migration into North Texas.

### Local Termite Species Threatening Dallas–Fort Worth Properties

1. **Eastern & Dark Southern Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes flavipes* and *R. virginicus*):** Native across North Texas, these soil-nesting colonies enter slab-on-grade homes through expansion joints, hairline Blackland Prairie clay settlement cracks, and open plumbing bath-trap cutouts. In pier-and-beam homes, they build pencil-width earthen mud shelter tubes straight up cedar posts, concrete piers, and perimeter skirting into floor joists and subflooring.
2. **Formosan Subterranean Termites (*Coptotermes formosanus*):** Once confined mainly to Houston and coastal Texas, Formosan colonies have established verified pockets across the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex—frequently introduced through recycled railroad-tie retaining walls and landscape mulch. Formosan colonies grow **five to ten times larger** than native subterranean colonies and can construct moisture-retaining **aerial carton nests** inside exterior walls or flat-roof cavities without continuous soil contact.
3. **Drywood Termites (Occasional Localized Introductions):** Less widespread than subterranean colonies in North Texas, but occasionally imported via antique furniture, reclaimed lumber, or attic framing—identified by uniform, sand-like fecal pellet (**frass**) piles rather than mud tubes.

## Texas Termite Disclosure Laws: Property Code § 5.008, TREC OP-H & TDA WDI Reports

Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, almost all sellers of single-family residential properties in Dallas must deliver a written **Seller's Disclosure Notice**—typically **TREC Form OP-H** or the expanded **Texas REALTORS® Form TXR-1406**—disclosing all known material conditions affecting the home. Selling a Dallas property "As-Is" under the TREC One to Four Family Residential Contract (Resale) does **not** excuse a seller from disclosing known active termites, past chemical treatments, or unrepaired structural damage.

### Exact Termite Questions Buyers Must Audit on TREC Form OP-H / TXR-1406

Review **Section 3 (Awareness of Conditions)** and **Section 4 (Prior Repairs & Inspections)** closely for four high-value answers:
- **Active Termites or Wood-Destroying Insects:** Whether the seller is aware of any current live infestation.
- **Previous Termite / WDI Treatment:** Whether the property has ever been treated for termites—and whether an active transferable termite bond or retreat/repair warranty exists.
- **Previous Termite or Wood-Rot Damage:** Whether structural framing, siding, or subfloors suffered past WDI damage (even if the seller claims the issue was "fixed years ago").
- **Prior WDI or Inspection Reports in Seller's Possession:** Texas REALTORS® Form TXR-1406 specifically asks sellers to disclose and attach copies of prior inspection or WDI reports received within the **previous four years**.

For a complete breakdown of every checkbox and statutory rescission trap across Texas forms, see our [Texas TREC Seller's Disclosure Notice guide](/blog/texas-trec-sellers-disclosure-guide/).

### Decoding the Official Texas TDA NPMA-33 WDI Report

In Texas, a standard **TREC-licensed home inspector** cannot issue an official termite report unless they also hold a separate structural pest license from the **Texas Department of Agriculture (TDA) Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS)**. Always order an official **TDA NPMA-33 Wood-Destroying Insect Report** during your Option Period and scrutinize three sections buyers overlook:

- **Section 6B & 6C (Obstructed or Inaccessible Areas):** Lists every zone the WDI technician could not see—such as slab flooring under glued hardwood, finished sheetrock walls, spray-foamed pier-and-beam undersides, or low-clearance crawlspaces. "No Visible Evidence" on Page 1 **never guarantees obstructed walls are termite-free**.
- **Section 7 (Conducive Conditions):** Flags physical defects inviting imminent infestation in Dallas soils: form boards left behind in crawlspaces, wood mulch piled against brick weep holes, stucco/stone veneer below grade, cellulose scrap debris under pier-and-beam floors, and faulty lot drainage.
- **Section 10 (Graph / Diagram):** Maps exact locations of live termites, dead wings, mud tubes, previous drill-hole treatment plugs, and structural wood damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Termite Disclosures & Inspections in Dallas, TX

### Are Dallas home sellers required to disclose past termite treatment or damage?
Yes. Under **Texas Property Code § 5.008**, sellers of residential single-family homes must complete the Seller's Disclosure Notice (TREC OP-H or TXR-1406) truthfully, including disclosing known active termites, previous termite treatments, and previous termite structural damage—even when selling a property "As-Is."

### Who pays for the termite inspection and treatment when buying a house in Dallas?
In Texas, the **buyer** normally pays the $85–$175 TDA NPMA-33 WDI inspection fee during the Option Period (including on VA loans, where updated VA guidelines allow buyers to pay pest inspection fees if agreed in the contract). If active termites or structural damage are found, **treatment and repair costs are fully negotiable**—and on VA loans or lender-conditioned FHA loans, active infestation clearance is typically negotiated for the seller to fund prior to closing.

### Can termites get through a concrete slab foundation in Dallas?
Yes. Subterranean termites do not eat concrete, but they squeeze through cracks as narrow as **1/32 of an inch**—the thickness of a credit card. Dallas's expansive Blackland Prairie clay soil shifts seasonally, opening slab settlement fractures, expansion joints, and under-slab plumbing bath traps that give colonies hidden entry straight into interior framing.

### How fast can Formosan or subterranean termites cause structural damage in North Texas?
Native Eastern Subterranean colonies typically take 3 to 8 years to inflict severe structural compromise, whereas an established **Formosan Subterranean colony** housing millions of insects—especially if fed by an under-slab plumbing leak or damp crawlspace—can cause measurable structural damage to sill plates, wall studs, and headers in **12 to 24 months**.
