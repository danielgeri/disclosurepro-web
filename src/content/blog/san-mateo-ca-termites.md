## The Hidden Threat: Termites and Wood-Decay Across San Mateo, CA

San Mateo’s coastal geography, fog dynamics, and historic residential construction create distinct pathways for wood-destroying organisms.

### How Marine Fog, Peninsula Soils, and Historic Mudsills Accelerate Infestations

Several localized environmental and structural factors make San Mateo homes prime targets for wood-destroying pests:

- **Marine Fog and Persistent Ambient Humidity:** During the summer months, ocean fog rolls across the coastal range, settling over the Peninsula from evening until midday. This persistent moisture cools exterior timbers and prevents crawlspace soils from drying out. When combined with dense shade from mature redwoods, oaks, and eucalyptus trees in neighborhoods like San Mateo Park and Baywood, exterior wood framing remains chronically damp, creating an ideal habitat for both subterranean and dampwood termites.
- **San Francisco Bay Alluvium and Shallow Aquifers:** Low-lying neighborhoods bordering the bay, such as Shoreview and Marina Lagoon, sit on unconsolidated bay mud and alluvium with high groundwater tables. Saturated soil beneath concrete slabs and raised foundations provides an inexhaustible moisture reservoir for subterranean termite colonies.
- **Architectural Stock and Construction Vulnerabilities:**
  - *Baywood & Aragon:* 1920s and 1930s period-revival homes featuring unreinforced masonry foundations, brick stem walls, and redwood mudsills resting directly on soil or mortar with minimal ground clearance. Decaying perimeter mortar allows subterranean termites to tunnel invisibly into structural subfloors.
  - *San Mateo Park:* Historic early 20th-century estates designed with intricate exterior wood siding, exposed rafter tails, and shaded eaves. Prolonged moisture retention in these shaded eaves creates dry rot pockets that attract airborne drywood and dampwood swarmers.
  - *Shoreview & Beresford Park:* 1950s single-story California ranches built on perimeter concrete footings with shallow crawlspaces. Insufficient foundation vents and lack of modern poly vapor barriers allow subterranean termites to build mud shelter tubes up interior concrete piers into structural floor joists.

### Pest Biology: Subterranean, Drywood, and Dampwood Termites

Understanding the biological profiles of Peninsula timber pests is essential when reviewing disclosure documents:

- **Western Subterranean Termite (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** The most widespread structural pest in Northern California. Colonies live underground in moist soil and can contain hundreds of thousands of workers. They construct vertical earthen mud tubes across foundation stems to reach wood framing, feeding along the soft springwood grain.
- **Western Drywood Termite (*Incisitermes minor*):** Independent of soil moisture, drywood termites colonize dry structural lumber, attic rafters, window jambs, and fascia boards. They produce tiny, grooved, hexagonal fecal pellets that accumulate in distinctive mounds beneath active infestation sites.
- **Pacific Dampwood Termite (*Zootermopsis angusticollis*):** The largest termite species in North America, dampwood termites are exceptionally common in fog-shrouded Peninsula communities. They do not require soil contact but require wood with high moisture content, often invading framing softened by plumbing leaks, roof valley overflows, or chronic marine condensation.
- **Swarming Patterns in San Mateo:** Subterranean termites swarm on warm, sunny mornings following early spring rains from February through April. Drywood termites swarm during warm, clear afternoons in September and October. Dampwood termites swarm primarily on humid late-summer and autumn evenings just after sunset.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Civil Code § 1102 and SPCB Requirements

California law provides extensive protections for residential property purchasers, placing clear legal burdens on sellers to disclose known property defects.

### California Civil Code § 1102 and the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS)

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of one-to-four unit residential properties must provide a completed Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) and Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):

- **Section II.C of the TDS:** Specifically requires the seller to declare whether they are aware of any *"Substances, materials, or products which may be an environmental hazard... or Wood Destroying Pests/Organisms."*
- **The Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):** Section V.A of the standard California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) SPQ form directly asks: *"Are you (Seller) aware of: (1) Any past or present infestation, damage, or treatment of termites, dry rot, or other wood-destroying organisms? (2) Any past or present structural repairs or alterations made to address pest damage?"*
- **Affirmative Duty of Truthfulness:** Disclosures must be made in good faith. A seller who answers "No" despite having observed termite droppings, obtained pest control estimates, or completed cosmetic cover-ups is liable for intentional misrepresentation or fraudulent concealment.
- **Statutory Right of Cancellation:** Under California Civil Code § 1102.3, if the TDS or any material amendment is delivered after contract execution, the buyer has a statutory right to cancel the agreement within three days of personal delivery (or five days if delivered by mail or electronic transmission).

### The California Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) WDO Inspection Report

In California, pest inspections are conducted by licensed Branch 3 Structural Pest Control operators and documented on a standardized Structural Pest Control Inspection Report:

- **Section 1 Items:** Conditions with active infestations or infections (active subterranean, drywood, or dampwood termites, or active wood decay fungus) or structural damage resulting from them. In standard purchase negotiations, buyers frequently request sellers to pay for complete Section 1 clearance before closing.
- **Section 2 Items:** Conditions deemed conducive to infestation or infection, such as earth-to-wood contact, plumbing leaks, faulty caulking, or lack of subfloor ventilation, where no active damage is yet evident.
- **Notice of Work Completed (Form 43M-44):** If the seller performed pest remediation prior to listing, California law requires them to furnish copies of all previous inspection reports, structural certifications, and official Notices of Work Completed filed with the SPCB within the preceding two years.

### Deceptive Disclosure Language in Peninsula Transactions

Buyers in San Mateo should watch for misleading phrases commonly used in seller disclosures:

- *"Section 1 clearance obtained prior to listing."* (Often masks the fact that the pest company performed cheap localized chemical injection while leaving structurally hollowed timbers in place).
- *"Periodic preventative pest maintenance performed under service plan."* (Frequently obscures uncompleted structural repairs that were deemed too expensive).
- *"Fascia boards repaired and repainted prior to marketing."* (Can hide structural rafter decay caused by drywood termites without addressing internal attic framing).

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in San Mateo, CA

### 1. Why are dampwood termites found in San Mateo when they are rare in other parts of California?
San Mateo’s maritime microclimate—marked by persistent summer marine fog, cool coastal air, and dense mature tree canopies—creates high-humidity microclimates. Pacific dampwood termites require timber with continuous high moisture content to survive. Crawlspaces with low clearance, unvented foundations, and roof runoff create the exact damp conditions dampwood termites need to flourish.

### 2. What is the difference between Section 1 and Section 2 on a California pest report?
In California, a Branch 3 Wood Destroying Pest and Organisms Inspection Report classifies findings into two categories. Section 1 covers conditions with active infestations or infections (live termites, beetles, or active wood decay fungus) and resulting damage that requires immediate treatment or repair. Section 2 covers conditions deemed conducive to infestation (such as plumbing leaks, earth-to-wood contact, or faulty subfloor ventilation) that should be corrected to prevent future pest invasion.

### 3. Can a seller in San Mateo legally sell a home "AS IS" without fixing termite damage?
Yes, a home can be sold "AS IS" in California. However, an "AS IS" clause only means the seller is not contractually obligated to pay for repairs; it does not exempt the seller from California Civil Code § 1102 disclosure requirements. The seller is still legally required to disclose all known termite infestations, previous treatments, and structural damage on the TDS and SPQ forms.

### 4. Is whole-house fumigation always necessary for drywood termites in San Mateo?
While localized spot treatments (such as chemical injection or heat treatments) can eradicate small, accessible colonies, whole-house fumigation (tenting with sulfuryl fluoride) remains the only foolproof method to guarantee complete eradication of drywood termites nesting throughout inaccessible roof trusses, wall framing, and vaulted ceilings.
