## Silicon Valley Structural Risks: Termite Pressures in Mountain View

Acquiring residential property in Mountain View requires a granular understanding of how regional geology, housing architecture, and wood-destroying insect biology converge.

### Biology and Mechanics of Local Termite Species

Residential framing in Mountain View is subjected to persistent structural attacks from two distinct biological organisms:

- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** These ground-nesting insects build expansive underground colonies numbering hundreds of thousands of workers. Because they are vulnerable to dehydration, they maintain continuous contact with subterranean ground moisture. To breach residential foundations, they construct protective mud tubes made of soil, saliva, and digested cellulose across concrete stem walls, plumbing chases, and interior slab seams. Subterranean termites devour structural Douglas fir timber along the soft annual growth rings, excavating internal galleries while leaving the outer wood shell deceptively intact.
- **Western Drywood Termites (*Incisitermes minor*):** In contrast to subterranean termites, drywood termites require no contact with soil or external water sources. They extract all necessary hydration directly from atmospheric moisture and the wood they consume. Entering homes aerially during swarming flights, they colonize sound structural timbers, rafters, and exterior fascia boards, carving wide, clean chambers against the wood grain.
- **Swarming Patterns Across Santa Clara County:** Drywood termites swarm during warm, sunny autumn afternoons (typically September through November), particularly when warm Santa Ana or offshore wind conditions push temperatures upward. Subterranean termites swarm primarily during warm, sunny mornings immediately following early spring rainfall (February through April).
- **Concealed Structural Excavation:** Because drywood colonies live entirely inside timber and seal their exterior entry portals, an infestation can progress silently for five to ten years before cosmetic paint cracking or external frass accumulations reveal the underlying structural collapse.

### Mountain View Architectural and Environmental Risk Factors

Mountain View’s distinct housing stock presents unique structural interfaces that heighten termite vulnerabilities:

- **Mid-Century Modern Eichler Homes (Monta Loma & Fairview):** Joseph Eichler homes feature concrete slab-on-grade construction with embedded radiant heating pipes, open-beam tongue-and-groove ceilings, and floor-to-ceiling glass walls. The radiant floor warmth creates year-round ground temperatures that prevent subterranean termites from going dormant during winter. Furthermore, horizontal post-and-beam construction and exposed exterior roof overhangs offer direct airborne landing zones for drywood termites.
- **Historic Downtown Bungalows (Old Mountain View & Castro Street Corridor):** 1920s and 1930s Craftsman residences feature raised crawlspace foundations with unreinforced masonry, inadequate crawlspace ventilation, and original old-growth fir or redwood mudsills resting close to damp soil grades, creating ideal ground-level bridgeheads for subterranean mud tubes.
- **Post-War Suburban Ranches (Waverly Park & Cuesta Park):** Homes constructed in the 1950s and 1960s commonly incorporate exterior stucco siding that extends below the exterior soil line, concealing mud tube construction from casual exterior visual inspections.
- **Mature Tree Canopies and Yard Irrigation:** Heavy urban canopy coverage, dense perimeter redwood trees, and automated sprinkler systems spraying against exterior home foundations maintain elevated soil saturation levels year-round.

### Physical Indicators Mountain View Buyers Must Identify

During on-site open houses and private walkthroughs in Mountain View, buyers should watch for critical warning signs:

- **Hexagonal Drywood Termite Frass:** Tiny, hard, sand-like fecal pellets with distinct six-sided concave grooves collecting on window sills, along baseboard ledges, or beneath exposed garage rafters.
- **Subterranean Mud Shelter Tubes:** Earthen, pencil-wide tubes extending along concrete perimeter stem walls, crawling up water heater platforms, or spanning gaps in utility closets.
- **Blistered Baseboards and Wavy Trim:** Wood surfaces along window mullions, sliding door sills, or door jambs that exhibit rippling paint or cave in under light pressure from an awl or fingernail.
- **Hollow Sounds in Structural Posts:** Structural 4x4 or 4x6 roof posts that produce a hollow, papery sound when tapped with a brass hammer or the handle of a screwdriver.

## California Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Seller Obligations in Mountain View

Residential property transactions in Mountain View are subject to statutory disclosure rules governed by California state law, designed to protect purchasers from undisclosed structural defects.

### Statutory Disclosures: The California TDS and SPQ

Under **California Civil Code § 1102 et seq.**, sellers of one-to-four residential units must complete and provide two mandatory statutory disclosure packets:

- **Real Estate Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS):**
  - *Section II.C:* Specifically requires sellers to disclose whether they are aware of any structural defects, environmental hazards, or past damage to walls, foundations, floors, and roofs.
  - *Agent Visual Inspection Disclosure (AVID):* Under California Civil Code § 2079 and the seminal decision in *Easton v. Strassburger* (1984), licensed listing and buyer brokers must conduct a competent, diligent visual inspection of accessible areas and disclose all red flags in writing.
- **Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ):**
  - *Section V.A:* Directly asks whether the seller has knowledge of any "Pests, wood-destroying pests or organisms, or pest control inspections or treatments." If answered affirmatively, the seller is legally obligated to explain the complete background, specify treatment dates, and produce all past inspection reports, warranty documents, and completion notices.

### The Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB) and Section 1 vs. Section 2

In California residential transactions, wood-destroying organism inspections are performed by Branch 3 licensed operators regulated by the **California Structural Pest Control Board (SPCB)**. A standard Wood Destroying Pest and Organisms Inspection Report segregates findings into two distinct categories:

- **Section 1 Conditions:** Active infestations, infections, or visible physical damage resulting from wood-destroying pests (such as live subterranean termite activity in a crawlspace, active drywood colonies inside attic beams, or decayed structural mudsills). In standard California Association of Realtors (C.A.R.) purchase agreements, Section 1 items are typically negotiated for seller repair or financial credit.
- **Section 2 Conditions:** Conditions deemed conducive to future infestations or infections, where no active pest activity is currently observed (such as earth-to-wood contact at exterior siding, faulty perimeter grading, or plumbing leaks under sinks).
- **The Partial Clearance Trap:** Sellers may present a "Standard Notice of Work Completed" clearing specific localized items while leaving expansive Section 1 recommendations in inaccessible attic or subarea sections untouched.

### Judicial Precedents and Seller Liability in California

Under California jurisprudence, sellers cannot rely on "as-is" boilerplate language to shield themselves from intentional fraud or material nondisclosure:

- **Affirmative Duty of Truthful Disclosure:** When an owner knows of active termite colonies or weakened structural posts, intentionally omitting or downplaying those facts constitutes actionable fraud and negligent misrepresentation (*Shapiro v. Sutherland*, 1998).
- **Statutory Damages and Rescission:** Buyers who discover undisclosed termite destruction following the close of escrow may file suit to recover total repair costs, loss in property value, and reasonable attorney's fees under California Civil Code § 1102.13.

### Misleading Terminology and Euphemisms

Sellers and listing agents frequently utilize euphemistic statements on disclosure paperwork that mask severe structural damage:

- **"Property received localized spot treatment for drywood termites in 2024":** Localized pesticide injections or heat treatments address only small isolated pockets. If drywood termites have penetrated deeper structural ridge beams, spot treatments leave hidden colonies active.
- **"Previous Section 1 termite clearance obtained by prior owner":** A past clearance certificate merely reflects conditions on the specific day of that prior inspection. It provides zero guarantee that subterranean mud tubes have not breached slab cracks in the intervening years.
- **"Portions of subfloor and attic framing inaccessible due to low roof pitch and ducting":** Disclaimers regarding restricted access areas frequently obscure extensive colony activity situated directly above main living spaces.
- **"Minor wood filler applied to exterior rafter tails for aesthetic upkeep":** Cosmetic repairs using epoxy or exterior Bondo often mask rotten and hollowed structural rafters that require complete structural replacement.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Mountain View, CA

### 1. Are termites common in Mountain View and the Silicon Valley area?
Yes. Mountain View experiences continuous pressure from both Western subterranean termites and Western drywood termites. The mild coastal climate, warm summers, and mature wood-framed housing stock—particularly mid-century post-and-beam homes—provide ideal living conditions for wood-destroying insects.

### 2. Can whole-house tent fumigation be performed on flat-roof Eichler homes?
Yes, but it requires specialized preparation. While tent fumigation with sulfuryl fluoride gas effectively eradicates drywood termite colonies throughout tongue-and-groove roof decks and structural beams, it does not treat subterranean termites living beneath concrete slab foundations. Subterranean infestations require targeted perimeter trenching and sub-slab termiticide injections.

### 3. What is the difference between Section 1 and Section 2 on a California pest report?
Under California Structural Pest Control Board regulations, Section 1 items identify active infestations, infections, or direct structural damage caused by wood-destroying organisms. Section 2 items identify conditions conducive to future infestations (such as earth-to-wood contact or plumbing leaks) where no active pest activity is currently observed.

### 4. What legal rights do California buyers have if a seller conceals termite issues?
If a seller intentionally conceals known active termite colonies or past timber damage on the Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) or Seller Property Questionnaire (SPQ), the buyer can file a civil lawsuit for intentional misrepresentation, fraudulent concealment, and breach of statutory duty, seeking actual repair costs and legal fees under California Civil Code § 1102.13.
