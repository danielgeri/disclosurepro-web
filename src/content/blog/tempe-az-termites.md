## The Hidden Threat: Subterranean Termites in Tempe and Maricopa County

Tempe is located in the Salt River Valley within the Sonoran Desert. The local environment features alkaline desert soils, intense summer heat with temperatures regularly exceeding 115°F, seasonal monsoon downpours, and residential irrigation systems that maintain elevated subsurface moisture levels year-round.

### Why Tempe Housing Stock and Irrigation Elevate Termite Risk

Several distinct architectural styles, foundation designs, and agricultural legacies accelerate wood-destroying insect activity across Tempe:

- **Flood Irrigation and Subsurface Soil Saturation:** Many historic and established Tempe neighborhoods—including Maple-Ash, Broadmor, and Alameda Park—feature traditional SRP (Salt River Project) flood irrigation. Saturating residential yards with several inches of standing water every few weeks creates sustained sub-slab moisture that enables subterranean termite colonies to flourish in an otherwise arid climate.
- **Slab-on-Grade Concrete Construction and Plumbing Penetrations:** Most homes built in Tempe between the 1950s and the present sit on monolithic concrete slabs. Concrete inevitably develops expansion cracks and cold joints over time. Subterranean termites require an opening only 1/32nd of an inch wide to enter through plumbing rough-ins beneath kitchen islands, bathtubs, and laundry rooms.
- **Buried Stucco Weep Screeds and Desert Landscaping:** In newer and remodeled Tempe residences, exterior stucco finishes frequently terminate below finished grade, buried under decorative granite gravel, flagstone patios, or raised garden planters. When the metal weep screed is obscured, termites construct mud shelter tubes directly into exterior wall framing without leaving any visible evidence on the exterior foundation.
- **Architectural Vulnerabilities Across Tempe Neighborhoods:**
  - *Maple-Ash and University Heights:* 1940s to 1960s red brick, slump block, and wood-framed homes. Original wood-framed interior partition walls and wood roof rafters directly contact masonry perimeter walls that harbor subterranean entry pathways.
  - *Broadmor and Alameda Park:* 1950s and 1960s ranch-style homes with finished Arizona rooms and enclosed patios. Cold joints between the original slab and subsequent concrete pours provide direct subterranean entry corridors into load-bearing wall studs.
  - *Optimist Park and Shalimar:* 1970s and 1980s stucco single-family residences. High summer attic temperatures drive subterranean termites down into interior drywall backing paper and door framing where moisture is trapped by HVAC condensation.

### Pest Biology: Desert Subterranean (*Heterotermes aureus*) and Western Subterranean (*Reticulitermes hesperus*)

Tempe properties face significant risk from specialized desert wood-destroying insects:
- **Desert Subterranean Termites (*Heterotermes aureus*):** Uniquely adapted to extreme desert conditions, this species can actively forage at temperatures above 110°F. They build slender, drop-tube mud structures from ceiling drywall and wall studs and can feed on dry wood timbers that other termite species avoid.
- **Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*):** Highly active during the cooler winter and spring months and following summer monsoon rains. They live in large subterranean colonies numbering in the hundreds of thousands, rapidly consuming structural softwoods along the grain.
- **Concealed Internal Feeding:** Termites consume framing timbers and drywall cellulose from the inside out, leaving a paper-thin veneer of intact paint on the surface. Extensive structural damage can occur before any visible outward warning signs appear.

## Arizona Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Legal Duties and Landmark Precedents

Arizona real estate transactions are governed by comprehensive statutory disclosure rules and robust common law seller disclosure obligations.

### The Arizona Residential Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS)

In Arizona, sellers complete the standard **Residential Seller’s Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS)** created by the Arizona Association of REALTORS® (AAR):

- **Section 4: Environmental & Pest Conditions:** The seller must explicitly answer:
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present wood-destroying insects or organisms (termites, powder post beetles) on the Property?"*
  - *"Are you aware of any past or present damage to the Property caused by wood-destroying insects or organisms?"*
  - *"Has the Property been treated for termites or wood-destroying insects? If yes, provide dates, treatment types, and warranty information."*
  - *"Is there a current termite warranty or transferable service agreement on the Property?"*
- **Section 3: Building and Safety Information:** Mandates disclosure of slab settlement, unpermitted structural additions, roof leaks, and plumbing leaks that create conducive moisture conditions for pest colonies.

### The Landmark Arizona Precedent: *Hill v. Jones* (1986)

Arizona holds a unique place in national real estate disclosure law due to the landmark Arizona Supreme Court ruling in ***Hill v. Jones* (1986)**:
- In *Hill v. Jones*, the court established that a seller has an affirmative common law duty to disclose all known material facts that materially affect the value or desirability of the property—specifically regarding termite damage and treatment history—even if the buyer fails to ask directly.
- The court ruled that an "As-Is" provision in an Arizona purchase contract does not protect a seller from liability for fraudulent non-disclosure or intentional concealment of known termite history.

### The Arizona Wood-Destroying Insect Inspection Report (WDIR)

In Arizona, pest inspections must be performed by certified inspectors licensed by the **Arizona Department of Agriculture - Pest Management Division (PMD)**. The inspector issues the official **Wood-Destroying Insect Inspection Report (WDIR)**:
- **Section II (Findings):** Documents active infestations, prior infestations, mud tubes, damaged wood, and visible evidence of prior chemical drill holes in exterior concrete slabs or garage floors.
- **Section IV (Inaccessible Areas):** Highlights obstructed zones, including finished garage drywall, sub-slab plumbing rough-ins, crawl areas with insufficient clearance, or foundation walls obscured by landscaping.

### What Tempe Sellers Reveal vs. Conceal

Sellers in Maricopa County frequently attempt to minimize past termite history on the SPDS:
- *"Perimeter preventive treatment completed in 2022."* (Conceals that mud shelter tubes were discovered hanging from the master bedroom ceiling drywall).
- *"Replaced damaged drywall in hallway during remodel."* (Hides the fact that structural studs had been hollowed out by desert subterranean termites).
- *"Standard Arizona home maintenance."* Under Arizona law, downplaying significant structural damage as routine maintenance does not shield a seller from liability under *Hill v. Jones*.

### The Risk of Disconnected Clues

Material termite hazards rarely appear as an explicit red flag in a single document. Instead, critical indicators are fragmented across multiple reports:
- The Home Inspector writes on page 42: *"Monsoon irrigation pooling detected along south foundation wall; stucco terminates below finished dirt grade."*
- The PMD Pest Inspector writes in Section IV: *"Finished sheetrock and built-in closet organizers in master suite prevented visual inspection of bottom plates."*
- The Seller checks "Yes" to prior treatment on the SPDS, noting: *"Routine preventive termite barrier renewed with local exterminator in 2021."*

Evaluated independently, each note appears ordinary. But when cross-referenced, they reveal a dangerous scenario: flood irrigation water pooled along the foundation, subterranean termites entered behind the buried stucco, and traveled through the bottom plates behind the master closet while the homeowner maintained only a perimeter spray service.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Termites in Tempe

### 1. Are termites common in Tempe, Arizona?
Yes. Desert Subterranean Termites (*Heterotermes aureus*) and Western Subterranean Termites (*Reticulitermes hesperus*) are prevalent throughout Tempe and Maricopa County. Residential flood irrigation, automated landscape watering, concrete slab expansion joints, and buried stucco weep screeds create optimal conditions for colonies to thrive.

### 2. What are Arizona sellers legally required to disclose regarding termites?
Under Arizona common law established in *Hill v. Jones* and standard AAR contract requirements, sellers must complete the Residential Seller’s Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS). Sellers are legally obligated to disclose all known active infestations, prior structural damage, historical treatments, and active warranty agreements.

### 3. What is an Arizona WDIR report?
A Wood-Destroying Insect Inspection Report (WDIR) is an official inspection document prepared by a structural pest inspector licensed by the Arizona Department of Agriculture - Pest Management Division (PMD). It details visible evidence of active pests, prior damage, past treatment drill holes, conducive conditions, and inaccessible structural areas.

### 4. Does homeowners insurance cover termite damage in Arizona?
No. Standard homeowners insurance policies across Arizona exclude damage caused by termites, dry rot, and wood-destroying organisms. Insurance carriers classify termite damage as a preventable maintenance issue, requiring homeowners to pay for all structural and cosmetic repairs out of pocket.
