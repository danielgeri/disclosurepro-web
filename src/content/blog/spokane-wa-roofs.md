## Why Spokane Roofs Fail: Heavy Snow Loads, Ice Dams, and Pine Needle Decay

Understanding why residential roofs deteriorate in the Inland Northwest requires an examination of local building styles and severe winter weather patterns.

### The Spokane Ice Dam Cycle
Ice dams are the single most destructive roofing phenomenon in Eastern Washington:
- **Mechanics of Ice Dam Formation:** In older homes across South Hill and Shadle Park, heat escapes from living areas into poorly insulated or improperly ventilated attics. This heat warms the underside of the roof deck, melting the bottom layer of accumulated snow. The meltwater runs down the roof pitch until it reaches the cold unheated eaves and gutters, where it promptly refreezes.
- **Water Backing Up Beneath Shingles:** As the freeze-thaw cycle repeats, a thick wall of ice builds up along the roof edge. Trapped meltwater ponds behind the ice barrier and is forced backward underneath the shingles. It penetrates roofing felt, soaks through wooden sheathing, rots fascia boards, and trickles down interior exterior walls.

### Heavy Snow Load and Structural Sagging
Winter storms in the Inland Northwest frequently drop heavy, moisture-laden snow:
- **Dead Weight on Aging 2x4 Rafters:** Historic bungalows built in the early 1900s often utilized 2x4 dimensional lumber rafters spaced on 24-inch centers, designed well before modern snow load engineering standards were adopted. Heavy accumulations—particularly when combined with rain-on-snow events—cause noticeable roof deflection and rafter bowing.
- **Split and Sagging Skip Sheathing:** Many historic Spokane roofs originally used 1-inch spaced "skip sheathing" designed for cedar wood shakes. When previous owners installed modern asphalt shingles over this open sheathing without laying solid plywood, the heavy snow loads crack the unsupported shingle joints.

### Ponderosa Pine Needle Accumulation and Moss Infestation
Spokane is proudly known as the "Lilac City," but its towering Ponderosa pine canopy creates an ongoing maintenance headache:
- **Acidic Needle Traps in Valleys:** Pine needles accumulate rapidly in roof valleys, behind chimneys, and in gutters. As the needles decompose, they release tannic acid that breaks down the protective asphalt shingle granules and accelerates flashing corrosion.
- **Pervasive Moss and Lichen Growth:** Shaded north-facing roof slopes stay damp for weeks beneath Spokane’s pine trees. Moss spores flourish, sending micro-roots beneath shingle tabs. As the moss grows, it lifts the shingles, breaking the wind seal and allowing wind-driven rain to penetrate.

## Washington Real Estate Disclosure Laws: Understanding Form 17

Washington State provides clear statutory disclosure rules designed to protect residential purchasers.

### The Washington Seller Disclosure Statement (RCW 64.06)
Under RCW 64.06.020, sellers of residential real estate must deliver to the buyer the standardized **Washington Form 17 (Seller Disclosure Statement)** within five business days of mutual acceptance:
- **Section 4: Structural and Roof Inquiries:** Form 17 contains explicit, mandatory questions regarding the roof system:
  - *"How old is the roof?"* (The seller must provide the approximate age or state "Don't Know").
  - *"Has the roof leaked within the last 5 years?"* (Must answer Yes, No, or Don't Know).
  - *"If yes, has it been repaired?"* (Requires full explanation).
  - *"Has there been any damage to the roof or other structure caused by fire, wind, flood, or other catastrophe?"*
- **Section 1: Title and Encumbrances:** Requires disclosure of any contractor liens or roofing warranties that may or may not transfer.

### Buyer Rescission Rights (RCW 64.06.030)
Washington law grants buyers a powerful statutory remedy:
- **Three-Day Rescission Window:** Once the buyer receives Form 17, they have three business days to examine the disclosures and cancel the transaction for any reason, receiving a full return of their earnest money deposit.
- **Consequences of Non-Delivery:** If the seller fails to provide Form 17, the buyer retains the right to rescind the contract up until the moment of closing.

### Limits of Form 17 and Legal Recourse for Fraud
Under Washington jurisprudence (*Alejandre v. Bull*, 159 Wn.2d 674), Form 17 does not constitute a warranty. If a seller answers "Don't Know" or conceals an active leak, the buyer cannot easily sue for breach of contract. 

However, if a seller actively misrepresents the condition of the roof or takes deceptive actions to hide water damage (such as spraying KILZ primer over ceiling water stains without fixing an active ice dam leak), the buyer can pursue claims for **fraudulent concealment** or **negligent misrepresentation**.

## The Manual Review Problem: Navigating Spokane Disclosure Files

In Eastern Washington transactions using standard Northwest Multiple Listing Service (NWMLS) purchase and sale agreements, the **Inspection Contingency (NWMLS Form 35)** gives buyers a narrow window—typically 10 calendar days—to complete their physical due diligence.

During this tight timeframe, buyers must dissect an intimidating stack of records:
1. The completed Washington Form 17.
2. A 50-to-70-page private home inspection report with routine winter disclaimers (e.g., *"Roof surface covered by 4 inches of snow and ice; roof inspected from ground and attic access only"*).
3. City of Spokane Development Services historical permit records for re-roofing or structural alterations.
4. Invoices from roofing contractors, chimney sweeps, or pest control operators.

Winter purchases in Spokane present extreme risks: when a roof is blanketed in snow, visual roof inspections are virtually impossible from the outside. Buyers who fail to cross-reference subtle clues in Form 17—such as a seller admitting to *"past gutter clearing"* or *"minor interior paint touch-ups"*—often find themselves paying for catastrophic hidden rot once the spring thaw arrives.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Roof Issues in Spokane

### 1. Does the City of Spokane require building permits for residential re-roofing?
Yes. The City of Spokane Development Services department requires a building permit for residential roof replacements whenever old roofing is torn off down to the decking or when structural sheathing is repaired or replaced. The city enforces the International Residential Code (IRC), requiring ice barrier underlayment along eaves in cold climate zones.

### 2. Is ice-and-water shield required on roofs in Spokane?
Yes. Under the Washington State Building Code and Spokane municipal amendments, an approved ice barrier (self-adhering polymer-modified bitumen sheet) is mandatory. It must extend from the lowest roof edge to a point at least 24 inches inside the exterior wall line of the building to prevent water intrusion from ice damming.

### 3. How do Ponderosa pine needles damage roofs in Spokane?
Ponderosa pine needles collect in roof valleys, behind chimneys, and in gutters. They retain moisture against the shingles for weeks at a time, preventing them from drying out and promoting thick moss growth. Furthermore, decaying needles release acidic tannins that dissolve the protective mineral granules on asphalt shingles, causing premature cracking and leaks.

### 4. What should a homebuyer do if snow covers the roof during an inspection?
If snow prevents a physical roof inspection, a buyer should never simply waive the contingency. Instead, negotiate an **Inspection Contingency Extension** until the snow melts, require an escrow holdback of funds pending a post-thaw inspection, or hire an inspector with high-resolution attic thermal imaging equipment to thoroughly inspect the roof framing from inside the attic.
